# Chinese Brick MOC Tutorial: From First Build to Original Design

Chinese brick brands have grown fast, and more builders than ever are trying MOC (My Own Creation) building as a result. Where an international-brand parts order can run into the hundreds, domestic brands make it cheap enough to experiment freely. This guide walks you from your very first rebuild to original designs, covering parts selection, technique and structure along the way.

## Why Build MOCs with Chinese Bricks?

Chinese bricks have improved noticeably in recent years on mould precision, clutch power and the range of available parts. On value alone, the same budget buys roughly 3-5 times as many parts as the international brands. For MOC builders that means a much lower cost of failure — you can try a risky structure without wincing at the parts bill. On top of that, many Chinese brands produce exclusive specialised parts and colours that open up ideas you simply cannot build with the standard palette.

## Getting Started: Your First Steps

### 1. Start by Rebuilding Someone Else's Model

If you are new to MOC building, begin by rebuilding somebody else's design rather than inventing from scratch. [brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn) hosts a large library of free MOC instructions, from simple vehicles to complex architecture. Pick a design that interests you, gather the parts from its parts list, and build it. Working through an existing model teaches you the common building techniques and shows you what each part is actually good for.

### 2. Learn the Core Building Techniques

**SNOT** (Studs Not On Top) is one of the central MOC techniques. It lets you attach parts in non-standard orientations, which gives you far more sculptural freedom. The stud precision on Chinese bricks is now good enough to hold SNOT assemblies together reliably.

**Layer-by-layer building**: whether you are designing a building or a vehicle, split the model into three layers — base, main body and top — and design each one separately. The advantage is that revisions stay cheap: if one section is not working, you rebuild that layer only instead of starting over.

For a deeper look at technique, read our companion article: [Brick Building Basics: Techniques and Structural Design](/en/blog/brick-building-basics).

## Leveling Up: From Copying to Original Design

### 1. Build Your Own Parts Library

What usually limits a MOC is not the idea but the parts bin. Sort your parts by colour and by function, and keep generous quantities of the everyday basics (1x1, 1x2 and 2x4 bricks) plus the specialised pieces — hinges, slopes and curved elements. Loose Chinese bricks are inexpensive enough that buying several hundred basic parts at once costs very little.

### 2. Learn Digital Design Tools

**BrickLink Studio** and **LDraw** are the two mainstream brick design programs, and both are completely free. Designing on a computer lets you adjust and preview at any time, and it generates a parts list and step-by-step building instructions automatically. Chinese bricks are fully dimensionally compatible with the international standard, so these tools work exactly the same way with them.

### 3. Take Inspiration from the Real World

Architecture, vehicles, machinery, creatures — anything can be an MOC subject. Start with objects you know well: the housing estate you live in, the bus you take every day, a character you like. Photograph your reference from several angles, then try to capture its defining shapes in brick. Block out the rough silhouette in software first, then refine it gradually.

### 4. Get Structural Stability Right

A good MOC has to be sturdy as well as good-looking. A few key principles:

- **Stagger the joints**: as in a real brick wall, upper bricks should bridge the seams between the bricks below. Continuous vertical joints running through the model make it weak.
- **Fill the core**: large buildings need plenty of basic bricks packed inside to form a solid skeleton. Hollow shell construction tends to fall apart the moment you move it.
- **Use the right connectors**: pins, axles and clips dramatically increase structural strength.

For more on designing sturdier models, read: [Advanced Structural Design: Balancing Stability and Aesthetics](/en/blog/advanced-structural-design).

## Common Questions and Fixes

**Q: Is the clutch power on Chinese bricks strong enough?**
A: The leading Chinese brands are now very close to international-brand clutch power. If an individual part is too loose or too tight, contact the seller for a replacement, or apply a small amount of wax at the connection point to adjust the fit.

**Q: How do I find the right parts?**
A: Every instruction page on [brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn) includes a detailed parts list. You can copy the list straight into a marketplace search, or use BrickLink's buying tools to match sellers automatically.

**Q: How should I display and share a finished model?**
A: Photograph it and upload it to the BrickPlans community to swap notes with other builders. Shoot in natural light or under a softbox, keep the background plain, and cover the model from several angles so the details read clearly.

## How to Keep Improving

MOC building is a journey with no finish line. Every new model will show you techniques and possibilities you had overlooked before. Join a Chinese brick MOC community and spend your time looking, asking, disassembling and revising. The Chinese brick ecosystem is developing fast, which makes right now an excellent moment to start.

And when you are done, upload your work to [brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn) — every set of instructions you share can become the starting point for the next MOC builder.
