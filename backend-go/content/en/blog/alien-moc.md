# Alien MOC Review: 1,400-Piece Mech and UFO Build

The short version: this alien MOC instruction set is genuinely inventive. Around 1,400 pieces build one alien mech and one small flying saucer, four of those parts are light bricks, and the whole package comes to roughly ¥180. Sci-fi builders should give it a look — just plan on supplying the button cells yourself, because the light bricks do not include batteries.

## 1. Design and Accuracy

The alien mech is modelled on classic sci-fi silhouettes, and a light-up brain element is visible inside the trans-green dome. Accuracy to the reference look is about 85%. The saucer pairs a dish-shaped base with a trans-clear cockpit, measures roughly 25 cm across, and sits on three landing struts underneath. The set leans hard on purple-and-green contrast for its alien feel — displayed side by side, the two models read as a small off-world scene.

## 2. Building Experience

The manual runs 240 steps, and the test build took about 6 hours. The mech's legs use a double-joint design, so it stands stably once finished. The saucer's curved shell has to be layered plate by plate in numbered order, and that is where your patience gets tested. The four light elements sit in the dome, the cockpit and the underside of the saucer — press to switch them on. The battery compartment is small, so button cells are the sensible choice.

## 3. Value and Playability

Instructions cost about ¥28 and the parts pack about ¥152, for a total near ¥180 — roughly ¥0.129 per piece, which is a normal price for sci-fi MOCs. The mech stands about 30 cm tall with 12 points of articulation, enough to hold a range of combat poses. The rare parts are mainly trans-green and glow pieces, making up around 12% of the count. **Q: Is the alien MOC hard to build?**
