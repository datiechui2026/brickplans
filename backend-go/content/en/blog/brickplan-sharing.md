# Sharing LEGO MOC Instructions: Platforms, Steps and Copyright

Anyone who builds with bricks has run into the term MOC, short for My Own Creation. The moment you stop following someone else's manual and start designing a model yourself, a clean set of instructions becomes the best possible home for that work. Sharing those instructions is what turns a private build into something other people can actually construct — and what connects builders around the world. Below is a look at why instruction sharing matters, where to do it, how to do it well, and what to watch out for on the copyright side.

## Why sharing instructions matters

Sharing a build file is never just "uploading a document." It carries three kinds of value.

**Continuity and inspiration.** Many classic models have influenced generation after generation of builders precisely because their instructions were published openly. Later builders can recreate them, study the structural techniques inside, and then push the idea further. A good set of instructions is a teacher that never speaks.

**Community connection.** When you publish your MOC instructions, what comes back might be thousands of likes, feedback from builders on the other side of the world, or even lasting friendships. Sharing turns solo building into collective creation.

**Personal growth.** Turning a design process into clear instructions is itself a review of your own work, and it noticeably sharpens both your design ability and your ability to explain what you built.

## Platforms and communities for sharing instructions

There is no shortage of channels today. They roughly fall into four groups:

- **Large general brick communities:** international platforms such as BrickLink and Rebrickable are the main base for many experienced builders, bundling instructions, parts lists and player communities in one place.
- **Domestic communities and forums:** active builder and MOC circles also exist closer to home, where instructions and building tips are exchanged in forums, message boards and group chats. The atmosphere is friendly and the material is plentiful in the local language.
- **Content platforms:** video and social platforms such as Bilibili, Douyin and Xiaohongshu host plenty of builders sharing MOC work and instruction tutorials. Video is an intuitive way to show the build process.
- **Dedicated instruction sites:** these focus purely on publishing, managing and downloading instructions, usually with a parts list and step-by-step building notes attached. The experience is cleaner and more focused.

Audience and style vary widely between platforms. Newcomers can start with local communities and content platforms, then gradually expand to the international ones.

## How to share your own MOC instructions

Getting your work out there is easier than it sounds. Follow these steps and you will rarely go wrong.

**Step 1: Tidy up the design.** Finish the model in building software such as BrickLink Studio or LEGO Digital Designer. Make sure the structure is stable and that every part is actually available, then generate a clear parts list (usually called a BOM).

**Step 2: Produce the instructions.** Break the build down into step-by-step instructions or a video so others can follow along. Clear step numbering and highlighted parts make an enormous difference to the experience.

**Step 3: Photograph and write an introduction.** Shoot the finished model from several angles, then add a short description covering the concept, the design inspiration and the part count, so readers grasp the essentials at a glance.

**Step 4: Pick the right platform and publish.** Choose a community or a dedicated instruction site depending on what the model is, then upload the instructions, the parts list and the notes, and label the part system used and the difficulty level.

**Step 5: Keep the conversation going.** After publishing, reply to comments, collect feedback and iterate on your design based on what builders tell you.

## Copyright points to keep in mind

Copyright is the part of instruction sharing that needs the most care. Keep these points firmly in mind:

- **Respect original work.** Do not copy, repost or sell someone else's instructions without permission. Crediting the original author is basic courtesy when you share.
- **Know the line between reference and copying.** Drawing inspiration from another build is normal, but lifting the core structure wholesale and putting your own name on it is infringement.
- **Read the license.** Many instructions carry terms such as Creative Commons licenses. Read carefully what uses are permitted, including whether commercial use, modification and re-sharing are allowed.
- **Be careful with commercial use.** If your instructions will be used commercially, make sure you hold the full intellectual property rights or that you have been properly authorized.
- **Protect your own rights too.** If someone misappropriates your work, learn to defend your interests through platform complaints and similar channels.
