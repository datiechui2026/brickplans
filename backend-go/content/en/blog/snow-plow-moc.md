# Snow Plow MOC Build Guide: Lifting Blade and Orange Body

Here is the short version: this snow plow MOC uses about 1,800 pieces to build a front-blade plow roughly 45 cm long. Expect about 7 hours of building, a budget of around 360 CNY, and a medium difficulty. The two things that make it worth building are the front blade that actually raises and lowers, and the bright orange bodywork that looks great parked in a winter scene.

Snow plows are one of the most recognizable machines in any winter layout: a heavy front blade, a lifting linkage, and a cheerful orange cab that instantly says "it snowed last night." This guide walks through the build in four steps so you can get the blade mechanism, the cab, and the lighting details right the first time.

## Step 1: Chassis and Frame

Start with the chassis. Use extended Technic beams as the longitudinal rails and fix the front and rear axles to cross beams so the whole vehicle carries weight without flexing. Leave mounting holes in the front axle area for the blade lifting mechanism, and fit wide dual tires at the rear to sell the snow-ready look. Once the frame is together, push it across the table to check it rolls smoothly. This foundation decides whether every mechanism you add later stays solid.

## Step 2: Blade and Lifting Mechanism

The blade is the visual center of the whole model. Build the large curved face from curved bricks, shape the cutting edge with slope bricks, and run a ring of reinforcing ribs along the inside so the face does not deform. For the lifting mechanism, two parallel linkages plus a support rod work best: turn the handle on the side and the linkages raise or lower the entire blade, with a travel of about 6 cm off the ground, and pins lock it at any height. Make sure the linkage anchor points land on the main chassis rails, not on the front bodywork, or the assembly will come apart when the blade is raised to its top position.

## Step 3: Body and Cab

Orange dominates the body. Use SNOT (studs not on top) side building to round off the silhouette, add reflective stripe stickers along the sides, and mount an orange warning light base on the roof. Inside the cab, fit a seat, steering wheel, and mirrors, and use transparent parts for the windshield so the interior stays visible. At the rear, add a removable storage box for spare parts or the snow chunks used in your scene. It is practical and it photographs well.

## Step 4: Lighting and Details

Finish with lights and small details. Put a transparent orange round plate or an LED element inside the warning light, use transparent parts for the headlights, and add reflective stickers front and rear. For extra character, hang a small snow shovel accessory on the side of the vehicle and add a tow hook at the back so it can pair with a salt spreader or a snowman scene. The finished model is about 45 cm long; surround it with white plates for snow, pine trees, and minifigures, and the winter atmosphere takes care of itself.

## FAQ

**Q: How does the front blade raise and lower?**

A: Two parallel linkages plus a support rod. Turning the side handle raises or lowers the blade with about 6 cm of travel, and pins lock it at any height. Anchoring the linkage to the chassis rails keeps it stable.

**Q: How much does this snow plow MOC cost?**

A: Around 360 CNY. The model uses roughly 1,800 pieces. The orange body panels and the curved blade pieces need to be ordered specifically from the BrickPlan parts list; most of the rest are common Technic parts, so digging through your own bins saves a fair amount.

**Q: Are the parts easy to find?**

A: Yes. Apart from the curved blade and the orange panels, more than 90% of the parts are common. You can buy them individually on BrickLink or from domestic brick parts shops, and ordering step by step from the list gets everything to your door within a week.

**Q: How do I display it in a winter scene?**

A: Lay white plates for the snow, add a snowman MOC, pine trees, and a minifigure shoveling snow, and use white round plates under the blade to simulate plowing. Add street lamps and light elements and you have a winter night scene.
