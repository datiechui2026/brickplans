# Brick Parts Guide: Every LEGO Part Type Explained

> When you first start building MOCs, a parts list can look like a foreign language. What is the difference between a "1x2 brick" and a "1x2 plate"? And what exactly are tiles and plates?

Don't worry — this guide walks you through the whole classification system for brick parts. **Get these categories straight and your ability to read a plan improves by roughly 80%.**

---

## 1. Why Learn How Parts Are Categorized?

Knowing the categories is not just about naming things. It helps you:

1. **Pick parts fast**: you know where to look when a step calls for something specific
2. **Read plans properly**: parts lists and step images stop looking like a riddle
3. **Design better**: choosing the right part type makes a model sturdier and cleaner
4. **Buy accurately**: you can describe exactly what you need on a second-hand marketplace or in a parts shop

---

## 2. The Six Main Categories

### 1. Bricks

**Key trait**: a brick is three plates tall — the workhorse of any build.

| Common type | How it looks | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| 1x1 brick | a single small cube | filling gaps, decoration, texture detail |
| 1x2 brick | two studs wide | the most-used basic brick |
| 1x4 brick | four studs wide | walls, beams, columns |
| 1x6 / 1x8 brick | longer | wide spans |
| 2x2 brick | square | corners, load-bearing walls |
| 2x4 brick | the standard brick | LEGO's most iconic part |

**MOC tip**: one long brick (1x6 or 1x8) holds better than several short bricks butted together, but short bricks are how you build a bumpy, textured surface.

---

### 2. Plates

**Key trait**: a plate is one third of a brick in height — the fine-tuning magician.

| Common type | Use |
|---|---|
| 1x1 plate | adjusting height by a hair, filling gaps |
| 1x2 plate | the most-used plate |
| 1x4 / 1x6 plate | wall decoration, roofing |
| 2x2 plate | square filling |
| 2x4 plate | floors, bases |
| 2x6 / 2x8 plate | large baseplates |

**MOC tip**: three plates stacked equal the height of one brick. This 3:1 ratio is the single most useful adjustment trick in MOC building.

---

### 3. Tiles

**Key trait**: no studs on top — the surface is completely smooth.

**What they are for:**
- floors, tabletops, roads
- decorative walls (a smooth face looks more realistic)
- carrying printed text or patterns
- the surface a minifigure stands on

**Common sizes**: 1x1 tile, 1x2 tile, 1x4 tile, 2x2 tile, 2x4 tile

**MOC tip**: in architectural MOCs, tiled floors look far more convincing than plain bricks. In cyberpunk scenes, tiles plus transparent parts equal neon lighting.

---

### 4. Slopes

**Key trait**: parts with an angled face, used for roofs, car noses, wings and more.

| Common type | Angle | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 30° slope | 30 degrees | roofs, ramps |
| 45° slope | 45 degrees | car fronts, castle roofs |
| inverted slope | slopes the other way | underside of eaves |
| curved slope | curved surface | rounded shapes |

**MOC tip**: use 45° slopes for castle roofs and 30° slopes for modern architecture — the result matches real-world proportions much better.

---

### 5. Technic Elements

**Key trait**: parts with holes and pins, used for mechanical structures and moving features.

**Core parts:**
- **Technic bricks and beams**: hole-punched beam parts, the skeleton of a build
- **Pins**: the connectors that join Technic beams
- **Gears**: drive and steering
- **Axles**: transferring rotational movement
- **Linkages**: parallelogram mechanisms
- **Tracks**: tanks, excavators and similar

**MOC tip**: mech MOCs need Technic parts at the joints. Joints built from basic bricks alone simply cannot carry the load.

---

### 6. Special Elements

**Key trait**: the odd-shaped parts that fit nowhere else, each with a very specific job.

| Type | Examples | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Door and window frames | doors and windows in many sizes | building openings |
| Fences and railings | 1x2x2 fence | balconies, barriers |
| Wheels and tires | many sizes | vehicles |
| Plants | leaves, flowers, trees | natural scenes |
| Minifigure accessories | weapons, tools, hats | dressing figures |
| Printed parts | bricks and plates with patterns | decoration, signage |
| Transparent parts | bricks in clear colors | windows, lighting effects, magic |

## 3. A Guide to Part Colors

The color range of brick parts is enormous. Here is how the common ones break down:

| Color family | Common colors | Where it works |
|---|---|---|
| Neutral | white, black, gray, dark gray | building bodies, mechs, vehicles |
| Warm | red, orange, yellow, brown | castles, vehicles, fire |
| Cool | blue, light blue, green, purple | water, sci-fi, cyberpunk |
| Special | transparent, pearl, metallic | windows, effects, decoration |

**MOC tip**: beginners should stick to 3-4 colors so the palette stays coherent. Classic combinations:
- Architecture: white + gray + brown (natural look)
- Mechs: gray + black + red (combat look)
- Sci-fi: silver-gray + blue + transparent blue (tech look)

## 4. The BrickLink Part Number System

If you plan to buy parts online, BrickLink part numbers are the worldwide ID card for a part.

**Format examples:**
```
3001 - 2x4 Brick
3003 - 1x1 Brick
3023 - 1x2 Plate
3068 - 2x2 Tile
```

**How to find a part number:**
1. In BrickLink Studio, right-click the part and choose "Part ID"
2. Search the part description on the BrickLink website
3. Check the parts list in your instructions

## 6. Common Questions

**Q: How do I tell a brick from a plate?**
A: Look at the height. A brick is three plates tall. The quickest test: the side of a brick has three raised ridges, a plate has only one.

**Q: Why won't my parts clutch tightly?**
A: It may be a batch issue (different brands use molds of different precision), or simply worn parts. The safest approach is to mix parts from the same brand.

**Q: What is SNOT?**
A: SNOT means Studs Not On Top — a technique where parts are attached sideways instead of upward. It is extremely common for building facades and mech detailing.
