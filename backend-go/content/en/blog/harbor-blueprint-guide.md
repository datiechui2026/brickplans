# Port Brick MOC Tutorial: From Docks to Containers

## Introduction

Ports are one of the most rewarding themes in brick MOC (My Own Creation) building. A busy quay, a towering gantry crane, neatly stacked containers and tugs threading through the harbor add up to a scene full of life. This tutorial walks you through a complete **port blueprint** build, from the basic modules to the advanced tricks, so you can put together a working harbor of your own.

## 1. Core Modules of a Port MOC

### Dock Platform

The dock is the foundation of the whole port. Build it on dark gray and light gray baseplates to get a concrete look. Line the dock edge with dark bricks to simulate rubber bumpers, and leave a mooring bollard at regular intervals. A platform height of 8-12 bricks is a good target, since it leaves room for the water effect below.

### Container Yard

Containers are the signature element of any port. Build standard boxes from 2x2 or 2x4 bricks, with a suggested size of 6x2x3 studs. Mix colors (blue, red, orange, green, white) to suggest heavy freight traffic. A **port blueprint** usually marks out the placement layout, so stack in layers and offset the boxes: it looks better and holds together more firmly.

### Gantry Crane

The gantry crane is the focal point of a port MOC. Build the main structure from technic frames, with a horizontal rail span of 20-30 studs. The boom can be designed as a raising and lowering assembly, with gears and string standing in for the real operation. The base needs to be solid, so use a large baseplate together with counterweight bricks.

## 2. Step-by-Step Build

### Step 1: Plan the Layout

Draw a floor plan before you build: dock platform 40%, container yard 30%, buildings and roads 30%. Reserve a channel entrance and vehicle lanes.

### Step 2: Build the Water

Lay transparent blue bricks as the base, then add 2-3 layers to build up a sense of depth. The water area should extend about one third beyond the dock platform, leaving room for boats to berth. Use white studs along the water's edge to suggest foam.

### Step 3: Build the Main Dock

Start at the dock platform and work upward layer by layer. Build the load-bearing frame first, then lay the surface. Every 6 studs, reserve a mooring bollard position (a 1x1 round brick paired with a string piece). Add details such as fire hydrants and light posts along the dock edge.

### Step 4: The Container Yard

Lay the ground markings first (white 1x1 tiles for the driving lanes), then stack the containers. Keep each stack 2-3 layers high and alternate colors so the yard reads as layered. Put a loading zone in one corner of the yard, connected to the gantry crane rail.

### Step 5: Install the Gantry Crane

Build the rails from technic beams with holes, and check that everything slides smoothly. Use a symmetrical design for the main body: 4-6 studs wide for each of the two side columns, with a top beam that matches the width of the yard. Fit the boom with a pulley and string system so the hoist can actually be operated.

### Step 6: Detail Pass

| Detail element | Recommended parts | Purpose |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Light post | 1x1 round brick + transparent sphere | Adds a night-time look |
| Direction sign | 1x2 plate + printed piece | Raises the realism of the scene |
| Small forklift | 4x2 chassis + wheels | Brings the yard to life |
| Rest shelter | 4x4 baseplate + roof | Adds layers to the scene |

## 3. Advanced Techniques

### Color Strategy

Gray is the dominant tone for a port theme (dark gray and light gray), accented with blue for water and bright colors for containers. Avoid too many saturated colors, or the industrial realism of the scene falls apart.

### Modular Design

Build modularly: construct each functional area on its own, then join the modules together. This makes it much easier to modify, expand and transport the model.

### Minifigure Staging

Place a reasonable number of worker, operator and crew minifigures, along with small tool carts, to make the scene feel alive. Position the figures carefully so they do not block important architectural details.

## 4. Common Problems and Fixes

**Problem 1: The dock platform is not stable enough.**
Fix: Add cross-bracing inside the platform and reinforce it with technic axles.

**Problem 2: Containers keep toppling.**
Fix: Add anti-slip studs to the bottom of each container (a studded plate), and offset every layer by half a stud when stacking.

**Problem 3: The gantry crane does not slide smoothly.**
Fix: Check that the rails are level, align the pulley parts, and if needed rub a little wax along the rail to lubricate it.
