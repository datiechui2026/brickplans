# How to Build a Dinosaur Park MOC: Planning to Skeleton

Here is the short version: when you build a dinosaur park MOC, put 80% of your effort into scene planning and skeleton structure, and save the remaining 20% for decoration. Most beginner builds fall apart for the same reason — jumping straight into detail work, then discovering the proportions are out of control and the dinosaurs no longer fit on the baseplate.

## Step 1: Plan Your Scene Zones

Start with a 48x48 stud baseplate as your site. Divide it into three functional areas: the left 30% becomes the enclosure zone, the middle 40% is the dinosaur display area, and the right 30% handles the entrance and visitor zone. Lay down baseplate bricks in different colors to mark each zone so you can find your positions easily later on.

## Step 2: Build the Dinosaur Skeleton

Take a brachiosaurus as your example. Begin with a pelvis base made from a 6x10 stud section, then connect the legs using 2x2 turntable pieces so all four legs can swing forward and back.

For the neck, use three sets of 1x2 hinge pieces and raise it joint by joint, roughly 15 degrees per section. Eight sections will give you an elegant 60-degree neck curve. Build the tail from 10 sections of 1x1 single-stud pieces, tapering down as it goes.

## Step 3: Fill In Muscle and Skin

Once the skeleton is done, cover the joints with 2x4 curved bricks and slope bricks. Thicken the belly with three layers of 1x2 plates so the body reads as a streamlined silhouette from the side. For the head, use a 2x2 round plate for the eye socket and a 1x1 transparent red piece for the eye. The teeth are four 1x1 white slope bricks arranged in a staggered line.

## Step 4: Build the Base and Vegetation

Lay 2x2 tiles to form a pond, then ring the edge with 1x1 transparent green pieces to suggest light reflecting off the water. For trees, stack two 1x1 round bars six layers high to make the trunk, then build the canopy from three sets of 4x4 leaf pieces into a sphere about 8 studs across. Six trees across the whole forest is enough to create a sense of depth.

## Step 5: Add Interactive Details

Ring the animal pen with 1x2 fence pieces to create a 12x12 stud enclosure, and add a gate built from a 2x3 door panel at the entrance. Finish with a 2x2 printed tile as a sign reading "Dinosaur Zone" — and the whole scene immediately feels like a theme park.

## FAQ

**Q: My dinosaur skeleton keeps falling apart. How do I reinforce it?**

A: At the connection points, favor friction hinges and turntable pieces rather than forcing plain single-stud connections. After finishing each section, press down on it by hand to test the load, and add a 1x2 bar as a diagonal brace wherever something feels loose. That can improve overall stability by roughly 50%.

**Q: A 48x48 baseplate is too big and I don't have enough parts. What now?**

A: Scale the scene down to a 32x32 stud baseplate and shrink the dinosaur to 60% of its size, swapping 1x1 pieces in for 2x2 pieces. A BrickPlan starter pack usually contains more than 800 pieces, which is plenty to finish a compact version of the park.

**Q: I can't get the dinosaur head details right. Any tips?**

A: First position the eye socket with a 2x2 round plate, then build slopes around the socket layer by layer to shape the curve of the snout. Don't install all the teeth at once — place four first and check the proportions, then mirror the rest once you're happy. It looks more natural than filling the whole jaw in one go.
