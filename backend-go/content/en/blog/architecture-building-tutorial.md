# Brick Building Tutorial: Build Your First Architectural MOC

The first time I sat down to build a building seriously, the foundation is where I fell apart. I stacked two layers of baseplate, started throwing walls up on top, and by the third course everything was leaning. I had to tear it down and start over. What I learned is that in architectural MOC (My Own Creation) work, the hard part is not how fancy the facade looks. It is whether the foundation, the walls and the roof lock into each other as one structure. This **brick building tutorial** follows the order I actually built in: start at the foundation and work up until you have a building that stands firm and still has enough detail to be worth looking at.

## Why Are Brick Buildings So Popular?

Architectural building has stayed popular for decades because it combines engineering thinking with artistic expression. Every clutch of two bricks echoes the stonework of a real building, while the color choices and shaping test the builder's eye. Architectural building is actually quite friendly to beginners: no gears, no drivetrain, and if you get the structure right a small building stands up in front of you with very immediate satisfaction.

Before you start, it helps to browse some architectural MOC instructions on [BrickPlans](https://brickplan.cn) and study how different styles of building are put together. That gives you a quick overall sense of how architectural brick building works.

## Step 1: Foundation and Base Design

### Choosing a Baseplate Size

Every building starts with its base. For brick architecture, the baseplate sets the overall proportions and the footprint. Common sizes:

- **16x32 stud baseplate**: good for small standalone structures such as a cottage or a pavilion
- **32x32 stud baseplate**: the standard building size, with good load-bearing capacity, suitable for most architectural models
- **48x48 stud baseplate**: large buildings or scene compositions, which need internal support structure to go with them

### Foundation Building Techniques

The foundation is the critical layer that connects the baseplate to the walls. Use **plates** as your foundation material: lay down one layer of 2x4 or 2x6 plates on the baseplate first and make sure it is perfectly flat. If you plan a multi-story building, you also need to leave space in the foundation for load-bearing columns. Stacking **bricks** vertically into pillars spreads the weight of the structure above so it does not all land in one place.

## Step 2: Wall Structure and Window Openings

### The Basic Ways to Lay a Wall

There are several common approaches to brick building walls:

**Staggered bond** is the most basic wall-laying method, similar to how a real brick wall is laid: each course is offset by half a brick's length from the one below. This dramatically increases wall stability, and in any brick building tutorial it is the first skill a beginner has to master.

**Sandwich reinforcement** means inserting a layer of plates between every 4-6 courses of brick, creating a "sandwich" layer. This not only increases the wall's resistance to lateral force, it also visually mimics the floor-division lines of a real building.

### Handling Window and Door Openings

When you leave openings for windows and doors in a wall, you have to think about structural support above the opening. The simplest solution: place a **lintel brick (such as a 1x6 or 1x8 brick)** above the opening to spread the weight of the bricks above onto the walls on either side. This works well in small buildings. For large window areas, use **inverted arch bricks** to add load-bearing capacity.

## Step 3: Roof Construction in Several Styles

The roof is what makes a building. Different architectural styles call for different roof forms.

### Flat Roofs

A flat roof is structurally simple and suits modern-style buildings. Cover the top with plates, but remember to add a ring of railing or a decorative trim at the edge so the finish looks clean.

### Sloped Roofs

A pitched roof is the most common roof form in traditional architecture. Build it with **slope bricks** to create the pitch. One practical trick: first build a triangular gable out of bricks as support, then lay slope bricks from both sides of the gable toward the center. That keeps the roof pitch even. For **brick architectural MOC builders**, mastering sloped roofs is the key step to raising the quality of a model.

### Domes and Towers

For churches, castles and similar buildings, domes and towers are essential elements. A dome can be built with **curved slopes** narrowing course by course, or with dedicated dome pieces. For a tower, build the main body from **cylinder bricks** and top it off with cone-shaped pieces.

## Step 4: Facade Decoration and Detail

### Wall Texture

A perfectly flat wall looks dull. You can add texture in these ways:

- **Relief method**: use 1x1 bricks set at intervals and protruding from the wall to imitate stonework
- **Line method**: alternate tiles and studded bricks to create horizontal banding
- **Climbing plants**: fix green plant elements to the outside of the wall to imitate vines

### Door and Window Details

Add frames and sills to your windows, and use 1x1 bricks with holes combined with transparent elements to make window mullions; the windows will look far more refined. In the [BrickPlans](https://brickplan.cn) instruction library you can find plenty of designs for facade details, and studying them quickly improves your own detailing.

## Step 5: Landscaping and Scene Setting

A building does not exist in isolation, and sensible landscaping makes a model far more alive. Common landscape elements include:

- **Street trees and shrubs**: green plant elements with brown trunk pieces
- **Street lamps and street scenes**: thin, long pieces for the lamp posts, with transparent yellow elements to suggest the light
- **Ground paving**: 1x1 tiles in different colors for sidewalks and roadways

Among brick building techniques, scene setting often decides how a model finally reads. A good architectural MOC does not just have a handsome building; the surroundings have to match the theme too.

## Common Problems and Solutions

**Problem 1: The walls keep falling over.** Make sure the foundation is solid enough and that the bricks are pressed fully together with no gaps. Using an interlocking structure at wall corners greatly improves overall stability.

**Problem 2: There is a big gap between roof and wall.** Check whether the roof's support structure lines up with the top of the wall. You can lay a layer of plates across the top of the wall to level it before installing the roof.

**Problem 3: The proportions look off.** This is the most common beginner issue. Sketch the building on paper before you build, or use an instruction set from [BrickPlans](https://brickplan.cn) as a reference and build to its proportions.
