# MOC Mecha Blueprint Tutorial: Build From Frame to Armor

Mecha is one of the most popular MOC subjects, and also one of the most demanding. The model has to stand, hold a dynamic pose, and still feel armored and heavy. A common beginner mistake is to pile on outer armor first; the result often cannot stand up. This tutorial starts with the torso frame, then works through leg joints, shoulder armor, and weapons so you finish with a mecha that stands securely and can hold a pose. The goal is a readable silhouette, not maximum part count.

## Set the Scale and Humanoid Frame First

Before you build, choose a scale. A mecha is usually 4 to 8 times taller than a standard minifigure; around 1:8 is common. Build too tall and the joints struggle to carry the weight; build too short and fine detail becomes difficult. The frame is the foundation. The key is the central torso axis: use a sturdy Technic beam or a column of plates as the spine, then connect the chest and hips with large pieces. The spine decides whether the mecha can stand straight, so treat it as one continuous structure. The chest and hips should feel like two solid masses tied into that spine, not like separate sections stacked on top. Plan the center of gravity during this frame stage. Do not make the chest too heavy, or the model will lean backward. Get this step right and later changes become much easier.

## Leg Joints: Stability Comes First

Legs show whether the builder understands structure. Use ball joints or toothed hinge pieces at the hips and knees; they carry load well and can lock an angle. Make the lower legs wider, and use large baseplate-style pieces for the feet. More contact area means more stability. Give the legs a slight natural bend in the standing pose; it looks more powerful than two perfectly straight legs. The hip and knee are the load path, so if either one is loose, the whole model will feel unstable no matter how good the armor looks. Test the build by posing it on one leg. If the foot does not wobble and the knee does not collapse, the leg joints and center of gravity are strong enough. Continue upward only after that test.

## Torso and Arms: The Frame's Connection Hub

The torso frame needs shoulder structures that extend symmetrically from both sides of the spine. Use large ball joints at the shoulders so the arms can lift to horizontal or higher. Build a bone inside each arm first: run a beam from the shoulder to the wrist, then wrap the mechanical structure around it. The beam inside the arm is the strength; the outer parts are the shape and detail. This keeps the arm sturdy and gives it layers. You can leave a cockpit space in the chest and use a transparent piece as the canopy; it immediately changes the mecha's character. If you plan to fit a motor or battery inside the torso, reserve that space during the frame stage, not after the armor is closed.

## Shoulder Armor and Plating: Thick, Not Bulky

Armor is the mecha's second skin. Shoulder armor can extend a full ring past the shoulder, using slope bricks and curved bricks to create weight and hide joint gaps. For chest and leg armor, use plates with details such as rivets and air intakes. Leave a small gap between armor sections to strengthen the mechanical look. Armor should follow the frame rather than fight it: it covers seams and adds mass, but the joints still need room to move. More armor is not always better; the lines at key points should stay clean. For color, use one main color for the body, one secondary color for the joints, and a tiny accent color for details. Do not use more than three colors, or the model will look busy.

## Weapons and Accessories: Complete the Silhouette

Add weapons last to complete the design. The three most common choices are a large sword, a rifle, and a shield. Build the sword from long plates plus blade pieces, the rifle from thin rods plus barrel pieces, and the shield from a large baseplate plus an emblem. Design the weapon around the hand's grip structure so the mecha can hold it securely. The final check matters because a weapon can change the balance of the model, so make sure the arms can still hold the pose after it is attached. Check every joint for tightness and range of motion. Reinforce anything loose and adjust anything that binds. At that point the mecha is finished.

Work through this MOC mecha blueprint tutorial and your understanding of scale, center of gravity, and joints will improve.
