# Brick Gift Guide: Choose by Age, Budget, and Interests

Bricks are the easiest gift on the list to get right — and the easiest to get wrong. Pick well and the recipient happily builds for a month; pick badly and it is either too hard to start or so simple it collects dust. A good brick gift is not about chasing whatever set is trending. It comes down to three things: who the recipient is, how much you want to spend, and what they actually like. This guide works through all three, line by line.

## 1. Choose by age: part size and difficulty come first

**Ages three to five:** go for a large-part set. Fewer pieces, moderate clutch power, and themes built around animals, vehicles, or everyday scenes. Safety and a quick sense of achievement matter more than piece count.

**Ages six to nine:** standard-size parts in a small set are fine here. A build of roughly thirty to sixty steps is the sweet spot. Follow whatever the child is currently obsessed with — dinosaurs, fire trucks, and space all work well.

**Ages ten to fourteen:** step up to a mid-sized set where Technic parts and mechanical elements start to appear. A build with gears and structure to it holds attention far longer.

**Adults:** prioritize themes like modular street scenes, architecture, flowers, and mechs, where the building experience and the finished display piece matter equally. The harder the build, the more satisfying the challenge.

## 2. Choose by budget: three price bands, three strategies

**Under 100 yuan:** small scene sets or collectible minifigure blind bags. Ideal as a small token gift or as an add-on to something else — neat, compact, and no pressure on either side.

**300 to 600 yuan:** the best value band. This buys a complete small street building, a mid-sized vehicle, or a classic reissue, and it is enough to satisfy almost any recipient.

**Over 1,000 yuan:** large modular buildings, flagship Technic sets, or out-of-production reissues. Save these for birthdays, anniversaries, and other big occasions — and confirm the recipient actually has space to display it before you buy.

One money-saving trick: if the budget is tight but you want to give something substantial, put together a basic parts pack plus a MOC instruction file. The thought and the creativity both come through, and this is a popular approach to brick gifting right now.

## 3. Choose by interest: match the hobby, not the spec sheet

Watching what the recipient already enjoys tells you far more than comparing set specifications.

**Into cars:** Technic sports cars or construction vehicles, where they can build working suspension and gears.

**Into architecture:** modular street scenes and landmark builds are a safe bet.

**Into films and games:** look for licensed IP tie-ins — the nostalgia bonus does a lot of work.

**Into flowers:** a brick flower bouquet needs no watering and stays fresh on a shelf for years.

**Already a MOC builder:** skip ordinary retail sets. A pack of rare parts, baseplates, or a membership to an instruction-sharing platform hits the actual need far better.

If you are still unsure, default to a scene-based build. Houses and street corners are close to universally liked.

## 4. Buying details and small touches that add value

Check four things before you order:

1. **Part size** — confirm it suits the recipient's age group.
2. **Piece count** — don't hand a beginner a 5,000-piece monster as their first build.
3. **Theme popularity** — obscure IPs can be hard to trade or resell later, while popular themes move easily.
4. **Retirement risk** — if a retired set you want shows up at a good price, take it, but don't chase one at a steep premium.

Then add a few small touches when you hand it over: include a handwritten card with a short message, photograph the instructions and send them as a digital copy so the recipient can check them any time, or stick notes on the numbered bags inside the box so they can start building the moment they open it. Details like these are what people remember longer than the gift itself.
