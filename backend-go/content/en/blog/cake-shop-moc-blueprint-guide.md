# Brick Cake Shop MOC: Window, Bakery and Cream Color Guide

Among street-scene subjects, a brick cake shop MOC is one of the most photogenic. Soft cream tones, a row of delicate cakes in the window and the imagined sweetness drifting from the back kitchen make the color scheme alone enough to lift your mood. The structure is not complicated, but it really tests your small-part stacking and dessert-shaping skills. That makes it an excellent stage for beginners to practice detail work and for experienced builders to show off.

## 1. Overall Color Scheme: Tell the Sweet Story with Color

The atmosphere of a cake shop depends almost entirely on color. Lock the main palette to cream white, light pink and taro purple, with caramel brown as a small accent. The overall effect should feel soft and airy, like a freshly cut slice of mousse. Use light-colored plates for the exterior walls, outline the door frame and window mullions in white or pearl gold, and make the shop sign in dessert colors: a pink background, white lettering and a ring of small light-bulb pieces around it. That immediately pushes the sweetness up.

Use the 70/30 rule: keep 70 percent of the build in cream and light base colors, then spend the remaining 30 percent on accent colors such as strawberry red, matcha green and chocolate brown for the cakes and the sign. Do not let the accents take over. If they do, the shop becomes cloying rather than fresh. For the floor, alternate light pink and off-white tiles to imitate a macaron-colored tiled surface. Keep the exterior wall texture calm and let the window and sign carry the visual weight.

## 2. Window Display: The Visual Center of the Cake Shop

The window is the core display face of the cake shop MOC. Build it with large transparent glass window panels, then raise the interior by one level to create a cake display platform. Arrange a birthday cake, cupcakes and mousse across it in sequence. Cake shaping is the main event: stack round plates to build the cake layers, use cream-colored small pieces around the middle to form a piped border, add red small pieces such as strawberries and cherries on top, then insert a thin bar as a candle. The layering appears at once.

The display platform can be built as two steps. Put the wedding cake on the taller step and sliced desserts on the lower one so the arrangement has a pleasant staggered rhythm. Remember to install a warm white light inside the window so the cakes seem to glow. Place a cat minifigure on the windowsill as the shop cat, and the charm doubles immediately. Keep the transparent glass clean and clear, because this window is the face of the whole shop.

## 3. Checkout Counter and Seating Area: The Second Sweet Stop

Step inside, and one side is the checkout counter while the other is the seating area. Give the counter a white top with a pearl gold cash register. Put a row of display cases in front, filled with cake slices and pudding, and cover them with transparent door panels for a strong fresh-kept feel. On the back wall behind the counter, build a dessert-shaped wall: use small pieces in various colors to form three-dimensional outlines of donuts, cream puffs and macarons. It works as both decoration and menu.

For the seating area, set out two or three small round tables with pink-and-white chairs. Put a small cake and a glass of sparkling water on each tabletop. Add a row of banquette seating along the wall and hang a few small framed piping pictures above it. Suspend several macaron-colored pendant lights from the ceiling and lay pink tile pieces on the floor. The whole shop then feels gently wrapped in cream. Do not forget a rotating cake display stand in the corner, holding a small cake that turns. It adds movement and catches the eye.

## 4. Baking Room Details and Finishing Advice

The baking room at the back is a bonus feature. Separate it with a semi-open partition, then place an oven, a mixer and shelving inside. Stack flour sacks and sugar jars on the shelves. A baker minifigure wearing an apron and carrying a freshly baked cake instantly gives the scene the realism of a shop that bakes and sells on site. Put cake boxes and ribbons on the packaging station in the corner, with a staff member packing an order, and the story line is complete.

For the build order, work from the window to the seating area and then to the baking room. Practice the cake shapes before assembling the storefront, because the cakes are the stars. Stock up on round plates, cream and pink small pieces, transparent window panels and warm-light pieces. If you want more refined cake shapes, look for cake-themed instructions on **BrickPlans**. You can study the piping and layer-building methods directly and avoid unnecessary detours.
