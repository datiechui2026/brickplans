# Jigao Architecture Series Review: Bricks, Build, Accuracy

I have been building with bricks for more than ten years, from LEGO to a wide range of Chinese brands. Recently I worked through several popular sets in the **Jigao Architecture Series**: the European Clock Tower, Jiangnan Water Town, and Glass Curtain Wall Office Building. Here is a real builder's view on whether the series is worth buying.

## Part Quality: Clutch and Color Separation Hold Up

The parts are the first thing most builders want to know about. The ABS material in the **Jigao Architecture Series** feels harder overall. Clutch power is tight and secure without feeling rough or stubborn, and a standard brick separator can split pieces smoothly. It avoids the two extremes common in Chinese bricks: pieces so tight you cannot pull them apart, or pieces so loose that they fall apart at a touch. Sprue cleanup is clean with almost no burrs, and the transparent pieces, such as the glass curtain walls and skylights, have good clarity with defect rates kept in check. Color separation also deserves praise. Similar grays and off-whites are distinct enough that sorting pieces by weak night-time light is not likely to lead to mistakes.

## Build Experience: Sensible Bagging and Clear Instructions

The build order is key to the experience. The **Jigao Architecture Series** bags parts by floor, with each bag corresponding to an independent building module. Following the instructions one bag at a time feels logical and smooth. Bricks are printed at 1:1 in the instructions, and the positioning lines on the clear base help beginners avoid misaligning a floor. Building the Jiangnan Water Town set took me about four hours, with almost no need to tear down and redo sections along the way. The pace was very comfortable. One thing to note: some wall internal skeletons use many small pieces for reinforcement, so the interior structure can feel a little repetitive. The bagging helps ease that, so it does not become frustrating.

## Architectural Accuracy: Recognizable and Faithful in Spirit

Accuracy is the soul of architecture bricks. Jigao puts effort into signature elements: the curved spire of the European Clock Tower, its arched window proportions, the horse-head gable walls of Jiangnan Water Town, and the curvature of its stone arch bridge all use dedicated molds rather than simply forcing standard bricks into place. With the finished models side by side, the overall silhouettes look very close from a distance, and the details stand up to closer inspection. One minor regret is that some sets have minifigures that are small in scale and not well proportioned to the buildings. If you mainly want pure architectural display, this is not a big issue. If you want to photograph minifigure scenes, you will need to add a few larger-scale minifigures yourself.

## Value and Buying Advice

Compared with imported-brand architecture sets of the same piece count, the **Jigao Architecture Series** costs roughly one third to one half as much, while clutch and finished appearance are already very close. That makes the value stand out. It is especially suitable for three groups: students on a limited budget, beginners who want to practice the feel of architecture-style building, and MOC builders who need a large number of bricks. Jigao's generic brick system is highly compatible with mainstream bricks, so models can be dismantled and used as a parts library. My suggested buying order is to start with a mid-size scene of 600 to 1200 pieces to test the feel, then move up to a flagship large set if you like it. That avoids an impulse purchase that ends up gathering dust.

## Final Thoughts

Overall, the **Jigao Architecture Series** is balanced across parts, assembly, accuracy, and value. It is a strong contender among Chinese architecture brick lines.
