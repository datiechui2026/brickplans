# Brick Fountain MOC Guide: Styles, Water Effects and Layouts

## Start With the Water

When a street scene is nearly finished, the missing piece is usually not another building — it is a bit of water. A **brick fountain MOC** looks like a small accessory, but it is often the part that steals the scene. The same transparent parts and a ring of curved bricks will bring a whole street to life if you place them correctly. What follows covers picking a plan, making the water read as water, and connecting the fountain to the rest of your layout.

## 1. Fountain MOC Types and Styles

### 1.1 Classic European Fountain

The classic European fountain is the most traditional type of **brick fountain**, typically found in a plaza, garden or in front of a palace.

**Design features**:
- Multi-tier round or oval structure
- Curved bricks and round plates used to build the basin
- A central column decorated with angels, dolphins or mythological figures
- Strongly sculpted decorative detail

**Suggested settings**: European town squares, palace gardens, in front of a town hall

### 1.2 Modern Minimalist Fountain

Modern-style **fountain MOCs** put more weight on line and geometry:

**Design features**:
- Clean geometric shapes (square, round, irregular)
- Tiles used to suggest the sheet of flowing water
- Single-column or multi-column water features
- Style that matches the surrounding architecture

**Suggested settings**: modern city plazas, shopping districts, office tower forecourts

### 1.3 Natural Style Fountain

Natural fountains imitate a mountain spring or a waterfall:

**Design features**:
- Irregularly stacked bricks standing in for rock
- Multi-level cascading water
- Plants and moss as decoration
- A looser, more organic look

**Suggested settings**: parks, gardens, resorts

## 3. Fountain Building Techniques

### 3.1 Creating the Water Effect

The core challenge of a **brick fountain MOC** is making bricks read as moving water. These are the common approaches:

**Stacked transparent bricks**:
- Stack transparent blue and light blue bricks to form the water column
- Make the colour lighter as it rises, mimicking the natural transition of a flow
- Cap it with 1x1 transparent round plates to suggest spray

**Curved arrangement**:
- Line up transparent blue curved bricks into the arc of a jet
- Offset several rows of curved bricks to create a curtain of water
- Add translucent white bricks for foam

**Splash at ground level**:
- Scatter transparent blue 1x1 round plates around the basin
- Lay light blue plates along the pool edge to suggest water spilling over

### 3.2 Structural Stability

A fountain that stands alone as a display piece in a **street scene** needs solid structure:

- **Reinforce the base**: double up the plates underneath so the centre of gravity stays low
- **Central column**: run technic parts through the middle to add strength
- **Pool edge**: use curved bricks for a smooth transition and avoid sharp corners
- **Tie the tiers together**: connect each level with studs, and add technic pins where needed

### 3.3 Lighting

Adding lights makes a big difference to how a **brick fountain** displays:

- **Uplighting**: fit LEDs into the pool floor and point them upward
- **Jet lighting**: run fibre-optic lights inside the transparent water column
- **Ambient lighting**: place warm ground lights around the fountain
- **Colour change**: use RGB LEDs for shifting colours

## 4. Fitting the Fountain into a Street Scene

### 4.1 Plaza Fountain Layout

In a street layout the fountain usually acts as the centrepiece of a plaza:

- **Placement**: centre of the square, a roundabout, or in front of a building
- **Surroundings**: benches, street lamps, flower beds, a flock of pigeons
- **Ground treatment**: lay light grey plates across the plaza so it contrasts with the fountain

### 4.2 Garden Fountain Layout

In a garden scene the fountain can become the visual focus:

- **Placement**: centre of the garden, where paths meet, or close to a building
- **Surroundings**: flower beds, shrubs, trees, footpaths
- **Backdrop**: a pavilion, archway or boundary wall

### 4.3 Indoor Fountains

In larger building MOCs, an indoor fountain also works well:

- **Lobby fountain**: the centrepiece of a hotel or office lobby
- **Courtyard fountain**: a rock-and-water feature in a traditional courtyard
- **Conservatory fountain**: a small fountain inside a glasshouse

## 5. Fountain MOC Review Summary

### 5.1 Design Highlights

- **Overall look**: a good fountain MOC is convincing at a glance — you read the movement of water immediately
- **Detail**: from spray to droplets, from highlights to reflections, the richer the detail, the more believable the result
- **Scene fit**: a good fountain merges with its surroundings and becomes the highlight of the street

### 5.2 Building Advice

- **Start simple**: beginners should begin with a single-tier fountain and work up to complex designs
- **Study the real thing**: watch how actual fountains are structured and how the water moves
- **Experiment**: don't limit yourself to traditional designs — add your own ideas
- **Use BrickPlans**: the platform holds plenty of **brick fountain MOC** plans and inspiration

## 6. Final Thoughts

A **brick fountain MOC** is a small project that demands real craft. Size is not the point — the water is. How the transparent parts stack, where the light comes from, whether the angle of the jet is right: get those few things correct and the water feels alive.

Start with a three-tier fountain on your first attempt, then move up to a larger lit water feature once you are comfortable. There are plenty of **fountain MOC** plans in the community to reference — picking one and starting to build beats reading about it.
