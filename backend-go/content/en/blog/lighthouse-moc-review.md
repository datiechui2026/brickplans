# Lighthouse MOC Review: Structure, Lighting, and Coastal Scene

The short version: this lighthouse MOC plan is structurally solid, and its lighting solution is well developed. The tower is built in four sections that taper as they rise, and the build logic is clear. With a rocky shore and wave base, it makes a strong display scene for a parts budget of around CNY 600.

## 1. Tower Structure: Four Tapered Sections + Internal Support Axle

The tower uses a four-section design. From the bottom up, the diameter narrows from 16 studs to 10 studs, section by section. A cross axle runs through each joint for reinforcement, and the overall center of gravity stays stable. The finished tower is 48 cm tall and uses 1,120 pieces, with about 700 of them in the tower body. That makes the tower the main part of the build and the place where most of the structural decisions matter.

The four-section approach also gives the model a classic lighthouse profile: wide at the base, then gradually slimmer toward the lamp room. Because the tower is split into vertical stages, it is easier to check alignment as you go instead of discovering a problem at the very top. The cross axles are the key reinforcement here. A tall, mostly hollow tower needs that internal spine to stay rigid, and the plan uses it through every section joint. The overall center of gravity is stable, which matters for a model that is meant to stand on display.

Inside, the plan keeps a spiral staircase with 26 steps. There is room for two minifigures to climb the tower. This interior detail is not just hidden filler; it gives the tower a usable internal structure and makes the model more convincing when you look through the windows or remove a section.

## 2. Lighting: How Three Light Sources Look at Night

Lighting is the heart of a lighthouse, and this plan uses three light sources: one main light at the top, one window light in the middle of the tower, and one coastal light in the base. The main light uses a warm white LED plus a rotating lens structure. Once powered, it produces a rotating beam effect with a nighttime reach of about 30 cm. That reach is best suited to a dim room and close viewing rather than a large, brightly lit space, but it is enough to make the lighthouse effect readable.

The middle window light and the base coastal light support the main beacon and tie the scene together after dark. The wiring follows the internal support axle and is fully hidden, so it does not spoil the exterior. This is a mature approach: the electronics stay inside the model, the cable route is protected by the structure, and the outside keeps its clean silhouette. When building, the main thing to watch is that the wiring stays tucked along the support axle before the tower sections are closed up.

## 3. Coastal Scene: Rocks, Waves, and Anchor Chain Details

The base builds the wave shape from dark blue pieces. It also includes 12 irregular rock pieces and 1 section of ship anchor chain, forming a complete seaside dock scene. The dark blue wave layers give the base movement, while the irregular rocks break up the shoreline and make it look less like a simple display stand.

The scene includes two minifigures: a lighthouse keeper and a sailor. Each has accessories, a lantern and binoculars, which gives the display more of a story. The keeper and sailor also help show scale next to the 48 cm tower. Because dark blue and white pieces make up most of the model, the scene is relatively easy to source if you are gathering parts for the build.

## 4. Build Experience and Things to Watch For

The instructions cover 428 steps across 8 booklets. That is a substantial build, but splitting it into eight booklets keeps each stage manageable. The tower is highly symmetrical, so it is best to build the left and right halves side by side and check them against each other to avoid misalignment. This is especially useful on the tapered sections, where a small offset can carry upward through the next stage.

The lighting battery box sits inside the base. Changing batteries requires removing six pins from the bottom, which is a little inconvenient. When building, it is worth leaving some access room for battery changes and checking that the hidden wiring is not trapped before the base is closed. The model is stable on a flat table, but the base should still be handled carefully when the battery box is being accessed.

**A:** It is very stable. All four tower sections are reinforced with cross axles running through them, and the base is wide with sensible weight distribution.
