# Brick Hardware Store MOC Tutorial: Tool Wall and Window

Short version first: this hardware store MOC uses about 3,200 parts on a 32x32 baseplate to make a two-storey trade-style shop roughly 26 cm tall, for around CNY 280 and about 9 hours of building. The tool wall and the shop window are the two features that give the model its identity.

Hardware stores are rare in brick street scenes, which is exactly why this one works so well: an orange main colour, walls packed with tools and a metallic-looking shop window make it unmistakable from across a town layout. This tutorial walks through the tool wall, shelving, window and shopfront in four steps. Instructions and the parts list are both available on brickplan.cn.

## Step 1: Baseplate and Window Frame

Lay dark grey plates for the ground. The front window opening should be 10 cm wide and 6 cm high, glazed with large transparent glass elements, with 1x1 bricks around the edge so the glass sits flush. Choose orange for the main walls and use dark grey for the skirting and the window sill, and the industrial look appears immediately. This step takes about 850 parts. Reinforce the corners of the window frame with 1x2 Technic bricks so the second floor does not distort under load.

## Step 2: Designing the Tool Wall

The tool wall is the heart of a hardware store. Fit a dark grey back panel to the second-floor wall, build peg holes with 1x2 Technic bricks, then push in wrench, screwdriver and hammer elements, roughly 18 of them in a staggered horizontal and vertical arrangement to create that wall-of-tools impact. If you run short of tool elements, make your own from 1x1 round plates plus short bars: it is cheap and looks close enough. Finish with an orange cornice line along the top of the wall to frame the tool wall neatly.

## Step 3: Shelving and Hardware Display

Place two dark grey shelving units along the ground-floor walls, each about 8 cm tall. The upper level holds paint buckets and toolboxes; the lower level holds bolts, nuts and other small parts, sorted by colour in 1x1 round plates. Leave a 2 cm aisle between the units, put a hand cart and two metal drums on the floor, and stand a "Grand Opening Special" chalkboard by the door. The small hardware pieces come to roughly 120, all common parts, so they put very little strain on your collection.

## Step 4: Shopfront and Window Details

Build the canopy over the entrance from orange 1x2 slopes. Spell out "HARDWARE" on the sign with letter bricks, and add a row of warm yellow transparent elements underneath to suggest a lit light box. Inside the window, place a drill display stand and two tool gift sets, with a worker minifigure for the display. At the door, set out a toolbox, a broom and some pipe pieces to suggest the shop has just opened for the day. The full model is about 3,200 parts; once it is finished, press the whole thing flat and check the seams.

**Q: Is this hardware store MOC hard to build?**
A: It sits above medium difficulty: about 3,200 parts and roughly 9 hours of work. The tool wall and window are detail-heavy and the sub-builds are somewhat fragmented, so previous street-scene experience helps. Beginners can spread it over two days.

**Q: How much does it cost to build a hardware store MOC?**
A: Instructions are about CNY 35 and the parts pack about CNY 245, for roughly CNY 280 in total. Tool elements make up a high share of the special parts; if you want to stay under CNY 250, build more of the tools yourself from round plates and short bars.

**Q: How big is the hardware store MOC?**
A: It uses a standard 32x32 baseplate, so it occupies about 25.6 x 25.6 cm and stands about 26 cm tall. That is slightly taller than a typical street-scene shop, which gives more variety when it sits beside a grocery store or a clothing shop.

**Q: What makes this different from an ordinary street-scene shop?**
A: Ordinary shops put the emphasis on the window and the shopfront exterior. A hardware store's character comes from the interior tool wall and the metallic detailing, and the orange colour scheme is highly recognisable. It is the rare "hard industrial" shop in a street layout.
