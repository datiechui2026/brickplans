# Big Ben MOC Review: 9,200-Piece London Landmark Tower

## The Bottom Line

This Big Ben MOC uses 9,200 bricks and finishes at 1 meter tall, with a 32 cm base per side and a weight of roughly 8.5 kg. Plan on about 65 hours of building, and around 1500 RMB as a reference price. The verdict: it is one of the better-value tall-tower architecture MOCs available. All four clock faces have turning hands and an hourly chime structure, and the shaping is first-rate. The weak point is the slender tower body, which demands a level display surface. It suits builders with some experience who like landmark architecture. The instructions can be found on the brickplan.cn platform.

## 1. Shaping and Detail Accuracy

The defining feature of Big Ben is its 96-meter tower, and this MOC compresses the scale to 1:96, which lands the finished model at exactly 1 meter. The tower's four-sided taper is built by staggering 2x2 and 2x4 bricks layer by layer, and each of the four elevations carries a clock face 10 cm in diameter. The hands are gold elements, and the Roman numerals are reproduced with printed bricks. The spire and the carved detailing at the top are assembled from wedge bricks, and 64 trans-gold bricks are used across the four sides as window trim, which photographs beautifully in sunlight. The overall palette is cream white with light gray, and the accuracy ranks in the top tier of MOCs on this subject.

## 2. Mechanics and the Chime Feature

The standout feature is the internal mechanism. Four hand axles are linked through a bevel gear set, so turning the knob at the tower base drives all four clock faces in sync. A chime structure is also hidden at the top: pulling the lever on the side triggers a small hammer against a metal reed, producing a bell-like ring that measured about 5 seconds of sustain. All the mechanical parts run on technic axles through the center of the tower, and the routing is completely concealed by the stairs and walls, so nothing shows on the exterior. One thing to watch: the gear set is demanding on assembly precision, and a meshing offset of more than 1 mm will jam the hands.

## 3. Building Experience and Value

The instruction book runs close to 600 pages and is split into 9 numbered bags, roughly 1,000 bricks each, at about 1 hour per bag. The most time-consuming stages are the four clock faces and the top spire, each taking around 20% of total build time. At about 0.16 RMB per brick, the price sits in line with official architecture sets, but you get mechanical play on top. That said, most of the parts are basic bricks, so sorting is easy and even newcomers can manage. The biggest shortcoming is the slim tower body: the top level sways about 3 mm in testing, so mount it on a display base and keep it out of reach of children. Overall, it is excellent value for anyone who likes mechanisms and landmark buildings.

## FAQ

**Q: How big is the finished Big Ben MOC, and will it fit at home?**

A: It stands 1 meter tall with a 32 cm base per side and weighs about 8.5 kg. You need a display spot at least 40 cm square with 1.1 meters of clearance above it; a TV cabinet or entryway console usually works.

**Q: Do the clock hands really turn, and can it chime?**

A: Yes. Turning the knob at the tower base drives all four faces in sync, and pulling the side lever triggers the internal hammer against the reed for about 5 seconds of bell sound. It is a manual mechanism, not an electronic chime.

**Q: How much does this Big Ben MOC cost, and is it worth buying?**

A: The reference price is around 1500 RMB for 9,200 bricks with linked hands and a chime structure, which is high value among architecture MOCs of this size. It is well worth it for builders who enjoy mechanical functions or London landmark architecture.

**Q: Can a beginner build the Big Ben MOC?**

A: Yes, but builders with at least one 5,000-piece model behind them are the better fit. The difficulty lies in the four clock faces and the top spire, each about 20% of the build time, plus the tight gear meshing that requires careful work from the instructions.

**Q: How is this different from the official architecture Big Ben?**

A: The difference is scale and function. This one has 9,200 bricks and stands 1 meter tall, with turning hands and a working chime. Official sets are generally around 2,000 bricks and about 40 cm tall, focused on static display, so the play value is on a different level.

**Q: Does the tower wobble, and how do I make it more stable?**

A: The tower body is slender, and about 3 mm of sway at the top level is normal. Build it onto a 32x32 baseplate, reinforce with structural bricks, then place it on a level surface away from children, and stability is more than sufficient.
