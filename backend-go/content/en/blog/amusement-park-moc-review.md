# Amusement Park MOC Review: Coaster, Carousel, Ticket Booth

If modular street scenes are the everyday side of the brick hobby, an amusement park is the carnival side. A complete **brick amusement park MOC** needs a tall coaster, a turning carousel and a busy ticket booth, plus a park layout that actually works as a walking route. I gathered several amusement-park instruction sets from BrickPlans (brickplan.cn) and built them; this review follows the same order the park does: coaster, carousel, ticket booth, then the layout as a whole.

## 1. Coaster Track: Undulation, Curves and Drop Height Are Everything

The visual centre of any park is the coaster. This design uses a classic long lift hill plus two undulating bends, with the track assembled from curved track pieces and a helix worked into the drop, so it has real presence when you look down on it. The track sections clutch tightly and the joints show almost no gap, which keeps the transitions between gradients smooth.

The slowest part of the build is lining up the curvature of the curved sections against the straights. The instructions help here: every track segment is numbered, and if you follow the numbers in order you will not go wrong. One thing worth doing before you start is reading the track map from beginning to end, because the numbering is what keeps the helix and the two bends in the right sequence, and sorting your curved pieces by number first is far easier than discovering a mismatch halfway up the lift hill.

If you want the track to actually run, leave clearance on the downslope for a car to travel; a launch element is enough to send it rolling.

## 2. Carousel: The Romantic Centrepiece That Really Turns

The carousel is the gentlest ride in the park and the best-looking part of this instruction set. The base is built from round disc pieces, with a rotating main axle at the centre and a striped canopy on top; around it hang several small horses in different styles plus a two-seat bench.

The rotation mechanism is a simple axle-and-bushing arrangement: turn the outer ring of the base and the whole circle of horses goes round with it. The small mechanism that makes the horses rise and fall is there too, which gives the model a lot of atmosphere once it sits in a street scene. One mark down: the canopy stripes come in a limited choice of colours, so if you want a brighter result you will need to swap colours and add parts yourself.

## 3. Ticket Booth and Side Builds: Small Scenes Sell the Realism

Rides alone are not enough; a park feels real because of its supporting scenes. This set includes a ticket booth with a pointed roof, an opening window, a turnstile and a price-list sticker, and the entrance is dressed with queue barriers, a balloon cart and a bench, so the detail is dense. Coloured floor tiles and planters link the booth to the rides and the transition looks natural; stand a minifigure at the door and the queue-and-enter picture appears immediately. The effort spent on these supporting pieces is exactly what makes the park feel busy rather than empty.

## 4. Park Layout: A Sensible Route and Display-Friendly Modules

The layout has been thought through: the coaster sits at the back and builds high, the carousel takes the middle, and the ticket booth and food stalls sit at the front. That produces a low-front, high-back arrangement, so the rides do not block each other in photographs.

The park is divided across 32x32 baseplates, with rides and buildings built as independent modules. You can display the whole park at once or split it up and drop individual modules into different street scenes, which is a big plus for anyone with limited shelf space. Because each module is self-contained, the build also breaks into comfortable sessions: the carousel one evening, the booth the next, rather than one long push. The colour scheme is built on bright red, yellow and blue, so it stands out wherever you put it.

## 5. Verdict and Buying Advice

Overall, this **brick amusement park MOC** covers the three core elements, coaster, carousel and ticket booth, with moving parts, rich scenery and a layout that holds together. It is a very complete park design. The part variety is wide and the total count is on the high side, but the parts list is sorted clearly by module, so sourcing what you need is not difficult.

The marks against it: a limited colour choice for the carousel canopy, and a track that only supports free rolling rather than powered running. If you build amusement-park themes and want a themed park with movement and colour in one go, this instruction set is worth picking up.
