# Street View MOC Blueprints Review: Build Your Own Brick City

## Product Overview

Street view MOCs are among the most artistic and immersive things you can build from bricks. Whether it is a retro European corner coffee house or a cyberpunk storefront, a street view lets you assemble a miniature city block with your own hands. On BrickPlans, street view blueprints consistently sit at the top of both download counts and reader ratings.

Almost every design in this category uses a standardized footprint — a 32x32 stud baseplate or a 48x48 stud baseplate — and that convention is what makes the whole hobby work. Because designers agree on the footprint, buildings from different blueprints can be lined up side by side into one continuous street, and a collection built over several years still fits together. The dominant styles are **European classical**, **Japanese machiya**, **modern minimalist**, **traditional Chinese architecture** and **sci-fi future**.

Each style brings its own vocabulary of parts. Classical European builds lean on awnings, bay windows and decorative cornices; machiya designs live on timber facades and narrow frontage; minimalist modern builds use large tiles and clean colour blocking; sci-fi storefronts justify bright signage and unusual angles. Picking a style first is usually easier than picking an individual building.

## Build Difficulty

Street view MOCs are graded like this:

- **Beginner (★★☆☆☆)**: small shops and market stalls, 300-800 parts, finishable in a session or two
- **Intermediate (★★★☆☆)**: a standard 32x32 street building, 800-1500 parts, with a basic interior
- **Advanced (★★★★☆)**: large corner buildings and landmarks, 1500-3000 parts, with a full interior on every floor
- **Expert (★★★★★)**: flagship building complexes, 3000+ parts, multi-storey structures plus a lighting system

The hard part of a street view is rarely the bulk of the walls — it is the **facade detailing**. How windows, doorways, signage, balconies and roofing are combined decides how the finished model reads from across the room, and it is where a good blueprint separates itself from a mediocre one. Two plans can use the same part count and produce completely different results if one of them treats the ground-floor shopfront as an afterthought.

If you are new to the category, start at intermediate rather than beginner. A 300-part stall teaches you very little about how a facade is layered, while a standard 32x32 building shows the whole process — baseplate, walls, floor split, roof — without the piece count becoming unmanageable.

## Parts Review

Parts demand for street views is heavily architectural:

- **Brick types**: large quantities of standard bricks, tiles, window frames, door panels, railings and roof slopes
- **Colour range**: building tones dominate — tan, white, brick red and dark grey — with brighter accents reserved for signage and trim
- **Special parts**: arched windows, round columns, planters and lampposts add a surprising amount of perceived quality for a small number of pieces

On brands, **SEMBO** and **Gobricks** both perform well on street view parts, especially in tan and brick red, where colour consistency between batches matters most and where cheap alternatives tend to show. Window and door frame elements among **LEGO-compatible bricks** are highly interchangeable, which makes substituting a colour or topping up a shortage straightforward instead of a hunt.

Because street views are parts-hungry in a narrow colour band, it pays to check the parts list before you buy bricks. Sorting by colour first and by element type second will save you hours on a 2000-part build — the walls go up fast once tan brick and tan tile are separated.

## Building Experience

Building a street view is a genuinely soothing process. You normally start at the foundation and work upward one floor at a time — ground floor for the shop or public space, second floor for living quarters, third floor for an attic or terrace. Each level is designed to be lifted out on its own, so you can look at the interior without dismantling the facade.

Good blueprints design real **interior detail**: an espresso bar with tables and chairs on the ground floor, bookshelves and a fireplace above, a roof garden on top. These touches turn the model from a hollow shell into a lived-in miniature space, and they are also what makes the removable floors worth having. On [brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn) you can find plenty of street view plans with floor-by-floor instructions, where each level's parts list is labelled separately so you can build in stages rather than sorting one enormous pile.

## Displaying the Finished Model

Put two or three street view buildings side by side and a living brick street appears. A standard 32x32 building typically stands 25-35cm tall; add lampposts, street trees, a mailbox and a few pedestrians and the scene comes alive immediately.

Many builders add **lighting kits** so the windows glow warmly after dark — the effect on a corner cafe or a shopfront sign is far bigger than the cost of the kit suggests. Seasonal dressing works the same way: a Christmas tree, cherry blossom or pumpkin lanterns let the same street change theme through the year without a single rebuild of the architecture.
