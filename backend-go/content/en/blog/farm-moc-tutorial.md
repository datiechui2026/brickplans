# Brick Farm MOC Tutorial: Build a Countryside Layout

## Introduction

City life moves fast, and a quiet farm scene is a nice counterweight to it. Building your own farm out of bricks is both a relaxing creative project and a small tribute to country living. This **farm MOC tutorial** walks you through it from a bare baseplate to a working-looking farmstead, one section at a time.

## 1. Planning and Layout

### 1.1 Deciding on Scale

Plan the overall layout before you place a single brick. For a **brick farm**, two 32x32 baseplates side by side give you a 64x32 footprint. That is enough room for the main farm buildings without eating up your whole desk.

### 1.2 Dividing the Farm into Zones

A complete farm needs these functional areas:
1. **Living area**: farmhouse and garden
2. **Livestock area**: cattle pen, chicken coop, stable
3. **Growing area**: fields, vegetable plot, orchard
4. **Storage area**: barn, tool shed, silo
5. **Work area**: machinery shed, threshing floor

### 1.3 Planning the Traffic Flow

Good circulation makes the farm feel real:
- Put the farmhouse near the entrance so it is easy to get in and out
- Connect the barn to both the pens and the fields so feed can move easily
- Keep the machinery shed close to the tool shed and the fields
- Place the silo right next to the barn and the transport road

## 2. Building the Main Structures

### 2.1 The Farmhouse

The farmhouse is the centerpiece of any **countryside brick scene**. A classic rural style works best:

- **Main structure**: a two-story house with red or white bricks as the dominant color
- **Roof**: dark brown or gray slope bricks for a pitched roof
- **Porch**: a covered wooden porch at the front door, built from 2x4 brown plates
- **Chimney**: build it on one side using red and dark gray bricks
- **Garden**: green plates for the lawn, dotted with flowers and fencing

### 2.2 The Barn

The barn is the farm's signature building:

- **Size**: aim for 12x16 studs and 8-10 bricks tall
- **Walls**: mostly red brick with white window and door frames
- **Main door**: a large sliding door on the front, assembled from 2x6 door panels
- **Loft**: a loft space under the roof for hay storage
- **Vents**: small vent windows on the roof sides

### 2.3 Pens and Coops

In any **farm MOC tutorial**, the animal pens are what sell the sense of daily life:

- **Cattle pen**: brown fencing around an area, dark green plates on the ground to suggest grass, with cow minifigures inside
- **Stable**: split into separate stalls, one horse per stall, each with a feed trough and water trough
- **Sheep pen**: white fencing in a circular layout, the ground covered in 1x1 white round plates to mimic fleece
- **Chicken coop**: a small red wooden hut with a little yard, using 1x1 orange round plates for eggs

### 2.4 The Silo

You can build a silo from round bricks, or try these techniques:
- Stack 1x2 curved bricks into a cylinder
- Cap it with round plates
- Use silver or gray bricks to suggest metal
- Add a discharge chute at the base

## 3. Fields and Crops

### 3.1 The Wheat Field

Wheat is the signature landscape of a **brick farm**:
- Alternate light green and dark green plates to show wheat at different stages of growth
- Scatter 1x1 yellow round plates across the field as ripe heads of grain
- Lay brown plates between plots as field ridges
- Put tools and a scarecrow at the field edge

### 3.2 The Vegetable Garden

Set aside a vegetable plot next to the farmhouse:
- Use brown plates to mark out tidy rows
- Different colored 1x1 round plates stand in for different crops: red for tomatoes, green for leafy vegetables, orange for carrots
- Build trellises from green plant pieces
- Add a small irrigation system, using transparent blue bricks to suggest water

### 3.3 The Orchard

Plant fruit trees along the edge of the farm:
- Use tree trunk pieces combined with green foliage
- Dot the canopy with red and yellow 1x1 round plates for fruit
- Lean a ladder and place a picking basket under each tree

## 4. Machinery and Vehicles

### 4.1 The Tractor

No farm is complete without a tractor. Build a simple brick tractor:
- Technic pieces for the chassis, with four large tires
- A cab built from transparent bricks
- A towing hitch at the rear
- Pick red or green as the main color

### 4.2 The Harvester

Park a harvester in the fields:
- A wider chassis with larger tires
- A harvesting unit mounted on the front
- An exhaust pipe and warning light on top

### 4.3 Transport Vehicles

- **Pickup truck**: for hauling tools and produce
- **Trailer**: hitched behind the tractor for moving hay bales
- **Feed wagon**: for carrying feed around the farm

## 5. Details and Decoration

### 5.1 Fences and Roads

- Wooden fencing (brown bar pieces) along the field boundaries
- Gray plates for the main road, lined with street trees
- Side paths built from alternating brown and dark brown 1x2 plates

### 5.2 Animals and Poultry

Animals are the part of a **brick farm** you cannot skip:
- Use official LEGO animal pieces: cows, horses, sheep, chickens, pigs
- MOC-built animal designs work too
- Place each animal where it would realistically spend its time

### 5.3 Everyday Farm Details

- Clothesline: string one up behind the farmhouse
- Well: build an old-style well at the center of the farm
- Beehives: set a few in the corner of the orchard
- Mailbox: install one at the farm entrance
- Signpost: wooden signs pointing to the different areas

## 6. Advanced Techniques

### 6.1 Seasonal Variations

You can theme your farm around a season:
- **Spring**: pink blossom trees, green seedlings
- **Summer**: full leafy trees, ripe vegetables
- **Autumn**: golden wheat, trees losing their leaves
- **Winter**: white rooftops, bare branches

### 6.2 Lighting and Motion

- Fit warm LED lights inside the farmhouse windows
- Add lighting in the barn
- Use a motor to drive the barn doors or a windmill

## 7. Wrapping Up

This **farm MOC tutorial** covers the main line: farmhouse, barn, fields, pens, and detail work, layered on one step at a time. Do not cover the whole baseplate on day one. Build the farmhouse and one small field first, and only expand outward once it looks right.

A **MOC building tutorial** only gives you the skeleton. The character is yours to add: switch the season, run a new path, get the windmill turning. That is when the farm starts to feel alive.
