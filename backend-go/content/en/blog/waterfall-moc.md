# How to Build a LEGO Waterfall MOC: Transparent Bricks & Tiers

The short version: a 45cm-wide waterfall MOC takes roughly 1,000 bricks, a budget of about 350 yuan, and around 3 hours if you follow three steps. Transparent bricks do the water, stepped tiers do the momentum, and small white pieces do the splash — combine all three and a static model still reads as moving water. Full instructions are available on brickplan.cn.

## Step 1: Lay the Water Stream with Transparent Bricks

The main body of the stream is built from about 300 transparent light blue bricks, laid 8 studs wide and tilted 30 degrees downward to create the sense of water pouring over an edge.

The key technique: every 3 layers, insert a row of transparent clear (colorless) bricks to break up the pattern into subtle "ripples." Without this, a single flat sheet of light blue looks stiff and artificial, no matter how good the angle is.

Edge both sides of the waterfall with dark blue transparent pieces to close the stream off. This gives the water visible depth — a bright core with darker, denser water at the margins — instead of a flat blue ribbon. One more rule to keep in mind: the stream should be narrow at the top and wider at the bottom, which is how real waterfalls spread out as they fall.

## Step 2: Use Two Tiers for a Stronger Drop

Resist the urge to build a single straight wall of water from top to bottom. Two tiers give far more presence.

The first tier drops 20cm into a middle platform. The second tier then falls another 15cm into the pool below. That interruption in the fall is what makes the model feel tall — the eye reads two separate plunges rather than one long slide.

Build the middle platform from light green transparent pieces to suggest a shallow shelf, where the water spreads out before gathering itself again for the second drop. Frame the pool with dark blue plates into a 20cm × 30cm area, and line the bottom with transparent pieces so the water looks like it has depth rather than sitting on bare plastic. With the tiers and the pool in place, the whole scene gains vertical drama immediately.

## Step 3: Add Splash Effects with Small White Pieces

Splash is what makes a waterfall feel alive. At each of the two landing points, scatter about 30 white 1×1 bricks and transparent round plates to mimic flying droplets. Then run a ring of white curved pieces around the rim of the pool as foam.

If you want more life in the scene, hide blue light bricks under the pool floor. The light passes up through the transparent bricks and the whole waterfall suddenly lights up from within.

Finally, flank both sides with dark green trees — roughly 150 pieces — to complete the scene and frame the water against something solid.

Totals: about 1,000 pieces, 350 yuan, 3 hours. Once it is built, take a photo and share it on brickplan.cn to see how your waterfall compares with other builders'.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q: How do I make the water in a brick waterfall MOC look real?**
A: It comes down to the layering and the angle of the transparent bricks. Tilt the stream 30 degrees, keep it narrow at the top and wider at the bottom, mix in a row of colorless transparent bricks every 3 layers for ripples, then add the two-tier drop and white splash pieces. That combination is what sells the effect.

**Q: Can I use ordinary blue bricks instead of transparent ones?**
A: You can, but the result suffers. Standard blue bricks do not let light through, so you lose the translucent quality of water. If your budget is tight, use transparent bricks only on the visible surface layer and fill the interior with ordinary blue bricks — the visual difference is small.

**Q: Is a waterfall MOC worth building?**
A: Yes. A waterfall is the ideal entry point for water-scene MOCs: modest part requirements, simple structure, about 3 hours of work, and a finished model that looks dynamic and photographs extremely well. It is a good practice project for beginners.

**Q: What does it cost to build one?**
A: About 1,000 pieces and 350 yuan following this plan. If you already own transparent bricks and green foliage pieces, the cost drops to under 150 yuan. Transparent bricks are the biggest line item, so buy what you need rather than stockpiling.

**Q: What is the difference between a waterfall MOC and a fountain MOC?**
A: A waterfall emphasizes vertical drop and the sense of pouring water, achieved through tiers and transparent bricks. A fountain emphasizes an upward jet and the arc of falling water, which requires curved pieces and more splash detailing. Fountains are structurally more complex and take longer to build.
