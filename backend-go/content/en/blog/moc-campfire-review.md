# Brick MOC Campfire Review: Glowing Trans-Orange Flames

The short version: this campfire is a small, atmosphere-first build. It runs about 80 parts and 40 minutes, gets its glow from trans-orange pieces plus a light brick, and sits at the very easy end of the difficulty scale. As a finishing touch for a camping or street scene it does a lot of work for its size, and it is well worth picking up.

## Flame Parts and Burning Shape

The flame is built from 1x1 and 1x2 trans-orange and trans-yellow pieces, stacked slightly out of line rather than in tidy columns. The outer layer uses dark orange for the flame tips, while the inner layer uses yellow-white for the core, copying the colour difference between the outside and inside of a real fire. The review copy uses roughly 25 trans pieces to build three layers of flame, and then adds two more 1x1 trans pieces off to the sides as flying sparks. Those two small pieces are what give a static campfire its sense of flicker.

Offset stacking is the whole trick here. Line the trans pieces up neatly and the flame reads as a solid orange cone; stagger them and the light catches at different depths, so the fire looks like it is moving even though nothing on the model actually moves. The three-layer structure also means the colour transition happens gradually, from the bright core out to the darker tips, instead of jumping from yellow to orange in one step.

## Log Pile and Light Effect

The log pile uses brown 1x2 and 1x3 round bricks stacked in alternating directions, crossing horizontally and vertically to mimic the way real firewood is stacked. A small number of dark grey pieces are spread around the base to stand in for charred ash.

The lighting solution is a single 1x1 light brick set into the centre of the flame. Once it is switched on, the warm light spreads through the trans-orange pieces and can illuminate an area of roughly 3x3 studs around the fire, which makes a very obvious difference to a night scene. Because the light source sits inside the flame rather than beside it, the glow comes from the right place and the surrounding transparent parts do the diffusing for you.

One practical note: keep the light brick buried in the inner layer of the log pile. Hidden that way, it is completely invisible from the outside once the model is finished, so the fire appears to light itself.

## Camp Scene, Difficulty and Cost

The builder paired the campfire with one tent, two chairs and one minifigure to form a small camping vignette, placing the fire slightly in front of centre so the composition stays visually balanced. The full set costs about 15 to 30 yuan, and less if you already own a light brick.

What you are paying for:

- About 25 trans-orange and trans-yellow pieces for the flame and sparks
- Brown 1x2 and 1x3 round bricks for the log pile
- A handful of dark grey pieces for the ash
- One 1x1 light brick, the only part that is not a common brick

Trans-orange parts account for roughly 40 percent of the cost; everything else is ordinary brown and grey stock. The build logic is direct and the parts are common, which makes it a good first MOC for beginners: you get the full experience of layering transparent parts and hiding a light source without needing a large parts collection. Value for money is high. If camping is your theme, brickplan.cn has plenty of matching tent and campsite builds worth a look.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I stack the flame so the campfire actually looks real?**

A: Stack trans-orange and trans-yellow pieces out of line with each other, using dark orange on the outside for the flame tips and yellow-white on the inside for the core. About 25 trans pieces across three layers of flame, plus two 1x1 trans pieces as sparks, gives the fire its flickering look.

**Q: How do I add a lighting effect to a brick campfire?**

A: Set one 1x1 light brick into the centre of the flame. When it is switched on, the warm light spreads evenly through the trans-orange pieces and covers roughly a 3x3 stud area. Hide the light brick in the inner layer of the log pile so it cannot be seen from outside.

**Q: How much does a brick MOC campfire cost?**

A: About 15 to 30 yuan for the full set. Trans-orange pieces make up around 40 percent of that, with the rest being common brown and grey bricks. If you build from parts you already have, you only need to buy the light brick, which keeps the total under 10 yuan.
