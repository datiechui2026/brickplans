# How to Build a Cute LEGO Snail MOC: Step-by-Step Guide

The short answer up front: a brick snail MOC is not a hard build. Follow the instructions one step at a time and a new builder can finish in roughly 40 minutes using about 180 pieces, for under 30 yuan in parts. The two things that matter most are the build order — base first, shell second — and the shell technique: a spiral shell made with the offset-layer method looks best.

Step 1: gather the parts. A snail is normally made of four sections — base, body, shell and antennae — and a standard finished model measures about 12 cm long and 8 cm tall. For the basic color scheme, use tan for the body and brown or green for the shell. The eyes take 2 round 1x1 plates plus 2 short 1x2 bars. Once everything is together, sort the parts into piles by color first; the build goes much more smoothly that way.

Step 2: build the base and body. Start with a 4x6 baseplate as the foundation, then stack the body from back to front using 1x2 bricks, extending forward to form the head. Note that the head should sit two or three layers higher than the tail — that is what gives the snail its lively "peeking out" look, and it also leaves room for the antennae later.

Step 3: build the spiral shell. This is the heart of the whole model, and the recommended approach is the offset-layer method. Each layer uses 6 1x2 bricks arranged in a circle, and the next layer is offset inward by half a brick. After 5 or 6 layers a spiral pattern forms naturally. If you want more detail, start adding 45-degree slope bricks from the third layer up to suggest the ridges on a real shell; alternating light and dark colors gives a stronger effect.

Step 4: fit the antennae and details. Push the two short bars into the front of the head and attach a round 1x1 plate to each one as an eye. Add a small flower or a leaf on top of the shell as decoration and the model instantly becomes cuter.

Step 5: check and display. Check that every layer is clutched tightly, and gently press down on any loose sections to firm them up. Add two 2x4 plates underneath and the snail will stand steadily. The overall proportions are about 1.5:1 (shell height to body length), close to a real snail, so it looks great on a desk or beside a plant pot.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the fastest way to build a brick snail MOC?**

A: The fastest method is to build in the order base, body, shell, antennae — lay down the 4x6 baseplate first, then add layers upward. Following step-by-step diagrams, a beginner can finish one in 40 minutes with no modification experience needed.

**Q: How many parts and how much money does a brick snail take?**

A: A standard-sized model needs about 180 pieces. If you pull them from the loose parts you already own, the cost is roughly 20 to 30 yuan. Buying a complete kit with instructions usually runs 40 to 60 yuan and includes all the parts plus a printed instruction booklet, which is convenient and good value.

**Q: The spiral shell is too hard — is there an easier way?**

A: The offset-layer method makes it much simpler: 6 1x2 bricks per layer arranged in a circle, with the next layer offset half a brick inward. Stack 5 or 6 layers and you already have a spiral effect. Once you are comfortable with that, add 45-degree slope bricks for a more three-dimensional shell texture.

**Q: Is a snail MOC worth sharing?**

A: Very much so. Snails are small and charming, they photograph well, and they are a popular subject for beginners practicing their skills and for parent-child building sessions. Take a few photos of your finished model and post them — you will often get improvement suggestions from other builders too.
