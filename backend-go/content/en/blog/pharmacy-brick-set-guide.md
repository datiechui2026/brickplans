# How to Build a Pharmacy Brick Street Corner (16×16) Step by Step

Short answer first: a pharmacy build is not difficult. Pick the right instructions, stock up on white and green parts, and work through the order base → counter → shelves → sign. Even a first-time builder can finish a 16×16-stud corner pharmacy in 4–6 hours, and the parts usually come in under 300 yuan.

## Step 1: Choose Instructions and Gather the Parts

### 1. How to pick instructions

Look for a design in the 1,500–2,200-piece range with a 16×16-stud footprint and two floors — the most classic layout. The ground floor holds the sales area, the upper floor a pharmacist's workroom, so the two functions stay clearly separated. Search "pharmacy" or "street corner pharmacy" on BrickPlan to download high-resolution instructions that come with a complete parts list, so you don't find a gap halfway through the build.

### 2. Parts list

White and green carry the model. Expect roughly 700 white exterior wall bricks, about 200 light gray plates, around 50 green transparent pieces (for the cross sign) and about 40 transparent plates (for the shop window and shelf layers), plus a handful of printed parts: a cash register, medicine bottles and medicine boxes. If you build from loose parts, buy by color in batches from an online marketplace using the list.

## Step 2: Build the Base and the Ground-Floor Sales Area

### 1. Base and walls

Lay a 16×16 baseplate first, then reinforce the four corners with 2×4 bricks as columns. Build the walls with a "two bricks, one plate" bonding pattern and add a plate beam every four courses to stop the wall face from splitting. Plan the door and window openings ahead: a door 4 studs wide lets minifigures walk in and out easily, and the shop window uses large transparent plates for daylight.

### 2. Checkout counter

Build an L-shaped counter against one wall. The countertop is light gray tile, the front face is white plates, and the corner holds the cash register — a dark gray base with a printed screen piece. Leave a 2×4 gap in front of the counter for the pharmacist minifigure in a white coat with a stethoscope accessory, and the scene instantly reads as professional.

## Step 3: Medicine Shelves, Sign and Lighting

### 1. Medicine shelves

Run one shelving unit along each of three walls. Use off-white plates for the uprights and transparent plates for the shelf layers, then fill each layer with medicine boxes and colored 1×1 bottle pieces. Stand a few larger bottles on top of the units and stagger their heights for a more realistic look. Leave 2 studs between shelf runs so minifigures can "reach for medicine", and add a 1×1 support at both ends of every shelf layer so it cannot collapse.

### 2. Green cross sign

Build the cross from 2×2 green bricks, three cells across and three cells down, with a white high-gloss tile on the front face. For more impact, make it a three-dimensional light box: surround it with a 4×4 white frame, inset a green transparent cross, and run a light strip behind it. At night the green glow shows the pharmacy from the whole street — the single most effective detail on the build.

### 3. Interior extras

Place a vending machine by the door (dark gray panels with a transparent door), stick a printed opening-hours tile on the side of the counter, and put two small potted plants on the windowsill. Once warm white lighting is added, the counter and shelves are lit up, and in a late-night street scene the pharmacy looks like a reassuring lamp — it also photographs much better.

## FAQ

**Q: How much does a pharmacy brick set usually cost?**
A: Domestic-brand pharmacy sets of 1,500–2,200 pieces mostly run between 150 and 300 yuan. Buying only the instructions and sourcing the parts yourself costs even less, which suits builders on a tight budget who want practice.

**Q: How do I make the green cross sign look good?**
A: Build the cross from 2×2 green bricks, three cells across and three down, and add a white high-gloss tile on the front. For a three-dimensional light box, wrap a 4×4 white frame around it, inset a transparent green cross and add a light strip — the night-time effect is the best.

**Q: Can a pharmacy build sit next to other street corner builds?**
A: Yes. As long as the baseplate size and floor height match, the modules join up cleanly. The white-and-green palette is very clean, and placing the pharmacy between a dessert shop and a flower shop balances the color layers of the whole street.

**Q: Is a pharmacy build suitable for beginners?**
A: Yes. The pharmacy has a regular structure and simple interior. Build in the order base → counter → shelves → sign and you can finish in 4–6 hours, which makes it a high-value entry point into street corner building.
