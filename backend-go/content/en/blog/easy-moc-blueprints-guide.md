# Simple MOC Instructions: How Beginners Choose and Use Them

Newcomers to brick building and MOC (My Own Creation) models are often put off by complicated instructions. Files with thousands of steps and thousands of parts can make a first original build feel out of reach. Yet simple MOC instructions are entirely possible. If you know how to choose them, read them and use them, your first original model is far less daunting than it looks. This guide focuses on how new builders can find genuinely simple instructions and how to build from them once they are in hand.

## How to Choose Beginner-Friendly Instructions: Start With Piece and Step Counts

The two most direct indicators of whether MOC instructions are simple are piece count and step count. Beginner-level simple instructions usually stay within dozens to one or two hundred pieces, and the steps are simplified to match, often within 30 to 40 steps. Open the file and scan the parts list first. A low piece count and basic part types—mainly bricks, plates and slopes rather than many specialty pieces—are a strong sign that the model is beginner-friendly. A large number of specialty pieces means you may need to source individual parts separately, which raises costs and waiting time and makes the experience less enjoyable.

## Why Small Piece Counts Suit New Builders

Many experienced builders recommend starting with small models, and the advice is practical. A low piece count has clear advantages. First, the parts are easier to gather; you can often find what you need by taking apart a few old sets at home instead of placing a large online order for extra parts. Second, the build time is short—you can finish in a weekend—so the sense of achievement comes quickly and you are less likely to give up halfway. Third, mistakes are easier to trace: checking a few dozen parts from the beginning is quick work. Do not underestimate these benefits. What new builders need most is the positive feedback of finishing a model, and small-piece-count instructions are especially good at providing it.

## What Clear, Step-by-Step Instructions Look Like

A good instruction file needs more than a low piece count; clear steps are essential. Clear stepping means each step highlights the newly added parts in a contrasting color, uses a clear viewing angle, skips no steps and leaves out no connection details. The change between steps stays small, with only a few bricks added each time, so following along rarely gets you stuck. Some instructions also enlarge key connection points or provide side views, and these details are especially helpful to beginners. Before choosing a file, look at a few preview images. If the middle steps look chaotic or parts are heavily stacked on top of one another, skip it.

## How to Practice With Simple Instructions

Once you have simple MOC instructions, sort the parts by color and shape before you start building. This saves a great deal of searching time. Begin at step one and follow the instructions strictly; do not skip ahead by feel. The advantage of simple instructions is that the step order is the optimal order. If a part seems to be missing during the build, first check whether it is buried in the parts pile instead of immediately assuming the instructions are wrong. After you finish, photograph the model and keep the record, then try a small modification—change the color scheme or add a small decoration. That is your first step from following instructions to creating something of your own.

## Mistakes to Avoid and Moving On to Harder Builds

There are two pitfalls to avoid at the beginner stage. The first is reaching too high too soon and challenging a large-piece-count file right away. The second is hoarding instructions: downloading dozens of files but never finishing a single one. Build 3 to 5 simple instruction sets steadily first. Once you are familiar with the parts, structures and step logic, gradually increase the piece count and complexity. You will find that moving up to harder builds goes more smoothly than expected.
