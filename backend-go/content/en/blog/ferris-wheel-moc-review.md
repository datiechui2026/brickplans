# Ferris Wheel MOC Review: Structure, Gears and Night Lighting

The short version: this brickplan Ferris wheel MOC instruction set is a very complete package. The rotation mechanism runs smoothly, the night lighting is genuinely striking, and the whole thing works as an entry point into mechanical MOCs. It totals roughly 2,680 parts, and sourcing the loose parts costs about CNY 320 — more than 60% cheaper than an official set of the same size. Here is the breakdown across three areas.

## Design and accuracy

The model uses the classic twin-arm A-frame support. It stands about 62 cm tall with a wheel diameter of about 48 cm, so it holds its own in a display cabinet.

- 12 symmetrical gondolas, each built from 14 parts (168 parts of the total)
- White, red and grey colour scheme that reads as a city landmark
- Counterweight base under the frame, universal-joint gondola connections, cross-braced spokes

The detailing holds up at both distances: convincing from across the room, still interesting up close. Instructions are packed in clearly numbered bags, so checking parts step by step keeps mistakes rare.

## Build experience

The build splits into four stages: base and power unit, A-frame, wheel rim and spokes, then gondola lighting. The official estimate is 6–8 hours; the real build ran about 7 hours. There are 186 steps across the instructions, averaging 14 parts per step, which gives a comfortable rhythm without long stretches of hunting for a single piece.

The rotation mechanism is the highlight. An 8-tooth gear reduction set drives the rim through a rubber ring friction drive. One turn of the hand crank moves the wheel about a quarter turn, and the speed stays even with no catching or stuttering.

The weak point is tolerance. The clearance between the frame and the rim is on the tight side, and a few pins in third-party compatible parts needed light sanding to fit cleanly.

## Value and playability

Loose parts come to roughly CNY 320, and buying used or parted-out pieces can push that down to about CNY 260. Against official sets at CNY 800–1,200, that is a saving of roughly 60%–70%.

Adding an M motor raises the rotation speed by about 3x, which makes the model far better as a moving display piece. The lighting is a bonus rather than an afterthought: the instructions reserve 20 LED positions, and the 3V battery box wiring is routed inside the spokes. Lit up, the model looks a full step better; in daylight the wiring is essentially invisible.

Overall, this one suits builders who already have some experience and want to try a mechanical drivetrain.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I make the brick MOC Ferris wheel actually turn?**

A: This brickplan design supports both hand cranking and motor drive. The core is the 8-tooth gear reduction set plus the rubber ring friction drive on the rim. Fitting an M motor raises the speed by about 3x; the modification is simple and takes around half an hour.

**Q: How much does the brickplan Ferris wheel cost?**

A: The instructions themselves run about CNY 20–30. Parts work out to roughly CNY 320 based on 2,680 pieces at an average of CNY 0.12 each, and used or parted-out pieces can bring that to around CNY 260. That lands more than 60% below an official set of comparable size.

**Q: Can a beginner build this Ferris wheel straight away?**

A: It is better to have built a smaller MOC under 1,000 parts first. At 186 steps and about 7 hours, this one asks you to understand gear ratios in the drivetrain, so some mechanical grounding makes the process much smoother.

**Q: Are parts from different brands compatible?**

A: Most standard bricks interchange fine, but the pin connections between the frame and the rim are tight on tolerance, so compatible parts may need light sanding. Stick with the brands the instructions recommend, and do not mix brands in the drivetrain.

**Q: How is the night lighting done?**

A: The instructions reserve 20 LED positions, concentrated on the outer edge of the rim and the frame uprights, with wiring hidden inside the spokes. Power comes from a 3V battery box, and the lit-up look is a clear improvement — during the day the light wiring is barely visible.
