# Which Brick Brand Comes First? LEGO vs Chinese Brands

## Why the "which brand first" question never goes away

The first thing almost anyone does when they get into bricks is ask which brand they should buy first. It sounds like a simple question, but it is genuinely hard to answer in one sentence, because every builder has a different budget, a different building habit and a different reason for collecting. Some people care about part precision and the feel of the clutch. Some care more about licensed themes and price. Others just want something cheap and sturdy to pass an evening with. Once you know what you actually want, the answer tends to sort itself out.

## LEGO: the benchmark for precision and quality

Ask most experienced builders which brand they would recommend first, and their instinct is still LEGO. LEGO's moulding accuracy, the feel of the clutch between parts and the gloss of the plastic have long been the industry benchmark. Builds go together smoothly, parts come apart without damage, and a model that sits on a shelf for years does not loosen up. Add the large catalogue of licensed IP and official sets, and you get strong collectability plus a secondhand market that holds value relatively well. The downsides are just as clear: it is expensive, the cost per brick is high, and buying individual small parts or loose pieces to fill gaps is not cheap either.

## Chinese brick brands: value and subject matter in one

Chinese brick brands have improved very quickly in recent years, with real leaps in both part precision and design quality. Brands such as SEMBO, Qiji, Mould King and Double Eagle each have signature models in themes like technic-style machinery, space, military and modular street scenes, and their prices are often only a fraction of what LEGO charges for a comparable size. If you are new to the hobby, on a limited budget, and want the experience of a large build, a major Chinese brand is a fairly safe choice — especially when the plan is to build it once and put it on display, where the value is hard to beat.

## How to choose by budget and need

Put plainly: if your budget is comfortable and you want the best possible building feel plus collectability, LEGO still deserves first consideration. If you want value for money, want to build bigger and more numerous models on a fixed budget, or are drawn to subjects that only Chinese brands produce, the leading Chinese brands suit you better. For a child learning the basics, or for something you will build, play with for a few days and then take apart, an affordable Chinese brand is completely sufficient. For a detailed piece you plan to display long term, look at LEGO or the higher-end Chinese technic brands.

## A bit of experience from my own builds

After years of building, my take is this: don't worship a brand name, and don't write off Chinese brands wholesale either. What actually determines your experience is the design and part quality of one specific set. Before buying, read reviews and look through the parts list — that is far more reliable than agonising over a brand name. So when someone asks me which brick brand comes first, I usually turn it around: work out what you are building for, and the answer follows.

## FAQ

**Q: Which brick brand should I buy first?**
A: There is no single best answer. If your budget allows and you want the best building feel and collectability, LEGO comes first. If your budget is limited, you want a larger model for the same money, or you prefer subjects unique to Chinese brands, go with a leading Chinese brand such as SEMBO, Qiji, Mould King or Double Eagle.

**Q: LEGO costs more than Chinese bricks — is it worth it?**
A: It depends what you are after. LEGO's moulding precision, clutch feel and part finish are the industry benchmark, it has many licensed IPs, and it holds value relatively well secondhand. If the bricks are mainly for practice, structural filler or kids to play with, Chinese brands offer better value.

**Q: Is the quality of Chinese bricks reliable now?**
A: It has improved very quickly, with real gains in part precision and design. SEMBO, Qiji, Mould King and Double Eagle all have representative models in themes like technic-style machinery, space, military and modular street scenes, and their prices are often only a fraction of LEGO's for a comparable size.

**Q: When buying bricks, does the brand or the specific set matter more?**
A: The specific set matters more. What decides your experience is the design and part quality of that one model. Reading reviews and checking the parts list before you buy is far more dependable than agonising over a brand name.
