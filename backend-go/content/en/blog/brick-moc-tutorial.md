# Brick MOC Tutorial: From First Build to Advanced Techniques

MOC — short for My Own Creation — is the highest level of brick building. Moving from following instructions to designing your own models is the path every builder travels. This brick MOC tutorial takes you from zero to the core skills of MOC work, so you can start a building journey of your own.

## What Is a MOC, and Why Should You Try It?

### Defining the MOC

A MOC is a model you design and build entirely yourself rather than assembling from official instructions. It reflects the creator's design ability, spatial imagination and sense of aesthetics.

The real value of a brick MOC tutorial is that it is not just a set of instructions — it is a way of guiding how you think about building.

### Why build a MOC?

- **Release your creativity**: no longer limited by official instructions; let your imagination run
- **One of a kind**: your model is the only one of its kind in the world
- **Skill growth**: you move from assembler to designer, a qualitative jump in ability
- **Community recognition**: strong MOCs attract a lot of attention and praise in the community

## Beginner Section: The First Step Into MOC

### Start by copying

Don't feel that copying means you are not original enough. Every MOC master started by copying.

**Recommended copying exercises**:
1. **Take apart official sets**: analyse official design and understand the structure and building techniques
2. **Rebuild classic models**: try recreating known models with different parts
3. **Local modification**: make small-scope changes on top of an official set

### Basic technique training

In any brick MOC tutorial, these basics are mandatory:

**SNOT**
SNOT (Studs Not On Top) is the core technique of MOC work: building with the studs facing sideways or downward. It lets your model break out of the flat plane and achieve three-dimensional shapes.

**Structural mechanics**
- Triangular structures are the most stable
- Staggered stacking increases strength
- Internal support structure prevents collapse

**Colour pairing**
- No more than three main colours
- Use neutral colours to balance
- Accent colours to highlight key points

### Tools to prepare

**Software**:
- **Studio 2.0**: the most popular MOC design software, free and powerful
- **LDD (LEGO Digital Designer)**: official software, good for beginners
- **BrickLink Studio**: includes a parts library and rendering

**Hardware**:
- Brick separator (essential)
- Sorting and storage boxes
- Plenty of light

## Advanced Section: The Core MOC Workflow

### Finding inspiration

In a brick MOC tutorial, inspiration is the starting point of creation. Where to find it:

- **The real world**: architecture, nature, machines, animals and plants
- **Film and TV**: characters and scenes from movies, animation and games
- **Artworks**: shapes and composition from painting and sculpture
- **Other MOCs**: browse other creators' models on BrickPlan.cn

### Design planning

**Settle the theme**
Be clear about what you want to create: architecture, mecha, a creature, or a scene?

**Choose a scale**
- **Minifig scale (1:48)**: suited to street scenes and dioramas
- **Micro scale (1:100+)**: suited to large scenes and cities
- **Minifig scale**: suited to mecha and vehicles

**Plan the functions**
- Static display: focus on appearance and detail
- Posable structure: add joints, wheels and other moving parts
- Interactive functions: add lights, mechanisms and so on

### Digital design

Using Studio for digital design:

1. **Build the basic structure**: finish the main frame first
2. **Refine the exterior**: add details step by step
3. **Optimise the structure**: check stability, adjust weak points
4. **Generate a parts list**: the software calculates the parts needed
5. **Render an image**: show the final result

### Buying parts

In a brick MOC tutorial, parts sourcing is the key step before physical building:

- **Inventory what you own**: pick usable parts from your existing collection
- **Buy as needed**: top up missing parts from the parts list
- **Colour substitution**: when a colour is unavailable, use a close match
- **Buy loose parts**: buy individual parts on BrickPlan.cn or BrickLink

## Expert Section: Techniques of MOC Masters

### Texture and material feel

**Wall texture**:
- Brick wall: alternating 1x1 and 1x2 bricks
- Stone wall: irregular part combinations
- Wood: brown tiles with stripes

**Curved shapes**:
- Use hinge parts to make arcs
- Slope bricks for transitions
- Tires and hoses for curves

### Internal structure

**Hidden support**:
- Standard bricks for the internal skeleton
- Thin plates on the outside as decoration
- Removable design so the interior can be shown

**Modular design**:
- Split large models into modules
- Build each module independently
- Connect modules with technic pins

### Display and photography

Once the model is finished, good presentation makes it shine:

- **Background**: a plain background sets off the subject
- **Lighting**: light sources from multiple angles show detail
- **Staging**: use accessories to create atmosphere
- **Angles**: shoot from several angles to highlight the model's features
