# Brick Golf Course MOC Review: 9 Holes, Sand Traps, Playability

The short version: this brick golf course MOC runs about 2,640 pieces and builds into a 42 cm x 30 cm x 9 cm layout. The instruction file costs 35 yuan, and at BrickPlan's average of 0.20 yuan per part the full build comes to roughly 560 yuan. Expect around 6 hours of building time. Scene fidelity is high, the model packs a 9-hole mini course with a ball that actually rolls, and it stands out as a sports-themed MOC you can genuinely play with. It also works well as a parent-and-child project.

## Scene Fidelity

The course is laid out across nine holes. Putting surfaces are picked out in light green tiles so the green reads clearly against the fairway, sand traps are ringed with tan curved bricks, and water hazards use trans-light-blue bricks. Overall the model captures about 75% of what the real thing looks like. Every hole has a flagstick and a cup, and the slopes between fairways are built up with baseplates so the elevation changes give the ball a more believable line as it rolls. The inside diameter of the cup is sized to hold a standard brick ball snugly, and the amount of drag on a putt that drops in has clearly been tuned rather than left to chance.

## Mechanisms and Playability

What sells this model is that it plays. It ships with a standard brick golf ball plus two brick clubs, a driver and a putter, so you can recreate both the tee shot and the putting stroke. The sand trap section has deliberately raised lips, which means a ball that lands there comes to a natural stop, matching the real rule where finding the sand costs you a stroke. The instructions also include suggested par figures for all nine holes, and racing a partner to finish on 18 strokes is genuinely fun. Among sports MOCs, the family-interaction factor here is right at the top of the pile.

## Value and Recommendation

Instructions are 35 yuan, and 2,640 parts at the 0.20 yuan average works out to about 528 yuan, so budget roughly 560-600 yuan in total. Against modular street-scene MOCs of a similar footprint, that is mid-range value rather than a bargain. The colour palette is dominated by light green and sand yellow, and because those colours are concentrated in a few areas, sorting is efficient. The trans-clear water bricks are the exception: they mix easily with other transparent parts and need careful separation before you start.

This one is for builders who like sports themes and want an interactive display piece. If there are younger children in the house, put it near the top of your list. Display-only builders may want to hold off and see whether BrickPlan later lists a larger 18-hole version.

## FAQ

**Q: Where can I download the instructions for this brick golf course MOC?**

A: Search for "golf course" on brickplan.cn. After purchase you download a PDF that includes the step-by-step build, a complete parts list, and the rules for the 9-hole par scoring game.

**Q: How hard is this MOC to build?**

A: Medium-low. The whole thing takes around 6 hours, and the main challenge is picking the right curved bricks for the sand traps and the trans-clear bricks for the water. If you have basic building experience you can finish it solo, and children aged 8 and up can take part with guidance.

**Q: What does the whole thing cost?**

A: 35 yuan for the instructions plus roughly 528 yuan for about 2,640 parts at the 0.20 yuan average, so around 560-600 yuan in total. If you already own a stock of green parts, buy the instructions only and fill the gaps from your own bins to bring the cost under 200 yuan.

**Q: Can you really play golf on this course?**

A: Yes. It comes with a brick ball and two clubs, and both the fairway slopes and the cup are designed around a rolling ball, so you can hit, roll and sink putts. The instructions' par figures let you play a proper 18-stroke competition.

**Q: How big is the finished model, and where does it fit?**

A: It measures 42 cm long, 30 cm wide and 9 cm high. Because it is a flat layout it takes up almost no vertical space, which makes it a good fit for a middle shelf or a coffee table as a desktop display.
