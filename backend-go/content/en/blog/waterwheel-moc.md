# Waterwheel MOC Review: A Turning Countryside Scene in 380 Pieces

Bottom line first: this waterwheel MOC, with instructions from brickplan.cn, is a very complete little build. The shaping is faithful and the moving parts actually work. I'd give it 8.8/10. It comes in at around 380 parts, stands 10 cm tall, and takes about an hour to build — a standout small scene for a desktop countryside display.

## 1. Design and Shaping

The main body uses a classic spoked-wheel structure. Twelve wheel blades are arranged in a staggered pattern, so the wheel reads with real depth as it turns. The colour scheme is built on natural wood brown and grass green, set against off-white walls and a stone-grey base — one look and you're in a quiet village. The proportions are well judged: the wheel measures about 8 cm across, and it connects naturally to the mill house and the water channel beside it. It doesn't eat up desk space, yet it's big enough to carry the whole picture.

## 2. Detail Work

The channel uses transparent blue pieces to suggest flowing water, running from a high point down to the top of the wheel. The visual line of "water travelling up onto the wheel" is very clear. The mill house has a chimney, a porch and a small flower bed, and the brick walls are laid in a staggered bond, so from the side you get a genuine masonry feel. Worth calling out too are the plants and fence on either side of the base: only about twenty parts in total, but they immediately make the scene feel alive.

## 3. Build Experience

The instructions are divided sensibly into 4 major stages, roughly 95 parts each, and the logic is clear throughout. The wheel axle section, which pairs gears with axle bushes, is the most interesting part of the whole build: once assembled, a light flick of the wheel sends it spinning smoothly for about 5 turns before it stops. The damping is just right — not sloppy, not binding. There were no obvious missing parts, clutch power is firm, and repeated assembly and disassembly left no white stress marks.

## 4. Value for Money

Given 380 parts, an hour of building time and excellent moving features, this waterwheel MOC offers very strong value. Comparable official sets of the same size often run 200–300 RMB, while sourcing the bricks yourself and building from the brickplan instructions costs less and lets you recolour freely. As an entry-level MOC in the countryside theme, it balances looks, play value and collectability.
