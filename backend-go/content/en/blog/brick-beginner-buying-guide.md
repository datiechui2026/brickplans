# What to Buy as a First Brick Set: A 5-Step Beginner Checklist

Short answer first: for your first set, pick something in the 300–800 piece range, priced at 100–300 yuan, built around a city street scene or a vehicle, with a build time of 3–6 hours. Do not start with a 1000+ piece monster — most people stall halfway and never finish. Follow the five steps below and you will almost certainly buy well.

## Step 1: Set the budget before you set the part count

For a beginner, 100–300 yuan is the sweet spot. In that band you can get a complete set from a top-tier domestic brand with 300–800 pieces, including numbered bags, an instruction booklet and a baseplate. No-name sets under 50 yuan have loose tolerances and a high rate of missing parts — not a good first purchase. Sets above 500 yuan are also poor value for a beginner; save those for an upgrade later on.

## Step 2: Choose a theme you can actually finish

Pick your first theme from three families: city street scenes (café, bookshop, flower shop), classic vehicles (sports car, truck), or small technic-style mechanisms. These themes use a wide variety of parts, have clear build logic, and the finished model works as a display piece. Themes like spaceships or castles rely on heavy part repetition, so the middle of the build gets boring — leave those for later challenges.

## Step 3: Match part count to build time

Part count directly drives how long you sit at the table: about 300 pieces runs 2–3 hours, 500 pieces runs 3–5 hours, and 800 pieces runs 5–8 hours. For a first set, stay under 800 pieces so you can finish in a single weekend and get the full sense of achievement. Sets of 1000 pieces and up should wait for your second or third build, so you do not give up partway through.

## Step 4: Check the brand and the brick quality

Go with established domestic brands that have a track record — they typically state part tolerances within 0.05 mm and offer a missing-part replacement service. Once the box arrives, run three checks: how much plastic dust is in the bags, whether the clutch power feels consistent, and whether there are obvious mould seam lines. If all three pass, the quality is solid. If any one clearly fails, contact after-sales support right away.

## Step 5: Inspect the numbered bags and the instructions

A well-made set usually has 8–12 numbered bags, each covering one section of the instruction booklet, so you are not hunting across a table full of loose parts. The instruction diagrams should be large, and each step should add only 2–4 pieces. These two details decide the beginner experience. Before buying, look at real photos of the instruction booklet on the product page — if the print is blurry or the step diagrams are tiny, cross that set off your list.

## Beginner shopping list

- **100–150 yuan:** a small street scene or vehicle set with 300–500 pieces, roughly 2–4 hours to build.
- **150–300 yuan:** a 600–800 piece street scene or mechanism set, roughly 5–8 hours to build.

## FAQ

**Q: Which brand should a brick beginner buy?**
A: Start with a top-tier domestic brand. Part tolerances are tight, missing-part replacement service exists, and 100–300 yuan gets you a set that delivers more than 80% of the LEGO experience — ideal for building confidence while you learn.

**Q: How many pieces should a first set have?**
A: 300–800 pieces. Around 300 pieces takes 2–3 hours and 800 pieces takes 5–8 hours, so either fits neatly into a weekend: enough for a sense of achievement without dragging on long enough that you abandon it.

**Q: What budget makes sense for a beginner?**
A: 100–300 yuan is the most comfortable range. Under 100 yuan you are mostly looking at no-name sets with no quality guarantee, and there is no need to spend over 500 yuan on a first set — upgrade to bigger builds once you know you enjoy the hobby and understand your own taste.

**Q: Should a beginner buy domestic brands or LEGO first?**
A: Domestic first. You get the full building process for roughly a third of the price. Once you have built three or more sets and know you are in for the long haul, consider a flagship LEGO set for collecting and holding value.
