# Mid-Autumn Festival MOC: Moon, Jade Rabbit and Courtyard Build

Short version: a Mid-Autumn MOC works best as a quiet courtyard scene. The moon and the jade rabbit carry the theme, mooncakes and an osmanthus tree supply the tabletop detail, and warm lighting plus a restrained Chinese palette finish it off.

The Mid-Autumn Festival is one of the more poetic subjects in Chinese-style brick building. The moon, jade rabbit, mooncakes and osmanthus tree are instantly recognizable, and they carry the idea of reunion with them. Compared with the bustle of a Spring Festival build, a Mid-Autumn MOC suits something calm and atmospheric — a courtyard at night. Here is a practical plan, from the core symbols through courtyard layout to lighting and color.

## Moon and Jade Rabbit: The Core Symbols

The moon is the visual center of the scene. For a large moon, build the round body from light yellow curved bricks and tiles, then run a ring of off-white slope pieces around the edge for a soft transition. If you want the look of a full moon rising into the sky, lift the moon up into mid-air and back it with a dark blue baseplate.

The jade rabbit follows the usual animal-MOC approach: a rounded white body, long ears angled outward on wedge pieces, small red eyes and a round tail. The pose that reads best is the rabbit hugging a "mooncake" built from stacked 1x1 round tiles.

## Mooncakes and Osmanthus Tree: The Tabletop Details

Build mooncakes from dark yellow or light brown 1x1 round tiles stacked two layers high, with a thin gold line between them to suggest the pattern on the crust. Arrange a set on a round turntable piece and you have a mooncake gift box.

The osmanthus tree can stay small. Use a brown round bar for the trunk and build the crown from yellow-green studs, dotted with a few small white flower pieces to stand in for osmanthus blossoms. Put two minifigures facing each other under the tree, each "holding" a mooncake, and the scene immediately reads as a reunion.

## Designing the Moon-Viewing Courtyard

The courtyard is the stage for the whole MOC. Lay a grey-white baseplate for the ground and enclose it with dark brown bricks for walls or railings, leaving an open area in the middle for a stone table and stools. Stack tiles to make the stools, and set the table with a teapot — a hemisphere piece with a thin bar for the spout — plus the mooncakes. Plant the osmanthus tree against the wall and scatter a few fallen leaves on the ground.

If you want more depth, add a short flight of steps and put the moon and the tree on a raised platform, so the viewer's eye travels up and down and the courtyard feels three-dimensional rather than flat.

## Using Warm Lighting

Mid-Autumn is about moonlight and lantern light together. A warm yellow light brick placed inside or behind the moon simulates that soft glow. Hang one or two small lanterns under the eaves — red curved slope bricks with a tassel — and once they are lit, the whole courtyard warms up. Choose warm light rather than cool white: warm light against a dark blue night sky is the classic Mid-Autumn combination and does the most to sell the feeling of reunion.

## Chinese Palette and Display Advice

Keep the overall palette to a deep blue night sky, an off-white moon and a brown courtyard, with red lanterns and golden yellow accents as supporting colors. Avoid stacking large areas of red and green against each other.

Build the finished scene as a display piece on a baseplate — it sits well on a bookshelf or in an entryway. If you have added a lighting kit, remember to leave an opening for the battery box so it can be swapped easily. Lit up under real moonlight, the finished courtyard makes a genuinely memorable brick Mid-Autumn display.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I make a Mid-Autumn MOC feel atmospheric?**
A: The core is the moon, the jade rabbit and a moon-viewing courtyard: a full moon from light yellow round pieces, a rabbit seated in white small parts, and a courtyard with table, stools and an osmanthus tree. Keep the overall mood quiet and focused on reunion.

**Q: How do I build the moon and the jade rabbit?**
A: Build the full moon outline from light yellow or off-white round and curved pieces, and add support pieces behind it to hold it in place. For the rabbit, use basic white parts in a seated pose and stand the ears up with two slope pieces — the long ears are the key detail.

**Q: How do I add detail to the mooncakes and the osmanthus tree?**
A: Make the mooncakes from square pieces topped with printed tiles and arrange them on a plate. For the osmanthus tree, use a brown stem piece as the trunk and pile small yellow-green round pieces into the crown, adding a few small gold pieces to suggest the blossoms.

**Q: How should I use warm lighting?**
A: Place one warm yellow light above the courtyard and one over the table, at a color temperature of 2700 to 3500K and kept dim. Use a weak cool white fill light for the moonlight — once that warm-cool contrast is in place, the Mid-Autumn mood lands immediately.
