# Which Sluban Series Is Best? Full Lineup Review

Most builders end up asking the same question before their first Sluban order: which series is actually worth buying? Sluban is a Chinese brand best known for military subjects, but its catalogue runs much wider than that, covering military, space, engineering and city themes. Below I compare the main lines across three things that matter most — part quality, subject appeal and value for money — so you can see which one fits how you build.

## Military Series: The Flagship Line for Detail Lovers

The military series is Sluban's best-known flagship, and it is the usual first answer when someone asks which series to start with. The line-up centres on tanks, armoured vehicles, artillery and warships — hardcore subjects with a high degree of shape accuracy. Sluban's military models hold up well on part precision: tracks, turrets and other key structures lock together firmly, and loose, floppy assemblies are rare during the build. Stickers and printed parts still fall short of LEGO's detail, but at the same price point the brand sits in the top tier of domestic manufacturers. If you are a military fan who wants a tank model with real presence on the shelf, this series is close to a no-brainer.

## Space Series: Fresh Subject Matter, Strong Display Value

Space has been a major focus for Sluban over the past two years, with launch rockets, space stations and satellites in the range. The big draw here is the subject matter itself — few comparable products exist on the market, and finished models photograph beautifully, giving a bookshelf a genuine sense of the future. The colour palette leans on white, grey and blue, and both printed parts and stickers are faithfully done. That said, the space series is positioned more for display: playability is thinner than the military line's richer gearing and mechanisms, and prices run a little higher. It suits collectors and anyone chasing an unusual theme.

## Engineering Series: Best Value and the Easiest Entry Point

If value is your priority, engineering is the line I would recommend to newcomers first. Excavators, cranes and loaders are subjects with broad appeal, the structures are relatively simple, and the build difficulty sits in a comfortable middle ground that works for both kids and adults. Prices across the engineering series are generally friendly, part quality is consistent, and some models include simple gear linkages. That combination makes it very strong value. If your budget is limited but you still want to see what Sluban's moulding and fit are like, engineering is the line that will not disappoint you.

## City Series: Rich Scenes, Good for Mixing Displays

The city series covers street scenes, vehicles and everyday life settings, leaning toward casual building and decoration. Its strengths are variety and part compatibility — the pieces mix easily with the military and engineering lines, so you can build out a small brick world of your own. Overall it is aimed at a broader, younger audience, and it carries less technical complexity than the series above. What it offers instead is visual appeal and combinability, which makes it a good fit for family builds and parent-child sessions.

## Verdict: Which Sluban Series Should You Buy?

Different needs point to different answers. For hardcore building and fine detail, go with the **military series**. If you want an unusual subject with collection value, choose the **space series**. If value for money and an easy entry point matter most, the **engineering series** is the best match. And if you want scene-building and family fun, the **city series** will win you over.

Across the board, Sluban's part quality sits in the upper-middle range among domestic brands, with value as its clearest strength. If you are still weighing up which series to buy, decide by the dimension you care about most — whether you are a military fan, a collector or a first-time builder, one of these lines will land.
