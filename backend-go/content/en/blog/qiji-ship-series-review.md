# Qiji Ship Series MOC Review: Freighters, Liners and Sailboats

## Product Overview

The Qiji ship series is one of the better-known Chinese brick lines built around a single theme: vessels. The range covers ocean-going container freighters, cruise liners, sailing ships and fishing boats. Qiji has a reputation for **strong value for money** and **detailed, well-laid-out instructions**, and its modular hull design lets builders assemble convincing ship models without hunting down specialist parts first.

This review focuses on the classic kits in the series and works through them from the parts bags to the finished display piece, so ship MOC fans get a realistic picture of what they are buying.

## Build Difficulty

| Set | Build difficulty | Part count | Best for |
|---------|---------|---------|---------|
| Ocean container freighter | ★★★★☆ | 2,500–3,000 pieces | Intermediate to advanced builders |
| Cruise liner | ★★★★★ | 3,200–3,800 pieces | Experienced builders |
| Three-masted sailing ship | ★★★☆☆ | 1,200–1,600 pieces | Beginner to intermediate builders |
| Coastal fishing boat | ★★☆☆☆ | 600–900 pieces | Beginners |

Across the range the difficulty curve is spread fairly evenly. The sailing ship and the fishing boat are good places for newcomers to learn how a hull is put together, while the freighter and the cruise liner are the more demanding step-up builds.

## Parts Review

**Clutch power** on the Qiji ship series is above average for its price bracket. Parts press together firmly, nothing feels loose mid-build, and the finished model holds its shape. The hulls lean heavily on a classic palette of **dark blue, pure white and red below the waterline**, with clearly separated deck levels and bulwarks that read well at a glance.

The standout parts choice on the **ocean container freighter** is the standardised container design: same-size coloured blocks you can stack in any arrangement, recreating the look of a working container terminal. The **cruise liner** instead makes heavy use of large curved bow pieces and transparent porthole elements, with a white hull set off by a light blue stripe that gives it the elegant profile of a real liner. Some sets also include small detail parts such as lifeboats and anchor chains, which add a lot to the finished build. ## Building Experience

The build process is **logically sequenced**. The instructions are broken into detailed steps, and every step gives a clear placement reference. Hull construction usually starts at the **keel and midsection**, then extends forward to the bow and back to the stern, with the superstructure last.

Rigging and mast assembly on the three-masted sailing ship is the highlight of the range. It takes some patience, but once the sails are in place the billowing, wind-filled look is genuinely dynamic. The freighter build is more mechanical in feel, and working through the dense rows of containers on deck gives you a real sense of loading a ship. Overall there is very little step repetition or misplaced-part trouble in this series; the pace stays smooth, which makes these kits well suited to a relaxed weekend build.

## Finished Model Showcase

The finished models **display very well**. The ocean container freighter comes out over 60 cm long, with neat stacks of containers across the deck and clean, flowing lines at bow and stern. It has real presence in a display cabinet.

The cruise liner is the more ornate of the two: multiple deck levels, funnels, portholes and lifeboat details, and a bright white hull that makes it a feature piece for a living room shelf. The three-masted sailing ship and the coastal fishing boat are more moderately sized and work nicely as desk models. Paired with the matching harbour and dock MOC instructions on [brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn), you can put together a complete maritime scene and take the display up another level.

## Verdict

★★★★☆ (4.5/5)

The Qiji ship series is a **strong mix of value and quality** for anyone building in a maritime theme. The big sets such as the cruise liner do take a long time to finish, but the solid part quality and smooth building experience more than make up for it. Newcomers should start with the three-masted sailing ship or the coastal fishing boat, then move on to the freighter and the liner once they know the hull system.
