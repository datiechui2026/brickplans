# Brick Fox MOC Review: Shape Accuracy, Poses and Build Tips

## Bushy Tail and Pointed Ears: The Craft Behind the Shape

The thing nobody forgets about a fox is the tail — a plume almost as long as the body itself. Among the animal MOCs I have reviewed, this brick fox blueprint handles the tail with real care. Instead of lazily making it one thick cylinder, the instructions build it from multiple hinge segments and staggered, interlocking wedge pieces, so the tail carries a natural curve and a genuinely fluffy silhouette. Tap it lightly and the tip sways a little, which gives the model a surprising amount of life.

The sharp upright ears and the narrow muzzle deserve equal credit. The blueprint uses a fair number of sideways building techniques in the head, which keeps the face from turning into a blocky square. The ears stand straight up, the nose pushes slightly forward, and the inset transparent eyes finish the job — suddenly there is an alert, quick-eyed woodland creature sitting on your desk. The one small letdown is the transition between the ear tips and the skull, which feels a touch abrupt. A softer curved slope piece in a future revision would make it close to perfect.

## Fur Gradient and Pose Design: From Accuracy to Playability

The red fox's coloring — fiery red across the back, snow white underneath — is the real test of an author's skill. This blueprint does not cut corners. It layers three base colors (dark brown, orange-red and cream white) across the side panels, so the color separation reads clearly from a distance while the structure still looks tidy up close. The instructions also include alternative parts for three poses: standing, lying prone, and glancing back over the shoulder. Swapping the parts at the joints is enough to change the pose, which extends the freshness of the finished model considerably.

On the building side, the step breakdown is sensible and the key stages are clearly called out. A builder at intermediate level will finish in roughly three to four hours with almost no rework. Structural rigidity is satisfying too — swing the tail back and forth and nothing comes loose. For anyone who likes animal models and also cares about value for money, a fox-themed MOC like this is an appealing proposition.

## Who It Suits and Building Tips

The blueprint sits at intermediate difficulty. It works well for adult builders with some experience, and it also suits a parent-and-child build session. A few suggestions for newcomers:

- Sort the parts by color family before you start breaking out pieces. It makes finding parts noticeably faster.
- The multi-segment hinge tail is the soul of the whole model. When you assemble it, double-check that the pin directions line up, or the tail will sag later on.
- If you want a more convincing fur gradient, prepare your own darker and lighter parts in the same color family and swap them in.
