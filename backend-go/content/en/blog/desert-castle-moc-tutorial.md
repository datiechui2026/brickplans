# Desert Castle MOC Tutorial: Build a Sandstone Brick Citadel

Castle MOCs are everywhere, but most of them stand in green medieval meadows. A desert castle MOC drops the same silhouette into blowing sand, rough sandstone, rounded domes and a dying oasis, and the whole thing picks up an Arabian Nights mood. This guide takes you from color choices to wall structure so you can start building straight away.

## 1. Set the Palette: Sandstone Texture Starts With Color

Half of a desert castle's success is decided by color. For the main walls, mix **Tan + Light Brown** in the same run to imitate weathered sandstone, then add a few orange and dark red pieces as accents to suggest rock strata at sunset. Avoid pure grey at all costs. A grey wall instantly turns the model into a plain grey fort and kills the desert feel.

**The rule of thumb**: warm walls, hot ground, gold trim. Use gold pieces for doors, windows and lantern brackets. Balance that warmth with a cool blue oasis pool. One warm, one cool, and the visual tension takes care of itself.

## 2. Wall Structure: Solid Core First, Surface Detail Second

Desert castles are mostly thick mud-brick walls, which makes them easier to build than European castles:

1. **Base**: lay a ring of 2x6 dark brown bricks as both foundation and rammed-earth plinth. It reads as more stable and grounds the model.
2. **Main wall**: run standard bricks in staggered bond up to about 12 studs tall. Every 4 courses, insert a row of 1x1 bricks standing one stud proud of the wall to create a peeling, broken-up surface. This is the key trick for getting wind erosion into a desert castle MOC.
3. **Corners**: reinforce all four corners with 2x2 corner bricks. This adds strength and matches the traditional desert castle layout with a tower at each corner.

Once the walls are tall, always add a ring of plates on the inside as lining. Without it, the top of the wall will bow outward when you start stacking crenellations.

## 3. Roofs and Domes: The Signature of a Desert Castle

The soul of desert architecture is the **dome and the crenellated parapet**.

- **Main dome**: ring four 1x2 curved slopes (dome wedge) around a 4x4 round plate to enclose a hemisphere, then cap the center with a gold 1x1 round piece. That gives you the classic onion dome. Under the dome, set a ring of 2x4 light gray plates as a drum, which adds an instant layer of depth.
- **Merlons and crenellations**: on top of the walls, alternate 1x1 bricks one stud out and one stud in to build the crenellated parapet, then cap it with a ring of sand-colored tiles as a walkway.
- **Corner towers**: raise a slim tower at each corner, 4 courses taller than the main wall. Top each one with a small spire made from 1x1 slopes. The changing heights give the model a much more appealing silhouette.

## 4. Oasis Water Feature: Put the Castle at the Water's Edge

A desert castle without water feels dead. Press an 8x8 blue baseplate into the ground in front of the castle to form the pool, edge it with sand-colored 1x2 bricks, and scatter a few trans-blue 1x1 round studs across the surface as glints of light. Then plant two palm trees: brown 1x2 hinge bricks bend into curved trunks, and green 1x1 slopes become the fronds. A patch of blue water against ocher walls is the focal point of the whole scene.

**Ground detail**: outside the walls, alternate sand and light brown plates to build low dunes, then sprinkle a few 1x1 light yellow studs as drifting-sand texture. That small step is what sells the sense of wind moving across the sand.

## 5. Details and Pitfall Checklist

- Add a working wooden gate in front of the castle entrance (two 1x4 plates plus hinge pieces), and place minifigures patrolling inside. A scene needs a story before it reads as anything more than a pile of bricks.
- **Pitfall 1**: tan parts vary noticeably in shade between production batches. Compare pieces before you commit to a layout, separate the yellowish ones from the greyish ones, and never mix them on the same wall face.
- **Pitfall 2**: the dome hemisphere is a fragile structure. Always carry the model by the drum underneath, never by the dome itself.
- **Pitfall 3**: store the trans-blue water pieces away from the rest of your stock. Transparent parts scratch easily, so keep them in their own bag.

## 6. Final Thoughts

A desert castle MOC tests your eye for color and your patience for structure in equal measure. Get the palette right, line the walls properly, and you end up with a small kingdom of your own sitting on the desk.
