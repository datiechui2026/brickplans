# Zelda Brick MOC Plans: Building Hyrule in Bricks

The short version: if you are new to Zelda MOCs, start with a small scene under 300 pieces, and only commit to a plan that ships with a complete parts list and multi-angle renders.

## Why Zelda MOCs Are So Popular

The Legend of Zelda series is built on open-world exploration and a free-roaming spirit of adventure, and **Zelda brick MOC plans** carry that landscape into the brick world. MOC stands for My Own Creation — a design made by a fan rather than a company. Compared with official sets, MOC plans tend to stay closer to how the game actually looks, and the details are usually richer, which is why Nintendo brick builders keep coming back to them. Zelda has long been one of the most popular themes in the brick community, and because official sets are limited in number, MOC plans fill that gap. If you are just getting started, the [BrickPlan blog](/en/blog) is a good place to pick up the basics of how MOCs work.

## Character Accuracy: Link and the Master Sword

When you judge a Zelda brick MOC plan, character accuracy comes first. A strong plan handles Link's green pointed cap and the Hylian shield emblem with real care, and the blade proportions of the Master Sword are a genuine test of the designer's skill. One designer, for example, used more than 2,000 pieces to recreate Link riding across the open fields on horseback — the finished model is genuinely striking in a display cabinet. On BrickPlan you can find plenty of popular Link minifigure plans, ranging from a few hundred pieces to over a thousand, so there is a build for every skill level.

## Scene Design: From Hyrule Castle to Breath of the Wild

Beyond characters, scene-based **Zelda brick MOC plans** are just as impressive. Hyrule Castle, the Temple of Time and Korok Forest are rebuilt landmark by landmark through brick stacking and color choices, and the visual impact is considerable. Good plans also include step-by-step building instructions and a parts list, so even a newcomer can work through them without stress. If you prefer large scenes, there are bigger plans that include shrine mechanisms and Guardians — the build itself becomes an adventure.

## How to Choose the Right Plan for You

When picking a Zelda brick MOC plan, look first at the piece count and the stated difficulty. Beginners are safer starting with a small scene under 300 pieces. Pay attention to the preview images and to what other builders say, so you avoid a plan that looks good and builds badly. It is also worth confirming whether the plan includes a complete parts list and multi-angle renders, since both directly affect how the build goes. More reviews and resources are collected on the [BrickPlan plan downloads](/en/blog) page, where the regularly updated Zelda plan collections are worth bookmarking.

Whether you are recreating a classic character or rebuilding a famous moment from Breath of the Wild, these plans open a door into Hyrule. Pick one you like and start your own adventure in bricks.
