# Best MOC Communities in 2026 for Brick Builders

For MOC builders, a good community does more than hand you a folder of instructions. It gives you a place to trade techniques, get honest feedback on a half-finished model, and find people who care as much as you do about whether that slope should be a 2x2 or a 1x3. Below is a rundown of the most active MOC communities in 2026, split between Chinese-language platforms and international ones, with notes on who each one suits.

## 1. MOC Communities in China

### 1.1 The BrickPlan Community

brickplan.cn is not just an instruction-sharing site — it is an active MOC community. Here you can:

- Upload and share your own MOC builds
- Download original instructions from other builders
- Join themed building challenges
- Swap building techniques with other players

The instruction library is the core of the site, and it is fully Chinese-friendly, which makes it the easiest starting point for builders who want feedback in their own language.

### 1.2 Baidu Tieba — Brick Bar

The largest gathering place for brick fans in China, with thousands of posts going up every day. The range is wide: beginners asking which part to substitute when a piece is missing, and veteran builders posting long technical write-ups on structure and part usage. If you want volume and variety, this is where you find it.

### 1.3 Xiaohongshu (RED) Brick Scene

A visual-first social platform built around photo posts and short videos, which makes it well suited to documenting a build in progress and showing off the finished model. Written technical depth is thinner here, but as a showcase channel it works well.

## 2. International MOC Communities

### 2.1 Rebrickable

The biggest MOC instruction marketplace in the world, with a huge library of original designs and an active community around them. You can browse by part, by theme, or by the parts you already own, which makes it the standard reference point for anyone building outside a single official set line.

### 2.2 Brickset

Primarily a news and review site for LEGO, but it also runs active forum sections where builders discuss sets, parts and techniques. Useful if you want release news and community discussion in the same place.

### 2.3 Eurobricks

A long-running brick forum with a high standard of technical discussion. Threads often go deep into structural questions, scale, and part availability. It suits more advanced builders, or anyone tackling a large build that needs to actually hold together.

## 3. Choosing the Right Community

| Community | Strengths | Best for |
|------|------|----------|
| BrickPlan | Instruction-focused, Chinese-friendly | All builders |
| Baidu Tieba | Wide range of topics, high interaction | Beginners |
| Xiaohongshu | Visual showcase, short video | Displaying finished builds |
| Rebrickable | Instruction trading, professional | Advanced builders |

There is no rule that you have to pick one. Most builders end up using a Chinese-language platform for day-to-day chat and an international one for browsing designs and part data.

## 4. How to Get More Help From a Community

1. **Share first.** Post your own builds and you will get feedback and suggestions. People are far more willing to answer a question from someone who contributes.
2. **Answer others.** Replying to someone else's problem is also a way to sharpen your own understanding — explaining a technique forces you to check whether you actually understand it.
3. **Join events.** Many communities run themed building challenges. A deadline and a topic are one of the fastest ways to push your skills somewhere you would not have gone on your own.

## 5. Community Etiquette and Communication Tips

When posting in an MOC community, a few habits make a large difference:

- Include clear photos and build notes when you post
- Ask politely and describe the problem in detail — "the roof keeps sagging" is far easier to answer than "help"
- Respect other people's original designs; credit the builder if you adapt their work

Good photos matter more than most builders expect. A single sharp shot of the joint that is failing, taken from the side, will get you a useful answer faster than five paragraphs of description.
