# How to Find LEGO Instructions: Official and Third-Party Sources

![Finished LEGO model built from downloaded instructions](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/lego-plans-find-guide/lego-plans-find-guide-1.jpg)

The booklet is missing, you want to rebuild the set you had as a kid, or someone handed you a box of loose parts with no paperwork. Sooner or later every builder has to answer the question: where do I get the instructions? There are more channels than most people expect, and the official route and the third-party route each have their own tricks and their own traps. Here is the order to work through.

## Official Instruction Downloads

![LEGO instruction step-by-step building pages](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/lego-plans-find-guide/lego-plans-find-guide-2.jpg)

LEGO offers free instruction downloads for the vast majority of sets currently on sale and for a good number of retired ones. The most authoritative place to start is the building instructions lookup page on the official LEGO website. Enter the set number and the matching digital booklet comes up, usually with both an online PDF preview and a download. You can zoom right in, and the digital version is often sharper than the printed booklet in the box.

Coverage on the official site is broad, running from recent releases back to classics that have been off the shelves for many years. The digital booklets also list the parts included in the set alongside the building steps, which makes them genuinely useful as a checklist when you are sorting a pile of bricks and hunting for what is missing. The catch is that some older sets and certain special collaboration releases were never uploaded, and that is when you move on to third-party sources.

## Third-Party Instruction Sites

When the official channel comes up empty, fan-run brick instruction sites are the next stop. These are usually maintained by hobbyists and host a mix of scanned official booklets, custom designs by individual builders, and MOC tutorials. The overall coverage is wider than the official archive, and long-retired sets or early booklets often turn up here when nowhere else has them.

When using a third-party site, favour ones with a tidy interface and clear sorting by set number and theme. It saves a lot of time. There are also plenty of good brick-sharing communities in China, such as brickplan.cn, which gathers a large amount of instruction resources and building tutorials compiled by Chinese builders and is very friendly to Chinese-speaking users.

## Searching by Set Number and Set Name

Whichever channel you use, the set number is the most precise key you have. It is normally printed on the box, on the front of the instruction booklet, or on the base of the model, in a form like 10256 or 21318. Note the number first, then type it into the search box on the official site or a third-party site. That gives you the highest hit rate.

If you do not know the number, search by set name and theme instead, for example "Harry Potter Hogwarts" or "Star Wars X-Wing." English names are more commonly indexed, so remembering the English set name noticeably improves accuracy. If you genuinely cannot recall it, browse by theme, year, or approximate piece count, since many sites let you filter by theme and release date.

## Free Versus Paid Resources

![Close-up of brick parts and instruction detail](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/lego-plans-find-guide/lego-plans-find-guide-3.jpg)

Instruction resources broadly split into free and paid. Official downloads cost nothing at all. Basic scanned booklets on most third-party sites are free too, although some sites put high-quality originals or very large files behind a paid download, or require registration and points to redeem them.

The value of paid resources usually comes down to sharpness, completeness, and how quickly they are updated. Higher-priced instructions tend to have been carefully cleaned up, with crisp pages and every step present. For an average builder, the free official channel plus one or two well-regarded free third-party sites is already enough. Only when you are tackling a large MOC or need high-precision drawings is it worth paying.

## Checking Image Quality

The single biggest factor in the experience is resolution. Look at a preview before you download. Low-quality scans have blurry text and parts whose shape is hard to make out, which leads to mistakes during the build. Prioritise vector PDFs or high-resolution scans that let you zoom in and check studs one at a time.

Also watch the version of the instructions. The same set can have more than one booklet revision, with slightly different building structure between them. Before downloading, confirm that the instruction number matches your batch of the set so you do not end up with pages that do not line up with your parts. If you print a paper copy, scale it to A4 so the step images come out at the correct size.

## Use the Resources and Enjoy the Build

Instructions are only a tool. The real point is the few hours you spend building. Bookmark the two or three sites you use most and search by set number when you need them. If something is truly out of print and you cannot find it, dig through builder community posts and teardown videos, which is often faster than hunting for the booklet itself. Finding instructions is a skill that comes together after a few tries.
