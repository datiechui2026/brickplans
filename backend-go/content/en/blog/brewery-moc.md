# Brick Brewery MOC Review: Tanks, Bottling Line and Value

The short version: this brick brewery MOC packs fermentation tanks, a bottling line and a red-brick factory onto a 32 x 32 stud baseplate. It is a highly complete design with medium-high build difficulty, and it is a strong pick for builders who like industrial street scenes.

## Design and Appearance

The brewery uses a clear industrial style: red brick walls under a dark grey sloped roof, with three copper fermentation tanks across the front as the visual center. The tanks are built from curved bricks stacked layer by layer into cylinders, then finished with gold valves and pressure gauges on top. The result is quite faithful. The curved-brick cylinders are the hardest visual element to get right because every layer has to line up cleanly, and the copper finish makes any misalignment obvious. A bottling line runs along the side of the workshop, where transparent beer bottles queue along a conveyor for a lively detail. The transparent bottles contrast nicely with the brick-built conveyor and make the production line easy to read at a glance. The sign is built from printed elements, which keeps the lettering crisp without sticker alignment problems, and a warm light kit gives the model more atmosphere at night.

## Structural Stability

The main frame makes heavy use of SNOT techniques, interlocking bricks horizontally and vertically so the walls hold together as a unit. That matters on a model with a long side conveyor, where a weak joint would show up as sag. The fermentation tanks are fixed to the base with cross axles and do not wobble when the model is moved. Cross-axle mounting is a sensible choice for cylindrical tanks because it keeps them upright without relying on the surrounding walls. The bottling line conveyor is built from track links; it turns smoothly and indexes accurately. In a desk-drop test, relatively few pieces came loose, which is a good sign of structural reliability.

## Playability and Value

The bottling line can be cranked by hand, sending bottles and jars forward along the conveyor. The hand crank keeps the mechanism simple and easy to repair. The top of a fermentation tank lifts off to reveal a small cellar scene inside, so the tank is not just a static shell. The full model is about 1,850 pieces. Using the parts list from the brickplan.cn instructions, sourcing all the parts costs about CNY 650, and the build takes around 7 hours. At 1,850 pieces the part count is dense but not overwhelming. Among comparable street-scene MOCs, that puts the value in the mid-to-upper range.
