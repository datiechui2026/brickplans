# Brick Rover MOC Review: Tracks, Turntable and Sampling Arm

The short version: this rover MOC is friendly to your parts collection. It uses just over 600 pieces, about 40 percent of them Technic, and includes no rare or retired elements. The track tensioning system and the three-section sampling arm are the biggest highlights, while the build lands at medium-to-high difficulty.

Space models have long been a popular direction for brick MOCs, and this rover, based on a Mars exploration vehicle, has built a solid reputation among builders thanks to its sturdy tracked chassis and extensive sampling mechanism. This review looks at four areas: likeness, structural design, parts and difficulty.

## Likeness

The designer used the real rover's six-wheel independent suspension as a starting point but switched to a dual-track layout. The overall silhouette and proportions are handled accurately: the low body, the tall mast-style turntable and the solar panels deployed at the rear make the rover instantly recognizable. The body is rich in detail, with the dashboard, antenna and hatch latches all accounted for, and the paint-style color blocking makes the finished model look great in a display case. As an unlicensed original MOC that does not simply copy an existing plan, it captures the subject unusually well; likeness earns four and a half stars.

## Tracked Chassis

The chassis is the heart of this brick rover MOC. Rather than dropping in a ready-made track assembly, the designer used Technic pins, beams and gears to build a custom tensioning structure. Combined with small rubber tracks, it fits well while running and is not prone to throwing a track. The suspension uses rocker arms plus spring shock absorbers, so the body attitude looks natural when climbing over obstacles, and pushing the model along feels quite smooth. One thing to note: the guide wheels on either side of the tracks are set fairly close together, so high-speed pushing across a rough desktop can occasionally cause drift. Adding two side skirt plates to the lower chassis sides would reinforce it.

## Turntable and Sampling Arm

The rotating turntable on the roof uses a standard turntable part. Its rotational damping is moderate, and it can carry the mast-mounted camera steadily. The real highlight is the sampling arm at the front: a three-section folding structure whose shoulder, elbow and wrist joints are all driven by Technic gear sets. The wrist can accept either a bucket or a drill as interchangeable attachments. When deployed, the arm's reach covers much of the area in front of the body, and the motions for scooping soil and drilling feel very smooth. In its price class, its play value sits in the top tier.

## Parts List and Build Difficulty

The plan includes a complete parts list of just over 600 pieces. All are common parts, with no rare or retired elements, and Technic accounts for about 40 percent. The build steps are clearly divided, with a reasonable number of parts in each step. The instructions include multi-angle views and close-up callouts for the two trickier areas: track tensioning and gear meshing. Overall difficulty is medium-to-high. It is best suited to builders with Technic experience; newcomers should expect roughly six to eight hours to finish. The parts cost relative to the piece count is well controlled, making it good value.

## Verdict

This brick rover MOC strikes a good balance between structural originality, poseability and parts friendliness. The tracked chassis and sampling arm are especially well realized.
