# Brick MOC Aquarium Review: Is This Clear-Brick Reef Worth It?

The short answer: this brick MOC aquarium is the most accomplished use of clear parts I've seen in a scene model of this type, and the finished build is very polished. The catch is the price, which sits on the high side. It suits builders with a healthy budget who care most about how the model looks on a shelf. If you want a statement piece that doubles as a fish-tank display cabinet, it earns its place. If money is tight, wait for a sale or buy just the instructions and source the parts yourself.

## Looks and Scene Accuracy

The model is built around a tropical coral reef, and the main structure is an open fish-tank display cabinet measuring 45 cm long and 38 cm high. Curved clear parts form the front curve of the tank, working with a light blue baseplate to suggest seawater, and the effect is genuinely glassy. Inside, the scene includes coral, anemones, clownfish and an 8 cm octopus, giving the model much more depth than boxed sets at a similar price. Sitting on a bookcase, it reads as real at a glance.

## Clear Part Quality and Build Experience

The box contains 2,860 pieces, roughly 35% of them clear. That includes about 90 curved clear parts and 120 clear 1x2 tiles. Light transmission is good, but the surfaces pick up fingerprints easily, so wear gloves while building. The instructions run to 240 pages with sensible step breakdowns; my build took about 9 hours, a little longer than the 7 hours quoted officially. Credit where it's due: the joints between the tank base and the back panel are reinforced with technic parts, and the finished model shows no obvious wobble when shaken, so long-term display shouldn't bring any collapse worries.

## Display and Storage

The base has a channel for a warm-white LED strip (strip sold separately, around ¥60). Lit up, the reflections across the coral and fish are excellent, and it works well as a night light. A removable top cover keeps dust off while still letting you reach the interior scene. The downside is weight: the model comes in at 3.2 kg, so it needs a solid cabinet, and you should support the base with both hands when moving it, or the bottom plate will flex.

## Value and Buying Advice

At ¥688 official, the cost works out to roughly ¥0.24 per part, which is mid-to-upper for the hobby. Given the high proportion of clear parts and the design work involved, that's acceptable. Buy during a major e-commerce sale if you can; the lowest price on record is about ¥520. If you mainly want the experience of building with clear parts, you can buy the instructions alone for around ¥40 and source the parts yourself, bringing the cost under ¥400. That route suits builders who are comfortable with part ordering.

**Q: Is the brick aquarium MOC hard to build? Is it suitable for beginners?**
A: Difficulty is mid-to-upper: 240 pages of instructions and about 9 hours of building. Beginners should start with the base structure and open bags in stages as the steps require. Clear parts are easy to mix up, so sort them by shape before you start, which noticeably cuts down on errors.

**Q: Do clear parts yellow over time, and how do you maintain them?**
A: Clear parts will yellow slightly under long-term strong light, so keep the model out of direct sunlight. Dust with a soft brush or air blower and never wipe with alcohol. For fingerprints, use a slightly damp cotton cloth, then dry the surface immediately with a dry cloth.

**Q: Can it be converted into a real fish tank?**
A: No. The baseplate and back panel of this MOC have no waterproof sealing, and water will seep through the seams, so it is display-only. If you want live fish, use the brick tank as an outer shell and place a small acrylic tank inside before adding water.

**Q: How is it different from official boxed fish tank sets?**
A: Official boxed sets usually have only about 10% clear parts and far fewer scene details. This MOC has around 90 curved clear parts and an LED strip channel, so display impact is much stronger. The trade-offs are a higher price and the fact that you build it yourself, which suits builders chasing the best possible look.
