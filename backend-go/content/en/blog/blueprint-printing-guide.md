# How to Print MOC Instructions at 100% Scale on A4

Short version first: three rules cover almost every printing problem — choose PDF over images, output at 100% actual size, and print single-sided at high quality.

You download a MOC you love, then spend the evening squinting at a phone screen. Printing the instructions should fix that, but the pages come out blurry, or the scale is wrong and nothing lines up. This guide walks through the fix in four steps: pick the right file, set the right size, tune the print parameters, then bind the pages so they survive a long build.

## 1. Check the File Format First: PDF Beats Images

The first step is confirming what kind of file you actually have. **PDF instructions** are the better option: vector content or high-resolution embedded images scale without distortion, the print parameters stay under your control, and most of the instructions on BrickPlan are offered as PDFs you can send straight to the printer. **Image instructions** (PNG, JPG) depend entirely on resolution — aim for at least 2000×2800 pixels, which is what A4 needs to look crisp. A low-resolution file will go soft the moment it is enlarged, and no printer can rescue it. The test is simple: open the image on your computer at 100% zoom. If the edges of the parts already show pixel blocks, the printed page will look the same or worse. Also check whether the file states a scale — if it does, print at that original size and do not rescale it on a whim.

## 2. A4 Page Setup: Scaling Is Where It Goes Wrong

The parameter that causes the most trouble is scaling. By default, printers often apply a "fit to page" setting that quietly resizes everything, so parts no longer match the real bricks — and you only find out halfway through the build. Set the print dialog to **Actual size** or **100% scale** and switch "fit to page" off. If the file includes its own margins and crop marks, print a single test page at original size first and check it against a standard brick: if the brick sits snugly on the printed outline, the scale is correct and you can print the rest in one go. Plain 80 gsm A4 paper is all you need. For long documents, recycled paper keeps the cost down; photo paper is unnecessary.

## 3. Print Quality Settings That Keep Lines Sharp

Sharpness is not only about the source file — the printer settings matter just as much.

- **Color mode:** use Color or Photo for full-color instructions, and Grayscale for black-and-white line drawings. Line-only pages often come out crisper in Draft mode.
- **Print quality:** choose Standard or High. Draft fades the lines, while Photo is slow and burns through ink.
- **Duplex:** print instructions single-sided. Laying pages side by side is easier to follow, and double-sided printing lets the reverse side bleed through and confuse your reading.
- **Color calibration:** if the printed colors are noticeably off, check the printer's color profile or output directly using the file's original colors.

When you are building, what matters is the lines and the part numbers. If the digits and outlines are clear, a slight color shift does not affect the build.

## 4. Binding and Everyday Use

Printing is only the start — the pages have to work well in your hands. **Binding:** for a few pages, clip them to a piece of stiff card with a binder clip; for a long document, punch holes and put it in a folder, or bind it in stages by build phase so you only carry the section you are working on. **Protection:** bricks and fingers get dusty, so slide the pages into clear plastic sleeves — they stay clean and can be reused. **Reading habits:** stand the current step upright on a stand rather than laying it flat; your neck will thank you. Pair that with sorted parts trays and your throughput roughly doubles.

## Final Thoughts

The whole method comes down to three lines: PDF first, 100% scale, single-sided high-quality printing. Follow that sequence and even a very complex instruction set will land on paper clearly and accurately. If you have not settled on a model yet, browse the [BrickPlan blog](https://brickplan.cn/en/blog) — there is a large library of instructions you can download and print directly. Pick one, apply the settings above, and you can start building.

## FAQ

**Q: The printed instructions are blurry and the part edges look soft. What should I do?**
A: Check the source resolution first — image files should be at least 2000×2800 pixels. Then set print quality to Standard or High; Draft fades the lines, and Photo mode is slow and ink-hungry.

**Q: Which prints better, PDF instructions or image instructions?**
A: PDF wins: no distortion when scaling and full control over parameters. Image files need to be 2000×2800 pixels or more, and should show no pixel blocks when zoomed to 100% on screen before you print.

**Q: The scale is wrong and the parts do not fit. How do I fix it?**
A: In the print dialog, choose Actual size or 100% scale and turn off automatic fit-to-page. Print one page first, compare it against a standard brick, and only print the rest once the fit is exact.

**Q: For a long document, which paper is cheapest and most durable?**
A: Plain 80 gsm A4 paper is fine. For many pages, recycled paper saves money, and photo paper is not needed. Print single-sided so pages are easy to compare, then put them in clear plastic sleeves to keep them clean and reusable.
