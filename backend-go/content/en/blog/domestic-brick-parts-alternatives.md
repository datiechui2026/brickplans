# Domestic Brick Parts Alternatives: Compatible and Cheaper

Anyone who builds MOCs knows the moment: you pull the parts list out of the instructions, price it out with original-brand parts, and close the tab. Domestic brick parts have stopped being a compromise, though. Brands like Gobricks, Double Eagle and SEMBO now run injection moulding at a genuinely high standard, and in plenty of builds a domestic part sitting next to an original one is impossible to spot. This guide is about doing the swap properly, so you save money without collecting a box of parts you regret.

## Why Choose Domestic Parts Alternatives

- **The price gap is real.** A given part typically costs a fraction of the original-brand version. The savings scale fast when you are buying hundreds of pieces.
- **Buying is easy.** Shops on Taobao and Pinduoduo sell individual pieces by the part number, so filling a handful of gaps is simple and flexible.
- **Quality control has improved a lot.** The tooling and moulding from the leading domestic factories has come a long way in recent years and comfortably handles most static MOC work.

One thing to be clear about up front: this is not a blanket replacement strategy. Load-bearing structure and precision drivetrain parts still deserve caution, which is covered below.

## How the Main Domestic Brands Compare

| Brand | Strengths | Best for |

## Compatibility: Can You Actually Mix Them?

### 1. Dimensions and Tolerances

Standard studded bricks follow one shared base unit, so in theory domestic parts and LEGO parts can be mixed. The catch is that tolerance control varies between factories:

- Parts that clutch too loosely will start to feel wobbly after a few layers of stacking.
- Parts that clutch too tightly wear down after a few rounds of assembly and disassembly.

Use one manufacturer's parts within a single build where you can, and if you plan to mix, test a small sample before committing to a bulk order.

### 2. Precision Differences

Basic bricks, plates and beams from the top brands are now very close to original tolerances. **Specialty parts** are where the gap still shows: ball joints, hinges, gears and long thin axles. Under load or in rotating assemblies, small differences in feel get amplified quickly. If your budget allows, go with the brand that has the better reputation for that specific part.

### 3. Colour Differences

This is the trap people miss most often. Two parts both called "red" or "sand" will not share a colour code or the same level of gloss across manufacturers, and mixing them in one MOC produces a mismatch that reads instantly. Two rules:

- Keep large colour-blocked areas to a single manufacturer and a single batch.
- When exact colour matching matters, buy a test piece first and confirm before placing a bulk order.

## How to Find Alternatives by Part Number

- **Pin down the part number.** Start from the parts list in your instructions. Every part has a standard number, for example a 2x4 brick is usually 3001.
- **Search the number in reverse.** In the instructions and parts discussion areas on [brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn), searching a number surfaces substitution notes other builders have shared.
- **Search "number + brand".** On marketplace platforms, try "part number Gobricks" or "part number domestic bricks". Most shops will sell by number.
- **Look for equivalent substitutions.** Different shapes can serve the same function, for example two 1x2 plates in place of one 2x2 to close up a structure. It saves money and sharpens your building technique at the same time.

## Buying Channels and What to Watch For

- **By-the-piece shops.** Best for filling gaps and missing parts. Read buyer feedback specifically for comments on clutch and feel.
- **Full boxes or bulk bags of loose parts.** Better value at volume and a good way for a beginner to build up a basic parts library.
- **Watch batch and colour code.** The same part number from different production batches can vary slightly in colour, so confirm with the seller before ordering.

One last note: don't take everything you save on domestic parts and spend it on more stock. Hold part of the budget back for the key parts that genuinely affect feel and structure.
