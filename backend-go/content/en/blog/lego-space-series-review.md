# LEGO Space Series Review: Classic to Modern Space Sets

The short version: space is a theme that works for every age group. New builders should start with a rover or a satellite, advanced builders can take on a station or a large rocket, and nostalgic collectors should look for reissues of the classic designs.

From the first brick-built "spaceship" of decades ago to today's space stations and Mars rovers, space has always been one of the most romantic themes in the brick world. This review is about that decades-long flight path: the sentiment of the classic sets, the engineering feel of the modern ones, and who each of them actually suits. Whether you are a long-time collector or someone who has just discovered an interest in the cosmos, there should be something here for you.

## 1. The Classic Space Era: Where the Nostalgia and the Design Language Started

Start with the classics. Early space sets were built mainly in blue, white and gray, with clean, simple ship shapes. Transparent canopies, hatches that open and close, and printed astronaut minifigures made up the distinctive "space aesthetic" of that era. The structures look simple today, but their real importance is that they established the basic vocabulary of the theme: a symmetrical hull, bold color blocking, a detachable cockpit. Those classic elements still influence modern sets.

For older collectors, classic space is pure childhood memory. For newcomers, classic-style builds are easy to assemble and use very common parts, which makes them a good warm-up project and a cheap way to collect minifigures. They also look good on a shelf as a bit of nostalgia.

## 2. Modern Space Sets: More Tech, More Detail

Modern space design has clearly moved up a level. Three changes stand out. First, **the structures are more precise** — modular station docking, robotic arm joints and solar panels that unfold all show off contemporary building techniques. Second, **the color palettes are bolder** — dark blue, orange-red and glow-in-the-dark elements give ships more sci-fi tension. Third, **the scenes are more complete** — rocket launch pads, Mars bases and orbiting satellites turn "space" from a single vehicle into a full story setting.

In terms of the build itself, modern sets use more steps and more varied techniques, with slope work and sideways (SNOT) connections appearing frequently. That makes them especially appealing to builders who want to push their skills.

## 3. The Classic Subjects: Rockets, Stations and Rovers

Three subject types are worth the most attention. **Rockets** emphasize a vertical silhouette and a launch pad setting, often with a transport vehicle and gantry tower. They are essential for spaceflight fans, and a finished one looks commanding on a desk. **Space stations** are all about modular design: each module is built separately and then docked, so assembling and disassembling teaches you the logic of how a real station is put together — the educational value is high. **Rovers and satellites** are mid-sized with fine detail, and their wheels, antennas and solar panels are instantly recognizable, which makes them a great first space set.

The three types complement each other well. Collect them all and you can build a complete "space launch center" diorama.

## 4. Buying Advice: Which Set Suits Which Builder

**Beginners** should pick a smaller or mid-sized set such as a rover or satellite. Build time is short and the sense of achievement comes quickly. **Advanced builders** can take on a space station or a large rocket, where the modular structure and more complex techniques give them more to chew on. **Nostalgic collectors** should watch for reissues of the classic designs to fill in the gaps in their childhood memories. **Parents building with kids** are best served by a launch pad scene with an educational angle, where you can explain spaceflight while you build.

Whichever you choose, space parts have a very high reuse rate. Transparent canopies, dish antennas and nozzle pieces are all common components in MOC spaceships, so taking a set apart never feels like a waste.

## Final Thoughts

Space is one of the few themes that genuinely works for all ages, with the nostalgia of the classics sitting alongside the technical evolution of the modern sets. ## FAQ

**Q: I'm new to space sets. Which one should I buy first?**
A: Go for a small or mid-sized set such as a rover or a satellite. Build time is short and the payoff is quick. Parts like transparent canopies and dish antennas are highly reusable, so you can break the set down later and use them in a spaceship MOC.

**Q: What is the difference between classic space and modern space sets?**
A: Classic sets use blue, white and gray with simple hulls and are easy to build. Modern sets are more precisely engineered, with modular docking, robotic arms and solar panels, bolder colors and more complete scenes.

**Q: How do I choose between a rocket, a space station and a rover?**
A: Spaceflight fans should go for a rocket, which comes with a transport vehicle and gantry tower for real presence. If you like the educational side, choose a modular station where the modules dock and separate. For a first set, a rover or satellite is best — mid-sized and finely detailed.

**Q: Are space set parts worth taking apart for MOC building?**
A: Yes. Space parts have a very high reuse rate. Transparent canopies, dish antennas and nozzle pieces are all standard components for building MOC spaceships, so there is no hesitation about dismantling a set — and classic parts bring back childhood memories too.
