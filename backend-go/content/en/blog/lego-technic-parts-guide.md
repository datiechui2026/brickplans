# LEGO Technic Parts for Beginners: Gears, Axles and Pins

Most builders start out with bricks and plates. Then comes the moment you want to build something that actually moves — a car, a robotic arm, a lifting platform — and you notice something is missing. What you're missing is the LEGO Technic system. These parts are what make a static model come alive, and they're essential learning for anyone moving past the basics.

## Core Part Families: Gears, Axles, Pins and Cross Axles

Technic is a whole family of parts built around gears, axles, pins and cross axles. Most of them carry round holes or cross-shaped holes so they can connect to one another.

- **Gears**: everything from the 8-tooth gear up to the 24-tooth and 40-tooth gears, plus bevel gears, crown gears and worm gears. They change rotational speed and direction.
- **Axles and cross axles**: axles carry drive from one point to another. Common lengths run from 2 studs to 12 studs, and they work together with bushes and axle connectors.
- **Pins and friction pins**: pins lock two parts together, while friction pins add resistance so a joint doesn't slip — critical for steering and suspension.

Get comfortable with these four families and you've taken your first real step into Technic.

## Gear Meshing and Gear Ratios

The heart of any gear train is the gear ratio. Put simply, when two gears mesh, the ratio of their tooth counts determines how speed and torque are traded against each other. An 8-tooth gear driving a 24-tooth gear drops the output speed to one third of the input while tripling torque — exactly the principle behind most reduction gearboxes.

For a larger reduction, chain several gear stages together or pair a worm gear with a regular gear. A worm drive delivers a big reduction and is self-locking, which is why it shows up in cranes and winches that need to hold their position. Run the logic backwards: if a small gear driving a large one slows things down, a large gear driving a small one speeds them up — useful for flywheels and fast-spinning turntables.

## Fitting Axles into Beams

The most common structural part in the system is the Technic beam, drilled with rows of round holes and cross holes. An axle pushed through a cross hole transmits torque to the beam; a round hole lets the axle spin freely.

Two things matter when you pick an axle. First, leave enough length to fit bushes or gears so nothing works loose. Second, decide whether the joint should be fixed or free — use a half bush to lock the axle against a hole when it needs to stay put, or run it through a round hole with a bush to reduce friction when it needs to rotate. After a few builds, the question of which hole to use and how to secure it stops being guesswork and starts following a clear pattern.

## Classic Mechanisms: Gearboxes and Steering

Once the basics are in place you can build the classic mechanisms. A **gearbox** uses sliding gears and a shift fork to switch between different gear combinations, giving several ratios from a single input. A **steering mechanism** typically pairs a rack with a gear to convert rotary motion into linear motion, and adding Ackermann geometry helps a model corner smoothly.

If you're new to this, build an official Technic set following the instructions first, then take it apart and study how the gears are laid out, and finally modify it with your own parts. Stripping and rebuilding teaches transmission principles faster than any diagram.

## Mixing Technic with Bricks, and What to Buy

Technic doesn't exist in isolation. The round holes in a beam accept standard pins, which lets you lock ordinary bricks and plates onto Technic parts. Plenty of large-scale car models use Technic for the skeleton and bricks for the bodywork, getting strength and shape in one build. Remember one rule: **looks go to bricks, loads go to Technic.** Build the chassis and suspension from beams, then skin it with regular bricks — it stays rigid and is easy to modify. You can upload everything you come up with to brickplan.cn and swap ideas with the community.

When buying Technic parts, collect them in two separate categories: drivetrain and structure. For drivetrain, keep a range of gear tooth counts on hand along with worm gears, universal joints and differentials. For structure, stock beams from 3 studs to 15 studs plus assorted pins, bushes and connectors. Start with one Technic set or a parts pack, then fill the gaps as your builds demand.

For storage, sort by part number or hole specification into compartment boxes, and look up the parts lists and instructions you need on brickplan.cn — buy to the plan rather than stockpiling parts you'll never use. Your budget goes furthest when it's spent on core drivetrain and structural components. Here's to a next MOC that runs, rotates and steers.
