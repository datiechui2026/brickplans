# Brick Motorboat MOC Blueprint Review: Sleek Hull, Water Stance

Here is the short version: this motorboat blueprint lives or dies on its streamlined hull and its water-skimming stance. Curved parts and SNOT work pull the bow curve tight, then a waterline treatment makes the finished model read as though it is riding the surface of the water.

Watercraft have always been one of the tougher MOC categories, because the hull has to suggest speed without giving up structural rigidity. I recently came across this brick motorboat MOC blueprint in the BrickPlan blueprint-sharing community, where the author focused squarely on a streamlined hull and a planing attitude, and the visual payoff is genuinely eye-catching. This review walks through how it actually performs.

## Blueprint Quality and Building Instructions

The steps in this brick motorboat MOC blueprint are broken down in fine detail, split across four phases: hull, engine compartment, deck and outboard motor, with each phase held to roughly forty steps. The instructions use color renders with highlight callouts, and newly added parts are marked with a dark outline, so experienced builders can skip ahead quickly while newcomers will not get lost at the complicated bow. The one small letdown is that the blueprint offers no sub-bag packing suggestions, so builders working from loose parts will have to sort by color themselves. Fortunately the total part count is moderate, so the sorting cost stays low.

## How the Streamlined Hull Is Built

The hull is the standout section of the whole design. The author uses 4x6 curved plates for the bow transition, stacks two layers of 1x4 slopes on each side to pull in the chined sheer line, and combines them with a 6x12 hull plate to form a continuous curved surface. To keep the bow from sagging, a keel made from a 1x15 technic beam and a cross axle is buried inside in advance; the axle passes through the holes in the hull plate and locks into the wedge piece at the bow, while 2x4 bricks serve as frames for lateral support. This "technic skeleton plus skinned exterior" approach gives the whole motorboat a solid feel in the hand when you pick it up to play, with none of that loose, wobbly give.

## Part List and Accuracy

The most recognizable elements in the part list are the pair of dark blue curved cockpit canopy pieces and the two white deflector plates at the stern. Those two categories of odd-shaped parts are what ultimately decide whether the finished model reads as a real motorboat. Everything else is mostly basic bricks and slopes, with a small number of transparent parts used for the dashboard and windscreen. By rough estimate the hull runs about thirty studs long and about nine studs wide, which at a scale of roughly 1:35 pairs nicely with standard minifigures. Compared with official sets, this brick motorboat MOC blueprint sits closer to a real racing boat's planing attitude in the upward angle of the bow, and the accuracy deserves credit.

## Build Difficulty and Who It Suits

Overall the difficulty sits at medium-to-high, with the challenges concentrated in two places. The first is the offset meshing of the curved parts at the bow, which has to be worked through layer by layer in blueprint order, since skipping steps easily means rework. The second is the outboard motor, which uses small hinge pieces and turbine blades, where the parts are tiny and it is easy to misjudge orientation. Builders with ten or more sets of experience who are comfortable with SNOT side-building are the right audience. Once it is finished, park it on a blue baseplate as a water scene and the photo setup instantly looks its best.

## Verdict

This brick motorboat MOC blueprint delivers a solid result across all three dimensions: streamlined hull, on-water stance and structural strength. The parts are common enough to be reusable and there is plenty of room for modification, which is exactly what community members have been doing in the BrickPlan comments, sharing ideas for recoloring the deflector plates and adding propeller drive. If you want to add a sense of speed to your watercraft collection, this blueprint belongs on your list.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this brick motorboat MOC blueprint worth picking up?**
A: Yes. Watercraft are demanding to design well, and this blueprint puts its emphasis on the streamlined hull and the planing stance, balancing the bow curve against structural stability. It suits builders who like water-themed subjects.

**Q: How do you build a streamlined hull?**
A: Combine curved parts with SNOT. Widen the bow outward layer by layer with curved bricks to pull out the curve, use SNOT on the sides to face the smooth surfaces outward for flat side panels, then step back inward layer by layer to create the waist, and the lines flow.

**Q: How is the water-skimming stance achieved?**
A: The key is the waterline treatment. Make the lower edge of the hull a straight horizontal line, use dark parts to suggest the waterline, then add transparent light blue parts for spray. Lift the bow slightly and let the stern sit lower, and the pose reads as speed.

**Q: How hard is the blueprint, and who is it for?**
A: Medium difficulty. Aligning the hull curves with the side panels is the main challenge, and the parts are mostly curved pieces and basic elements. It suits builders with basic experience who want to try a watercraft.
