# How to Build a LEGO Stage MOC: Lights, Backdrop, Working Curtain

Short version first: about 70% of a stage MOC's atmosphere comes from the lighting and the curtain, and only 30% from the backdrop. Roughly 700 pieces and two hours get you a complete stage with a swappable curtain and working lights, for around ¥180. To get it right the first time, grab the instructions from brickplan.cn and then work through the four steps below.

**Step 1: Build the stage base.** Use a 48x32 baseplate as the main stage floor. Along the front edge, add two layers of 1x4 dark red bricks to form the stage apron. The playing surface should sit 4 bricks above ground level — that height difference is what gives the audience viewpoint a sense of depth instead of a flat plate. Leave 4 studs free on each side; those are where the curtain track will attach later.

**Step 2: Add the backdrop and curtain.** The backdrop is built from three 8x16 plates pushed together and stands 16 bricks tall. Behind it, two 16L technic axles run diagonally as braces so the panel does not flex when you handle the model. For the curtain, hang three 1x6 red curved pieces on the track. Pulling the knob on the side opens and closes them, which mimics a real scene change mid-performance.

**Step 3: Install the lighting rig.** Mount 6 lamps on the beam across the top of the stage. Each lamp is a trans-clear 1x1 round plate combined with a 1x1 tile. The two centre lamps use yellow pieces as the key light; the four outer lamps use white pieces as side light. Then place two 2x2 trans pieces on the stage floor as footlights. Once the lights are switched on, the layering appears immediately.

**Step 4: Add minifigures and props.** Put 3 minifigures on stage in performing poses. In front, add a row of 1x2 trans pieces as the audience barrier, and place speakers and flower baskets on both sides. When it is finished, pull the curtain knob 3 times to test how smoothly it runs. If it catches, add a 1x1 smooth round plate as a pulley.

## FAQ

**Q: Can the lights on a brick stage MOC actually light up?**

A: Yes. Brick lights or an LED light set combined with trans pieces will genuinely glow, and 6 lamps cost around ¥30. If the model is for static display only, trans-yellow pieces are enough to fake the look — no extra wiring, and much easier for beginners.

**Q: What do I do if the curtain will not pull?**

A: First check whether the curved pieces on the track are catching on the edge of the backdrop. Then add one 1x1 smooth round plate at each end of the track to act as a pulley. Finally, confirm the axle that drives the knob is not bent. Working through those three points clears up 95% of sticking problems.

**Q: How is a stage MOC different from an ordinary street scene MOC?**

A: A stage MOC leans on vertical space and on light and shadow. You need the trio of backdrop, overhead lamps and curtain. A street scene MOC is instead about horizontal layout along a road. The stage has more moving parts, so it is slightly harder to assemble — but the display effect and the interactivity are clearly stronger.

**Q: Is a brick stage MOC worth building?**

A: Very much so. 700 pieces cost roughly ¥180. Once it is done you can display it as a model, use it with minifigures for stop-motion animation, or break it down and reuse the parts. Retail stage sets often start at ¥500 and up, so building your own works out around 60% better value.
