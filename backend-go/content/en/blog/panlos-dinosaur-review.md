# Panlos Dinosaur Series Review: T-Rex to Triceratops

## Before We Start

Dinosaurs are an eternal theme in the brick world. From Jurassic Park to natural history museums, these prehistoric giants keep firing up builders' imaginations. Among Chinese brick brands, the **Panlos dinosaur series** has quickly become a focal point for dinosaur fans thanks to its wide species coverage and aggressively competitive pricing.

This review works through the build experience of the main sets — T-Rex, Triceratops, Stegosaurus and Pterosaur — to give you a full picture of the line before you buy.

> If you are hunting for more dinosaur-themed MOC instructions or modding ideas, drop by the [BrickPlans community](/en/blog) to browse builds shared by other fans.

## The T-Rex Set: The Flagship of the Line

The T-Rex is the **most closely watched** set in the Panlos dinosaur range. The finished model stands about 25 cm tall and measures over 40 cm long, and at 1500+ pieces it delivers serious visual impact.

**Build experience**: The T-Rex uses a two-layer structure — an internal skeleton plus an outer shell. You build the spine and leg joints first, then cover them with muscle-textured shell parts. This approach keeps the model rigid and means it will not fall apart when you handle it. Panlos uses dedicated ball joints at the articulation points: the upper and lower jaws open and close, the forelimbs move, and the tail swings. For the price, the posability is impressive.

**Accuracy**: Panlos put real effort into the head. The teeth are individual printed pieces, and their number and arrangement reference actual fossil material. Sunken eye sockets and raised brow ridges give the face a menacing expression. The scale texture comes from ordinary stacked bricks rather than a dedicated mold, but at display distance it reads very well.

---

## Triceratops and Stegosaurus: Details Make the Difference

The Triceratops is one of the **highest part-count** sets in the line at roughly 1800 pieces. The highlight is the frill and the horns: the three horns use a soft rubber material, which is safer for kids while still capturing the feel of real horn, and curved bricks build the fan-shaped silhouette of the frill. The dark brown and off-white color scheme holds together nicely.

The Stegosaurus focuses on the back plates and tail spikes. Panlos combines wedge bricks and slope bricks to give the plates a three-dimensional look, and each plate can be posed independently. The tail spikes use special conical parts that feel rounded rather than sharp — good for children to handle.

**Build tip**: the leg joints on the Triceratops and Stegosaurus carry a lot of weight. Press every joint piece fully home during the build so the finished model does not sag or deform after sitting on display.

---

## Pterosaur and Brachiosaurus: Air and Ground

The Pterosaur set is built around a spread-wing pose, with a wingspan of up to 45 cm. Panlos uses plenty of plates and hinge pieces in the wings, so they flap up and down and can be locked in either an extended or folded position. The downside: the wing connectors may work slightly loose after repeated handling, so this one is best treated as a display piece.

The Brachiosaurus (or Diplodocus) set follows a "big is beautiful" approach. The extremely long neck is built from multiple ball joints and bends freely into any angle. Four thick legs keep the model stable — even with the neck bent down as far as it will go, it will not tip forward.

## Part Quality and Compatibility

Overall, part quality in the Panlos dinosaur series sits at the **upper-middle tier of Chinese bricks**:

- **Clutch**: on the tight side. There is a little resistance while building, but the finished model is solid rather than loose. The joint pieces get special treatment and have a clear, crisp detent.
- **Color and sprue marks**: the brown family (dark brown, sand, dark green) is evenly colored with no obvious variance. Sprue marks are average and mostly hidden inside seams.
- **Printed parts**: the printing on dinosaur eyes and teeth is high precision — sharp patterns, no burrs at the edges. Some sets' stickers (skin-texture decals, for example) could be cut more precisely.

On compatibility, the Panlos dinosaur series is fully compatible with the LEGO system. That means you can mix the dinosaurs freely into LEGO scenes, or use [dinosaur MOC instructions on BrickPlans](/en/blog) to rebuild a set into something new.

## Value for Money

| Comparison | Panlos dinosaur series | LEGO Jurassic World series |
|---------|---------------|---------------|
| Price (1500+ pieces) | 150–250 CNY | 800–1500 CNY |
| Part precision | Good | Excellent |
| Dinosaur species coverage | T-Rex, Triceratops, Stegosaurus, Pterosaur, Brachiosaurus and more | Mostly film IP, limited species |
| Articulation | Poseable joints, opening jaws | Some sets include mechanical linkages |
| Compatibility | Fully LEGO-compatible | Standard reference |

The table makes the picture clear: Panlos wins on **species coverage** and **value for money**. If what you want is display appeal and a large collection of dinosaur models rather than a specific film license, Panlos is a very attractive option.

---

## Shortcomings

1. **Instruction details**: in some steps the part callouts are on the small side and colors are not clearly separated — dark brown and black parts are easy to confuse on the page. Check against the parts list as you go.
2. **Minifigures**: the dinosaur sets usually ship without minifigures, so they offer less scene-building potential than LEGO Jurassic World. If you like dinosaur-plus-diorama builds, you may need to buy minifigures separately or fill the gap with MOC instructions.
3. **Bag subdivision**: in some sets the large bags are not split by build stage, so hunting for parts takes extra time. Sort into storage boxes by color after unboxing before you start.

---

## Verdict

The Panlos dinosaur series is **the benchmark dinosaur line among Chinese bricks**. On the main sets — T-Rex, Triceratops, Stegosaurus — both the build experience and the accuracy of the finished models are satisfying, and the T-Rex in particular is a must-buy for the line. Minifigure selection and instruction clarity still have room to improve, but given the competitive pricing and the breadth of species on offer, this is a strong choice for any dinosaur brick fan.
