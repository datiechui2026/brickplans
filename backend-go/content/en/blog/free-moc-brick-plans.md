## Build Difficulty

Free MOC instructions generally cover every difficulty tier from beginner to advanced:

- **Easy entry (★★☆☆☆)**: micro-buildings, small animals, simple vehicles — 50-150 parts, finished in 1-2 hours
- **Casual step up (★★★☆☆)**: medium buildings or vehicles — 150-400 parts, a good weekend project
- **Advanced challenge (★★★★☆)**: complex scenes or large machinery — 400-800 parts, the best of what free instructions offer
- **Master level (★★★★★)**: a small number of free plans also deliver full-size large MOCs — 800-1500 parts, genuinely collectible

Difficulty is usually clearly labelled on free plans, which matters more than it sounds. A builder with an hour to spare can pick a micro-build and finish it in one sitting, while someone with a free Sunday can go straight for a 600-part scene without guessing whether it is within reach.

## Parts Review

The parts demands of free brick MOC instructions have their own character:

- **Common parts first**: free brick MOC instructions tend to favour widely available parts, which makes sourcing much easier. Most of what a plan calls for can be pulled from the brick stock you already have at home — standard 2x4 bricks, plates, tiles, slopes and technic pins — with no extra purchase needed.
- **Recommended brands**: Gobricks and LEGO are the first choices for building free plans. Gobricks parts offer high precision, good compatibility and moderate pricing; LEGO parts cost more, but give the best colour consistency and the best feel in hand. Mixing the two is common practice and works fine for these builds.
- **Part count range**: free plans typically run from 50 to 800 parts. A small number of standout plans reach 1000+ parts, but they are not common.

Sorting the parts list against what you already own before you start is worth the ten minutes it takes — it is far less frustrating than discovering a missing plate at step 40.
