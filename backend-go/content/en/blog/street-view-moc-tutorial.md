# Street View MOC Tutorial: From Baseplate to Lighting

Street View is one of the most popular themes in brick MOC building. A corner building with a café, a tree-lined alley, or a vintage shop glowing with warm yellow light—bringing the street corner you imagine to life, brick by brick, is the real joy of MOC (My Own Creation). This street-view MOC tutorial lays out a complete, practical process from the baseplate to the lighting, so you can move from a first sketch to a finished scene with a clear plan.

## Why Street View Is a Good Entry Point for MOC Building

Street-view MOCs are **very forgiving**. Even when the scale or structure is slightly off, the overall look can still work, which makes the theme ideal for beginners who want to practice:

- **Familiar subject matter**: Street corners, shops, and homes are all around us every day, and reference material is easy to find.
- **Modular and easy to revise**: A building can be split into floors and rooms, so if something goes wrong you only need to rework a small section rather than the whole model.
- **Strong display value**: A single finished building can go straight onto a shelf, and when several are connected into a street scene, the effect becomes even stronger.

## Before You Build: Planning and Color

### Set the Scale and Baseplate Size

Most street-view builds use **1:48 or 1:64 scale**, and they start from a **32x32** baseplate. Before picking up bricks, use a drawing to define the **footprint and floor heights**. This planning step gives you a clear target for the walls, windows, and roof, and it helps you avoid rebuilding later because the proportions drifted.

### Choose the Color Scheme First

- Use **light and dark shades from the same color family** for exterior walls and interiors; this keeps the model from looking too busy.
- Add **one or two accent colors**—for example, a red brick wall paired with white window frames—to create a memorable focal point.
- Keep the roof, signage, doors, and windows in a consistent style. That consistency is what makes the whole street view feel coordinated rather than like separate buildings placed side by side.

## Core Building Steps

### Step 1: Baseplate and Walls

- Lay the **baseplate** first, leave openings for doors and windows, and then build the exterior walls with standard bricks.
- Use **corner bricks** or columns at the corners to strengthen the structure and keep the walls from leaning.
- Add **plates as floor slabs** between levels so the upper floors have proper support.

### Step 2: Interiors and Partitions

- Put a **shop or living room** on the first floor and a **bedroom or study** on the second floor. This functional separation makes the building feel more believable.
- Use **SNOT (Studs Not On Top)** techniques to build sideways and create details such as window sills and counters. SNOT is especially useful when you want smooth surfaces or details that face outward rather than upward.

### Step 3: Details That Bring the Street View to Life

- Small items such as shop signs, awnings, plants, streetlights, and mailboxes are the **finishing touches** that give a building personality.
- Add **minifigures and street-level interaction scenes**, such as pedestrians chatting by a window or a shopkeeper setting out goods at the door.
- Use **small parts to suggest clutter**. The more granular the details, the more realistic the scene feels.

### Step 4: Lighting and the Overall Scene

- Place **warm-colored light bricks** behind shop windows; the nighttime effect appears immediately.
- When joining several buildings into a street corner, keep the **style consistent while varying the heights**. A mix of taller and shorter structures keeps the scene from looking visually monotonous.

## Common Problems and How to Avoid Them

- **Leaning walls**: This usually means the base bricks are not aligned. Remove the affected section, re-lay it carefully, and reinforce the corner columns.
- **Bad proportions**: Build according to the scale marked on your drawing instead of enlarging or shrinking by eye.
- **Not enough parts**: Use common, generic parts first and add special parts later when you can.
