# Eiffel Tower MOC Review: 6,826 Pieces, 108 cm Tall

The short version first: this Eiffel Tower MOC uses 6,826 pieces, stands about 108 cm tall on a 46×46 cm square base, and recreates the Paris landmark at 1:300 scale. Expect roughly 36 hours of building, and the instructions cost 899 yuan. Overall it is a very complete design — appearance scores around 9.2 out of 10 — and the one real weak point is a fragile top section. If you like big landmark display models and the budget is there, it is worth picking up.

## Parts and Dimensions

The headline here is sheer part count: 6,826 pieces in total, of which standard 2x4 bricks make up about 38%. The rest is wedges, slopes and curved pieces. The tower is split into three stacked body sections plus the base, narrowing level by level, with a ring-shaped platform marking each transition. At 108 cm tall it takes up roughly the depth of a desk, so it reads well in a living room or study.

Colour-wise it is mostly medium grey with a small amount of dark grey used for outline detailing. The whole model stays within seven official colour codes, which makes bulk buying from the parts list on brickplan.cn straightforward. At current market rates, loose parts run about 1,600–2,200 yuan — roughly 300 yuan cheaper than buying a pre-packed kit — but you will have to sort everything yourself.

The three body sections are where most of the repetition lives, but they are also where the model gets its solidity.

## Build Experience and Structure

The build flows well overall. The instructions are divided into five major stages — base, first tower section, second tower section, third tower section and spire — across 42 large steps. The first 10 steps are the base, which uses a lot of reinforcement pieces and takes about 4 hours. Each of the three body sections then runs about 8–9 hours; repetitive, but structurally sound.

On stability: four central columns run through all three sections, backed up by diagonal bracing at each of the four corners of every level, and the result is genuinely rigid. The spire is the exception — it is supported by a single column and pops off easily if you handle the model, so plan on a fixed display spot. Even so, the structural design here is clearly better than the cut-down versions commonly sold.

## Price and Instruction Service

The instructions are available on the brickplan.cn platform for 899 yuan, and include a 3D interactive manual with step-by-step animation, plus a parts list you can check at any time. Compare that with the official Eiffel Tower set — about 10,001 pieces, 149 cm tall, and consistently above 2,800 yuan — and this MOC takes up less floor space with a finer level of detail, which suits builders with limited room.

Overall scores: appearance 9.2, structure 8.8, build experience 8.5, value for money 7.8. Altogether it is a highly finished landmark MOC, best suited to builders with some experience who want a precise display piece.

Building the base first is not optional — it sets the geometry for everything above it.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I buy the parts for this Eiffel Tower MOC — can I get a complete kit?**

A: The instructions do not include parts. You source them yourself from the list provided on brickplan.cn, or buy a pre-sorted package from a seller. Loose parts cost roughly 1,600–2,200 yuan; beginners are better off buying a sorted package and saving the sorting time.

**Q: Will it fall over easily? Is it stable on a table?**

A: The lower levels are wide and the centre of gravity is low, so it sits very stably in normal display. Above the spire it is a single-column structure, so keep it in a fixed position and avoid frequent handling — it should not loosen within three months.

**Q: Compared with the official Eiffel Tower set, is the MOC version worth it?**

A: The official set has 10,001 pieces, stands 149 cm tall and costs over 2,800 yuan. This one has 6,826 pieces, stands 108 cm tall, takes up about half the footprint and packs in denser detail — the better recommendation if your budget is under 3,000 yuan.

**Q: How long does the build take, and can a beginner finish it?**

A: An experienced builder needs about 36 hours. Beginners can split it into 10–12 sessions of 3–4 hours each. Work through it in order from base to spire, and do not skip the reinforcement structure in step 12.
