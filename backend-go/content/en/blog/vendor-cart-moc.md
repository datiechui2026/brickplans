# Brick Food Truck MOC: Street-Scene Vendor Cart Build

The short version: this vendor cart MOC uses about 650 pieces, takes roughly 2.5 hours to assemble, and measures 8 studs wide, about 20 cm long and about 16 cm tall. It has side panels that fold up into awnings, a drawer-style serving counter and a lit canopy, and it parks straight into a street-scene layout as a snack stall. Loose parts run about 150–200 RMB, and the instructions are available at brickplan.cn.

The build is split into three clear stages — chassis, cabin, canopy — which makes it easy to sort parts in advance and easy to stop and pick up again later. Below is what each stage involves, how the color scheme works, and how to reskin the cart for a different season.

## Step 1: Chassis and Wheels

The chassis takes about 150 pieces. Build a base roughly 20 cm long from plates and bricks, then mount two pairs of large wheels, front and rear. The front axle gets a steering linkage so the cart can be pushed and steered rather than just rolled in a straight line. Wrap each wheel in arch pieces (half-circle parts) so the tires do not catch on the body when the cart turns.

Leave a compartment inside the chassis for a battery box — this keeps the door open for adding lighting or a motor later without rebuilding the base. The finished chassis should push smoothly without wobbling. Choose large-diameter off-road wheels: they have better ground clearance and roll over the seams between baseplates instead of jamming in them.

## Step 2: Cabin and Serving Counter

The body accounts for about 300 pieces and is designed as a small food truck that opens up. The left and right side panels are hinged and fold upward to work as awnings; the drawer-style counter at the front pulls out to hold drinks and food. On the roof, add an exhaust vent and a menu board, using printed parts or stickers for signage such as hot dogs, burgers and milk tea. This is the section that gives the model its street-corner character, so it is worth spending time on the sign and the counter layout.

## Step 3: Canopy Lighting and Accessories

The canopy takes about 120 pieces. Build a wavy awning from curved slopes and plates; a red-and-white stripe reads best in photos. For night lighting, combine trans-clear parts with light bricks. The counter is dressed with small accessories — grilled sausages, drink cups and a cash register — for roughly 80 pieces in total. Across the whole cart, red, white and wood tone work well together: warm, but still eye-catching on a shelf or in a layout.

## Color Scheme and Customization Ideas

- Main colors: about 200 red, 180 white, 120 wood tone and 50 trans-clear; the rest is accent parts.
- Budget: about 150–200 RMB for 650 pieces, considerably cheaper than an official street-scene set of the same size.
- Play: park the cart in any street-scene layout beside a newsstand and a bakery and you have a busy food street.
- Reskin: for a summer ice-drink cart, swap the red for mint green and the white for cream yellow, add a mini freezer at the counter, and the style changes immediately.

## FAQ

**Q: How many pieces does the vendor cart MOC need?**

A: About 650 for the base version — roughly 150 for the chassis, 300 for the body, and 200 for the canopy and accessories. Loose parts cost about 150–200 RMB, and the model can be split into small bags and assembled in stages.

**Q: Can the vendor cart MOC connect to other street-scene models?**

A: Yes. The chassis is 8 studs wide and fully compatible with standard street-scene building modules. It parks directly along the edge of a 32x32 baseplate, sits alongside shops, and is easy to swap between positions in the layout.

**Q: How long does it take to build a brick food truck?**

A: About 2.5 hours for a beginner and 1.5 hours for an experienced builder. Work in three stages — chassis, body, canopy. The drawer counter and the hinged side panels take the most time. Overall difficulty is medium, suited to builders aged 12 and up.

**Q: What is the difference between a vendor cart and a fixed shop MOC?**

A: The cart has wheels, can be moved, and takes up little space, so it works well as a mobile stall in a street scene. A fixed shop needs a baseplate and full walls; it occupies more room but offers a richer interior. The two combine well in a single layout.

**Q: How much do the instructions cost?**

A: Some steps can be previewed for free at brickplan.cn; the full version costs under about 10 RMB. If you want to save money, you can also follow open-source street-scene instructions and change the colors and signage yourself — the modifications are modest.
