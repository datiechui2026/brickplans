# Parking Lot MOC Review: Dual-Level Garage for 8 Cars

Bottom line first: this parking-lot MOC comes in at roughly 1,800 pieces, parks 8 cars across two levels, measures 48 x 32 studs and costs around 240 RMB. It takes about five hours to build and scores 8/10 overall. The two-level deck, the entry barrier and the vehicle placement are all handled with care, which makes it a cost-effective way to give a city layout a proper garage scene. The instructions are downloadable from brickplan.cn and include builds for six small cars and one motorcycle.

## 1. Two-Level Structure: The Load-Bearing Design Is the Highlight

The ground floor is left open as a column grid, and each column is reinforced with a cross-braced core. The upper deck is assembled from full-size baseplates rather than small plates, and in testing it held four cars with no sagging at all. Two ramps link the levels, one going up and one coming down, pitched at roughly 30 degrees so a car rolls up smoothly.

The trade-off is that the ramps eat up one whole side of the footprint. That leaves 4 spaces per level, 8 in total. **Structure score: 8.5/10.**

## 2. Entry Barrier: Maximum Ceremony

The entrance gets a boom barrier driven by gear pieces — turn the knob on the side and the arm lifts. Next to it sit a toll booth and a printed element standing in for the display screen. The boom is 6 studs long, which is exactly one car wide, so driving a vehicle in and out feels genuinely satisfying. The weak point: there is no auto-return, so you reset the arm by hand every time. **Barrier score: 8.5/10.**

## 3. Vehicle Placement: The Cars Are the Hidden Surprise

The set includes six small cars plus one motorcycle, each car built from about 30 pieces and covering sedans, SUVs and a small van, every one in a different colour scheme. The bay lines are laid out in white 1x1 tiles, which contrast sharply with the dark grey floor, and the cars drop into them perfectly. **Vehicle score: 8/10** — one extra sports car would have made this section perfect.

All told, this parking-lot MOC suits builders who want a garage scene to sit alongside their city layout; the two-level structure and the linked barrier raise the play value considerably. The same series also offers a car wash and a gas station, both searchable on brickplan.cn.

## FAQ

**Q: Will the upper deck collapse under weight?**

A: No. Beneath the upper deck there are cross-braced cores and corner columns carrying the load, and in testing it held four small cars plus minifigures without sagging. As long as you line up the column and deck connections as the instructions show, the structure is very stable.

**Q: Can cars roll up and down the ramps on their own?**

A: Yes. The ramps are pitched at roughly 30 degrees and the surface uses anti-slip elements, so a car pushes up smoothly. Rolling one down the descending ramp is the fun part; going up just needs a light push to reach the top.

**Q: Can this parking lot MOC connect to a city layout?**

A: It can. At 48 x 32 studs it equals about 1.5 standard city modules, so it can sit alongside a 32-stud city block or stand on its own. The connection points are all standard studs, so one adapter baseplate gives you a seamless join.

**Q: Is 240 RMB expensive?**

A: No. At about 1,800 pieces plus the included vehicles, that works out to roughly 0.13 RMB per piece, and the instructions for six car designs come with it. Scenes of this size usually run above 300 RMB, so the ramp and linked barrier here amount to extra content at no extra cost.

**Q: Does the barrier arm break easily?**

A: Not easily. The gear linkage is sturdy and holds up to dozens of normal uses. If it loosens over time, just press the gear axle back into place; if a part does break, you can order it individually using the part numbers in the brickplan instructions.
