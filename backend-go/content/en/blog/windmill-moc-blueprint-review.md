# Windmill MOC Blueprint Review: Dutch Cross-Sail vs Castle Tower

The short version: windmill MOCs split into two families, the Dutch cross-sail type and the castle stone-tower type. Want moving parts, pick the first; want stability and a smaller bill, pick the second. Either way, check the gear steps in the instructions before you download.

The market is flooded with brick windmill MOC blueprints, but look closely and they sort cleanly into two schools: the cross-sail camp led by Dutch windmills, and the stone-tower-with-timber-sails camp led by castle and tower windmills. Styling, drivetrain design and building experience differ enormously between them, and picking the wrong camp can mean wasted money. This review starts with that split so you can decide before you download.

## Dutch Windmills: Cross Sails and Geared Linkage

Dutch-style brick windmill MOCs usually use a mixed brick-and-timber structure: red brick walls paired with dark plank strips, a tower that is wide at the base and narrows toward the top, and the signature cross sail frame on top. What matters most in these blueprints is whether the sail rotation mechanism actually works. Good blueprints connect the sail main shaft to a gear reduction set, so turning the handle rotates all four sails together while the millstone inside the tower is linked to the same drivetrain. That is the full dynamic effect. Some simplified blueprints just run a cross axle straight through, and the sails turn, but stiffly, with no damping and no sense of rhythm. When shopping for a Dutch windmill blueprint, check first whether the step images show a gear set and a linkage explanation; that is what decides whether the model has a soul.

## Castle Windmills: Stone Towers and Timber Sail Frames

The other school is the castle and tower windmill MOC. These builds use large quantities of grey-white and beige bricks to imitate a stone tower, with thick walls and narrow window openings; the sail frame on top is usually wooden-colored long rods plus canvas pieces, giving the whole thing a medieval character. Because the tower is a solid brick-wall structure, internal transmission space is limited, so most castle windmill blueprints have a sail mechanism that is manually turnable but not linked: the sail frame rotates, but it does not drive anything inside. For builders who want a static display piece, that is actually a plus. The structure is more rigid, the parts list is leaner, and nothing works loose on the shelf.

## Difficulty and Part Cost Compared

On build difficulty, Dutch windmill blueprints are consistently harder. Gear alignment, axle hole orientation and the symmetric mounting of four sails are all common failure points, so it is worth having a mid-size set's worth of experience before you take one on. Castle windmills are mostly orderly brick-wall stacking, highly repetitive and very forgiving, and a beginner can finish one.

On part cost, the Dutch windmill's expense sits in gears, cross axles and plank strips and other specialty pieces; the castle windmill's sits in large runs of same-color wall bricks. Between two blueprints of similar total piece count, the Dutch windmill's real-world spend is often 20 to 30 percent higher, which budget-conscious beginners should note.

## Blueprint Clarity

Blueprint clarity decides the build experience outright. Among the windmill MOC blueprints reviewed here, the reliable ones share three traits: step images that highlight newly added parts, a part count listed for every step, and multiple viewing angles for the sail and gear sections. A few free blueprints offer only a single top-down view for the transmission stage, leaving part orientation to guesswork, and beginners very easily install something backwards and jam the mechanism. Before downloading, look at the preview pages and count how many views the gear steps get, then decide.

Whether you prefer the mechanical motion of a Dutch windmill or the solid mass of a castle windmill, choosing the right school makes the build far smoother.
