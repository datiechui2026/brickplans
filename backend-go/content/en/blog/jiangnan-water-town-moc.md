# Brick Jiangnan Water Town MOC: Rivers, Bridges, White Walls

The short version first: lay out the river before you place a single house, leave enough headroom under the arch bridge for a boat to pass, get the upturned eaves right on the roofs, and build the water from dark blue plates topped with trans-light-blue for the glint.

A Jiangnan water town is the most recognisable subject in Chinese-style brick building: white-walled houses with dark tiled roofs along a canal, a bluestone arch bridge, and a black-canopy boat being sculled along. Compared with a Western street scene, the hard part is where the water meets the bridge, plus how to express the curve of a Chinese roof. This guide walks through river layout, arch bridge structure, house construction and water treatment so you can build a diorama that actually feels like the real thing.

## River Layout and Baseplate Planning

Water is the soul of the scene, so fix the river first and the houses second. A 48x32 stud baseplate works well, with the canal cutting diagonally across or curving through in an S shape — much more convincing than a straight channel. Divide the baseplate into three zones: the river bed in dark blue plates, the banks in light grey plates as stone embankments, and the riverside zone where houses sit with a stone landing (steps down to the water) on the canal side. The remaining land carries alleys, the stone bridge and trees. Keep the channel at least 6 to 8 studs wide; any narrower and neither the arch bridge nor the boat will fit. The proportion of water shown on your plan matters — a water town MOC where the river is too small just reads as an ordinary street scene.

## Arch Bridge Structure: The Visual Anchor

## The arch bridge is the most structurally demanding part of the build

For a small bridge, form the arch from curved elements, build the body from slope bricks on both sides, pave the deck with light grey plates to suggest stone slabs, and add a low railing. For a longer stone arch span, build an internal frame from technic pieces first and then clad it in brick — and leave clearance under the arch for the black-canopy boat to pass. That clearance is what makes the scene playable. An advanced option is a stepped bridge: a flat centre section with a few steps at each end, so a minifigure can genuinely walk across. Use blue-grey tones for the bridge so it contrasts with the white walls; one good bridge brings the whole scene to life.

## Building White-Walled Houses with Dark Tiled Roofs

The core elements of a Jiangnan house are white walls, dark roof tiles and timber doors and windows. Use white or off-white plates for the walls, dark grey slope bricks at the eaves to suggest the tiles, and give the roof a double pitch with upturned eaves — build the curve by staggering slope bricks across layers. That eave line is the biggest visual difference between Chinese and Western roofs. For a two-storey house you can use SNOT to create timber window lattice, with a row of dark brown plates below the window standing in for a wooden beam. Houses along the canal need their stone landing: a run of steps projecting from the wall down to the water, which instantly raises the level of detail. Adding a dark trim line between roof and wall makes the whole thing look more refined.

## Water Treatment and the Black-Canopy Boat

How well you handle the water decides whether the scene succeeds. The basic approach is a bed of dark blue plates with a layer of trans-light-blue elements on top to mimic the shimmer; a more advanced version adds light-coloured plates underneath to suggest shallows, with white pieces at the edges for splashes. The black-canopy boat is essential kit — shape the hull from dark brown plates, add a curved black canopy in the middle, turn up the bow and stern, and finish with an oar and a minifigure poling it along. Park it under the bridge arch and the composition is complete. You can also add lotus, reeds and a few ducks, using green plant elements along the embankment.

## Colour Palette and Atmosphere

A water town should feel restrained: white, grey and dark brown dominate, lifted by a few red lanterns and green planting. Hang lanterns under the eaves in red trans pieces for a festive touch, and plant a few willows along the bank — green foliage on dark brown trunks, with trailing branches that echo the classical image of willows brushing the embankment. For display, run a light strip inside the river channel so warm light glows up through the water; combined with warm yellow light in the house windows, the night version photographs beautifully. A mid-sized water town scene costs roughly ¥400–1,000 in parts, and because white and grey plates are used in bulk it pays to buy from a parts list in quantity.

## FAQ

**Q: How should I plan the river in a Jiangnan water town MOC?**
A: A 48x32 stud baseplate works best, with the canal cutting diagonally or curving in an S shape for a more natural feel than a straight channel. Split the baseplate into three zones: dark blue plates for the river, light grey plates for the stone embankments, and houses along the bank. Keep the channel 6 to 8 studs wide.

**Q: How do I make an arch bridge a boat can pass under?**
A: Build the arch from curved elements for a small bridge, use slope bricks for the body, and pave the deck in light grey plates to look like stone. For a long stone arch, build an internal technic frame first and clad it afterwards. The space under the arch must be tall enough for the black-canopy boat — that is what makes the scene playable.

**Q: How do I build white walls with dark tiled roofs?**
A: Use white or off-white plates for walls and dark grey slope bricks at the eaves for the tile effect. Give the roof a double pitch with upturned eaves, building the curve from staggered slope bricks — the clearest difference between Chinese and Western roofs. On a second storey, SNOT gives you timber window lattice.

**Q: How should I handle the water and the boat?**
A: Lay dark blue plates as the bed, then a layer of trans-light-blue elements for the shimmer. For more depth, put light-coloured plates underneath as shallows and white pieces at the edges as splashes. Build the boat hull from dark brown plates, add a curved black canopy, turn up bow and stern, and add an oar plus a minifigure poling it.
