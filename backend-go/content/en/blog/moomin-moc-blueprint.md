# Moomin LEGO MOC Blueprint: Characters and the Blue Moomin House

Bottom line first: the Moomin MOC wins on cozy atmosphere. The character figures come together in about half an hour, the Moomin House takes roughly two or three evenings, and the low-to-medium difficulty makes it a good starting point for newer builders.

## Why the Moomin valley suits brick MOCs

Among the many IP themes out there, the Moomin valley created by Finnish author Tove Jansson was practically designed for brick building. Rounded shapes, low-saturation colors and simple lines mean this subject has almost no rival when it comes to expressing a sense of comfort. Compared with complex mechanical builds or realistic architecture, the Moomin theme cares far more about expression and mood, which keeps it friendly to beginners and still leaves experienced builders plenty of room to improvise.

## The classic character figures: Moomin, Snufkin and Little My

The characters are the heart of any Moomin MOC. Moomin himself is a white hippo-like little creature: builders usually stack 1x2 curved slope bricks to get that round belly, use black studs for the eyes, and add a small white slope brick for his signature little tail. Snufkin is all about the green hat and worn coat — brown trousers plus a harmonica element immediately give him the air of a wandering poet. Little My is the smallest of the three, and her red hair is the biggest identifying feature; a red hair piece paired with a white skirt piece and a couple of steps gets you a chibi-style figure. Many Moomin blueprints turn all three characters into a set with interchangeable heads, so you can pose them in all sorts of interactive scenes.

## The Moomin House: a round blue little tower

The landmark of the whole valley is, naturally, the Moomin House — that cylindrical building with blue walls and a white roof. The most common approach in MOC instructions is to ring the tower body with large cylindrical bricks or curved plates, close the roof in with white slope bricks layer by layer, and cut a round window into the second floor. The porch and staircase are the finishing touch, built from brown bricks to suggest wood. More advanced builders also layer the interior to create a living room, kitchen and attic bedroom, then light it at night with light-up elements so the whole house takes on a fairy-tale warmth.

## Forest and coastline scenes

Moomin stories always unfold between forest and shore. Instructions typically spread green plant elements across the valley floor, alternating dark and light green to mimic the play of tree shadows. For the coast, blue transparent plates become the sea surface, white wave bricks add foam, and a small red boat — the one Moominpappa is always rowing — sits alongside. Swap in orange and yellow leaf elements for an autumn scene, or cover everything in white studs for snow in winter: the same blueprint, a different color scheme, and the scene feels new every season.

## Color and parts planning

The core of the Moomin world is white and cream, supported by blue, green and a touch of red as accents. White parts are consumed in huge quantities, so it pays to stock up on basic bricks and curved slope bricks up front; blue goes mainly on the roofs and the exterior walls of the Moomin House. Because so many shapes are rounded, curved slopes, round plates and cone pieces are the big consumers. Blueprint authors usually list substitution options in the parts list, which makes it easier to fill the build from parts you already own.

## Difficulty and how to start

Most Moomin blueprints sit at low-to-medium difficulty: character figures finish within half an hour, the Moomin House takes about two or three evenings, and large dioramas are better suited to advanced builders. Getting started is simple — pick a well-rated blueprint on BrickPlan, check the parts list against your collection, then build step by step. Whether it ends up on a bookshelf or as decoration in a kid's room, this gentle white slice of a Nordic valley is worth building with your own hands.

## FAQ

**Q: I have zero building experience — which Moomin character is easiest to start with?**
A: Start with the character figures. Moomin is built from 1x2 curved slope bricks stacked into a round body with black studs for eyes; Snufkin gets a green hat and brown trousers, and a chibi figure takes just two or three steps.

**Q: How long does the whole Moomin House take, and is it hard?**
A: It sits at low-to-medium difficulty and takes roughly two or three evenings. Cylindrical bricks form the tower body, white slope bricks close the roof, and a round window goes into the second floor. Advanced builders can add interior layers and lighting.

**Q: Will I run out of white parts when building a Moomin MOC? How should I stock up?**
A: White and cream are the dominant colors, and white basic bricks and curved slope bricks are consumed in huge amounts, so buy a batch outright. Rounded shapes dominate, which makes curved slopes, round plates and cone pieces the heaviest consumers.

**Q: Can one Moomin blueprint produce four different seasonal scenes?**
A: Yes — just change the colors. Use dark and light green plant elements for grass, swap to orange and yellow leaves for autumn, cover the ground in white studs for snow, and use blue transparent plates for the sea.
