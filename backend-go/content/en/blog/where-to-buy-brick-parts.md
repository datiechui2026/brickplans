# Where to Buy Brick Parts in 2026: 5 Sources Compared

Finish an official set and a handful of key pieces always seem to be missing. Start designing an original MOC and you suddenly need piles of odd-coloured bricks and rare specialised elements. That is when most beginners ask the same question: where do you actually buy brick parts? There are plenty of channels out there, and each one differs in price, delivery speed, range and reliability. Here is a rundown of the main options so you can skip the trial and error and keep both your budget and your patience intact.

## Online Marketplaces: The Easiest Place to Start

If speed is your priority, mass-market e-commerce platforms come first. Taobao, JD, Pinduoduo and the 1688 wholesale site are all packed with brick parts sellers. You will find individual pieces sold by the unit, "meat buckets" sold by weight, and even curated packs assembled to match specific MOC instructions.

The advantages are obvious. The range is enormous — almost any colour or shape you can imagine is searchable. Pricing is transparent, so you can compare sellers side by side, and dispatch is quick, with ordinary couriers arriving in three to five days. The downside is inconsistent quality: some cheap parts have dimensional tolerances that make them clutch too loosely or too tightly, so read reviews and check seller reputation carefully. Also note that many listings price per single piece, which looks cheap until shipping is added — it usually pays to build up a decent-sized order before checking out.

## Specialist Parts Shops: Veteran Builders' Favourite

Compared with the big general platforms, dedicated brick parts shops work more like a parts supermarket. Stock is usually organised by brand and part number, and you can search precisely by element ID. Quality is more consistent here: much of the inventory is parted out from genuine sets or comes from proper supplementary packs, so colour matching is accurate — ideal for builders chasing fidelity to the original model.

Value sits in the upper-middle range. Unit prices may be slightly higher than on no-name platforms, but you get what you see, and it is much harder to end up with substandard pieces. Some higher-end shops even let you upload a parts list and will fill the whole order automatically, which suits serious MOC builders. The catch is that the catalogue tends to concentrate on mainstream brands, so unusual colours still have to be sourced elsewhere.

## Official Brand Replacement: Easiest, But Priciest

If you need to replace a piece lost from an official set, which channel is most reliable? The brand itself, every time. LEGO and the major domestic brands all run official replacement services — supply the set number and the part number, and the missing element is mailed out from the factory.

The big advantage is that it is absolutely original: colour, clutch and printing match the rest of the set exactly, which makes it the first choice for collectors who want perfection. The drawbacks are just as clear. The process is slow, often taking one to two weeks or more, and prices are high — single-item shipping frequently costs more than the part itself. Official replacement therefore suits small quantities of critical pieces; for bulk buying, other channels make far more sense.

## Secondhand and Clearance Markets: Bargain Hunting

Builders who enjoy the hunt often strike gold on secondhand trading platforms. Sellers who part out sets sell bricks by weight at prices that are hard to resist — a great way to build up volume and colour variety. Many people leaving the hobby also sell entire collections of loose parts, and the value can be outstanding.

The pros and cons are both sharp. It is cheap, high-volume and a reliable source of scarce pieces, but descriptions may not match reality, condition varies (scratches, yellowing), and returns are a hassle. Favour sellers with good feedback and real photos of the actual lot, and inspect your delivery as soon as it arrives. For builders on a tight budget planning large MOCs, the secondhand market is absolutely worth trying.

## How to Combine Channels and Spend Wisely

With those options in mind, the answer to "where do you buy brick parts" should be clear. A practical rule: **send critical pieces through official or specialist parts channels, and buy bulk basic bricks from secondhand markets or e-commerce platforms.** Write out your parts list first, then grade each line by rarity and quantity before ordering, and you will assemble the full inventory at the lowest total cost.

Do a little homework before you buy. Check reference market prices on parts databases such as BrickLink, watch for platform sales and spend-threshold discounts, and always confirm the part number so you do not end up with a near-identical substitute shape. With those habits in place, whether you are completing a set or finishing a large MOC, you will have the pieces you need and can get back to the pure fun of building.
