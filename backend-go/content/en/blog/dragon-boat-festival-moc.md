# LEGO Dragon Boat Festival MOC: Boat and Zongzi Tutorial

The short version: roughly 600 pieces will get you a 45 cm dragon boat MOC for about 80 yuan, and a first-time builder can finish it in around 3 hours. The zongzi (rice dumpling) vignette is even lighter — about 150 pieces and 30 minutes. Below is the full breakdown, from parts list to build order to modification ideas; more reference builds live in the brickplan.cn community.

The Dragon Boat Festival is one of the best Chinese holidays to build around. A dragon boat has clean, flowing lines and reads instantly, while zongzi, mugwort and sachets are small objects that are easy to render at minifigure scale — and the result works as a desk display. Compared with commercial sets that run into the thousands of pieces, a festival MOC you design yourself has more personality and teaches you more. What follows goes dragon boat first, zongzi second.

## Before You Build: Parts and Color Scheme

Settle two things before you open a single bag: size and color. Keep the boat under 45 cm (about the length of a 40-stud baseplate) — longer hulls get fragile, shorter ones leave no room for detail. For color, lead with red and gold, then work in a little dark brown for the hull grain. That combination gives the strongest festive read.

Here is a recommended list for a 600-piece budget:

- About 260 red plates and bricks (main hull);
- About 120 gold and yellow parts (dragon head and tail decoration);
- About 100 dark brown and black parts (hull texture and stand);
- About 40 white transparent parts (water splash);
- 2 minifigures + 8 paddle elements (the rowers).

You can tick these off on the brickplan.cn parts library page to generate a purchase list and buy everything in one go.

## Step 2: Build the Keel and Hull First

Always start with the keel. Run 4x16 plates lengthwise to form the bottom spine, then stack 2x4 bricks in layers on both sides to raise the hull walls, stepping each layer in by one stud so the sides taper inward. Leave 4 studs of clear space at the bow and 4 at the stern for the dragon head and tail.

Take the hull to about 40 studs long and hold the width at 6 studs; that proportion looks right in the water and on the shelf. When you reach layer 5, bury two 1x2 bricks with holes on the inside of each wall — these will hold the oar mounts. Plenty of builders skip this, then find the paddles have nothing to attach to.

## Step 3: Dragon Head, Tail and Decorative Details

The dragon head is the soul of the build. Stack 3 curved slope bricks to suggest an opening jaw, use two 1x1 round tiles for eyes, and push two 2-stud hoses in beside the nostrils as whiskers. For the tail, layer 3 curved plates in reverse to create the upward curl.

Set a zongzi amidships: butt two 2x2 green slope bricks against each other, sandwich a dark green plate in the middle, and top it with a 1x1 white tile to stand in for the cotton string. That is a mini zongzi done. Finish by laying white transparent parts along both sides of the hull as splashing water. The finished boat measures 45 cm and uses about 580 pieces.

## Step 4: A Small Zongzi Scene

Short on time? Build the zongzi vignette instead: one 8x8 baseplate, 3 zongzi in the middle (same method as above), a sachet stacked from 1x1 cylinder bricks beside them, and a minifigure with a tray. That is roughly 150 pieces and 30 minutes of work, and it photographs beautifully on a desk.

## FAQ

**Q: How many pieces and how much budget does a Dragon Boat Festival MOC need?**

A: The dragon boat is about 600 pieces and around 80 yuan; the zongzi vignette is about 150 pieces and around 20 yuan, depending on color choices and size.

**Q: Can a beginner finish a dragon boat MOC on the first try?**

A: Absolutely. Following the steps here, expect about 3 hours. It helps to test-build once in the brickplan.cn virtual builder before ordering parts.

**Q: Will the finished boat fall apart easily?**

A: As long as the keel plates interlock across the spine and the hull layers are staggered rather than stacked in line, it holds together in normal display. Just don't fold it in half at the middle when handling it.

**Q: Can I find these instructions on brickplan.cn?**

A: Yes. Search for the dragon boat or festival terms and you'll see complete instructions and parts lists from several authors. This design is based on a popular community build.

**Q: Can a finished boat be entered in community contests?**

A: Yes. brickplan.cn runs themed contests around every holiday, and the dragon boat category is a permanent one; submitting earns you points as well.
