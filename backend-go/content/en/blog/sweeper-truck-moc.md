# Brick Sweeper Truck MOC: Build Guide for Brushes & Hydraulics

The short version: this brick street sweeper MOC uses roughly 1,600 pieces to build a road sweeper about 38 cm long and 20 cm tall. Plan on around 6 hours of building and a budget of about 320 CNY. Difficulty sits at medium-high. The heart of the model is a brush assembly that actually spins and a hopper hydraulic arm that actually lifts, which makes it a very playable build once it is finished.

Among municipal vehicle subjects, the sweeper is the one that looks like it is doing real work. Two rotating brushes under the chassis, a recovery intake in the middle, a hopper at the rear, and the occasional lifting hydraulic arm add up to a structure far richer than a plain truck. This guide walks the build in four steps so you can assemble the whole thing from standard Technic parts. Before you start, check the parts list on brickplan so you do not run short halfway through.

## Step 1: Chassis and Wheels

Start with the chassis rails. Use two long Technic beams as the longitudinal rails and lock them together with cross beams in the middle so the frame carries weight without flexing. Build the front axle as a steerable structure, linked to a steering wheel handle, and reinforce the rear wheels with a dual-axle setup. Keep the chassis length around 30 cm, which leaves mounting space for the brushes and the hopper later. This step is the foundation of the whole vehicle, because every drivetrain and lifting mechanism ends up hanging off it, so press every pin in firmly.

## Step 2: The Brush Mechanism

The signature feature of a sweeper is the pair of rotating brushes underneath. Go with a gearbox approach: power from the rear axle runs through a bevel gear set to the brush main shafts on the left and right sides. Fix cross axles and round plates onto those shafts to imitate the nylon bristles. When you turn the crank, the two brushes counter-rotate and sweep the "debris" toward the center, just like the real machine. When building, leave ground clearance under the brush shafts, because mounting them too close to the chassis will jam them. Keep the two brushes symmetrical in angle, and they will slip far less when reversed.

## Step 3: Hopper and Hydraulic Arm

Build the hopper as a rear tipper with enough volume to hold about 40 1x1 round plates. For the lift, use a gear rack plus support rods: turn the side handle, the rack pushes the front of the hopper upward, and hinge pieces let it tip out its load. Simulate the hydraulic arm with Technic pneumatic cylinders or sliding rods, anchored to the chassis rails. Do not let the force press only on the hopper shell, or it will fall apart at high lift. Make the hopper lid removable so you can demonstrate loading and dumping.

## Step 4: Cab and Livery

Finish with the cab. Use SNOT side building to shape a rounded outline, then add a seat, a steering wheel and two mirrors, with a warning light on the roof. For the livery, yellow as the main color with black anti-slip steps as a secondary works well. Add city-cleaning style lettering stickers to the body and reflective strips at the rear for night visibility. The finished vehicle is about 38 cm long and photographs beautifully on a street diorama base.

## FAQ

**Q: Do the brushes under the sweeper actually move?**

A: Yes. With the gearbox approach, power from the rear axle runs through a bevel gear set to the brush main shafts on both sides. Turning the crank counter-rotates the two brushes and gathers debris toward the center, so you can genuinely demonstrate the sweeping action.

**Q: How much does this brick sweeper MOC cost?**

A: Budget around 320 CNY. That covers roughly 1,600 pieces, more than 90 percent of them common Technic parts. Compare your loose parts against the brickplan parts list and fill only the gaps, which is cheaper than buying full assorted packs.

**Q: Can kids play with it?**

A: It suits children aged 10 and up with some building experience. Difficulty is medium-high, and the brush gear train and hydraulic lift benefit from adult help. Once it is built, kids tend to love the gear linkage and the lifting, tipping hopper.

**Q: How is it different from official sweeper sets?**

A: Official sets are mostly static display models or simple pull-back toys, while this MOC has three linked mechanisms: rotating brushes, a lifting hopper and steerable front wheels. Playability and accuracy are higher, but you source the parts yourself and build from the instructions.
