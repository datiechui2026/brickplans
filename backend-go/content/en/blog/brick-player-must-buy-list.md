# Brick Building Starter Gear: What to Buy at Every Budget

The short version: a must-buy list for brick builders splits into three budget tiers. Under 500 yuan, start with one good set and a compartment box. Around 1000 yuan, add a brick separator, baseplates and a storage cabinet. Past 2000 yuan, move into big display builds and lighting kits. Get the order right and the money lands where it actually matters.

## 1. Entry tier (under 500 yuan): sets come first

Put roughly 70% of your first budget into a set. Aim for a themed model in the 1000-2000 piece range, typically priced at 250-450 yuan. Spend the remaining 30% on a 40-compartment sorting box (about 30 yuan) and a brick separator (about 15 yuan). A compartment box cuts the time you spend hunting for parts by around 70%, and a separator protects both your fingertips and the bricks themselves.

## 2. Intermediate tier (around 1000 yuan): fill in the equipment

At this stage you add three things. First, two or three 32x32 baseplates at roughly 40 yuan each, which let you expand a scene outward. Second, a drawer-style parts cabinet (about 150-300 yuan) so bricks can be stored sorted by colour. Third, a white-light desk lamp (about 80 yuan) — long build sessions are much easier on the eyes with proper light.

## 3. Advanced tier (2000 yuan and up): go for the big pieces

Once the budget is comfortable, prioritise flagship sets of 3000 pieces or more (800-1500 yuan), along with a display cabinet and lighting components — the finished result looks noticeably better on show. You can also point the budget at a parts wall and buy loose bricks by weight, then rebuild popular designs from brickplan.cn yourself.

## 4. Three rules beyond the list

- Buy sets before accessories. Don't stockpile tool kits you have no use for yet.
- Sort parts by colour or by element number — pick one system and stick to it instead of mixing both.
- Keep the instructions and the leftover parts from every build, so future MOC modifications are easier.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a beginner spend on a first set?**

A: A themed set in the 200-400 yuan range with 1000-2000 pieces is a good fit — moderate difficulty and a high chance you actually finish it. Too cheap means too few pieces for any sense of achievement; too expensive makes it easy to give up halfway.

**Q: Do I need both a compartment box and a storage cabinet?**

A: For a beginner, the compartment box is enough. Only consider a drawer cabinet once your part count passes 5000. The compartment box serves the building process, the cabinet handles day-to-day storage — different jobs.

**Q: What are baseplates for, and are they essential?**

A: Baseplates anchor a scene and make it easy to move and stack. For street scenes and architectural MOCs they are close to mandatory. If you build cars or mechs, you can skip them for now and put the saved budget into a set instead.

**Q: Is a brick separator a waste of money?**

A: No. A separator costing a dozen yuan prevents injured nails and scratched parts, and it is near-essential when pulling apart small pieces or tightly fitted connections. Low cost, high return.

**Q: With a limited budget, should I buy a set or accessories first?**

A: Buy the set first. Accessories can be added gradually once you are building often — one good set delivers far more enjoyment and practice value than hoarding tools in advance.
