# Brick Wupeng Boat MOC: Arched Canopy and Hull Guide

The short version first: everything that makes a wupeng boat look right comes down to curves and proportion. Build the internal keel as a grid of Technic beams, form the vault from three 1x4x1 curved bricks, keep the canopy in dark grey or pure black, and run sand-white tiles along the gunwales.

The wupeng boat is one of the most recognisable symbols of the Jiangnan waterways — a long, narrow hull under a black arched awning, carrying a quiet, understated classical beauty. Turning that "black canopy on the water" into brick form hinges on two things: the curves, and the proportion. This guide takes you from keel to canopy, step by step, so you can build your own little river boat.

## Hull Structure: Start With the Keel

The hull is the foundation of the whole model. For the internal keel, Technic parts are the way to go: take two rows of 16-stud Technic beams as the port and starboard frames, then tie them together laterally with 4 cross-axle connectors spaced roughly 4 studs apart, forming a ladder-style grid frame. Once the keel is solid, skin both sides with tiles to build up the hull shell. At the bow, switch to 1x3 or 2x3 curved slope bricks to pull the shape into a sharp point.

To capture the classic upturned bow and stern, bed in one layer of 1x2 hinge pieces at each end so the hull tips rise about 15 degrees. That single detail is what gives the side profile its life — without it the boat reads as a flat box floating on studs.

## The Arched Canopy: Using Curved Parts Correctly

The canopy is the soul of the wupeng boat. The obvious approach — inverting one large curved piece such as a 6x6x2 curved plate — makes the roof look bloated. A more refined method is to lay 3 curved 1x4x1 bricks side by side, with the middle one sitting half a plate higher to create a three-segment vault. Then run 1x2 tiles along the seams as a woven-bamboo texture, leaving roughly a 1 mm gap between segments to suggest the bamboo strips of the original matting.

Size the canopy to cover about two-thirds of the hull, roughly 12 studs, leaving 2 studs of deck at the bow and 2 at the stern. If you want the roof to open, fit 2 hinge pieces where the canopy meets the hull and turn it into a flip-top lid — much better for handling the interior.

## Cabin and Scull Oar Details

Open the canopy and the cabin can hold a small bed built from 2x4 tiles, with a 1x1 round plate as a pillow. Not much of it will be visible, but the honesty of having a real interior changes how the whole model reads.

The stern scull oar is the finishing touch. Use an 8-stud Technic axle as the shaft, cap the end with a 1x1 Technic brick with hole as the handle, and mount it to the stern side with two 1x2 bracket pieces with bar handles so the oar can swing up and down. Add a 2x2 curved plate at the front of the shaft to suggest the blade entering the water — the boat instantly comes alive.

## Color Scheme

The wupeng boat lives by the rule of a black canopy on a white hull. Use dark grey or pure black curved parts for the roof, sand white or beige tiles for the gunwales, and medium grey for the deck, which gives you a three-tier color rhythm. For a "moored at night" scene, swap the canopy to dark blue and hang a trans-clear 1x1 lamp piece at the bow — the Jiangnan night atmosphere lands immediately.

The whole build runs about 300–400 pieces: roughly 40 curved parts, 6 hinge pieces and 30 Technic elements. That puts it at medium size, an ideal practice build for beginners moving up to intermediate level.

## FAQ

**Q: There are always gaps between the curved canopy pieces. What should I do?**
A: Level the base layer with tiles first, then mount the curved bricks sideways using SNOT — the gaps shrink considerably. Switching the vault to 3 curved 1x4x1 bricks side by side with the middle one half a plate higher also gives you a clean three-segment step.

**Q: The hull never feels stable enough. How do I fix it?**
A: Run Technic beams the full length of the keel: two rows of 16-stud beams with 4 cross-axle connectors at roughly 4-stud spacing. Then make sure the center of the hull bottom has a 2-stud-wide flat surface sitting on the water — the center of gravity sorts itself out.

**Q: How should I handle the colors?**
A: Black canopy, white boat. Dark grey or pure black curved parts on the roof, sand white or beige tiles on the gunwales, medium grey on the deck for a three-tier rhythm. For a night scene, switch the canopy to dark blue and hang a trans-clear lamp piece at the bow.

**Q: Can the canopy be made openable?**
A: Yes. Connect the canopy to the hull with hinge pieces and it opens and closes. Put a 2x4 tile bed and a 1x1 round plate pillow inside — it shows much better with the roof up — and finish the stern with an 8-stud Technic axle scull oar.

**Quick fixes**

- Gaps between curved parts: level the base with tiles, then mount the curved bricks sideways with SNOT.
- Hull still unstable: run Technic beams the full length, and keep a 2-stud-wide flat area at the center of the hull bottom touching the water.
- Canopy arch not round enough: bed 1x2 bricks underneath the curved parts as a stepped ramp, adding half a plate per segment for a more natural curve.
