# MOC Instruction Design: From First Sketch to Published PDF

The whole path comes down to one line: learn the software first, then the structure, then the presentation. Follow it at one hour a day and within three months you can design original instructions ready to publish on brickplan. Keep your first model under 300 parts.

## Step 1: Choose the right software and learn the interface

The most widely used MOC design tool right now is Bricklink Studio, and it is free. Spend a week on the four core areas: the parts library, the build canvas, the step manager and the render window. Start by rebuilding the official 100-piece sample plane, and drill three operations until they are automatic: dragging, rotating and recoloring. Everything you do later sits on top of those three.

Once the interface feels familiar, set yourself a two-week practice plan. Week one: 30 minutes a day on small objects under 50 parts, such as a chair, a street lamp or a mailbox. Week two: move up to small scenes of around 100 parts. While you practice, focus on three shortcuts, rotate, duplicate and undo. Get fluent with them and your building speed roughly doubles. Save your practice models as template files so that new designs can start from a template instead of from zero.

## Step 2: Master the basic structural techniques

Structure is what holds an MOC together. Three techniques matter most. Clutch reinforcement, where bricks interlock so the model does not fall apart. SNOT, which turns the stud direction 90 degrees to produce smooth surfaces. And color zoning, using 3 to 4 main colors to keep the model from looking chaotic. Then test it: pick the finished build up and shake it. It should survive more than 10 shakes before you can call the structure sound.

Structure work only pays off when you test it. Beyond the shake test, put the model in the render window and look up at it from underneath to check for floating parts and weak connections. Wide roofs or bridge decks need beams or support columns, spaced no more than 8 studs apart. Color zoning is not a case of fewer colors being better either. A ratio of 60% main color, 30% secondary color and 10% accent works well, keeping the build unified but still layered, and it photographs better too.

## Step 3: Learn step breakdown and instruction layout

Good instructions let someone build without reading a word. Split the steps automatically with Instruction Maker, then adjust by hand: keep each step to 3 to 5 new parts, use arrows to make the insertion direction clear, and add close-up views at critical connections. A 300-part model usually breaks down into 60 to 80 steps, and a newcomer can build it in about an hour.

When the breakdown is done, set the instructions aside for two days and then come back to them. Once the novelty has worn off you spot step jumps far more easily. If you can, find a friend to act as a test builder and have them build the model from your instructions exactly as written. Note down where they get stuck. Those spots are where you need extra close-up views or text notes. One or two rounds of testing and revision makes a noticeable difference in quality, and scores typically come out 0.5 points higher or more.

## Step 4: Publish, gather feedback and iterate

Export the instructions as a PDF and upload them to brickplan, along with a render of the finished model and a note of the part count. After publishing, read the comments carefully. 80% of poor reviews come down to unclear steps and missing part callouts. Fix what the feedback points to and bump the version number. The second version usually scores noticeably better than the first, and if you keep iterating your level improves in a way anyone can see.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I get started with MOC design from zero?**
A: Install the free Bricklink Studio, build a small model following the official sample, then learn structural techniques and step layout. The brickplan blog also has companion illustrated tutorials you can follow.

**Q: Can I design MOC instructions if I cannot draw?**
A: Yes. Studio is drag-and-drop modeling, so you never touch a paintbrush. As long as your sense of space is normal and you are willing to adjust things repeatedly, you can produce good-looking original instructions with no art background at all and publish them.

**Q: How much does it cost to learn MOC instruction design?**
A: The software is completely free. The main costs are time and practice parts. Buying small practice parts by weight is very economical, and a few hundred yuan is enough to keep you busy for a long time, far less than buying sets directly.

**Q: How much can a finished set of instructions sell for?**
A: brickplan supports paid instructions, with common prices between 10 and 50 yuan. Top authors earn from a few hundred to a few thousand yuan a month. Newcomers are advised to build up ratings with free releases first before opening paid downloads.
