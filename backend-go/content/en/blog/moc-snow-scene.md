# Brick Snow Scene MOC Tutorial: Slopes, Trees and Ski Runs

Short version first: the trick to a convincing snow scene is white tiles for open space, grey-white for shadow, and a few dark focal points like pines and ski runs. A 40×40 cm scene runs roughly 800–1200 pieces, costs about 300–500 RMB, and takes a beginner 3–5 hours.

## 1. Gathering Parts: White Is Not Just White

The most common way a snow scene fails is a flat expanse of pure white that looks like an unfinished build. The fix is to lead with white but support it with grey-white and light grey: 300–400 white tiles, 200–300 white plates, 150–200 grey-white and light grey plates, and 100–150 white 1x1 round plates. Total budget lands around 300–500 RMB.

## 2. Building the Snow Slopes and Snowfield Texture

**Step one: lay the snowfield.** Cover the ground with white tiles in large runs — the smooth surface reads most like packed snow. Studded plates suggest loose powder. Mixing tile and stud across the ground is what gives the snow its texture.

**Step two: build the slope.** Stack slope pieces in layers; roughly 45 degrees looks most like a natural snow slope. Cover the surface with white tiles, then pile a ring of white round plates at the base to suggest the curve of drifted snow.

**Step three: add texture.** Dot grey-white or light grey plates into the low spots for shadow, and scatter white 1x1 plates or rounds loosely across the slope as falling snow. The more asymmetric the texture, the more natural it looks.

## 3. Focal Points: Winter Trees and a Ski Run

**Winter trees:** use brown or dark grey for the trunk, then stack white plates into a cone that narrows layer by layer, like a pine loaded with snow. A 15 cm snow-covered pine takes about 60–80 pieces, and 3–4 of them are enough to anchor the composition.

**Ski run:** run 2–3 parallel white slopes down the scene as pistes, line both sides with dark grey plates as rock or safety fencing, and place skier minifigures at the bottom. Add a cable car built from transparent rods as the visual hook.

Finish by framing the scene with white baseplates and putting a light blue plate behind it as a snowy sky. Shoot under cool-toned light and the winter atmosphere lands immediately. For more snow scene inspiration, browse the gallery at brickplan.cn.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I keep a snow MOC from looking like an unfinished white wall?**

A: The problem is colour monotony. Always bring in grey-white and light grey for shadow, mix tiles with studded surfaces for texture, and add dark elements such as pines or skier minifigures as focal points. Keep white at around 70 percent of the scene so it doesn't feel like it's floating.

**Q: Which parts give the most natural snow texture?**

A: Tiles for packed snow, studded plates for powder, white 1x1 round plates for snow grains — the three together look most natural. Add a few light grey plates in the dips for shadow and pile white round plates along the slope edges to suggest drifting, which is far more lively than plain white plates.

**Q: What slope angle works best for a ski run?**

A: Around 45 degrees is closest to a natural snow slope. Steeper and the minifigures won't stay put; shallower and you lose the sense of motion. Build it up in layers of slope pieces, cover the surface with white tiles, and leave a stretch of flat ground at the bottom as a run-out so the skier's pose looks natural.

**Q: Does white brick get dirty easily, and how do I clean it?**

A: White does show dirt more than dark colours, but ordinary handling isn't a problem. Dust surfaces with a soft brush or a slightly damp cotton swab. For a full wash, soak in warm water with a mild neutral detergent and air-dry naturally — avoid direct sun, or the white will yellow.

**Q: Can a snow scene be combined with a regular street scene?**

A: Yes, and it works beautifully. Treat the snow as a winter edition of the street: swap the original ground for white baseplates, replace roofs and tree canopies with white snow-covered pieces, and scatter white round plates as falling snow. One street layout then covers two seasons, which is excellent value.
