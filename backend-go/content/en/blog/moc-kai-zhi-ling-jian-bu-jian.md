# Qiji Missing Parts: Replacement Guide for Builders

Few things kill the momentum of a build faster than opening a bag and finding a critical part missing, or watching a tiny element bounce off the desk and vanish mid-assembly. For anyone who builds seriously with Qiji sets, knowing how to get a replacement part is a core skill — not an emergency. This guide covers every route available, the paperwork each one expects, and the shortcuts experienced builders use to keep a project moving.

## Why Qiji Sets End Up Short of Parts

Before chasing a replacement, it helps to know where the gap came from. Qiji sits in the upper-middle tier of domestic brands for quality control, but no manufacturer achieves zero shortages. The usual causes:

- **Packing omissions**: a part simply never made it into its bag at the factory
- **Shipping damage**: a bag splits in transit and elements escape
- **Lost during the build**: small pieces such as 1x1 bricks and technic pins roll away
- **Damaged elements**: moulding defects or parts crushed in transit
- **MOC top-ups**: nothing is wrong with the set at all — you just need more of a given part for your own design

The good news is that replacement options are far broader than most builders assume.

## Official Qiji Replacement Channels

### Filing a Claim With Customer Support

Qiji runs a reasonably complete after-sales parts service. If a set you bought is short an element, the most direct route is official support:

1. **WeChat official account**: follow the Qiji bricks account, then go to After-Sales Service → Parts Replacement. Submit your order number, the missing part number and the quantity needed.
2. **Tmall / JD flagship stores**: message the store's online support, describe the shortage, and attach a photo of the packaging plus a screenshot of the parts list.
3. **Official email**: send your replacement request to the after-sales address with proof of purchase and an itemised list of what is missing.

Requests are normally handled within 3–7 business days. Missing parts ship free, with the brand covering postage.

### What to Have Ready Before You Ask

To improve your odds on the first attempt, prepare the following:

- **Set number**: the product code printed on the box (for example KZ-XXXX)
- **Missing part number**: taken from the parts list in the instructions
- **Proof of purchase**: an order screenshot or a photo of the invoice
- **Packaging photo**: the unopened inner bag, which shows the shortage was not user-caused

## How to Look Up Qiji Part Numbers

### The Parts List in the Instructions

Every Qiji instruction booklet closes with a detailed parts list, and this is the single most important reference when requesting a replacement. It records the number, name, colour, quantity and illustration for each element, so you can confirm exactly what is absent before you file anything.

### Online Part Databases

Beyond the printed list, Qiji is gradually building an online parts database. You can look up information through:

- The parts lookup page on Qiji's official website
- Third-party brick databases, including Qiji entries on platforms such as BrickLink and Rebrickable
- Qiji parts threads on brick community forums

## Alternative Routes for Missing Parts

### Generic Compatible Elements

Qiji sets use standard LEGO-compatible dimensions, which means the large majority of shortages can be solved with generic parts. The domestic parts market is mature, and these channels all stock compatible replacements:

- **Taobao / Pinduoduo**: search for Qiji brick parts or compatible brick parts; plenty of shops sell individual elements
- **1688 wholesale**: the better option if you need parts in bulk, with lower unit prices
- **Xianyu secondhand market**: builders regularly sell off spare parts bags at very good value

### Borrowing From Another Set

If you already own several Qiji sets, or are about to buy a new one, you can also pull the part from an existing model. Replacement does not have to mean buying loose elements — using the parts library you already have is often the fastest fix.

### Planning Ahead for MOC Work

If you build your own designs regularly, set up a personal restocking routine:

1. After each MOC is finished, sort the leftover parts by colour and type
2. Before starting a new project, check the parts list and fill the gaps in advance
3. Keep a standing stock of basic elements — 1x1, 1x2 and 2x4 bricks in your most-used colours

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Qiji charge for replacement parts?

**Official route**: parts found missing within 7 days of purchase are replaced free, with the brand paying postage. Past 7 days, or without proof of purchase, you may be asked to cover postage or the cost of the part.

**Unofficial route**: compatible parts bought through Taobao are priced by quantity and specification, typically 0.05–0.3 yuan per element.

### How long does a replacement take?

Official replacements generally take 7–14 business days including shipping time. Third-party sellers on Taobao usually deliver in 2–5 days.

### Are Qiji parts compatible with LEGO?

Fully. Qiji uses standard brick moulds and its elements fit LEGO and other mainstream domestic brands well, which is exactly what gives you so many replacement options.

## Summary and Practical Advice

Getting a replacement part is not complicated — the key is picking the right channel. If you are new to Qiji, go official first: it is the least effort and the most reliable. If you build MOCs constantly, build up your own parts reserve and buy compatible elements across several channels so day-to-day creativity never stalls.

One last tip: lay a light-coloured building mat over your desk while you work. If a part drops, you will find it in seconds instead of crawling around on the floor. And if the missing piece is a rare colour, ask around in the brick community — trading spares with other builders is part of what makes the MOC hobby enjoyable.

With these routes in hand, a missing element becomes a minor detour rather than a dead end, and your builds keep moving.
