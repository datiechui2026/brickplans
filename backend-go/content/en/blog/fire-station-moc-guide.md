# How to Build a Fire Station MOC: Red Street Building Tutorial

Short answer first: a brick fire station MOC is not a hard build. Work through four stages — base, walls, fire-service details, interior — with roughly 800 to 1,200 pieces and a budget of 300 to 600 yuan, and you end up with a three-storey red fire-station street building that a first-time builder can finish in under 8 hours. Here is the whole process broken down stage by stage.

**Step 1: Fix the scale and plan the parts budget.** For a street-scene fire station, use a 32x32 stud baseplate (about 25.6 cm square) with three storeys about 30 cm tall. That matches the scale of common modular street buildings, so the station can stand side by side with them. Red parts should make up about 60 percent of the model, accented with white window frames and gray steps. Keep the total near 1,000 pieces; at roughly 0.4 yuan per piece that lands the budget at about 400 yuan. Sort the red bricks by size before you start — a fire station uses so much red that hunting through one big pile is the slowest part of the build.

**Step 2: Build the base and the wall structure.** Lay the 32x32 baseplate first, build the foundation from dark gray plates, then raise the walls course by course. Stack the red bricks in a staggered bond and add a plate every two layers as reinforcement — the walls stay noticeably stiffer, and the corners hold square. Leave the bay for the roller-shutter door in the entrance section, open windows on both sides so the interior gets light, and close the corners with white columns for extra depth. Build in rings rather than finishing one wall at a time, and check the footprint against the baseplate edges every couple of layers.

**Step 3: Add the fire-service details.** The garage holds a red fire engine (about 200 pieces). Fit warning lights and a water tank on the roof, and hang a fire ladder, a fire axe and a hose reel on the outside walls. Spell out "119" in white parts above the door header — 119 is the fire emergency number in China, and the red-and-white contrast makes the building instantly readable as a fire station from across the street. Treat these small exterior details as one batch; they do more for the theme than any amount of extra wall.

**Step 4: Interior and lighting to finish.** The ground floor takes the duty room and garage, the second floor a rest lounge, and the third floor a lookout. Pop in about 4 firefighter minifigures with walkie-talkies, fire extinguishers (3 to 4 small parts each) and similar accessories. If budget allows, a lighting kit adds about 80 yuan and looks far better switched on at night.

## FAQ

**Q: How many pieces does a brick fire station MOC need?**
A: Typically 800 to 1,200 pieces; around 1,000 is the sweet spot at street scale. Too few and the details feel thin, too many and beginners struggle — scale up or down with your budget and experience.

**Q: Can a beginner build a fire station MOC straight away?**
A: Yes. Follow the four stages — base, walls, details, interior — with instructions and bagged parts, and a beginner can finish in under 8 hours. It helps to build a small set first to get a feel for the parts before attempting a three-storey street building.

**Q: How is a fire station MOC different from an official set?**
A: Official sets follow fixed instructions; a MOC is entirely your own call, from floor count to colour scheme to interior. MOC parts come loose and you source them yourself, but the result is one of a kind and much easier to modify later.

**Q: What does a fire station street building cost to build?**
A: At 1,000 pieces and about 0.4 yuan per piece, materials run about 400 yuan; add roughly 100 yuan for the fire engine and minifigures, so about 500 yuan total. If you build from loose parts already in your stock, it can come in under 300 yuan.

**Q: Is a fire station MOC worth displaying?**
A: Yes. A red frontage stands out in any street layout, and the three-storey structure with garage and lookout displays well. At about 30 cm tall on a 32x32 footprint it does not eat much shelf space, and it holds its value as a collectible too.
