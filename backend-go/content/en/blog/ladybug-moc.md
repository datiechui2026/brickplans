# Ladybug MOC Review: A 320-Piece Bug You Can Build in an Hour

Bottom line first: this ladybug MOC goes for small and cute rather than big-display spectacle. It uses only about 320 parts, measures 12 cm long once finished, and captures the red-with-black-spots seven-spot ladybird look at roughly 92% accuracy. The instructions are a free download from brickplan.cn, the loose parts come to about CNY 68, and you can have it built in under an hour — which makes it a good fit for first-time MOC builders and for parents building alongside kids.

## 1. Appearance and accuracy

The red shell is put together from curved elements so it reads as a rounded half-dome rather than a faceted box. All seven black spots are printed pieces — moulded in one pass, no stickers to line up — so the surface keeps a clean gloss and doesn't look cheap up close. The head is a small black round piece fitted with two poseable antennae, and six small black legs are tucked underneath the shell, which is where most of the charm comes from. Overall resemblance to the real insect lands at around 92%.

## 2. Structural design

The shell is split into a left and a right half, joined at the base by hinge elements. That means it opens slightly the way a real ladybug lifts its wing cases, showing the body inside. The underside is closed off with plates, which keeps the centre of gravity low so it doesn't tip over on a desk. At 12 cm it sits neatly in one hand, so it works equally well as a shelf ornament or as something to fiddle with while you're thinking.

## 3. Build experience

A short parts count and short steps are the big advantages here. The whole model runs to just 5 stages, and no single step asks for more than 15 parts, so a beginner finishes inside an hour and an experienced builder wraps it up in about half an hour. Working from the PDF instructions on brickplan.cn, you rarely have to flip back and re-read a step, which is what makes it a good pick for short build sessions between other things.

## 4. Value for money

The instructions are free, and the loose parts run about CNY 68. Red and black are parts almost everyone already has in the drawer, and plenty of builders report getting the whole thing together for around CNY 40. That works out to roughly CNY 0.21 per piece, which makes it a handy filler to push an order over a free-shipping threshold, a cheap parent-and-child activity, or a small gift. For anyone stepping into MOC building for the first time, it's hard to beat on cost.

Taken as a whole, the ladybug delivers a complete building experience at a very low price and in very few steps. As a first MOC or as a family starter project, it does exactly what it needs to do.
