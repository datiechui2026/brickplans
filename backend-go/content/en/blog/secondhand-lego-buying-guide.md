# How to Buy Secondhand LEGO: Inspection, Pricing and Deals

Short answer first: secondhand LEGO is worth buying, and the going rate is usually 50%-70% of the original price — but you have to know how to inspect it. Four checks cover most of the risk: verify the set against its instruction manual, look for yellowed white bricks, examine minifigure printing, and confirm that no parts are counterfeit. Pay through a platform with buyer protection and your chances of getting burned drop sharply.

## 1. Channel Comparison: Where Your Money Goes Furthest

Four channels dominate the secondhand market: individual sellers on Xianyu, secondhand toy specialty stores, BrickLink, and offline flea markets.

- **Xianyu** has the largest volume and the lowest prices. In 2026, sets in the 300-800 piece range commonly sell for 50%-60% of retail. More listings also means more risk, so stick to escrow payment.
- **Specialty secondhand toy stores** cost roughly 10% more, but that premium buys you a 7-day return window.
- **BrickLink** is the place to fill gaps in a collection or complete a set; parts are sold in single-unit lots, so you buy exactly what you need.
- **Offline flea markets** let you inspect the set face to face before money changes hands, which makes them the safest option for beginners.

## 2. The Four-Step Inspection Checklist

Do these four things the moment the set arrives, before you confirm receipt.

1. **Check the manual against the set.** Compare the page count and the step numbering with what you actually received. A set missing pages or parts justifies an immediate 20% price cut.
2. **Inspect the white and light gray bricks.** These are the first to show age. If more than a third of them have yellowed, renegotiate from 70% of the asking price.
3. **Look at the minifigure printing.** Check for worn or flaking print on faces and torsos. Figures with fully intact printing justify a 10%-15% premium, so knowing this helps you judge whether an asking price is fair.
4. **Tap the parts and listen.** A dull, dead thud instead of a crisp click almost always means a clone-brand part rather than the real thing.

## 3. The Pricing Formula: Working Out a Fair Number Fast

The reference formula is simple: **fair price ≈ original price × condition multiplier.**

- New and sealed: 0.75
- 90% condition (used, like new): 0.6
- 70% condition (visible wear): 0.45

Worked example: a 2026 modular building with an original price of 899 yuan comes out to about 540 yuan at 90% condition. If the seller opens above 650 yuan, it is no longer a good deal. Retired, high-demand sets do not follow this formula — check the current market price for those separately before you commit.

## 4. Timing the Bargains and Hidden Value

Three windows are worth watching for a genuinely good find:

- **Around new releases**, when older sets drop 10%-15% as sellers clear space.
- **Year-end clearance**, when stores and private sellers both unload inventory.
- **Moving season**, when people sell in bulk because they have to shift everything quickly.

Also keep an eye on sellers who part out minifigures. Once the figures are sold, what is left is often a pile of loose bricks sold by the bag — sometimes as low as 20-40 yuan per bag. That is ideal for MOC builders who want to stock up on basic parts rather than a complete set.

## 5. Recommendation

Beginners should start with mid-size sets under 500 yuan on Xianyu and pair the purchase with an in-person handover in the same city, so you can inspect before paying. More advanced builders do better with a combination: BrickLink for specific parts, plus offline hunting for bulk bargains.

Whatever channel you use, hold two lines. First, always check the set against its instruction manual. Second, always pay through an escrow or buyer-protected transaction, and record an unboxing video. To confirm a set's original price and part count, look up the parts list on the BrickPlan site (https://brickplan.cn) and tick items off one by one during inspection.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the most reliable way to inspect secondhand LEGO?**

A: Four steps: match the parts against the manual, check how badly the white bricks have yellowed, examine the minifigure printing, and tap the parts to tell real from fake. Insist on a platform-escorted transaction and film the unboxing — if something is wrong, that footage makes a refund straightforward.

**Q: What discount should I expect to pay?**

A: The mainstream range is 50%-70% of the original price. New and sealed runs about 75%, 90% condition about 60%, and 70% condition about 45%. Retired, in-demand sets do not follow this and need a separate market price check.

**Q: What if the set is missing parts?**

A: Talk to the seller first. If more than 5% of the parts are missing, you can reasonably ask for a refund of around 20%. If you cannot agree, replacement parts can be ordered by part number from BrickLink or from the BrickPlan site (https://brickplan.cn); individual parts usually cost 1-5 yuan each.

**Q: Can you still find real bargains on Xianyu, and is it worth it?**

A: Yes. The easiest windows are just before a new release, at year end, and during moving season, when sets commonly go for 40%-50% below the original price. It is worth it, but only if you can inspect properly — beginners face the least risk with small sets and same-city handovers.
