# Chinese Wet Market MOC: 6,800-Part Street Scene Guide

The short version: this wet market MOC uses roughly 6,800 parts, measures 60 x 48 cm when finished, takes about 22 hours at 2 to 3 hours a day, and costs around CNY 1,100. The scene covers four functional zones — a vegetable stall, a meat counter, a seafood area and a cooked-food stall — with a central aisle wide enough to place minifigures, so the busy morning-shopping feel comes through. The instructions come from the brickplan platform, and the parts list is split by the four stall modules so you can buy and build in stages.

**Step 1: Build the base and the central aisle**

Start with a 32 x 24 stud baseplate. Lay a layer of dark gray bricks for the ground, leaving a 6-stud-wide strip down the middle as the main street, then build half-brick-high stall bases along both sides of that strip. Use beige pieces for corner posts at the four corners of the base, and run a drainage channel along the edge, using light gray plates to suggest the gaps between covers. This step uses about 1,400 parts and is the structural foundation of the whole scene — fix the baseplate joints with cross axles.

**Step 2: Build the vegetable stall and meat counter modules**

For the vegetable stall, build shelving in wood tones and fill it with small green and red pieces standing in for greens and tomatoes, then add a red-and-white striped parasol on top. The meat counter gets a wooden cutting board with dark red and light pink pieces built up as cuts of meat, and a row of meat hooks behind it held on clip pieces. Build both stalls as modules you can lift out in one piece, so you can swap their positions and recombine them. This step uses about 2,100 parts.

**Step 3: Build the seafood area and its canopy**

Use transparent blue plates for the water tank, set a few fish built from specialised fish-shaped pieces along the edge, and cap the module with a blue canopy shaped with a wave profile to echo the chilled cabinets of a real seafood counter. Support the canopy on columns and hang a row of small light bulb pieces under its edge for lighting. This is the visual centrepiece of the build and it is transparent-piece heavy, so press gently during assembly to avoid scratches. This step uses about 1,600 parts.

**Step 4: Signage and finishing details**

Finish by building a wooden sign above the aisle entrance, using printed pieces to spell out the morning market lettering, and hang red lantern pieces on either side. Put the cooked-food stall in the corner with a braised-food display cabinet and small steamer baskets, and scatter a few loose pieces on the ground to suggest dropped vegetable leaves. When everything is assembled, check your parts against the stall module list on the brickplan instruction page, then add shopper minifigures and a pushcart and you are done.

## FAQ

**Q: Roughly how much does this wet market MOC cost to build?**

A: About CNY 1,100 for the whole thing. Baseplates and basic wall bricks run about CNY 300, transparent pieces and special printed pieces about CNY 200, and the rest is ordinary bricks and decoration. You can buy in several batches following the brickplan list, which makes it easier to hit free-shipping thresholds.

**Q: How big is the finished model? Will it fit at home?**

A: The footprint is 60 x 48 cm and the height is about 20 cm, roughly the top of a small coffee table. A desk corner or a display cabinet works well, and 60 cm of width fits on an ordinary desk.

**Q: I have never built a street-scene MOC. Can I start with this one?**

A: Yes — the difficulty is medium-low. All four stalls are independent small modules, so building any one of them first works as practice. The one thing to watch is the baseplate foundation: follow the instructions exactly there so the four modules line up flush.

**Q: How is this different from an official street-scene set?**

A: Official street scenes lean toward ornate European facades. This wet market is a Chinese everyday-life scene, and its character comes from open stalls and small lived-in details. The parts are also considerably cheaper — a comparable official set of this scale usually costs two or three times as much.

**Q: Are the vegetable and meat shaped pieces easy to find?**

A: Yes. Small green and red round pieces and fish-shaped pieces are all common parts, and domestic suppliers such as Gobricks stock them. If you cannot find a particular mould, substitute a small piece in a close colour — it will not affect the overall look.
