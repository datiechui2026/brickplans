# Pokemon Brick MOC Blueprints: Pikachu to Starter Evolutions

Short answer first: judge Pokemon MOCs on accuracy and structure. Beginners should start with a single creature of around a hundred pieces, then move up to three-stage evolution lines and battle dioramas.

## Why Pokemon MOCs Stay So Popular

The Pokemon series has over a thousand creatures in wildly different shapes, which makes it a natural fit for brick building. From the round little Pikachu to the imposing Charizard, builders turn the idea of "catching" them into something you can hold in your hand, one brick at a time. **Pokemon brick MOC blueprints** are popular for two reasons: official sets only cover a limited roster of characters, and fan-designed plans can push a build much closer to the look of the animated characters. MOC means My Own Creation, a model designed by a builder rather than a set produced at scale, and its freedom and fidelity often go beyond what a retail set can do. Pokemon fans and brick builders overlap heavily, which keeps this theme thriving. Newcomers can start by getting familiar with how MOCs work over at the [brickplan blog](https://brickplan.cn/en/blog).

## Character Accuracy: From Pikachu to the Starter Trio

Accuracy is the first standard for judging a Pokemon brick MOC blueprint. Take Pikachu: a good plan reproduces the signature yellow body, the red cheeks and the jagged tail precisely. Charizard is a test of wing curvature and how the flame pieces are combined. Strong designers use ball-joint parts for a posable head and tail so the finished model can be displayed in different poses. On brickplan (https://brickplan.cn) you can find plans for Pikachu, Squirtle, Charmander, Bulbasaur and other high-profile creatures, with part counts ranging from around a hundred up to more than a thousand pieces, covering a spread of difficulty levels.

## Evolution Lines and Battle Scenes

More advanced builders tend to prefer the three-stage evolution theme in **Pokemon brick MOC blueprints**: the base form, first evolution and final form of the same creature built side by side, which makes for a very strong display. Battle scene plans usually come with a base built from rock, grass and water elements, so the creatures stand on an actual "battlefield" and read with more presence. These plans demand more from structural strength, and a good designer builds in internal supports to keep the model from becoming top-heavy. Beginners are better off starting with a single small creature and getting comfortable with ball joints and clip techniques before taking on a large diorama.

## Choosing Blueprints and Sourcing Parts

When picking a Pokemon brick MOC blueprint, watch three things. First, whether the part count matches the stated difficulty, since beginners should start with small creatures. Second, whether the preview images are clear from multiple angles, so you can see the side and back structure. Third, whether a complete parts list is included, which makes it easy to check your stock. Reading other builders' build reviews is an effective way to avoid plans with structural flaws. ## FAQ

**Q: How do I judge the accuracy of a Pokemon MOC blueprint?**

A: With Pikachu, a good plan reproduces the yellow body, red cheeks and jagged tail precisely; with Charizard, look at the wing curvature and how the flame pieces are combined. Quality work also uses ball-joint parts for a posable head and tail.

**Q: Which Pokemon should a beginner build first?**

A: Start with a single small creature, something in the hundred-piece range like Pikachu or Squirtle. Get comfortable with ball joints and clip techniques first, then take on a three-stage evolution line or a battle diorama.

**Q: Why are three-stage evolution blueprints more expensive and more difficult?**

A: Because they require the base form, first evolution and final form of the same creature to be built side by side, which doubles the part count. Battle scene plans also demand higher structural strength and need internal supports to avoid a top-heavy model.

**Q: What are the three things to check when choosing a Pokemon blueprint and its parts?**

A: First, whether the part count matches the stated difficulty, since beginners should start with small creatures. Second, whether the preview images show the side and back clearly from multiple angles. Third, whether a complete parts list is included so you can check your stock.
