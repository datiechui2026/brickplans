# How to Move a LEGO MOC Safely: Step-by-Step Packing Guide

The short answer first: the safest way to move a brick-built MOC is to photograph it, break it down layer by layer, then bag the parts by category. Moving a model as a whole piece is actually the fastest way to watch it come apart. According to experience shared by thousands of builders in the BrickPlan community, careless transport leads to part loosening in up to 70% of cases, while proper disassembly and packing drops the damage rate below 5%.

After spending hours on a MOC that runs to thousands of bricks, the move or exhibition trip is the part most builders dread. How you transport it decides whether it arrives intact.

**Step 1: Photograph the original structure.** Before you touch anything, take one photo each from the front, side, top and bottom with your phone so you can rebuild later. If the model has more than 3,000 parts, also record a 30-second walk-around video that captures the internal structure too.

**Step 2: Disassemble by module and layer.** Do not move the model in one piece. Remove the top decorations, minifigures and fragile sections first, then work down in the order of base, middle layer, top layer. Keep each layer as complete as possible to reduce wear from repeated rebuilding.

**Step 3: Sort small parts into bags.** Use resealable bags, grouped by colour and function. Ten to fifteen bags is enough for an average model; for a large build, prepare 20 or more. Label every bag with the layer it belongs to so reassembly is obvious at a glance.

**Step 4: Wrap large connected sections in cling film.** For assemblies you cannot easily take apart, wrap 2-3 turns of cling film and then cover with bubble wrap. Cling film keeps parts from shaking loose in transit — a low-cost, high-impact step.

**Step 5: Pack the box with cushioning.** Lay 5 cm of foam or old clothes in the bottom of the cardboard box, fill the gaps between layers with newspaper, and make sure nothing can shift inside. Before sealing, mark the outside "Fragile — This Side Up" so handlers know to be careful.

Following this process, a mid-size 5,000-part model takes about two hours for two people to pack. For a short same-city move you can simplify things, but the three core steps — photograph, disassemble, bag — should never be skipped. They are what keeps the model safe.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I move a brick MOC without it falling apart?**

A: The key is transporting it as disassembled parts rather than as one piece. Photograph it first, break it into several layers by module, bag the small parts by category, secure large assemblies with cling film, and fill the box with cushioning. That cuts the risk of collapse by more than ninety percent.

**Q: Can I move a large MOC in one piece?**

A: Small models under 300 parts can be moved whole. Once you pass 1,000 parts, it is not recommended. Vibration travels through the structure and loosens connection points; even with bubble wrap on the outside, interior parts can shift out of place.

**Q: What tools do I need to move a MOC?**

A: Resealable bags, cling film, bubble wrap, cardboard boxes and label paper are the essential five, and they cost under 30 yuan. If your model has a lot of parts, add a sorting tray — it noticeably speeds up packing. The whole kit can be reused.

**Q: Is long-distance shipping worth it for a brick MOC?**

A: If the model has 2,000 or more parts, or contains rare and discontinued parts, shipping is worth it. For an ordinary few-hundred-part practice build, take it apart and carry it instead. When shipping, choose the insured service; the fee runs about 1% to 3% of the declared value and buys peace of mind.

**Q: How do I rebuild the model quickly after moving?**

A: Work from the photos and labels, starting at the base and rebuilding layer by layer. The more detail you put on the labels, the faster it goes — a 3,000-part model typically takes 1 to 2 hours to reassemble, about 80% less time than building it from scratch.
