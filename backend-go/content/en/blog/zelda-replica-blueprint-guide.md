# Zelda MOC Blueprint Guide: Divine Beasts to Master Sword

Since *Tears of the Kingdom* launched, more and more builders have gone looking for high-quality **Zelda replica blueprints**, hoping to recreate the floating islands, Zonai devices and the four Divine Beasts in brick. Compared with official sets, a replica MOC lets you choose your own scale, colour scheme and display format. This guide shares a build approach that has been tested in practice, so you can skip the mistakes beginners usually make.

## Why Tears of the Kingdom Is So Well Suited to Replicas

The Zonai technology, sky islands and wing-suit paraglider added in *Tears of the Kingdom* give MOC builders a huge amount of fresh material. Zonai devices are mostly built from teal, grey and brass parts, so a small number of specialised pieces is enough to make a highly recognisable energy core. That is exactly why blueprints of this kind have multiplied over the past two years, ranging from miniature desk pieces to full-scale weapons — you can pick a starter project that matches the parts you already have in your bins.

## Comparing the Difficulty of the Four Divine Beasts

The Divine Beasts are the most visually striking machines in *Breath of the Wild*, and the target most replica builders want to take on. They are large, their surfaces are full of curves, and a frame that is even slightly off will fall apart. Here is the difficulty comparison I put together:

| Divine Beast | Theme colour | Core difficulty | Approximate part count |
|------|--------|----------|------------|
| Vah Ruta (elephant) | Blue-grey | Trunk joints and water-spout nozzle | About 1,600 pieces |
| Vah Rudania (lizard) | Lava red | Volcanic crater on the back and the tail | About 1,800 pieces |
| Vah Medoh (eagle) | Cream | Wing-spread structure and balance | About 1,400 pieces |
| Vah Naboris (camel) | Gold | Hump energy rings and four-leg centre of gravity | About 1,900 pieces |

Start with Vah Medoh, because its main body is symmetrical left to right — build the two halves and join them. Vah Naboris is a different story: its four legs need repeated centre-of-gravity adjustment, so build the internal frame from technic parts first and only then attach the curved outer shell. Otherwise the finished model tends to splay its legs apart.

## SNOT Techniques for the Master Sword and Hylian Shield

Weapon builds are the best practice pieces for anyone new to **Zelda replica blueprints**. The Master Sword's blade calls for SNOT (studs-not-on-top) work: stand smooth tiles on edge in a long, narrow row to form the blade, then add a slope brick along each side to create the cutting edges. For the guard, two turntable pieces locked face to face produce the signature hexagonal shape. The Hylian Shield's challenge is the emblem on its face — lay out the base image with 1x1 tiles first, layer dark blue and gold printed pieces on top, then finish the rim with transparent pieces to suggest metallic reflection. Displayed on a stand, the result really stands out.

## How to Choose Your First Blueprint

When you are picking **Zelda replica blueprints**, check three things first: the part count, whether a parts list is included, and whether step-by-step diagrams are provided. For a beginner, a blueprint under 500 pieces with a parts list is the safest bet; experienced builders can go straight for a large model such as one of the Divine Beasts. One warning: some blueprints on the market show only a render of the finished model with no building steps, leaving you to guess your way through. Approach those with caution.

Whichever blueprint you start from, you can find matching parts lists and high-resolution step-by-step instructions at [brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn), and the community has plenty of Hyrule-themed replica builds to reference for colour schemes and structure.
