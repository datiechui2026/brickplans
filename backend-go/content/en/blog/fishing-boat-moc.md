# Fishing Boat MOC Build Guide: Rounded Hull and Catch Details

Short version first: a fishing boat lives or dies on a rounded bow and a deck with a story. Close the bow by stepping 1x4 curved bricks outward half a stud per layer, back the hull with a Technic beam keel, then dress the deck with nets, a stern davit and a few fish.

A fishing boat is not a warship and not a yacht. It doesn't sell speed or swagger — it sells the feeling of a working life at sea. This MOC runs on two rules: the hull lines have to be round and easy on the eye, and the deck has to carry catch stories you can't finish telling. Below are the hull, the keel, the deck and the catch details, followed by the questions that come up most often.

## Hull Lines: Curved Bricks and SNOT

The biggest difference between a fishing boat hull and a speedboat hull is the bow. You don't want a sharp deep-V cutting the water; you want a rounded bow with a slight flare. Start at the keel line with 1x4 curved bricks and step each layer outward, offsetting by half a stud per layer. Four or five layers is enough to close a full, round bow curve. For the bulwarks on both sides, use SNOT (Studs Not On Top) and lay tiles sideways so the topsides read as one continuous smooth surface from bow to stern. The stern needs a slight upward sweep: shim two 1x2 hinge pieces to roughly a 3-stud rake and the fishing boat's signature raised transom appears immediately.

## Technic Beam Keel: Frame First, Skin Later

A beautiful hull shell is worthless if the structure won't hold. Build the keel from Technic first: run a single 1x15 Technic beam along the hull's centreline, lock both ends with cross axles into wedge pieces at the bow and stern, and add a frame every 6 studs across the beam, each made from 2x4 bricks and Technic pins. When you skin the hull, clip the curved bricks straight onto the frame studs and close the seams with 1x1 filler bricks. This "Technic skeleton plus cosmetic skin" approach means the whole boat won't come apart in your hand, and it leaves room to add a motor setup later.

## Deck and Cabin: Layering Matters

Deck layout is what gives a fishing boat its lived-in feel. Work in three levels: tile the lower deck fully in dark gray, leave space in the middle for the wheelhouse, then add a top level with a hatch that lifts off. Ring the wheelhouse with transparent pieces as windows, and put a steering wheel piece plus two 1x2 tiles inside as the instrument console. Build the roof searchlight from a transparent dome piece on a short cross axle so it swivels left and right. Don't forget the stanchions along both sides — fix 1x1 bricks with side studs in place, six per side at even spacing, and the boat finally looks like a proper fishing vessel.

## Nets and Catch: Give the Deck a Story

The soul of a fishing boat MOC is in the details. Simulate a net with 1x1 Technic bricks with holes plus flexible hose, coil it up at the stern, and build a retrievable stern davit from hinge pieces. Scatter a few fish across the deck: stack two layers of 1x2 slopes, add a 1x1 triangle piece at the tail, and mix in two or three dark blue transparent pieces to suggest the wet shine on a fish just out of the water — the colour contrast does the rest. For more life, add a catch tank: transparent pieces for the walls, a few 1x1 round plates inside as ice cubes. All of these are common parts; the whole boat needs no more than 50 extra pieces.

## FAQ

**Q: The curved pieces at the bow never line up. What do I do?**
A: Don't skip steps. Dry-fit the curved bricks along the keel line first to confirm the arc, then press each layer down in order. Step outward at half a stud per layer starting from the keel line, and four or five layers will close a full rounded bow.

**Q: Why does the middle of the hull sag?**
A: Usually the frames are spaced too far apart. Add a frame of 2x4 bricks and Technic pins every 6 studs across the hull, and add another one anywhere the gap exceeds 8 studs. When skinning, clip the curved bricks directly onto the frame studs — both the seams and the sagging will improve.

**Q: How do I build the net and the catch details?**
A: Use 1x1 Technic bricks with holes plus flexible hose for the net, coiled at the stern, and hinge pieces for a retrievable stern davit. For fish, stack two layers of 1x2 slopes with a 1x1 triangle piece, and mix in a few dark blue transparent pieces to read as seawater.

**Q: How should I plan the colour scheme?**
A: A white hull with red and blue waterline stripes works well, with a dark gray or wood-tone deck. A light-coloured rub rail along the topsides adds depth. For a moored-at-night look, add a transparent warm-tone light piece on the cabin roof.

The three questions that come up most: bow curved pieces that won't align — dry-fit first, then press, and don't skip layers; a sagging midsection — check frame spacing and add a frame beyond 8 studs; hinges that feel too loose — switch to friction hinges or reinforce them with Technic pins. On colour, go white hull with red and blue waterline stripes, a dark red bottom to suggest antifouling paint, and a wood-tone or dark gray deck. For a night scene, hide two transparent light bricks under the wheelhouse and deck; the warm glow coming through the windows photographs beautifully.
