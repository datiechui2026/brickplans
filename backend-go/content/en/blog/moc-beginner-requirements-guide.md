# What You Need to Start Building MOCs: Parts, Tools, Instructions

You want to build something of your own, but it always feels like one more thing is missing. "What do I actually need to start building MOCs?" is the first question nearly every new builder asks. The answer is less complicated than it looks: parts, tools, instructions, plus a bit of patience and the right mindset. Work through this list and you will have the basics covered without wasting money.

## What parts do you need for MOCs? Start by taking apart old sets

Sorting out your parts supply is the most concrete item on the list. Many newcomers immediately want to buy loose bricks by the box, but that is not necessary. The most cost-effective first step is to dismantle official sets you have already grown tired of, then sort them into bricks, plates, slopes and connectors. That is your first parts library. Once it has some scale, you can top it up on demand against the parts list of a specific set of instructions, buying only what you are missing rather than hoarding. It saves budget and gives your old sets a second life.

## What tools do you need? These few will last a long time

The tool list is usually overestimated too. At the start you do not need professional power tools, just three things: a brick separator for fixing mistakes, a baseplate for planning your foundation structure, and a set of sorting boxes for managing parts. Choose a brick separator with a tight, secure grip so it takes less effort and does not damage parts; start with a large baseplate so you have room to lay out the skeleton of a small model. Once you go deeper, adding a plate press or sanding tools can wait, and there is plenty of time for it.

## What instructions do you need? Good instructions are the starting point

Without a direction from instructions, many people just stare at a pile of parts, which is why instructions belong on the list. For newcomers, recreating a proven design is easier than designing from scratch and builds your feel for the hobby faster. Instructions come from several channels: official instruction libraries, shares from player communities, and dedicated instruction communities. When choosing, look for clear steps and a complete parts list so you can check and supplement your supply. BrickPlans (https://brickplan.cn) is a community for sharing brick instructions, with plenty of beginner-level builds and original creations that work well for practice or inspiration.

## What budget and mindset do you need? Small steps, fast iterations

Budget and mindset matter just as much. Set yourself a monthly budget you can comfortably afford, start with small and mid-sized models, and spend on commonly used basic parts rather than chasing rare or obscure ones. On mindset, accept that your first build will not be perfect: taking it apart to start again, or tearing down half a build, is normal. Do not mistake collecting instructions for creating; finishing one small model gives you far more satisfaction than hoarding a hundred instruction files.

## The action checklist: just follow it

Put the earlier points into an action list and the question has its answer. First, dismantle idle sets to build a parts library. Second, get a brick separator, a baseplate and sorting boxes. Third, pick a beginner-friendly set of instructions on BrickPlans (https://brickplan.cn). Fourth, set a budget and start with a small scene or small building. Fifth, publish what you finish and exchange feedback with fellow builders. Five steps and your MOC journey is officially under way.

In the end, starting MOCs is not about how expensive your gear is. It is about having enough parts, tools that feel right in your hand, clear instructions, and a willingness to actually build. Put the basics together with this list and your first brick MOC can begin this weekend.
