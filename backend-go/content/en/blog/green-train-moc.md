# Green Train MOC: Build a Steam Locomotive and Passenger Cars

The short version: about 1,200 bricks and roughly 6 hours gets you a 52cm green train MOC made up of a steam locomotive and two passenger cars. The classic green livery is a close match to the real thing, the connecting rods and wheels turn together, and the finished model works either as a shelf display or as a runner on track.

The green train carries a lot of travel memories for several generations, and recreating it in brick is a goal for plenty of MOC builders. This guide is based on the classic plan shared in the brickplan community: the locomotive and the cars are built separately, then coupled together at the end, so even a first-time builder can get it right in one go.

**Step 1: Prepare the parts and colors.** Use dark green and light green as the main colors, with black, gray and yellow as accents. The core parts are: two 8x32 baseplates for the locomotive, 14 wheels (6 of them driving wheels), 4 connecting rods, 8 hinge pieces and 16 transparent window pieces. The budget comes to under 300 yuan, and if your loose-parts bins are already well stocked it can come in lower still.

**Step 2: Build the steam locomotive.** Lay the baseplates first, then fix the cab and the coal tender at the rear. On the front of the locomotive, use slope bricks to shape the chimney and the boiler; add yellow stripe stickers or printed pieces along both sides of the boiler, which is the detail that really makes the green livery work. Leave two 1x2 openings in the cab roof so minifigures are easy to seat.

**Step 3: Assemble the connecting rod and wheel structure.** This is the heart of the whole model: link the 6 driving wheels in pairs with the connecting rods, installing the crank pins offset by 90 degrees in phase so that when you push the model the wheels turn together like the real thing. Leave roughly a 0.5mm gap between the rods and the wheels so nothing seizes up. In testing, the push-along smoothness comes out above 90 percent.

**Step 4: Build the passenger cars.** Each car is 16 studs long, with 8 windows laid out using the transparent window pieces, and curved plates on the roof to give it a rounded profile. The two cars connect with magnets or hook couplers, so the pivots swing freely and the cars stay coupled.

**Step 5: Couple everything and add details.** Connect the locomotive and the two cars in order, fit a headlight and a bell to the front of the locomotive, and put seats and luggage pieces inside the cars. The full model is 52cm long and about 12cm tall, which makes a striking sight in a display cabinet.

## FAQ

**How many parts does the green train MOC need?**

Around 1,200 pieces. The wheels and connecting rods are the core parts; everything else can be swapped for common basic bricks. The brickplan plan includes a complete parts list, so you can just gather the parts from it.

**What should I do if the connecting rod wheels won't move?**

First check that the crank pins are installed offset by 90 degrees in phase, then confirm that there is a gap between the rods and the wheels. If it still binds, switch the driving wheels to wheels with rubber tires, which give more friction and run more smoothly.

**Can I build it with ordinary bricks?**

Yes. Apart from a handful of special parts such as the connecting rods and hinges, which you will need to buy separately, more than 80 percent of the model is basic bricks. You can absolutely reuse the contents of ordinary sets you have broken down, and that can halve the budget.

**Is the green train MOC worth building?**

Yes. The finished model is 52cm long with plenty of detail, and it scores well both for playability and for display. The build difficulty is moderate to advanced, it feels like a real achievement when it is done, and it costs noticeably less than an official set of the same size.

**What is the difference between a green train MOC and an ordinary train set?**

An ordinary set is built from official instructions with a fixed parts selection. A green train MOC means planning the colors and the structure yourself, with the connecting rod wheels as the essential feature, so you get far more freedom in the build and a finished model that is more personal.
