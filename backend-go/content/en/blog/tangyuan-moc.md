# Tangyuan MOC Build Guide: 120-Piece Lantern Festival Bowl

Here is the short version: this tangyuan MOC uses about 120 parts, stands 6 cm tall and takes roughly 30 minutes to finish. The round rice-ball shape sits in a bowl-shaped base, so it doubles as a holiday centerpiece for your desk. brickplan.cn supplies the full building diagram and parts list, so you can follow along with zero guesswork.

## Step 1: Gather Your Parts and Tools

Start by checking your stock against the brickplan.cn diagram. You need roughly 40 white 2x2 curved slope bricks for the tangyuan body, 30 beige 1x2 bricks for the bowl wall, 15 dark red bricks and 15 gold bricks for the bowl rim decoration, plus one baseplate and one tray plate. That comes to about 120 parts in total. Sort them by color before you start, and you will build roughly twice as fast.

A few practical notes before the first brick goes down. The curved slopes are the only parts that really matter for the final look, so check that none are scratched or dusty, since a scuffed slope shows up clearly on a smooth sphere. Keep the gold bricks in a separate pile, because you only need four of them for the rim and it is easy to lose them among the red ones. A small tray or a shallow dish works well for holding each color group, and it also stops round 1x1 parts from rolling off the table mid-build.

## Step 2: Build the Tangyuan Body

The key to making the tangyuan look naturally round is how you arrange the curved slope bricks. Begin on a 2x2 base plate. The first ring uses 8 curved slope bricks closed into a circle. The second ring stacks 8 more bricks offset from the first, so the seams do not line up. Keep adding rings upward and draw each ring in by 1 to 2 bricks. At the top, close the shape with a single 1x1 round dot brick.

Built this way, the tangyuan measures about 5 cm across and the surface has almost no visible gaps, so it feels smooth and round in the hand. The offset stacking is what does the work here: each ring bridges the joints of the ring below it, which locks the sphere together and hides the seams. If a ring feels loose, press down firmly around the whole circumference rather than pushing on one spot, and the ring will seat evenly.

## Step 3: Build the Bowl Base

The bowl wall is built with beige bricks in a slanted profile, working from the bottom up. Lay a 6x6 baseplate first, then place 12 bricks in the first layer, 14 in the second layer and 16 in the third, expanding outward layer by layer to form the bowl opening. Ring the rim with dark red bricks all the way around, then set 4 gold bricks at intervals as accents, which reads like a pattern around the edge of the bowl. Finally, set the finished tangyuan gently in the center of the bowl opening and put a tray plate underneath as the footing. The whole assembly comes out at exactly 6 cm tall.

Because the wall widens with every layer, the bowl holds its shape without any internal support, and the tray plate keeps the base flat on the shelf. Check the rim ring before you add the gold accents: the red bricks should sit flush and level all the way around, since any high spot will be obvious once the gold pieces go in.

## Step 4: Details and Display

If you have spare parts left over, add 2 to 3 small tangyuan beside the big one, or use transparent bricks to suggest rising steam. The finished piece looks good in an entryway, on a desk or on a TV cabinet. Building one on the day of the Lantern Festival makes a more personal decoration than anything store-bought, and it photographs well for sharing online.
