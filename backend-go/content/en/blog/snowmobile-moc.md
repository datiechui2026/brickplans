# Brick Snowmobile MOC: Tracked Chassis & Ski Build Tutorial

The short version: this snowmobile MOC uses roughly 480 pieces, takes about 2 hours to build, and measures around 24 cm long by 14 cm high. It runs on rear tracks with front ski steering, and sits on a white snow base — push it across a desk and it rolls, or park it in a winter street scene as a centrepiece. The instructions come from brickplan.cn.

## Step 1: The Tracked Chassis

The tracks are the heart of a snowmobile, and they account for about 200 of the pieces. Build two track modules, each 6 studs wide and 18 studs long, with a geared drive wheel and idler wheels supporting them from the inside. Curved track links handle the corners, closing the loop into a continuous belt.

A technic axle runs through the middle of the chassis, connecting the left and right sides, and a pair of gears keeps the two tracks turning in sync. Push the model and both tracks travel at the same speed without drifting off line — it rolls smoothly on a desk or a sheet of cardboard. Take your time lining up the teeth here; a single misaligned link is what makes a track jump or bind later.

## Step 2: Body and Cab

The body takes about 180 pieces. Blue and white bricks and plates build up a streamlined shell, with transparent parts standing in for the insulated windshield, and there is room inside the cab for one seated minifigure. Behind the seat, add a luggage rack — it holds a ski or a small backpack and gives the whole thing a proper "heading out for the winter" story.

Four studs lock the body onto the chassis. That is deliberate: pop the body off and you can reach the tracks for cleaning, tension checks or a gearing swap without rebuilding the shell.

## Step 3: Skis and the Snow Scene

Two dark ski pieces at the front act as the steering runners. They connect to the nose through ball joints, so they swing freely as you turn the model. For the base, about 100 white plates build rolling, uneven snow, dotted with a few small white pine trees and a frozen lake surface. Park the snowmobile on the crest of a slope and the sense of motion appears on its own.

## Parts List and Modification Tips

- Main colours: about 200 white pieces, 120 blue, 100 dark grey, 30 transparent, plus decorative parts.
- Budget: around 120 to 160 yuan for 480 loose pieces. White bricks dominate the count, so buying white by weight works out cheaper.
- Modifications: adding a motor and gearbox makes it drive under power; lengthening the tracks by two studs gives it a more off-road stance.

## FAQ

**Q: How many pieces does this snowmobile MOC need?**

A: The base version is about 480 pieces — roughly 200 for the tracks, 180 for the body and 100 for the snow base. Loose parts cost around 120 to 160 yuan, and any shortfall can usually be topped up from bricks you already have at home.

**Q: How long does it take to build?**

A: About 2 hours for a beginner, around 1 hour for an experienced builder. The track section needs careful tooth alignment, so build the chassis before the body and follow the instructions in their three stages. Difficulty is moderate throughout, and it suits builders aged 10 and up.

**Q: Can the snowmobile actually drive?**

A: The pure brick version rolls when you push it, with both tracks turning in sync. Add a motor and gearbox and it drives under power, and you can fit a remote-control module too for more play value — the conversion costs around 50 yuan.

**Q: How is a snowmobile different from a regular off-road car MOC?**

A: A snowmobile steers with tracks plus front skis, which spreads the contact area and suits soft ground like snow or sand. An off-road car uses large tyres and suspension and is built for rough terrain. The two structures are completely different.
