# Fire Station MOC Review: Garage Door, Fire Truck and Watchtower

A fire station is the most dynamic building on any modular street. The red and white color scheme, the roll-up garage door and a fire truck waiting on standby make it a scene that tells its own story. I picked up a **fire station brick MOC** plan on BrickPlans (brickplan.cn) and built it to test the parts that matter most: the garage door, the fire truck, the watchtower and the training pole. Here is whether this plan really delivers a station that looks ready to roll out at any moment.

## 1. The Garage Door: It Opens and Closes, and That Is the Biggest Win

The garage door sets the first impression of the whole fire station, and it is the strongest part of this plan. Instead of a flat panel glued to the wall, the designer built a **working roll-up door** from slide rails and hinged plates. The door panels are hinged in sections, so you push it up and it tucks into a channel under the garage ceiling. A minifigure can drive the fire truck in and out smoothly.

After several dozen open-and-close cycles in testing, there was no binding and the structure held up well. One thing to watch: add a few extra reinforcement pieces on both sides of the door frame. Otherwise, after repeated use, the door channel works slightly loose.

## 2. The Fire Truck: High Accuracy, the Soul of the Plan

The soul of a fire station is the **fire truck**, and this plan puts real effort into it. The body uses a red and white scheme, and the front end follows the standard fire engine shape. A row of water pipes runs along the roof, built from bent tube pieces with nozzle tips, the sides are finished with fire department badges, and the rear carries a rotating ladder. The ladder is made from multiple jointed sections that both raise and extend, so it is genuinely fun to handle.

The interior of the truck body leaves room for extinguishers and a hose reel. Detail accuracy sits in the upper-middle range for plans of this type, and the result is a fire engine you would be happy to display.

## 3. The Watchtower: Great Layering, but Tall

The watchtower is the high point of the station. The tower body narrows as it rises from one side of the garage, and the top level is an open observation deck ringed with railing pieces and capped by a red warning light. From up there you can look out over the whole street, which makes for a great photo angle and adds obvious vertical layering to the build.

The catch is that the tower is a bit taller than the usual height shared by modular street buildings. If it sits next to a single-story shop, it will look out of place. Pair it with two- or three-story neighbors to balance the skyline of the street.

## 4. The Training Pole: A Small Mechanism That Adds Real Fun

The second-floor dormitory area includes the classic **training pole**, running from the second-floor floor down to the ground-floor garage. A thin round rod passes through both levels, held at the top by clip pieces and resting on the floor at the bottom. Park a minifigure on the pole and you have the standard emergency call-out pose. Combined with the garage door and the fire truck, it lets you recreate the full roll-out sequence. This plan clearly invests in playability.

The small letdown is that the area around the pole lacks the little handrails that would add realism. Builders who care about that detail can add two short bar sections themselves.

## 5. Overall Verdict and Buying Advice

Taken together, the selling points of this **fire station brick MOC** plan are clear: the working garage door, the accurate fire truck and the training pole mechanism all lift it above the average street plan. The parts list is sorted clearly by color, which makes sourcing missing pieces easy.

Points come off for the watchtower being tall enough to disrupt the neatness of the street, and for the thin detailing around the pole. If you build modular streets and you are missing a public building with a story, this fire station is worth picking up. Add a spare fire truck and a couple of firefighter minifigures and the call-out scene comes alive immediately.
