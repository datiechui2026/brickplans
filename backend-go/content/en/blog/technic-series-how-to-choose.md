# How to Choose a Technic Set: Gears, Motors and Flagships

The short answer: when you pick a technic set, three things matter most — how smoothly the gear train runs, whether the motor setup delivers enough power, and whether the part count suits your experience. Beginners should stay under 500 pieces, intermediate builders should look at powered sets with two or more motors, and experienced builders can go straight for a 2000+ piece flagship.

**Step 1: Inspect the gear transmission system first**

Transmission is the soul of a technic model. Turn the exposed gears or roll the wheels and feel whether the movement is smooth or whether it catches and jams. Then check the parts list in the instructions for high-value transmission pieces: a differential lets the driven wheels turn at different speeds in a corner, a worm gear gives you a big reduction in a small space, and a universal joint carries drive through an angle. BrickPlan technic sets list the number of gear types on the product page — eight or more types is generally the threshold for a genuinely rich transmission.

**Step 2: Confirm the motor and power configuration**

For a powered set, look at the number of motors, the motor types and the battery box design. Beginners do fine with a single L motor. If you want remote control, choose a combination with two motors plus a steering servo, so drive and steering work independently. Check whether lithium rechargeable batteries are supported: a standard box of six AA batteries runs for about 2-3 hours, while a lithium pack will run for more than 4 hours.

**Step 3: Match the scale to your budget and use case**

Under CNY 500, pick a small 300-800 piece set to practice on. Between CNY 500 and CNY 1500, look at mid-size sets of around 1000 pieces. Above CNY 1500, you can consider a 2000+ piece flagship — BrickPlan's supercar flagship, for example — where build time is typically over 20 hours, which suits patient builders.

**Step 4: Check compatibility and part quality**

Confirm that pins and gears are compatible with mainstream brands so you can expand your collection later. Look at whether the instructions state tolerances; an axle-to-hole fit that is too tight will grind the gears over time. BrickPlan uses ABS throughout, with a gear module of 0.5, compatible with mainstream technic systems on the market, so mixing parts is no problem.

**Step 5: Look at after-sales support and parts replacement**

Technic parts are small and easy to lose, so check whether individual replacement parts are available. That is especially friendly for new builders — and a brand willing to send out spare parts usually has better quality control behind it.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the difference between a technic set and ordinary bricks?**

A: Technic sets are built around gears, axles and pins, and can produce real mechanical transmission and movement, whereas ordinary bricks are mainly for static building. Technic parts are more precise and usually more expensive, and they suit builders who enjoy studying structure and power.

**Q: How many pieces should a first technic set have?**

A: Start with a small set of 300-500 pieces. Build time is around 4-6 hours — enough to experience gear transmission properly without being put off. Buying a 2000+ piece flagship straight away often ends with the box gathering dust, so starting small and scaling up is the safer route.

**Q: Are technic motors interchangeable?**

A: BrickPlan motors use the standard PF connector and are broadly compatible with mainstream technic motors. Before buying, confirm that the connector type and voltage match — mixing motors of different voltages can burn out the mainboard, which is not a risk worth taking.

**Q: What should I do if the gears keep slipping?**

A: First check that the gears mesh properly and that the axles are pushed all the way in, then check whether the model is overloaded — an overly heavy body, for example. You can simplify the linkage, or add a small amount of dedicated lubricating grease between the gears, but keep it away from friction pins.

**Q: Are flagship sets worth buying?**

A: If your budget allows and you already have building experience, a flagship is well worth it. BrickPlan's 2000+ piece flagships typically include four or more motors and a complex gearbox, and the build itself works as an advanced lesson in mechanical knowledge.
