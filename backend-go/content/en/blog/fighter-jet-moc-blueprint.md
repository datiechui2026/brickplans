# J-20 vs F-22 vs Su-57: Brick Fighter Jet MOC Blueprints Compared

As a builder who follows both military models and brick models, I have assembled more than a dozen fighter jet MOC blueprints over the years. Fighter jets are the hardest military subject to capture in brick form: the silhouette depends on small changes in angle, and one wrong curve is enough to break the look. This review looks at three popular brick fighter jet MOC blueprints — the J-20, F-22 and Su-57 — from a builder's perspective, comparing accuracy, difficulty and the finished result.

## J-20: Canards and Lifting-Body Character

I have built three J-20 blueprints, and the canards and nose are the hardest parts to get right. A strong brick fighter jet MOC blueprint keeps the canards flat with a slight upward angle and narrows the nose step by step with slope bricks; that is how the model captures the sharp character of the 'Vigorous Dragon.' The weak points are just as clear. The canard joints are tight, so the model often needs to be pressed back together when you move or pose it. The part count is around 1,500 pieces, which is enough for a beginner to lose momentum halfway through. Those same features are where a weaker blueprint falls apart: if the canard angle is too steep, or the nose narrows in one big step instead of several small ones, the model reads as a generic delta-wing jet rather than a J-20. The tight canard joints are a practical annoyance rather than a design flaw, but they are worth knowing about before you start.

## F-22: The Standard Answer for Diamond Wings

The F-22's diamond-shaped main wings are highly recognizable, which is actually good news for a blueprint: large trapezoid plates can establish the outline without a pile of tiny angled pieces. The blueprint I have stands out for accurate proportions. The cockpit uses transparent parts to make a teardrop canopy, and the exhaust nozzles are layered with curved bricks, so the finished model is very displayable. Difficulty is medium at around 1,200 pieces. The only complaint is that the underside intakes are hollow inside and betray the model from the side view. I suggest adding two reinforcement beams yourself; it is a small fix that makes the model look more solid.

## Su-57: The Advanced Pick with a Complex Shape

The Su-57 blueprint is the hardest and most surprising of the three. It can reproduce the movable tail surfaces, conformal fuel tanks and tandem weapon bays, and the compound sweep angle looks very good from above. However, this blueprint is demanding about parts: special pieces alone make up 20 percent of the total, and assembly takes more than ten hours, so the value for money is average. That complexity is exactly why the finished model is rewarding, but it is also why this is not a first project. Without solid military-model experience, build the first two as practice.
