# Baseball Field MOC Build Guide: 3,200 Pieces in 5 Steps

Here is the short version first: this guide walks you through building a brick baseball field about 3,200 pieces strong, measuring 56 cm long and 48 cm wide, for a budget of roughly CNY 700 and about 9 hours of building time. The challenge here is not the piece count — it is controlling proportions across the red-dirt infield, the fan-shaped outfield and the curved grandstand. Follow the five steps below and even a first-time sports-scene builder can turn out a recognisable ballpark. Instructions are available on brickplan.cn, free or at low cost.

## Step 1: Baseplate Planning and Zone Marking

Start by fixing a standard 48 x 56 baseplate in place, then run 1x1 tiles along the diagonal to draw the dividing line between infield and outfield. For the red-dirt infield, lay dark red bricks into a square roughly 14 studs on a side; the outfield gets filled with grass green bricks across the fan-shaped area. The critical move here is outlining the borders with light-coloured bricks before you fill anything in, which saves you from tearing work apart later. Budget about 40 minutes for this stage.

## Step 2: Building the Infield and the Bases

The infield is the heart of the model. Position the four bases — home, first, second and third — with white 2x2 tiles, keeping them 6 studs apart so the proportions stay believable. Raise the pitcher's mound two bricks using a brown round plate, and enclose the catcher's area behind home plate with curved slope bricks. A good working order: build the centre axis from second base to home plate first, then lay both sides symmetrically. That way the infield will not drift out of alignment.

## Step 3: Outfield and the Home Run Wall

Cover the outfield with a large field of grass green bricks. At the home run wall position, stand up a row of dark green fencing 4 bricks high and cap it with white 1x1 bricks as boundary markers. Let both ends of the outfield taper naturally into the fan curve, using curved slope bricks at the corners. Outside the home run wall you can add a ring of advertising sticker tiles for extra realism. This stretch is the most time-consuming part of the whole scene, at roughly 2 hours.

## Step 4: Grandstand and Dugouts

Build the main grandstand along the outside of first base and third base, using stepped bricks to rise 4 levels, with seats alternating red and white to suggest a crowd. Behind home plate, add a two-level main grandstand plus a scoreboard — a black baseplate finished with white printed bricks for the innings and the score. Put the two teams' dugouts on the first base and third base sides, simply enclosed with low walls, and fill in the detail with minifigures.

## Step 5: Light Towers and Final Checks

Stand four light towers at the boundary between infield and outfield, each about 18 bricks tall, topped with transparent round bricks to simulate floodlights. For the wrap-up, check three things: that base spacing is even, that the grandstand steps line up, and that the baseplate sits fully flush. Adjust anything that looks off as you go. When everything is assembled, run through the parts list once more — if something is missing, the comment section on the brickplan platform is where fellow builders share substitution solutions.

## FAQ

**Q: Roughly how many pieces does this baseball field MOC need?**

A: The full version runs about 3,200 pieces, more than half of them in the green family. A simplified version with just the infield and the home run wall comes in at around 1,800 pieces, which suits builders on a tighter budget.

**Q: What does the whole thing cost?**

A: At the typical loose-part price on brickplan of CNY 0.20 each, 3,200 pieces comes to about CNY 640, plus roughly CNY 30-50 for the instructions, for a total budget near CNY 700. If you already have a deep green parts bin at home, you can shave a noticeable amount off that figure.

**Q: How long does the build take?**

A: About 9 hours for a beginner start to finish. Splitting it over five evenings of roughly 2 hours each keeps it relaxing. Experienced builders can compress it to under 6 hours.

**Q: Can this ballpark sit next to modular street buildings?**

A: It is a flat-laid scene, so it takes up no vertical space and works well placed in front of a modular building's plaza or in a park zone stylistically. Some builders also add a ring of railings and an entrance gate specifically for that setup.
