# Brick Bookstore MOC Build Guide: 2,750 Parts in 5 Steps

The finished model: a two-story brick bookstore roughly 2,750 parts, 25 cm long, 16 cm wide and 28 cm tall, with a budget around 560 yuan and about 8 hours of building time. What makes a bookstore MOC work is atmosphere — warm wood-tone shelving, a floor-to-ceiling window with a reading nook, and soft interior lighting. Skip any of those three and the building reads as an office. Follow the five stages below and you end up with a shop that lines up cleanly with other street-scene modules on the brickplan platform.

## Step 1: Lay the foundation and first-floor frame

Start with a standard 16x32 baseplate. Build the foundation out of dark gray bricks and raise all four corners by 2 studs to leave room for the door posts. The priority on the ground floor is the front window: assemble a 10-stud-wide display window from transparent glass bricks, edge the frame in dark brown, and cap it with a dark green awning so the building reads as a bookstore at a glance. Don't rush into the walls after the foundation — lay the first-floor floor tiles first, then build upward. It saves a lot of rework later.

## Step 2: Build the wooden shelves and the books

Shelving is the star of this build. Use dark brown bricks for the uprights and brown plates for each shelf board, and make 3 double-sided shelf units, each 6 studs tall and 4 studs wide. For the books, insert 1x1 colored bricks sideways into the shelf gaps, alternating red, yellow, blue and green — one shelf unit holds around 40 "books." If you want a cleaner look, use printed bricks for the spines. This stage takes about 2 hours. Build more shelving than you think you need: 2 units on the ground floor and 3 on the upper floor is what makes the interior feel full.

## Step 3: Add the reading nook and the checkout counter

Put the reading nook just inside the display window. Start with a 4x4 light-colored rug plate, then add a small brown round table, two chairs and a floor lamp with a transparent round brick as the shade. Place the checkout counter beside the door: a cream-white counter with a small cash register and a coffee cup on top. A potted plant on the corner completes the relaxed feel.

## Step 4: Build the upper floor and facade details

Run the second-floor walls straight up from the first floor. Put two arched windows on the front, with a small balcony and railing outside them. Inside, make the upper level a reading area plus a drinks bar, using dark brown and white bricks to suggest a marble pattern on the counter. Use curved bricks to soften the building's outer corners, put a "BOOKS" sign above the door in printed brick or sticker form, and hang a warm street lamp under the awning — the facade suddenly looks finished.

## Step 5: Interior details and lighting

This last pass is all about atmosphere. Hide a lighting kit in the ceiling cavity so warm yellow light filters down through transparent bricks; the store immediately feels like a shop about to close at dusk. Add a small chalkboard sign at the entrance reading "Today's Picks" and set two small succulent pots on the windowsill. When everything is in place, check the parts list for shortages and test that the doors and windows open and close and the shelf boards sit level — then slot the bookstore into your street.

## FAQ

**Q: How many parts does this bookstore MOC need?**

A: About 2,750 for the full version, roughly one third of them in brown tones. If you only build the ground floor as a corner shop for a street scene, around 1,600 parts will do, which suits builders on a tighter budget.

**Q: What does the whole thing cost?**

A: At the typical brickplan average of 0.20 yuan per loose part, 2,750 parts comes to about 550 yuan. Add roughly 30 yuan for the lighting kit, so budget around 560-600 yuan total. If you already have a deep stock of brown parts, you'll save a fair amount.

**Q: How long does the build take?**

A: About 8 hours for a beginner. Split it across three evenings: frame and shelving on day one, reading nook and facade on day two, interior details and lighting on day three.

**Q: Can it connect to standard street-scene modules?**

A: Yes. The base is a standard 16x32 baseplate at 28 cm tall, matching the module spec of other street-scene MOCs on brickplan, so it drops straight into a row of buildings. It sits particularly well between a cafe and a flower shop.
