# How to Choose a City Brick Set: LEGO vs SEMBO and Qiji

City-themed sets are the most familiar subject in any home build: fire stations, police stations, cafes, small street-corner shops. They are full of everyday life while you build, and they double as display pieces once finished. But the shelves are crowded with city brick sets, and LEGO City, SEMBO, Qiji and Gudi all have their loyalists. So how do you actually choose? This comparison breaks the decision down along the dimensions that matter.

## Part Accuracy and Clutch Feel

City sets usually mean high part counts and a lot of repetitive building, so part accuracy has a direct effect on the experience. In testing, LEGO City has the most consistent tolerances. Connections are smooth, go together without forcing, and do not leave your fingers sore after a long session. Mid- and high-end series from SEMBO and Qiji have improved noticeably in recent years and now sit very close to LEGO, which matters most on large builds where a hundred slightly-off connections add up.

Entry-level price tiers still occasionally produce connections that are too tight, or parts with rough edges, so those builds take hand strength and patience. If the set is mainly for a child, favor a brand with a looser clutch that is easy to take apart and rebuild, since kids tend to dismantle and re-assemble far more often than adults do.

## Scene Fidelity and Building Detail

The main draw of a city set is how well it recreates a scene. LEGO City is strong on interiors: a cafe has a bar counter and an oven, a police station has desks and monitor screens, and those details hold up to close inspection. Domestic high-end lines, such as the SEMBO street scene series, are equally impressive on exterior architecture and color schemes, with a much lower price attached.

When shopping, look closely at the interior space shown in the official photos. Plenty of sets have a flashy facade and an empty shell inside, which cuts the real play value sharply. A set with a furnished ground floor and a believable second floor will keep giving you something to notice, while a hollow one is finished the moment the walls go up.

## Minifigures and Accessories

Minifigures are the soul of a city set. A good city brick set should include figures in different occupations and different outfits, plus vehicles, signage, plants and other supporting pieces that make the street read as a place where people live. In testing, LEGO's figure printing and facial expressions were the most refined, while domestic brands often pack in more figures per set, which suits anyone who wants to populate a whole town at once.

One useful note: figure sizes are broadly compatible across brands, so mixing them is not a problem. That makes it easy to fill out a street with extra characters without worrying about which brand supplied them.

## Expansion and Value

If you plan to build a complete city, expansion matters: whether different sets share the same baseplate and can be joined into one continuous street. LEGO City and most domestic street scenes use standard baseplates, so connecting them is straightforward, and a mixed collection still lines up.

On value, domestic sets typically cost half as much as LEGO or less, which suits builders laying out large scenes where the total parts count matters more than the last five percent of finish. If your budget is comfortable and you want the finest detail and long-term collectibility, go with LEGO.

## Verdict and Recommendations

Put simply: choose LEGO City for detail and collectibility, and choose domestic street scenes from SEMBO or Qiji for value and scale. The two are compatible, so you can mix them into a single town rather than committing to one camp.

Before buying, decide whether the set is for a child to play with or for you to build a display scene. Then screen the candidates against scene fidelity, minifigure count and expansion potential, in that order. That is usually enough to avoid a bad purchase and to end up with a street that keeps growing.
