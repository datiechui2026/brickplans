# LEGO MOC Exhibitions: A Complete Guide to Visiting and Exhibiting

If instruction manuals and parts bins are the everyday side of MOC (My Own Creation) building, then a **LEGO MOC exhibition** is the end-of-year exam and the party rolled into one. At a live show you can stand in front of epic builds that run into the tens of thousands of pieces, and at the same time meet people who also lose sleep over a stubborn structural problem. Plenty of builders walk out of their first show with a new goal: bring something of my own next time. This guide covers both halves of that plan — how to visit, and how to exhibit.

## What a LEGO MOC exhibition actually is

A MOC exhibition is an offline event organised by brick fans, with original fan-built models as the main attraction. It is nothing like the displays in shopping malls where official sets are lined up on a shelf — every model here sits on weeks or even months of planning and building by a real person.

The main formats you will run into:

| Type | Characteristics | Typical form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AFOL convention | A gathering of adult fans of LEGO, high build quality, strong social atmosphere | AFOL conventions, fan meetups |
| Brick festival | Large mixed-format show, often tied in with commercial brands | Brick festivals and similar events |
| Themed exhibition | Built around one IP or subject area (modular buildings, military, sci-fi) | Modular street shows, mech shows, diorama shows |
| Regional salon | Small city-level show, beginner-friendly, low barrier to entry | Joint shows by local clubs |

## How to find a LEGO MOC exhibition near you

- **Follow clubs and communities.** MOC clubs in most big cities hold shows regularly. Joining a local brick group is the fastest way to hear about call-for-entries and ticket sales.
- **Watch official and media channels.** LEGO's official channels and domestic brick media publish exhibition calendars, and some shopping malls host themed shows themselves.
- **Search online for leads.** Searching "your city + brick exhibition" on Bilibili or Xiaohongshu usually turns up visit reports that other fans have put together.
- **Keep an eye on [brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn).** Community members often post show reports and share instructions for the models they exhibited — one browsing session and your bookmarks folder is full.

## How to visit: don't waste a good show

### 1. Do your homework first

- Confirm dates, ticket prices and whether booking is required. Popular weekend slots will almost certainly be packed.
- Study the venue layout in advance and circle the builds and interactive areas you most want to see.

### 2. How to look once you're inside

- **Macro first, micro second.** Step back a couple of paces and take in the overall shape and layout, then move in close for detail and structure. A lot of the cleverness hides around corners.
- **Ask questions and take photos.** Most builders are happy to explain how they approached a build, and a polite question often gets you a pile of practical tips. Check first whether photography is allowed.
- **Look for the thinking beyond the instructions.** Hidden SNOT techniques, clever suspended structures — these are study material you can take home and rebuild yourself.

### 3. After you get back

Sort the ideas you photographed into your own reference library, compare them against the instruction archive on [brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn), and turn the urge to build into your next project topic.

## How to get your own model into a show

Many builders assume the bar for exhibiting is very high. It isn't. The submission process at most shows comes down to four steps:

1. **Prepare the model.** A MOC with a high level of finish, solid structure and a clear theme. Plan dust protection and transport in advance.
2. **Find the call for entries.** Watch for exhibitor announcements from the organisers, or register through a club as a group.
3. **Submit your materials.** Usually photos of the model, its dimensions and a short description including piece count; some shows also ask for a transport and setup plan.
4. **Set up on site.** Place the model according to the organiser's layout, attach your builder information card, and be ready to talk about the build.

### Details that matter when you exhibit

- **The structure has to survive handling.** Transport and curious visitors will both test it. Reinforce fragile sections or add a display case.
- **Be on time and keep your commitments.** Respect the setup and teardown schedule — this is the basic courtesy the community cares about most.
- **Keep your expectations calm.** The result of a first show doesn't matter much. Meeting people, hearing feedback and gaining experience is the real payoff.

## Final thoughts

A **LEGO MOC exhibition** is the best proof there is that a group of adults take brick building seriously. Whether you are a newcomer visiting with your kids to see the spectacle, or a veteran submitting with instructions tucked under your arm, the moment you walk into the hall you feel the purest version of this hobby. If this has you itching to build, start with the nearest small local show you can find in the community — next time, you might be the builder with a crowd around your table.
