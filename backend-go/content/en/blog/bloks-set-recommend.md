# Bloks Brick Sets: Preschool, Car Model and IP Lines

Bottom line first: Bloks splits its catalog by age. Go with the large-brick preschool line for ages 1–6, the car model line for ages 6+ and adults, and an IP collaboration set if you collect licensed characters. Parts are not interchangeable between the three lines.

Whether you are buying a first brick set for a child or adding a Chinese-designed kit to your own shelf, Bloks (BLOKS) keeps turning up on recommendation lists. The brand does not follow LEGO's standard small-brick playbook; it built its market on oversize rounded bricks and a push-fit structure that parents and children can both handle. The range divides into three main lines: large-brick preschool sets, brick car models and IP collaborations. This guide looks at age fit, play value and price so you can choose the right set within each line.

## Brand positioning and age fit: who Bloks actually builds for

Bloks is built around "safe for young kids, build it and play with it." Its large bricks are roughly four times the size of a standard small brick, every corner is rounded, and the pins and slots engage with very little force, so a dropped brick does not hurt if you step on it. The official age range covers 1–10 years. The three lines differ noticeably, though: preschool is 1–6, car models run from 6+ up to adult, and the IP line works from 4+ upward. Before you shop, draw the age boundary first — that alone prevents most bad purchases.

## Large-brick preschool line: the default first set for ages 1–6

This is the line Bloks is best known for, with themes such as vehicles, rescue teams, dinosaurs and the animal farm. The bricks are big and the part count is low — a set runs 60–120 pieces and a scene goes together in a few minutes, so children finish on their own far more often. On budget: a basic small-scene set is about ¥50–100, while a larger set with motor or sound-and-light functions runs ¥150–300. The instructions are oversized and full color, with step drawings blown up enough to read the details, so a parent with zero brick experience can build alongside. For low-age families, this is the first place to look at this price point.

## Brick car model line: dynamic builds for ages 6+ and adults

The car line is the harder-core option, built around sports cars, construction vehicles and off-roaders, with gears, steering and pull-back or motorized mechanisms worked in — you build it, then you run it. Bricks are one size smaller than the preschool line, part counts typically run 300–1500, and build time ranges from an hour or two to a full day. Value for money is strong against other Chinese brands: an entry car model is around ¥100–200, a flagship construction vehicle ¥400–800. If you like working mechanisms but do not want to jump straight into LEGO Technic parts, this line is well worth a try.

## IP collaboration line: from Ultraman to Transformers

The IP line is Bloks' traffic driver. Licensed characters include Ultraman, Transformers, Kamen Rider and Spider-Man, with figures that have plenty of articulated joints and detailed sculpting. They suit kids aged 4+ for play and also draw adult collectors. A single figure box is about ¥30–60, a multi-in-one scene or deluxe set ¥100–300, and a single blind-box draw is cheaper still — though what you get is random. If character collecting is your thing, prioritize this line, and confirm the license matches the character you want so you do not end up with a counterfeit.

## Buying advice and price summary

In one sentence: for ages 1–6, pick a large-brick scene set (¥50–300); for 6+ or for yourself, pick the car line (¥100–800); for IP collecting, pick a collaboration set (¥30–300). Parts from the three lines do not fit each other, so do not plan on mixing them. With the preschool line, buy one small scene first as a test, then expand once you know the child enjoys it. Overall the logic is simple: choose the line by age, choose the set by interest. Get those two right and you will land on the right Bloks set.

## FAQ

**Q: What age is Bloks suitable for?**
A: The large-brick preschool line covers ages 1–6, the car line runs from 6+ to adult, and the IP line works from 4+. The large bricks are about four times the size of a standard small brick with fully rounded corners, so stepping on one does not hurt.

**Q: How do I choose between the three Bloks lines?**
A: For ages 1–6, choose a large-brick scene set (¥50–300). For 6+ or for your own building, choose the car line (¥100–800). For IP collecting, choose a collaboration set such as Ultraman or Transformers (¥30–300).

**Q: How much do Bloks large-brick preschool sets cost?**
A: A basic small-scene set is about ¥50–100, and a larger set with motor or sound-and-light functions is ¥150–300. A set has 60–120 pieces, a scene builds in a few minutes, and children finish on their own at a high rate.

**Q: Is the Bloks car model line worth building?**
A: Yes. The car line includes gears, steering and pull-back or motorized mechanisms, so the finished model runs and plays. Part counts are 300–1500; an entry car is about ¥100–200 and a flagship construction vehicle ¥400–800, which is strong value.
