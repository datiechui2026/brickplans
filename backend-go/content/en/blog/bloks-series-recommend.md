# Bloks Series Guide: Which Line Fits Your Builder?

![Finished models from the Bloks brick series](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/bloks-series-recommend/bloks-series-recommend-1.jpg)

Most parents shopping for Bloks hit exactly the same wall: **which Bloks series is actually the right one?** There is no single answer. Bloks runs several distinct lines, each aimed at a different age and a different kind of play, and the age ranges spread wide enough that a mismatch is easy to make — and easy to spot once the box is open and the bricks are sitting untouched on a shelf.

The reliable method is to narrow by age first, then narrow by theme. Age decides which line is physically appropriate; theme decides whether the child will actually care. Everything below is laid out the same way so the two filters are easy to apply side by side.

## Hero Unlimited Series: The Safe Bet for Younger Boys

![Assembly steps from the Bloks brick series](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/bloks-series-recommend/bloks-series-recommend-2.jpg)

Hero Unlimited is the bestseller of the Bloks range. It leans on mecha and dinosaur themes, and the defining feature is scale: the bricks and parts are large, so assembly stays simple and fast. It is aimed squarely at children aged 4 and up who are picking up bricks for the first time.

Large parts matter more than they sound. Small hands can grip them, position them and press them home without the fine pinch control that a standard brick demands, which means fewer failed attempts and far less frustration. Builds finish quickly, and a finished model in a child's hands is worth a lot at this stage — the sense of achievement is what keeps them coming back to the box.

If the child in question is into mecha or dinosaurs, this is the straightforward answer. As a first Bloks purchase it is also the most forgiving choice in the range.

## Brick Figure Series: The Pick for IP Collectors

Brick Figure is built around licensed characters from popular IPs, including Ultraman and Transformers. The parts here are noticeably smaller and the shapes much more detailed, and the finished figures carry more articulated joints than the other lines.

That combination — smaller parts, tighter detailing, more joints — puts the series at 6 and up, for children who already have some assembly experience and the patience to follow a longer build. It is also a genuine collector line: plenty of adult builders buy these for display rather than for play.

So if the question is which Bloks series to choose when IP and collecting are the point, Brick Figure is close to irreplaceable in the range. It is the line where the appeal is the character itself, not just the build.

## Early Learning Series: The Right Partner for Preschoolers

![Close-up of parts from the Bloks brick series](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/bloks-series-recommend/bloks-series-recommend-3.jpg)

Early Learning is designed for the youngest builders, from 1.5 to 4 years old. The parts are oversized and rounded for safety, and the focus is on colour, shape and basic logic rather than on finished models.

This is a learn-through-play line. Handling the pieces trains fine motor control, and matching colours and shapes builds early cognitive skills. There is no wrong way to put these together, which is exactly the point at this age — the goal is exploration, not a correct result.

For a toddler or a preschooler, this is the right entry point in the entire Bloks catalogue, and the safest one.

## Technic Series: The Next Step for Budding Engineers

The Technic line introduces mechanical structure — gears and motors — so the finished models can actually move. It targets children aged 7 and up with strong hands-on ability and the persistence to work through a mechanism rather than just a shape.

What makes this series distinct is that it moves a child past simple stacking and into the world of mechanical principles: how rotation transfers, how a motor drives a gear train, how a model is engineered rather than assembled. That is a genuine foundation for future STEM learning, and it is why the series suits the child who takes things apart to see how they work.

## The Wrap-Up: How to Actually Decide

The short version of **which Bloks series is best**: match the age, then match the interest.

- **1.5 to 4 years:** Early Learning — oversized, rounded, safe, and aimed at colour, shape and logic.
- **From 4 years:** Hero Unlimited — large parts, fast builds, mecha and dinosaur themes, the easiest entry into the brand.
- **6 years and up, into IP:** Brick Figure — Ultraman, Transformers and other licensed characters, smaller parts, more detail, more joints, and a real collector following.
- **7 years and up, hands-on:** Technic — gears and motors, moving models, mechanical principles and a STEM stepping stone.

Lock the age range first, then pick the theme the child already loves. Do that and the set gets built instead of shelved.
