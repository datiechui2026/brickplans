# Brick Bank MOC Blueprint: Complete Street Build Guide

A bank is the street scene building that best shows weight and solidity, and it is often the first full block many builders finish. This brick bank MOC blueprint guide separates the work into blueprint choice, ground-floor structure, counter and vault, facade color, and minifigure details so you can build a bank that looks right and plays well.

## 1. Before You Build: Blueprint, Scale, and Parts List
### 1. Blueprint Choice and Scale
The most common street bank uses 1:48 minifigure scale, a 16x16 stud footprint, and a height of two to two and a half stories. That keeps it level with a neighboring pizza shop or dessert shop. When choosing a brick bank MOC blueprint, check three things: clear sub-bag steps, marked orientation for key elements, and a vault section that stands as its own chapter. Beginners should look for a blueprint in the 1800-2500 piece range; it can usually be finished in four to five hours.

### 2. Basic Parts List
For the main build, plan on about 600 off-white or light gray bricks and plates for the exterior walls, about 300 dark gray and black plates for the base, about 60 transparent plates and window frames for the display windows, and a handful of printed elements for the bank sign, clock, and door number. Do not forget 4-6 16x16 baseplates and several backing plates for wall reinforcement.

## 2. Ground-Floor Structure: Foundation, Porch, and Load Bearing
### 1. Foundation and Base
Start with three steps made from dark gray plates, then add a base line in black plates to lift the whole building. That small lift gives the bank its sense of scale at once. At the foundation corners, interlock 2x2 bricks with holes so the floors cannot shift out of line.

### 2. Porch and Columns
Place a round brick column on each side of the main door. Use light gray for the shafts and white cone pieces as capitals, then set the printed bank sign above the lintel. Inside the columns, be sure to add 2x4 beams to connect them. The beams support the second floor and stop the columns from being knocked out of line.

## 3. Core Areas: Counter, ATM, and Vault
### 1. Teller Counter
Build a long counter against the wall on the ground floor. Use off-white tiles for the countertop and transparent panels on the front for a bulletproof glass effect. Put two computers inside: gray plates with printed screen elements. At the corner of the counter, add an ATM with a dark gray face and a green printed screen, then place a queue barrier beside it. With minifigures in position, the banking scene is complete.

### 2. Vault and Safes
The vault is the heart of a bank MOC. Enclose a 4x4 vault room with dark gray plates. Fit the door with a printed dial combination lock, using a 1x1 round brick with a stud as the dial. Inside, place two or three safes: use 2x4 bricks for the bodies and 1x2 plates for the drawers. Stack gold bars on top, made from gold 1x2 plates in two layers, and add two transparent printed banknote elements. The scene immediately looks valuable.

## 4. Facade Decoration and Color Scheme
For color, use an off-white exterior, a dark gray base, and gold accents. Keep the gold for the sign, door frame, and cornice only; a little goes a long way and keeps the bank from looking gaudy. On the second floor, add a row of Greek-style capitals made from white 1x1 cones and round bricks, paired with a pediment. That gives the classic bank look. Finish the cornice with dark brown plates to contrast with the off-white walls, so the building reads as a bank from across the street.

## 5. Interior Details and Minifigure Interaction
The best brick bank MOC blueprints add minifigure interaction. Put a guard at the door in a black uniform with a walkie-talkie, a teller behind the counter, and leave a spot at the vault door for a minifigure posed while opening the safe. Add warm yellow light strips so the counter glows through the bulletproof glass; the late-night street scene atmosphere is instantly stronger. ## 6. Common Problems and Structural Reinforcement
Second-floor walls tend to bulge outward. Add a backing plate to each wall every 8 studs. The vault door is heavy, so reinforce the hinge with a 1x2 plate with holes and pin elements. When glazing the display windows with transparent panels, install the frame first and then insert the glass; this helps avoid scratches. After the build is complete, look down from the front and check that the corners are interlocked to avoid a leaning building.
