# Brick Observatory MOC Review: Rotating Dome and Telescope

Bottom line first: this brick observatory MOC uses roughly 6,500 pieces to build a structure 46 cm in diameter and about 38 cm tall, takes around 24 hours of building time, and runs about 1,100 yuan. The rotating dome is the headline feature, the telescope is detailed where it matters, and it works well as a display piece for astronomy fans. Value for money is above average.

## 1. Accuracy of the Design

I'd give design accuracy 8.5 out of 10. The white dome is built from curved pieces that form a smooth hemispherical outline, with dark grey elements along the top seam to suggest the shutter track. A ring of observation windows and vents runs around the side, and the proportions sit close to a real observatory.

The telescope is the real highlight: the main tube is built from technic pieces into a large-aperture structure about 22 cm long, mounted on an equatorial-style base. The tube can be tilted up and down, and transparent pieces at the objective end stand in for the lens.

Inside, the star gallery uses glow-in-the-dark pieces to lay out constellation patterns — turn the lights off and you get a faint starfield effect. Points come off for the seams between the curved pieces on the dome's outer wall, which are a little visible and need a careful display angle to hide.

## 2. The Build

The roughly 6,500 pieces arrive in 12 numbered bags. You build the base and gallery first, then the telescope, then the dome last.

The dome rotation mechanism is the hardest part of the build. A fixed gear ring sits on the inner ring, and the outer dome is held on the track by four roller pieces. Turn the handle on top and the whole dome rotates around its axis, lining the shutter up with the telescope. This assembly demands precision — the gear ring has to mesh evenly — so follow the manual order and adjust slowly. The instructions use 3D renders and the steps are clear. At four hours a day, you're looking at about six days to finish. Nothing was missing from the box, but once the roller pieces are in you'll want to rotate the dome repeatedly to test how smoothly it runs.

## 3. Value and Verdict

About 1,100 yuan for roughly 6,500 pieces works out to around 0.17 yuan per piece. Given the high share of curved pieces and technic elements, that's mid-to-high but fair. Official sets at the same price tend to be architectural display models with no rotating mechanism or adjustable telescope — no linked play features like these.

Verdict: astronomy fans and architectural-scene builders can buy with confidence; it photographs beautifully on a desk. If you only want a big, all-in-one scene and the astronomy angle does nothing for you, the same budget can go to another theme. The full parts list and building instructions are on brickplan.
