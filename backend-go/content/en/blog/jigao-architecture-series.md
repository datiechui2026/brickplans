# Jigao Architecture Series Review: Bricks, Design and Value

If you have been watching the Chinese brick market, you have probably noticed **Jigao** moving fast over the past two years. Its architecture line spans European-style street scenes, Chinese ancient buildings, skyscrapers and world landmarks, and it is expanding the building-block map for Chinese collectors at a remarkable pace. This review covers four angles: brick quality, design standards, value for money, and how the brand stacks up against its rivals.

## 1. Brick Quality: A Serious Latecomer

In terms of brick quality, the Jigao architecture series sits in the **top tier** of Chinese-made bricks.

**Clutch power** is where Jigao shines. The company uses high-precision moulds and keeps tolerances tight — joints are neither so loose that a wall collapses at a touch, nor so tight that your fingertips ache by bag three. Compared with the old reputation that Chinese bricks meant sore hands, Jigao has made a clear step forward in the building experience.

**Colour and finish** are well judged. Saturation is moderate and the surface has a light matte texture, which avoids a cheap look without hiding fine detail. Transparent parts are especially good: high clarity, no bubbles, and they look great in windows and domes.

**Durability** holds up too. After repeated disassembly there is no obvious loosening of clutch or stress whitening, which matters to MOC builders who rebuild the same model again and again.

## 2. Design: From Following to Original Work

The Jigao architecture catalogue runs in three directions:

**World landmarks** — the Eiffel Tower, Sydney Opera House, Big Ben and similar icons. Exterior accuracy is high, and the sets strike a sensible balance between part count and cost. Instructions are clearly stepped with well-separated colours, so even a first-time builder can finish without trouble.

**Chinese ancient architecture** — this is Jigao's most distinctive line. From the Hall of Supreme Harmony in the Forbidden City to the Yellow Crane Tower, from Suzhou gardens to Huizhou-style dwellings, Jigao uses specialised brick colours and structural design to reinterpret upturned eaves, stacked dougong brackets and grey tile roofs against white walls in brick form. It fills a real gap in the market.

**Creative street scenes** — taking on the modular street format popularised by international brands, Jigao has launched its own series. The 2026 releases show a genuine leap in building proportions, interior detail and facade decoration, and some sets now include light elements and linked mechanisms, adding considerably to play value.

## 3. Value for Money: The Domestic Advantage

On price, the Jigao architecture series is hard to argue with. For street-scene sets of comparable specification, Jigao typically prices at **one-third to one-half** of what an international brand charges. Given how close the brick quality has become, that ratio is very attractive.

Just as important is the investment in **part variety** — curved bricks, slopes, decorative columns and special window frames. A large stock of specialised parts reduces the studded, coarse look of a finished facade and gives MOC builders far more raw material to work with. Plenty of builders on brickplan.cn have used Jigao parts in their own creations, with impressive results.

## 4. How It Compares to Other Brands

**Jigao vs. LEGO** — LEGO still leads clearly on architectural design experience and brand heritage, and its modular street series remains the benchmark. But Jigao has built its own moat in **Chinese ancient architecture**, a category LEGO has barely touched.

**Jigao vs. SEMBO** — both sit at the top of the domestic market. SEMBO is stronger on licensed IP products, while Jigao is stronger on **original architectural design**, and its quality control is slightly more consistent, particularly for specialised and transparent parts.

**Jigao vs. Wange** — Wange undercuts Jigao on large architecture sets, but the gap in brick quality and design refinement is obvious. Spend a little more and Jigao delivers a noticeably better build.

## 5. Verdict and Buying Advice

The Jigao architecture series is best described as a pleasant surprise. Brick quality reaches the high standard of Chinese-made product, and the design has found its own path — the Chinese ancient architecture line in particular fills an important hole in the brick market. Combined with aggressive pricing, it works well either as an entry point into the hobby or as a parts source for MOC work.

There is still a gap to the top-tier brands, of course, but Jigao's **rapid iteration** and **steadily improving** quality are closing that gap at a visible rate.
