# Castle Moat MOC Review: Is This 1,860-Piece Upgrade Worth It?

The short answer first: this castle moat MOC performs better than expected. The exterior detail is convincing, the build logic is clear, and anyone who wants a castle with more depth should consider it. The catch is price and footprint — it is expensive and it takes up a lot of shelf space, so builders on a tight budget or short on display room should read the full review before committing. Final score: 8.5 out of 10.

## 1. Exterior Accuracy: The Moat Is the Soul of the Build

The standout feature is the water surface. The designer uses roughly 320 transparent blue pieces to simulate flowing water, layering light and dark blue bricks to create a visible water-level difference — the effect reads as genuinely wet, not just blue plastic. The base of the city wall is finished with slope bricks and curved bricks to blend into the shoreline, and the moat-to-drawbridge width ratio comes out at about 3:1. Viewed head-on, the castle looks genuinely surrounded by water, and the whole model gains a sense of scale it would not have on a bare baseplate.

## 2. Build Experience: Clear Instructions, but a Long Sit

The complete model uses about 1,860 pieces and takes roughly 6 hours to assemble. The instructions are split into 7 numbered step bags at around 260 pieces each, so hunting for parts never becomes overwhelming. The drawbridge raises and lowers using a gear and rope system, and the linkage runs smoothly — turn the crank on the side and the bridge deck rises and falls evenly, which adds a lot of play value once the model is finished. One small complaint: the water surface involves a lot of repetitive building, and that section does start to feel tedious.

## 3. Part Quality: Tight Clutch, Good in the Hand

Clutch power is firm and tolerance control is excellent — after 20 rounds of disassembly and reassembly the connections were still tight. The transparent blue pieces are clear with no obvious scratches, and the color difference between the light and dark shades is acceptable in person. In texture and consistency the parts come close to original-factory standard.

## 4. Value for Money: Price Is the Only Barrier

Instructions plus the parts pack sell for around CNY 680, which works out to roughly CNY 0.37 per piece — mid-to-upper range among comparable castle MOCs. If you already own a large stock of blue bricks, buying the instructions alone (about CNY 45) is the smarter move. Judged purely on looks and structure, it earns its price, but the cost will put some builders off.

## FAQ

**Q: How is this castle moat MOC different from a standard castle set?**

A: The moat MOC adds a surrounding water feature and a working drawbridge mechanism on top of a standard castle design, with about 500 more pieces. That gives you more visual depth and more playability, but also a longer build and a bigger footprint.

**Q: Can a beginner finish this moat MOC?**

A: Yes. The instructions are divided into 7 step bags with detailed diagrams, but the full build takes about 6 hours. Builders with experience of three or more medium-sized sets will be comfortable; beginners should split it across two days to avoid fatigue and mistakes.

**Q: How much does this moat MOC cost, and is it worth buying?**

A: Instructions plus parts come to about CNY 680, or roughly CNY 0.37 per piece, which sits in the mid-to-upper price bracket. If you want a more complete castle and mechanical play features, it is worth it. On a tighter budget, buy the CNY 45 instructions and source the parts yourself.

**Q: How does the drawbridge actually raise and lower?**

A: A gear drive turns a rope spool. Rotating the crank on the side raises and lowers the bridge deck smoothly — the mechanism is simple, rarely jams, and is easy to maintain later. Just align the gears as shown in the instructions during the build.
