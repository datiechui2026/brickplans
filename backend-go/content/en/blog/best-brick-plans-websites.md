# Which Brick Plan Website Is Best? A Builder's Comparison

Most builders who type this question into a search bar have already been burned once. Either the file they downloaded had steps jumping around with no logic to them, or the parts list was missing rows, or the finished model turned out to call for colors that were never actually produced. There is no shortage of plan sites out there — what is in short supply is clarity about what each one is for. So this comparison is organized by scenario rather than by a single ranking, because the right answer changes depending on who you are and what you are building.

## Domestic Plan Sites: Chinese-Friendly and Quick to Learn

For most Chinese-speaking players, domestic plan sites deserve to be the first stop. The biggest advantage is straightforward: a Chinese interface and Chinese-language instructions remove the single most painful part of getting started — deciphering an English manual before you can even reach the first bag of parts.

Take brickplan.cn as an example. Its plans are sorted clearly by theme and by difficulty, so a newcomer can start straight from the "beginner" and "easy" tiers. That matters more than it sounds. On many international sites the front page is a wall of hardcore builds, and the frustration of picking something far beyond your level is what makes people quit in the first week.

The download experience is usually where domestic sites pull ahead too. PDF preview and packaged batch downloads are standard, step images are legible, parts lists are labeled consistently, and many plans ship with Chinese assembly notes attached. For someone who has just bought their first few sets and wants to try building something original, that combination is genuinely friendly.

## International Plan Sites: Huge Catalog, High Quality

When people talk about international plan sites, Rebrickable is the name you cannot avoid. Its MOC plan catalog is enormous, the community is active, and the overall level of the creators is consistently strong, with coverage running from vehicles through to architecture. It also supports reverse matching against your parts inventory — feed in the parts you already own and it will surface plans you can actually build with them. That is its signature trick, and no domestic site really replicates it.

But the barrier is real and obvious. The interface is entirely in English, a portion of the better plans require payment or a subscription, the share of Chinese-language plans is low, and before downloading anything you need to check carefully whether a parts list is included. It suits intermediate and advanced builders who already have some foundation and are willing to put in the effort.

## Free vs Paid: How to Decide

Free plans are plentiful, but quality is uneven. Skipped steps and mislabeled parts do happen, and a plan that looks complete at a glance can leave you stuck halfway through with no way to continue. That makes free plans best treated as practice material — good for developing your eye and your building habits.

Paid plans are usually polished by the author. Steps are rigorous, the fidelity to the original design is high, and you are paying for the hours someone spent testing the build rather than just designing it. If you only build occasionally, free resources plus careful screening are entirely sufficient. If you are chasing quality and want to replicate a specific model properly, paying the author whose work you like is well worth it.

A few screening habits help either way: confirm the parts list includes quantities and not just part names, check whether the steps break the model into sub-assemblies, and skim the parts for anything rare or expensive before you commit. That five-minute check saves far more time than it costs.

## Choosing by Need

- Chinese-speaking players and beginners: go with domestic sites — friendly interface, resources that match your level;
- Advanced, technically minded builders: international sites such as Rebrickable, where the volume is larger and the quality is higher;
- Limited parts on hand: prioritize sites that support parts matching;
- Budget-conscious: lean on free plans and screen them carefully.

Taken together, there is no absolute "best" site — only the one that fits your situation. The approach that works best is domestic as your base and international as your supplement. Pick Chinese-language plans by difficulty on brickplan.cn to practice, then move to international sites when you want to chase more advanced work. The two complement each other rather than compete.
