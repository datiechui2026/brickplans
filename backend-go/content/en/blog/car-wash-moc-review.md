# Car Wash MOC Review: A Street Scene That Really Washes Cars

Once your street diorama is past its first few buildings, the hunt begins for shops nobody else has. Restaurants, bookshops and flower stalls are everywhere; a corner car wash that lets a small car genuinely drive through the washer is still a rare theme. In this car wash MOC review we built the model from the instructions as supplied, tunnel washer and all, and laid out both the highlights and the rough edges.

## 1. Instructions and Parts: A Clean, Lightweight Build

The model is drawn at **1:48 street scene scale** and comes in at roughly **1600 pieces**, which makes it a lightweight entry by street-scene standards. The parts arrive in **five numbered bags**, so a weekend day is enough to finish it. The manual is easy to follow, and the sections with plenty of curved slopes carry extra callouts showing which way the parts should engage, so even a first-time builder is unlikely to fit them backwards.

The colour scheme leans on **sky blue, white, dark grey and lemon yellow**, and it reads clean and fresh the moment the box opens, which suits a wash theme. Small accessories such as the water gun and the bucket are **printed elements** rather than stickers, and they feel solid in hand. Missing parts were not an issue. Instructions and parts score **8.5/10**.

## 2. Structure and Core Mechanism: A Tunnel Washer Cars Drive Through

The real risk with a car wash is ending up with a shopfront shell and nothing behind it. This model avoids that, because its core structure does actual work:

- **Tunnel washer**: a wash tunnel **12 studs long**, with a top beam built from perforated Technic elements and roller pieces to form **three sets of rotating brushes**. A small car can really be pushed through the tunnel and the brushes turn as it passes, which is oddly satisfying.
- **Rail linkage**: two rails run along the tunnel floor, so a vehicle slides in without snagging and the full enter, scrub and dry sequence plays out as intended.
- **Zoned layout**: the frontage holds a reception desk, the interior is the wash bay, and the back wall has a drainage channel made from dark grey plates sunk half a plate down. Small detail, but considered.

The whole thing is compact with firm connections, and it held up through repeated handling without loosening. Structure scores **9/10**.

## 3. Playability: Splashes, Towel Wipes and a Queue

This is where the model is at its most charming. A row of transparent light-blue round elements sits along each side of the tunnel to stand in for spray, so a car driving through looks like it is being hosed down. A minifigure staff member holds a towel to wipe the car, and a row of towels hangs from a rack overhead.

Better still, the design leaves room for a queue: a lane is marked out at the entrance, and parking two small cars there immediately sets up a car-wash rush-hour story. Playability scores **9/10**.

## 4. Accuracy and Atmosphere

On the accuracy side, nothing obvious is missing: the scrubber, the spray nozzles, the drainage channel, the towel rack and the water-droplet sign are all present, and the result reads as a standard car wash at a glance. Add blue and white lighting and the inside of the tunnel photographs especially well.

The small disappointments are a fairly flat roof and a tunnel that cannot be lifted out for separate handling. Atmosphere scores **8.5/10**.

**Verdict: worth buying, especially if you want to drop an easter egg into your street.** This car wash MOC sits at medium difficulty (around **4 hours**) and is beginner friendly, and it is an uncommon lightweight street build that is both playable as a finished model and a stage for little stories. It suits builders who want to add car-themed interaction to a street scene and make the block feel lived in; builders who already own a few small car MOCs and want to set up a wash-and-queue scene; and creative builders chasing a shop theme that stands out from the usual lineup.

The weak points are mainly the flat roof shape and the non-removable tunnel, but for most street-scene fans that is a minor trade-off.
