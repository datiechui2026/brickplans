# Brick Airship MOC Tutorial: Frame to Envelope in 6 Hours

Short version first: an airship is one of the most recognizable subjects you can build in bricks, and it does not eat a huge parts budget. Following this tutorial, roughly 1,600 pieces on a 32x32 baseplate get you a 48 cm rigid airship, and the whole build runs about 6 hours — manageable for a first-time MOC builder.

## Step 1: Set the Scale and Build the Frame

The envelope is the visual anchor of an airship, so decide the length before anything else. At 1:150 scale, a 48 cm hull corresponds to about 7,200 part positions at real-ship size, which compresses down to 1,600 pieces in MOC form. Build two ring frames — one fore, one aft — from technic beams, then connect them with 3 long longitudinal rods. This skeleton is what the envelope bricks will attach to later. Keep the frame spacing even: put one cross beam every 8 studs, 6 beams in total.

## Step 2: Shaping the Envelope

The envelope is where the model really starts to read as an airship. Lay curved bricks and slope bricks ring by ring from the tail forward, locking the left and right sides of each ring before filling the middle — that keeps the curvature from drifting. For color, go light on top and dark underneath; a cream upper half over light gray lower half reads far more three-dimensional. To keep weight down the envelope is hollow, with only the frame inside. Total weight lands around 1.2 kg, which sits very stably on a display shelf.

## Step 3: Gondola and Power Details

The gondola hangs below the envelope. Build the cabin frame from 1x2 bricks, use transparent parts for the front and rear cockpit windows, and mount 4 engine nacelles along the sides. Each propeller is a propeller blade part pinned with a small round plate so it turns by hand. Connect gondola to envelope with 2 struts, and make sure those struts land near the envelope's center of gravity — otherwise the model tips forward.

## Step 4: Tail Fins and Markings

Add a cross-shaped tail assembly, one fin each on top, bottom, left and right, edged with slope bricks. Finish with stickers or small bricks for portholes and a registration number — for example, 3 portholes on each side of the hull. When it is done the airship measures about 48 cm long and 16 cm tall, and it holds its own either above a street scene or on a standalone display.

## FAQ

**Q: How much does an airship MOC instruction file usually cost?**

**Q: Can a beginner build an airship MOC?**
A: Yes. The structure is symmetrical and highly repetitive, and 1,600 pieces take about 6 hours. That makes it a better first project than a castle or street-scene MOC — just follow the instruction steps in order.

**Q: How many parts does an airship MOC need?**
A: Around 1,600. Curved bricks and slope bricks make up about 30% of that, plus roughly 40 transparent parts. Parts lists normally flag these, so you can check your inventory on brickplan.cn and order everything in one go.

**Q: Can I add lighting to an airship MOC?**
A: Yes. There is enough room inside the gondola for 1 LED light set, and the tail fin area can hide the battery box. Lit up, the portholes and cockpit windows look great — warm yellow light works best.

**Q: How is an airship MOC different from a regular aircraft MOC?**
A: An airship rises on its envelope and moves slowly, so the design focus is the streamlined envelope and the gondola. An aircraft MOC is about wing aerodynamics and landing gear. Airship building is more symmetrical and slightly easier, with a more unusual look on the shelf.
