# How to Start Building MOC Brick Models: A Beginner's Guide

![Finished MOC model built by a beginner](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/how-to-start-moc/how-to-start-moc-1.jpg)

"I can barely finish a set — how am I supposed to design my own?" It's the question beginners ask most. Honestly, MOC isn't magic: it's taking bricks and building the thing in your head, which is not fundamentally different from drawing a picture. This guide walks the whole path of starting with MOC building — choosing parts, copying instructions, and releasing your first independent model.

## What Is a MOC, and Why Should You Try It?

![MOC building steps laid out in order](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/how-to-start-moc/how-to-start-moc-2.jpg)

The biggest difference between a MOC and an official set: a set is a fill-in-the-blank exercise someone else designed, while a MOC is an open question you answer yourself. Official sets teach you building techniques, but the real creative pleasure comes from MOC — when a pile of loose parts becomes a one-of-a-kind model, no set can match that feeling.

For beginners, the goal isn't pulling off a masterpiece on day one; it's enjoying the process of creating from nothing. Even a simple animal or a tiny house counts as a successful MOC as long as you built it yourself.

## Step 1: Stock Basic Parts

### The starter parts list

The first question every beginner asks is "which parts should I buy?" You don't need a huge stockpile. This list gets you moving:

- **Bricks**: 2x4, 2x2, 1x2 and 1x1 in several colors — the actual building material
- **Plates**: 2x4, 2x2, 1x2, 1x1, for fine adjustments and wall surfacing
- **Slopes**: 2x1 and 3x1 slopes, for roofs and streamlined shapes
- **Tiles**: 1x1, 1x2, 2x2, for smooth surfaces and decoration
- **Specialty parts**: wheels, windows, doors, plant elements — add these based on what you want to build

Rather than buying in bulk, pick a theme first and buy parts for it. Most instructions on [BrickPlans](https://brickplan.cn) include a parts list, and buying to that list is the most economical route.

### How many parts do you need?

200-500 parts is plenty for a small first model. Don't chase quantity — build small well, then scale up.

## Step 2: Pick the Right Tools

### Essential tools

- **Brick separator**: the orange official separator is the most practical tool around; it saves your fingers and protects parts when disassembling
- **Sorting box**: a simple multi-compartment storage box noticeably speeds up building

### Advanced tools

- **Digital design software**: Bricklink Studio 2.0 or LEGO Digital Designer let you design on screen before you build, cutting trial-and-error costs dramatically
- **Vernier caliper**: for MOCs that need precise dimensions, a caliper helps you measure the clearance between parts

## Step 3: Start by Copying — Find Good Instructions

### Why start with imitation?

Every MOC designer started by copying. The answer to how to start MOC building is: copy first, create later. Copying other people's designs teaches you:

- how different parts work and what they're good for
- the logic and technique of structural building
- the rules of color matching and the experience behind them

### Where to find instructions

[BrickPlans](https://brickplan.cn) is one of the best MOC instruction communities, with a large library of free and paid high-quality instructions covering architecture, vehicles, mecha, animals and more. Each one comes with a detailed parts list and build steps, ideal for beginners to study and follow.

International platforms such as Bricklink and Rebrickable also host huge instruction libraries, though the all-English interface can be a barrier for some newcomers. BrickPlans' Chinese-language interface and community atmosphere are friendlier for builders based in China.

## Step 4: Build Something — Start With Simple Subjects

![Close-up of MOC parts and texture detail](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/how-to-start-moc/how-to-start-moc-3.jpg)

### Good first themes

- **Mini architecture**: a 20x20 stud house needs only 100-200 parts
- **Small animals**: a bird, dog or cat — simple shapes, few parts
- **Vehicles**: a micro car lets you practice wheel mounting and chassis structure
- **Plants**: a tree or a clump of flowers, practice using irregular parts to mimic natural forms

### The building process

1. **Choose a subject**: decide what you want to build
2. **Gather inspiration**: look at photos and instructions of similar builds
3. **Sketch it**: draw the rough shape and dimensions on paper
4. **Build in stages**: start with the main structure, add details gradually
5. **Revise**: if you don't like it, take it apart and start again — that's normal for MOC work

One key idea in MOC building: **there are no failed builds, only unfinished ones**. Teardown and rebuild isn't failure; it's progress.

## Step 5: Join a Community and Get Feedback

### Why you need a community

MOC is solitary making but a social hobby. A community gives you:

- feedback and suggestions from other builders
- more great builds to draw inspiration from
- themed challenges that spark motivation
- answers to technical problems mid-build

Beyond downloading instructions, the community is where people actually improve: post builds, ask questions, enter themed challenges. For beginners, one pointer from someone else beats three days of guessing alone.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I have to use genuine bricks for a MOC?**
A: No. Many Chinese brands' parts are now quite good and compatible. Which brand you choose depends on your budget and how demanding you are about quality.

**Q: What if I can't build anything without instructions?**
A: That's completely normal. Practice with other people's instructions first and gradually understand the building logic; try original design once you've built up some experience.

**Q: How long does one MOC take?**
A: For beginners, a small build of 100-200 parts usually takes 2-4 hours. Building gets faster as you gain experience.

**Q: What if I don't have enough parts?**
A: Every MOC builder runs into this. Disassemble sets you don't display often to top up your parts, or buy individual parts for the specific gap.
