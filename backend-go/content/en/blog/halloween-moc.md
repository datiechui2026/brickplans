# Halloween Brick MOC Guide: Jack-o'-Lanterns, Ghosts and Skeletons

Here is the short version: a Halloween MOC lives or dies on color and detail. The jack-o'-lantern is the star, ghosts and skeletons carry the mystery in white, and a dark palette plus a few minifig costume swaps finishes the scene.

Halloween is one of the most atmospheric topics you can build for. Compared with the red-and-gold bustle of Spring Festival, Halloween runs on mystery and mischief. Pumpkins, ghosts, skeletons and bats are instantly recognizable, and none of them demand complicated construction. A good **Halloween brick MOC** usually wins on palette and small details rather than part count. Below is a full build plan, from the classic elements to color schemes, minifig swaps and scene layout.

## The Jack-o'-Lantern: Undisputed Star of the Scene

The jack-o'-lantern is the element no Halloween MOC can skip. Ring orange curved bricks and slope bricks into the pumpkin's rounded body, then finish the stem with a small green bar or a cone. For the pumpkin's ridges, run vertical lines of dark orange 1x1 studs down the surface -- it reads as far more three-dimensional. The critical part is the light source: leave a hollow cavity inside the body and drop in a warm yellow light plate, then build the eyes and mouth as cutouts using black tiles. Switch it on and the atmosphere doubles instantly. If your parts supply is thin, start with small pumpkins and line up five or six of them as a pumpkin patch -- that photographs just as well.

## Ghosts and Skeletons: Mystery in White

Ghosts come together from white transparent pieces or milky white bricks. Use dome pieces or curved bricks for the body, scatter irregular white studs along the bottom edge to suggest a trailing sheet, and add black tiles for the eyes and mouth. Skeletons need the skull and ribcage to read clearly: a white round plate with a printed black eye-socket piece for the skull, then brown or white 1x1 studs spaced out for the ribcage to mimic the gaps between ribs. Both elements work well hung under an eave, standing beside a gravestone, or peeking out from a doorway -- anything that gives the scene a bit of story.

## Dark Color Schemes

Lock the palette to dark foundations: dark gray, dark brown and black for the main structure, with orange and purple as accents. Purple suits ghost robes, magic circles and backdrop walls, while orange should be concentrated in the jack-o'-lanterns so the eye has one clear focal point. For a moonlit graveyard effect, build light gray tombstones on a dark blue or dark gray baseplate, then lay white transparent pieces over the ground to suggest moonlight. Keep the ratio of bright colors low -- the whole point of a dark scene is that it stays under control.

## Minifig Costume Swaps and Scene Layout

Swapping minifig outfits is the best value you will get out of a Halloween MOC. Give existing figures a black cape, a witch hat, a pumpkin headpiece or a skull mask, and the character is new in a couple of steps. For the layout, build a small church, a graveyard or a haunted house, then add bare trees (brown branch pieces with a few leafless twigs) and bats hung upside down from black wedge pieces. If there are kids at home, design it as an interactive scene so each build is as fun to play with as it is to look at.

## Finished Builds and Display Advice

Seasonal builds like this do not need to stay out all year, so mount everything on a baseplate you can lift as one piece (32x32 works well) and pack it away once the holiday passes. If there is a light plate inside the pumpkin, leave an opening in the battery box so you can change cells without disassembling. Set the finished scene by the front door or in the entryway and the holiday mood arrives immediately. Build your own Halloween brick MOC and let this year's Halloween start with bricks.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I make a Halloween brick MOC feel atmospheric?**
A: Cover three element groups: jack-o'-lanterns as the star, ghosts and skeletons for white mystery, bats and bare trees for silhouettes. Keep the palette black, gray and deep purple, and light it with a small amount of warm orange.

**Q: What is the most effective way to build a jack-o'-lantern?**
A: Stack orange round bricks or dome pieces into the pumpkin body, set black pieces into the front as triangular eyes and a jagged mouth, and push a small green piece on top as the stem. Put a warm light piece inside and the mood lands the moment it switches on.

**Q: How do I build ghosts and skeletons?**
A: For ghosts, use white curved pieces to shape a flowing hem, keep the interior hollow and add transparent pieces. For skeletons, build the skull, ribcage and limbs from small white pieces, and watch the proportions so they do not end up oversized.

**Q: How should I set a dark color scheme?**
A: Use black, dark gray and deep purple as the main colors, reserving orange and yellow for light sources and pumpkin accents. Keep the overall scene low-lit and let a small amount of warm light pick out the subject -- that is where the mystery comes from.
