# Brick Saxophone MOC Review: A Bronze Jazz Display Piece

The short version: this saxophone MOC is built almost entirely in gold-bronze tones, uses around 860 parts, and stands 36 cm tall when finished. Curved slope bricks and round tube elements recreate the instrument's signature S-shaped body and key layout, which makes it one of the best-looking pieces in the music-themed display category. It is aimed at builders aged 14 and up.

## Structure and Accuracy

The hardest part of any saxophone build is the curved body, and the designer handles it by chaining multiple curved bricks and bent tube pieces together, running from the mouthpiece all the way through to the bell without a break in the line. The keys are made from small gold round plates set at staggered heights, while the tone holes are picked out with darker elements. The flare of the bell is achieved by layering plates outward, and the overall accuracy comes in at roughly 88 percent — viewed from the side, the silhouette reads very close to the real instrument.

## Parts and Colour Scheme

Gold-bronze elements make up more than 70 percent of the roughly 860 parts, balanced by a small number of black keys and silver accents. The metallic look comes from mixing bright gold pieces with matte ones rather than from any special finish. The body has a subtle gradient effect across its surface: up close it reads like aged brass, while from a distance it picks up the kind of sheen you would expect under stage lighting. Printed elements are few, but each one sits in a key position.

## Build Experience

The instructions come from the brickplan.cn platform, and our test build took about 3.5 hours across 4 stages. The curved body is the real challenge here — roughly 40 parts have to be aligned to the arc one at a time, so it pays to work through the instructions in order rather than sorting parts ahead. The key section is more repetitive, but the finished result is tidy and gives a strong sense of accomplishment.

## Value for Money and Who It's For

Buying the parts loose costs about 150–220 RMB, making this the most affordable of the five instrument MOCs in the series and a good fit for builders on a tighter budget. It suits jazz fans, music teachers, and anyone who wants a high-impact desktop display piece — it photographs especially well on a desk in a coffee-shop-style study.

## FAQ

**Q: Can the brick saxophone MOC actually be played?**

A: No. The body is a closed brick structure with no reed and no linked key mechanism, so it cannot be blown. It is designed as a display model for desktops and collections, and it is best not to expect any sound function.

**Q: What age is the brick saxophone MOC suitable for?**

A: 14 and up is recommended. Among the 860 parts there are plenty of small curved elements, and the curved body section calls for fine handling. Younger children are likely to lose pieces, and building with a parent alongside makes the process smoother.

**Q: How much does the brick saxophone MOC cost?**

A: Loose parts run about 150–220 RMB, and part of the instruction sequence can be previewed for free on brickplan.cn. If you already have a stock of common parts, the real cost can come in lower still — this sits at the entry-level price point for instrument MOCs.

**Q: Is the brick saxophone MOC worth buying?**

A: Yes. Gold-bronze colour schemes are rare in brick building, the finished model stands 36 cm tall and looks strong on a shelf, and the 3.5-hour build time is a comfortable length. It also makes a thoughtful gift for a music lover.
