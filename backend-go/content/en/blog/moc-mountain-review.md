# Brick MOC Mountain Review: Cliffs Built From Slope Pieces

Here is the verdict first: a mountain is one of the best subjects for turning ordinary bricks into something with real three-dimensional presence. A medium-sized build takes roughly 1,400 pieces and 8 to 10 hours of building time, and the difficulty sits just above intermediate. If you are chasing natural-looking rock walls with genuine layering, this one is safe to pick up.

## Slopes and Rock Texture

The soul of a mountain MOC is the arrangement of slope pieces and wedge plates. The build reviewed here uses three sizes of wedge plate to suggest the direction of the rock strata, mixing dark gray, light gray and tan to create the stone texture. The key during assembly is to let blocks of the same color overlap diagonally rather than sit in tidy horizontal rows. Once you have scattered roughly 30 small slope changes across the surface, the side of the mountain naturally starts to read as weathered fissures.

That single rule does most of the work. Straight courses of slope pieces look machined; staggered ones look eroded. The three-color mix matters just as much, because a single gray makes the whole face flatten out visually no matter how good the geometry is.

## Cliff Faces and Layered Building

The builder works bottom-up, splitting the mountain into three large layers: the base, the mid-slope and the summit. Inside each layer, a solid core carries the strength while the visible surface is covered separately, which keeps the structure rigid without wasting pieces on hidden interior detail.

The vertical cliff faces are hung on the outside using 1x2 and 1x3 bricks with studs on the side, pulled inward to create an overhang of about 45 degrees. At the top, contrast-colored pieces close off the edge and mimic ridge lines scoured by rainwater. The result is a model whose visual center of gravity stays remarkably stable, even with a pronounced overhang.

## Difficulty, Cost and Whether It Is Worth It

Loose parts run about 180 to 260 RMB. If you are pulling from your own stock, the cost is close to zero. The structure needs no special electronic components, so this falls squarely into the "lots of parts but a clear logical thread" category — well suited to an intermediate builder with some experience behind them.

Compared with a retail set, this MOC offers more originality and far more room to modify. The value for money is genuinely good. When you finish, remember to share it on brickplan.cn to swap instructions and rock-wall techniques with fellow builders.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I make a brick MOC mountain look natural?**

A: The key is to overlap slope pieces diagonally instead of lining them up in neat horizontal rows, and to interleave dark gray, light gray and tan blocks so they break up the surface. Aim for around 30 small slope changes and keep the rock strata from running parallel to each other — the mountain will immediately look more real.

**Q: Roughly how many pieces does a brick MOC mountain need?**

A: A medium-sized build takes about 1,400 pieces, of which slope pieces and wedge plates make up more than 40 percent, and it takes 8 to 10 hours. A large mountain can exceed 3,000 pieces. Sort your parts by color against the parts list before you start.

**Q: Is it worth buying instructions for a brick MOC mountain?**

A: Yes. Instructions usually cost 20 to 50 RMB and save you a large amount of trial and error. The layered building approach and the inward-leaning cliff structure are both valuable techniques, and even a newcomer following the instructions can produce a solid result.
