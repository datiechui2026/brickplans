# Brick Motorization Kits Compared: Which Drive System to Buy

Short answer first: for most builders, a domestic motor kit with standard connectors is the best buy at around CNY 120, and it will happily drive a model of roughly 1,500 pieces. If you want original-brand feel and compatibility, go with the LEGO power set — but budget at least three times as much. If you are handy with electronics, a DIY build saves money but costs you time. I have motorized models with all three approaches, and below I score them across five dimensions.

**1. Price: the domestic kit wins outright.** A domestic motor plus battery box runs about CNY 120 for a set. A single LEGO motor alone costs CNY 80–150, and a complete conversion lands around CNY 400. A DIY build using an ordinary motor and reduction gears can be pushed under CNY 50, but you still need to buy wire and a switch on top of that.

**2. Performance: the LEGO power set is the most stable.** LEGO motors deliver linear torque and fine low-speed control, so a motorized sports car model holds a steady pace. Domestic motors generally run at higher RPM — about 80% of kits need reduction gears added before the model can climb a slope — but they make up for it with raw power and can pull large vehicles of around 2,000 pieces.

**3. Installation difficulty: DIY is the hardest.** Domestic kits use standardized connectors, so the battery box, motor and extension cable are plug-and-play; a beginner can motorize a small car in under 30 minutes. A LEGO power set needs planned cable routing and takes about an hour. A DIY build requires soldering and tuning, usually half a day or more.

**4. Who it suits: domestic kits fit the vast majority of builders**, especially as a first powered project for kids. The LEGO power set suits experienced builders who want original-brand feel. DIY is only recommended for builders with an electronics background.

**5. Is it worth it? Overall scores out of 5** — Domestic kit: price 5, performance 4, ease of use 4.5. LEGO power set: price 2, performance 5, ease of use 4. DIY build: price 4.5, performance 3.5, ease of use 2. If your budget is limited, go straight for the domestic kit: the cost of a failed conversion is low, and buying a replacement set if something burns out is no big deal.

## FAQ

**Q: What parts do I need to motorize a brick model?**
A: The basics are a motor, a battery box, an extension cable and a gear set; a domestic kit covers all of it for around CNY 120. Before you start, check the plans on BrickPlan to confirm the part count, and practise on a small car of 1,500 pieces or fewer.

**Q: Can I mix domestic motors with a LEGO power set?**
A: The connectors are not compatible, and mixing requires an adapter cable — but the power delivery does not match reliably, so it is not recommended. Run one brand's system throughout: the conversion is more dependable, and later maintenance and upgrades are easier.

**Q: Is brick motorization safe for kids?**
A: Choose 3C-certified battery boxes and low-voltage motors (3–6V) and it is safe; an adult should supervise the conversion. Avoid wiring rechargeable lithium batteries directly to a motor, since over-discharge can damage it.

**Q: How fast will a motorized brick model go?**
A: An ordinary domestic motor driving a car of about 1,500 pieces reaches roughly 0.3–0.5 meters per second on flat ground — fine for indoor demonstrations. Going faster means switching to a high-speed motor, but climbing ability drops noticeably.
