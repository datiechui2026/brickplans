# Chinese Military Brick Sets: Tanks, Jets and Warships Compared

Military models are where Chinese brick brands have quietly taken over the value end of the hobby. You no longer need to spend four figures to own a detailed 99A main battle tank, a J-20 fighter or a carrier that actually earns shelf space. **SEMBO, Sluban, PANLOS, GUDI and Double Eagle** have pushed entry prices for military sets down into the tens-to-low-hundreds of yuan range. This guide walks through each brand's military line and gives straight answers on subjects, scale and price.

## SEMBO: the flagship of Chinese military bricks

**SEMBO** has the widest military catalogue of any domestic brand, covering land, air and sea. The land flagship is the **99A main battle tank**, with tracks, turret and add-on armour reproduced properly, scaled at roughly 1:32 to match minifigures, and priced around 100 yuan. In the air, the **J-20 stealth fighter** and the J-15 carrier fighter both hold their shape well, with printed cockpits that read convincingly. The naval line is the real centrepiece: the Liaoning and Shandong carrier sets run from two or three thousand pieces up to more than ten thousand, with full deck, island and carrier aircraft detail, and several hundred yuan is enough to take one home. SEMBO's printed elements and stickers are consistently good, so even a first-time builder can finish a set cleanly.

## Sluban: military specialists

**Sluban** was one of the first Chinese brands to treat military models as a standalone product line, and its **M38 WWII tank series** is the signature: Tiger, Sherman, T-34 and other classics released in rotation, with moving tracks and elevating gun barrels, plus soldier figures for small battlefield scenes. A single set runs 100-200 yuan. Sluban also offers a broad range of modern military vehicles and military trucks. Clutch is firm and value is excellent, which makes the brand a particularly good entry point for tank enthusiasts.

## PANLOS and GUDI: quality versus value

**PANLOS** sits in the mid-to-high tier. Its military line leans toward **large-scale tanks and warships**, with mould precision and printed parts a notch above the entry brands, and finished models that hold up well on display. Expect to pay somewhat more than for SEMBO or GUDI; this is aimed at experienced builders who want a refined finished piece.

**GUDI** goes the other way and leads on value. Tanks, armoured vehicles and helicopters are all covered, colours are accurate and the structures are sturdy, and most kits land between a few dozen yuan and 200-300 yuan. For military fans on a tight budget, it is the first place to look.

## Double Eagle: radio-controlled military bricks

If you want a tank that does more than sit on a shelf, **Double Eagle** is worth a look. Its tracked tanks and armoured cars use motorised drive, so they move forward and back and pivot on the spot, and some models can rotate the turret as well. You finish the build and immediately take it for a drive, which makes for the most interactive option here. Prices generally fall in the 200-500 yuan range, and the play value suits both adults who like moving models and families building together with kids.

## Choosing a scale and a budget

- **Minifigure scale (roughly 1:32):** takes soldier figures for dioramas, offers the widest subject choice and the most interaction, and is the most affordable. Best first purchase.
- **Small scale (around 1:72):** compact and easy to display, but detail is naturally more limited.
- **Large display scale:** highest piece counts and the richest detail, aimed at shelf builders. Budget at least 300-500 yuan.

On spending order: newcomers should start with a minifigure-scale tank to learn the techniques, then move up to a J-20 or a carrier once they are comfortable. Jumping straight into a huge set risks being put off by a mountain of same-colour parts.

## The short version

For value, look at SEMBO and GUDI. For detail and finish, PANLOS. For moving, driving models, Double Eagle. And if tanks specifically are your obsession, keep a close eye on Sluban's catalogue. Decide your scale and budget first, then match the set to your subject, and it is hard to go badly wrong.
