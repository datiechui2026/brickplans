# Courtyard Fountain MOC Review: A Crank-Driven Water Feature

Here is the short version: this Courtyard Fountain MOC is worth picking up. It uses 1,862 pieces to recreate a fountain with a moving water effect, sells for roughly CNY 268, and takes about 4 hours to build. It works well both as a display piece and as a family build, and it earns an overall score of 8.6/10.

## 1. Exterior Accuracy

The fountain body recreates the look of carved European garden stone, with three water basins stacked one above the other. The base carries 12 plants and two angel statues, and the colour scheme runs from off-white to light grey. Turn the crank and the water climbs in a spiral along the channels before falling back down — in terms of motion, this sits at the top of its class among comparable MOCs. The finished model measures about 25 x 20 x 18 cm, so it photographs well on a desk or in an entryway.

## 2. Build Experience

The instructions are split into 6 major steps, and the parts come in 18 numbered bags, so you can open one bag at a time and keep building. The core rotation mechanism uses 3 gears and 1 long axle, and the drive runs smoothly without jamming. A few of the smaller parts need a firm press, so a brick separator is worth keeping nearby. Overall difficulty is low-to-medium, and a newcomer should finish in around 4 hours.

## 3. Part Quality

Bricks clutch tightly and the tolerances are well controlled, so repeated assembly and disassembly does not loosen the connections. The parts have an even surface finish, with almost no scratches or flash to be found, and the manual is clearly printed with strongly differentiated colours, which makes sorting efficient. The one small letdown is the sticker on the inside of the water basin, which tends to lift at the edges — press it down firmly during assembly and it stays put.

## 4. Value for Money

CNY 268 for 1,862 pieces works out to roughly CNY 0.14 per piece, which is an above-average result for an original MOC of this size. Given the moving mechanism and the generous accessory count, the price is justified, and it suits micro-landscape builders working with a budget of CNY 200 to 300.

## FAQ

**Q: How does this courtyard fountain actually move?**

A: Turn the crank on the lower right-hand side. The gears and long axle drive the three water basins in a slow rotation, and the water rises along the channels before dropping back down. No batteries or motors are involved — it is purely mechanical, and children can operate it easily.

**Q: Can the brick courtyard fountain go outdoors?**

A: Long-term outdoor display is not recommended. The parts are mainly ABS plastic, and direct sun will speed up fading and ageing; rain that gets inside will make the mechanism stiff and prone to catching. Keep it on an indoor desk or a shaded balcony, and wipe it with a dry cloth for routine cleaning.

**Q: How big is the finished fountain, and is it worth buying?**

A: The finished model measures about 25 x 20 x 18 cm, with 1,862 pieces and roughly 4 hours of building. Both the moving water effect and the exterior accuracy are strong, and it scores 8.6 overall. Builders who enjoy micro-landscapes and mechanical structures will get their money's worth.

**Q: How is this fountain different from a normal set?**

A: It is a fan-designed original MOC rather than an official mass-produced set. The rotating three-basin structure is a bespoke design, so you will not find the same model on the shelf anywhere else. The building instructions remain the copyright of the original designer, so confirm the licensing before you buy.
