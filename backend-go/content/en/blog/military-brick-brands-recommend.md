# Military Brick Brands and MOC Reviews: 99A, Z-10, Type 055

Military hardware has long been one of the biggest branches of brick MOC building. From the imposing 99A main battle tank to the J-20 fighter jet, recreating the hard edges and mechanical beauty of military equipment in brick is a shared passion for armor fans and builders alike. Below is a focused look at three popular military MOC plans and the brands that make them possible, so you can pick a direction with confidence.

## Product overview

This review covers three popular military MOC plans: the **99A main battle tank** (1800+ pieces), the **Z-10 attack helicopter** (1200+ pieces) and the **Type 055 destroyer** (2800+ pieces). The three plans represent land, air and sea equipment respectively, with part counts spanning 500 to 3000+ pieces across the category, so both beginners and experienced builders are covered. We also tie in brand recommendations and look at where each manufacturer is strongest on military subjects.

## Build difficulty: ★★★★☆

Military MOCs are generally demanding builds, mainly because of the complex silhouettes and the multi-faceted structures of the real vehicles. The 99A's challenge sits in the faceted turret armor and the track and suspension system, which call for heavy use of wedge bricks and slope bricks to capture the tank's sloped armor. The Z-10 is tricky around the rotor head and the streamlined transition of the fuselage. The Type 055 takes the most work on the bridge and weapon systems, and the sheer number of small detail parts on the deck is striking. Overall, military MOCs ask for decent spatial reasoning and structural analysis.

## Parts review

On the brand side we recommend **Sluban, SEMBO and Panlos**. Sluban has the deepest catalogue in this genre: its military color families, including army green, desert tan and ocean grey, are unusually complete, and its custom-molded missile tubes, radar antennas and track links are genuine assets for military MOCs. SEMBO stands out on detail accuracy; its printed parts are high quality, and pre-printed insignia and numbering save you the sticker work. Panlos is strong on large ship models, with stable quality control on its hull-specific and deck parts. All three have a strong following among military fans and are the first stop when planning a military MOC.

## Building experience

Assembling a military MOC feels like putting together a miniature piece of equipment. The 99A plan is thoughtfully designed, from the torsion-bar-style suspension in the hull to the rotating turret mechanism, with every detail aiming at realism. The highlight is the composite armor on the turret: stacked layers of wedge bricks reproduce the 99A's arrow-shaped armor layout. The Z-10 uses plenty of transparent parts for the cockpit glazing, and the missile pylons and autocannon are detailed enough that the finished model has real presence. The Type 055 is the biggest build of the three: the hull is split into modules, and the deck structures step up from bow to stern, delivering a sense of accomplishment that is hard to describe. ## Finished models

A finished military MOC is the ultimate display piece for a hardcore fan. The 99A measures about 35 cm long, with a turret that rotates 360 degrees, an elevating gun barrel and working tracks you can roll it on. The Z-10's rotors turn by hand, the landing gear retracts, and the airframe is packed with detail. The Type 055 comes in at 50 cm long with a complete bridge, radar and vertical launch system, and it dominates a display cabinet. Displayed alone or as part of a diorama, these models keep people looking.
