# Toy Story LEGO Instructions: Characters, Sets & Andy's Room

Toy Story has been part of childhood for three generations now, and the friendship between Woody and Buzz remains one of Pixar's high points. If you want to bring that affection into the brick world, the first step is finding reliable Toy Story LEGO instructions. This guide walks you through the official sets, the character builds, the scene techniques and where to source the plans, one step at a time.

## 1. A Look at the Official Toy Story Sets

Most official Toy Story LEGO instructions exist as set booklets rather than standalone plans, but they are still valuable: the part selections have been tested, and the accuracy is dependable. Sets worth watching include:

## 2. Popular Character Plans: Woody, Buzz and Jessie

Most Toy Story LEGO instructions circulating today centre on the three leads. For Sheriff Woody, the focus is the cowboy hat and the checked shirt: the brim is angled with wedge bricks, the shirt is built by alternating red and yellow bricks, and a printed bandana finishes it off, so the minifigure reads almost like the real thing. Buzz Lightyear's challenge is the space suit: purple main armour with green shoulder plates, a transparent blue curved piece for the helmet visor, and hinge pieces so the wings can open and close. Jessie is a more niche minifigure, but the signature cowboy hat, braid and orange top make her instantly recognisable, and she is an excellent intermediate MOC exercise.

## 3. Scene Building: Putting Andy's Room Together

Once you have the characters, you need a stage. Andy's room is the most iconic scene in Toy Story LEGO instructions and the best one to practise on. Three core techniques matter:

1. **Bed load-bearing**: build an inner frame for the bed from technic pieces, then lay plates and tiles across it as the mattress surface, so it does not sag after long display.
2. **Give the walls depth**: do not apply the cloud wallpaper flat. Use SNOT techniques to mount bricks sideways and create a shallow relief effect.
3. **Hidden mechanisms**: leave space under the bed for the toy chest, and use hinge pieces so the lid opens and closes, recreating the film's idea of toys moving quietly on their own.

More advanced builders can also try Pizza Planet, where a rotating platform base and a neon sign add a great deal to the display.

## 4. Where to Get Instructions and How to Start
