# Lamborghini Sián 1:8 MOC Review: 2,680-Piece Technic Build

**Difficulty:** ★★★★★ (Advanced), not an entry-level purchase.  
**Part count:** 2,680 | **Recommended brands:** Double Eagle, Mould King  
**Finished scale:** 1:8 | **Finished length:** about 52 cm | **Estimated build time:** 15+ hours

Every Technic builder has a graduation exam, and for me it is the 1:8 supercar. This Lamborghini Sián MOC uses 2,680 pieces to build a working V12 piston engine, an 8-speed sequential gearbox, scissor doors, and an adjustable rear wing into one car. The first time I opened the parts list, my reaction was simple: this actually moves?

## 1. Accuracy: the body language of a 1:8 scale car

Lamborghini design is built on sharp angles and a wedge shape, and the Sián is an especially clear example. 1:8 is the common large scale for supercar MOCs, and the benefit is that body lines can be recreated section by section with slopes and curved pieces: the low, pressed-down nose, the side ducts, the central rear exhaust, and the electronically controlled rear wing all have real presence. At about 52 cm long, the finished model reads as a display model car rather than a toy.

## 2. Drivetrain: the real core of this car

The best part of the Sián blueprint is not the exterior. It is the transmission. The working V12 piston engine is linked to the gearbox through the crankshaft, so when the car is in gear the pistons rise and fall with speed. The 8-speed sequential gearbox uses paddle shifting and gives a real upshift and downshift feel. Steering is controlled by the steering wheel or a knob on the roof, with the front wheels turning together.

This kind of structure is extremely sensitive to part tolerances. Read the [LEGO Technic beginner guide](/en/blog/lego-technic-beginners-guide) first and get gear ratios, differentials, and axle systems clear in your head before you start. Otherwise you can easily end up with the classic frustration: fully built, but it will not turn.

## 3. Parts and color scheme: quantity and quality of Technic elements

At 2,680 pieces, this is the highest part count in this review batch, and Technic elements dominate: beams, pins, gears, axles, and connectors. The more parts a model has, the more important planning becomes. The instructions recommend exporting the parts list with the [how to order parts from a blueprint](/en/blog/how-to-order-parts-by-blueprint) method, then sorting everything into three piles: parts you already own, parts you must buy, and parts that can be replaced. That avoids getting stuck halfway through because one small part is missing.

For color, the Sián's signature fluorescent green, or teal, is the main exterior color. It is a rare color, so it costs more and is sensitive to batch-to-batch color differences. Buy the exterior panels in one batch if possible. Internal structural parts can use compatible parts from Chinese brands to save budget for the exterior. Double Eagle and Mould King are listed as suitable Technic-compatible sources, and their elements cover the internal structure well.

## 4. Build priorities

1. **Drivetrain first, body last:** build and hand-test the full power path from engine to gearbox to differential to wheels before you wrap it in bodywork.
2. **Do not change gear ratios casually:** the original ratios are tested. Swapping tooth counts can cause binding or even stripped gears.
3. **Push axles fully through:** a cross axle that is not inserted deep enough is the number one cause of a mechanism that will not turn. After assembly, turn the wheels by hand to confirm.
4. **Leave clearance for the scissor doors:** keep a 1-unit gap at the door hinge. Too tight, and the door will scrape the paintwork.
5. **Build in stages:** work through chassis, power, body, and doors as four stages. Test by power or by hand after each stage.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this Lamborghini Sián MOC suitable for beginners?**  
A: Not recommended as a first build. It is ★★★★★ difficulty with 2,680 pieces, and it involves a linked V12 engine and 8-speed gearbox. Complete two or three mid-to-large Technic sets first.

**Q: Does the V12 engine in the car really move?**  
A: Yes. The pistons are linked to the gearbox through the crankshaft. When the car is in gear and rolling, the pistons move up and down. The gearbox supports paddle shifting, and steering is controlled by the steering wheel or the roof knob.

**Q: If I am short of parts, can I use compatible third-party parts?**  
A: Yes. Compatible parts from Chinese brands are fully adequate for internal structure. For large exterior panels and special colors such as fluorescent green, use original or high-quality parts to avoid color differences and print deviation.

**Q: How long does it take to build 2,680 pieces?**  
A: An experienced builder needs about 15+ hours. Build in four stages and test after each one. Rework halfway through is expensive, so slow and correct beats fast and wrong.
