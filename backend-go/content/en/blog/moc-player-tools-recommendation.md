# MOC Builder Tool Guide: Separators, Press Plates and Sorting

A useful MOC tool kit is not about buying every gadget labeled professional. After years of testing and recommending tools for MOC builders, I have seen plenty of people buy a pile of flashy tools that mostly collect dust. This guide sorts the essentials from the optional extras based on hands-on use, so your money goes where it actually improves building, sorting, and disassembly.

## 1. Brick Separators: The Entry-Level Tool Worth Investing In

A brick separator is a core MOC tool. Almost every mistake, modification, or rebuild calls for one. There are two common types. The first is the original LEGO orange double-ended separator: the head can remove bricks and pry up plates, and it feels solid in hand. I strongly recommend that every builder owns one. The second is the domestic multi-in-one separator that costs tens of RMB and adds a pointed tip, scraper, and opener. It is versatile, but the head is thick, so removing small parts can damage bricks. My advice is direct: if your budget allows, buy the original; otherwise buy a domestic single-head copy of the original design, which costs under RMB 10. The double-ended multi-function version is not really necessary.

## 2. Press Plates: The Key to a Flat Finish

Many builders finish a model and feel that the surface is bulging. The usual cause is that plates were not pressed tight. Press plates, also called flattening tools, come in solid metal and roller versions. In testing, the solid press plate is the most practical. When you are building a large baseplate or a full wall, one pass can noticeably reduce lifted edges and gaps. The roller version suits large scene floors, but it can mar parts easily, so beginners should not buy it first. A cheaper option is to press the model overnight with a thick book or another heavy object. The result is close, just slower, so you can hold off on buying a press plate for now.

## 3. Parts Sorting Boxes: Storage That Doubles Prep Efficiency

Once an MOC builder has a lot of parts, finding them can take longer than building. Parts sorting boxes are worth buying, but do not jump straight to a luxury model with hundreds of compartments. The right approach is to use small boxes by color, medium boxes by part type — plates, bricks, and special pieces — and a separate compartment for common parts. Based on testing, I recommend two-section locking storage boxes. They stack stably, the transparent sides let you see what is inside, and prices run from tens of RMB to just over RMB 100. They are far more usable than loose plastic bags. If you are just starting out, existing divided food containers or disposable paper cups can work as a temporary solution; upgrade this item later.

## 4. Tweezers and Engraving Tools: Buy by Need, Not by Trend

Tweezers are mainly for stickers, small decorations, and removing tiny parts. If your MOCs often include interiors or minifigure scenes, a pair of pointed tweezers is handy, and one around RMB 20 is enough. If you only build large models, you will barely use them. Engraving tools are toys for advanced builders: they score texture and weathering effects into brick surfaces. Beginners should stay away at first because they can ruin parts very easily. Light kits depend entirely on the subject. They are very photogenic in building night scenes and street scenes, but drilling wire holes and hiding the battery pack is a lot of trouble. Learn the structure side first, then consider lights.

## 5. Storage Cabinets: Buy Within Your Means, Not All at Once

Storage cabinets solve the problem of parts scattered all over the floor, but you will never buy enough — because you will keep buying parts. My advice is to buy small drawer cabinets as needed. First house your most-used 1,000 to 2,000 parts, then add more when you truly feel short of space. Do not start with a large parts wall. If you really want to save money, an IKEA grid cabinet with transparent drawer inserts is a value pick for many veteran builders.

## Final Thoughts

The practical takeaway from this MOC builder tool guide is simple: buy a brick separator and a press plate right away, add sorting boxes as needed, choose tweezers by subject, and wait on engraving tools, light kits, and storage cabinets. Search for the subject you like, and you will find designs with complete parts lists — a combination that works especially well with a parts sorting box for prep.
