# How to Build a LEGO MOC Night Scene: Glowing Windows and Lamps

Start with the conclusion: the core of a brick night scene is to darken the surroundings and light only selected spots. Lay a dark baseplate underneath, place light bricks at the windows and street lamps, and push the bright-to-dark contrast to roughly 1:5 — the night mood appears almost immediately. A two-storey street building needs only 6-8 light points and about 30 yuan in parts to reach 90% of the night-scene effect.

## Step 1: Set the Tone with Dark Baseplates

The first step is making the ground go black. A dark grey or navy baseplate works best; pick the size to match your street scene, for example a 32x32 stud baseplate costs about 25 yuan. You can also lay black baseplates for the roadway and dark grey for the sidewalk, which gives the ground some layering instead of one flat dark surface.

Keep the walls dark as well — dark grey, navy, dark red — and hold light-coloured bricks to under 20% of the total, otherwise the night scene turns as bright as daytime. Leave gaps at the roof and around the signage for lamp positions, so that installing the lights later is a matter of dropping them in rather than rebuilding the facade.

## Step 2: Place Glowing Windows and Street Lamps

There are two ways to make a window glow:

1. Transparent window elements with an LED behind them — one LED per window, so six windows across two floors means about six LEDs.
2. Self-lit light bricks, roughly 5-10 yuan each, embedded straight into the wall.

For street lamps, use a 1x1 transparent bar plus an LED, standing 8-12 cm tall, and space them one lamp every 2-3 studs. Run all the wiring out of sight along the underside of the baseplate and gather it into a single battery box; runtime is about 4-6 hours, which is plenty for a photo session and a display shelf.

## Step 3: Build the Mood and Shoot It

The mood comes from contrast and colour temperature. Use warm yellow light (3000K) for the windows and a whiter light (4000K) for the street lamps — alternating the two temperatures reads more cinematic than one uniform colour. A little smoke or a dark backdrop cloth strengthens the night feel further.

When shooting, turn off the room lights and leave only the brick lighting on. Set ISO to 800 and the shutter to 1/30 s, and lock the camera on a tripod to prevent shake. Total build time is about 2-3 hours, of which the lighting work takes roughly 1 hour. For more night street-scene plans, browse the community MOCs at brickplan.cn.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I choose between light bricks and individual LEDs?**

A: Choose by need. LEDs are cheap and flexible at 1-3 yuan each, and suit windows and street lamps where the light is fixed in one spot. Light bricks are plug-and-play with even brightness at 5-10 yuan each, and suit large illuminated areas. If the budget is tight, use LEDs throughout; if you want convenience, mix the two — total cost for one building can stay around 30-50 yuan.

**Q: Is it worth building a dedicated night version?**

A: Yes. Night lighting is the shortcut to better-looking work: the same street scene displays noticeably better with the lights on, and likes on social platforms are typically 50% higher than for the daytime version. LEDs are cheap and can be removed and reinstalled repeatedly without affecting the existing structure.

**Q: How do I hide the wires so they can't be seen?**

A: Route them along the back of the baseplate and inside the walls. First leave 1x1 or 1x2 holes in the baseplate, pass the wire through to the underside, then run it along the baseplate grooves and gather it at the battery box. Wall wires hide between the two layers of a sandwich wall. Leave some slack at corners so the wires don't get pulled and break.

**Q: My night photos always come out blurry — what should I do?**

A: In low light, handheld shots will always blur. Use a tripod, or prop the phone against a book to hold it steady, keep the shutter at 1/30 s or faster, and use a 2-second delay to avoid shake when you press the button. You can raise ISO to 800, but not above 1600, or the noise becomes obvious. Take several frames and pick the sharpest one.

**Q: How big is the difference between a daytime and a night shot?**

A: Very big. In daylight the ambient light overwhelms the light bricks and you can barely see them; with the room lights off and contrast above 1:5, the warm glow from the windows and street lamps finally shows. The same model shot in daylight is a street scene — shot at night it becomes a night scene. Shooting one of each makes a good comparison pair.
