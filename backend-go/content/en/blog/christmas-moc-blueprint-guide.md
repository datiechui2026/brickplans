# How to Build a Christmas Brick MOC: Tree, Snow House, Lighting

Every December, a Christmas MOC is the theme builders most want to get their hands on. The colors are loud, the decoration options are endless, and the subject is forgiving: even if a structure is slightly off, the festive mood covers it. This guide focuses on combining red, green and gold with a white snow scene, breaking the build down layer by layer from the tree and snow house to the gifts and lighting, so you can put a miniature world on your desk that feels like Christmas the moment you look at it.

## 1. The Christmas tree: the hero column of the scene

The tree is the undisputed centerpiece of a Christmas brick MOC. Build it as a tapered, stepped cone: each layer from the bottom up is one ring smaller than the one below it. Alternate light and dark green pieces as you stack to suggest the layered structure of real branches, and finish the tip with a gold star element. Use brown round bricks or cylinders for the trunk, then lay a ring of white "snow" around the base.

For decoration, hang small gold and red ball elements at an angle between the branches, and clip a few short string-light elements near the top. A ring of red-and-white candy canes around the foot of the tree instantly gives it the busy, lit-up feel of a real one.

## 2. Snow house and chimney: warmth inside a winter cottage

For the snow house, go with a classic pitched-roof cottage. Build the walls from white and light grey pieces to suggest snow cover, then deliberately press a few white curved slopes onto the roof, the window sills and above the door — the snow layer reads immediately.

The chimney is the important part. Stack dark red or dark brown pieces into a square column, cap the top with white pieces to simulate settled snow, and add a puff of "smoke" at the mouth: two or three small white cotton-like pieces stacked together is enough. Place a warm yellow light brick inside the window so it glows through a translucent window pane; the warm interior against the white exterior creates the warm-cool contrast that does most of the atmospheric work.

## 3. Gift pile, snowman and small festive props

Arrange a gift pile in front of the house: stack small cube bricks in red, green, gold and blue three or four layers high, set a bow element at an angle on the top box, and vary the heights so it reads like the pile under a tree. For the snowman, stack three white round pieces, add an orange cone for the nose, small black pieces for the eyes, and a red scarf element.

Finish the scene with a green wreath at the door, a string of lights along the eaves, and a few red pieces on the roof standing in for Santa hats. Scatter white granules under the street lamp to simulate falling snow — that small detail is what finally lights the whole scene up.

## 4. Color scheme and lighting layout

The rule of thumb for a Christmas brick MOC is: red and green as the base, gold as the accent, white on top. Keep the buildings and tree mostly green and white, then use red and gold on the gifts and decorations to lift the composition — this keeps the whole thing from turning busy. Lighting is the atmosphere amplifier: sink transparent warm-light bricks into the windows and the treetops, and let the white-granule "snow field" underneath bounce it back for a soft holiday glow.

For the base, use a white baseplate with transparent pieces to make an ice surface. Group the tree, house and figures toward the middle and leave open space around the edges — that composition has the feel of a Christmas card.

## 5. Beginner advice and parts list

If you are new to this, start with a small scene: just the tree plus a gift pile. Once the tapered, inward-stepping structure feels natural, expand into the snow house. Parts list:

- Plenty of red, green and gold plates, plus small decorative ball elements
- White pieces for snow cover, the snowman and the base
- Transparent warm-light bricks and string-light elements — the key to the lighting
- Brown pieces for the trunk and chimney
- Festive accessories: snowman parts, candy canes, wreath, bow

Store the red, green and white parts in separate bins so everything is obvious at a glance while you build.

From the tree and snow house to the gifts and lighting, as long as you hold to the red-green-gold palette and put the warm light and falling-snow details to work, this Christmas brick MOC will have the whole room celebrating early.
