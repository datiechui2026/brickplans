# Stationery Shop MOC Review: 560-Part Cozy Street Scene

Here is the short answer first: this stationery shop MOC is an instruction set worth buying. A stationery shop is an unusual subject for a street scene, the detailing is charming, and it photographs beautifully. It runs to roughly 560 parts and takes about two hours to build, which makes it close to a safe first street-scene MOC for a beginner.

The colour scheme and the small details are the strongest part of the design. The mint green and white shopfront looks clean without being plain, and the pencils, books and globe in the display window are built as separate small models rather than suggested with stickers or left to the imagination. Drop the shop into a street layout and it immediately becomes the visual anchor of the row.

## 1. Part accuracy (9/10)

The shopfront pairs mint green with white, and the display window — pencils, books and a globe — is built from individual small sub-builds, so the stationery-shop character reads clearly from the street. The parts list runs to about 560 pieces, and more than 85% of them are standard bricks, so there is almost nothing rare or hard to source. If you have ever chased down discontinued colours for a modular building, you will appreciate how much difference that ratio makes: gathering the parts from the list here is unusually painless.

## 2. Building experience (9/10)

The build takes around two hours across just over 30 steps. The sequence is logical and smooth, and you will barely need to pull anything apart to correct a mistake. The single-storey structure occupies about 14x14 studs, which is compatible with mainstream street-scene modules; set it on a pavement baseplate and it lines up tightly with the shops on either side. Because it is only one storey, it slots in easily as a smaller unit between taller buildings, and the whole process is genuinely relaxing rather than fiddly.

## 3. Interior details and value (8.5/10)

Inside there is a cash register, a display cabinet and a bookshelf with an opening door, plus a mini class-timetable sticker on the wall — plenty of detail for a building this size. The instructions cost about CNY 12, the cheapest among comparable street-scene instructions, and the parts come to roughly CNY 70, so the value is high. The one weak point: there is only a single reserved position for interior lighting, so if you want to light the shop you will have to plan the wiring yourself.

## FAQ

**Is the stationery shop MOC suitable for beginners?**
Yes, very. Few parts, a straightforward step order and few unusual pieces mean you can finish it in about two hours, and the diagrams are clear. As a first street-scene MOC it is close to zero-threshold.

**Can it be combined with LEGO street scenes?**
Yes. The baseplate size and connection points follow standard street-scene specifications, so it connects directly to street scenes from mainstream brands. It sits about half a plate lower in height; add one filler plate underneath and it aligns perfectly.

**How much does the stationery shop MOC cost?**
The instructions are about CNY 12 and include the manual and parts list; some platforms also include the sticker file. Together with the parts it comes to roughly CNY 80, which is considerably cheaper than buying a finished set.

**How is it different from other shop instructions?**
The differences are subject and size. The stationery shop is about 560 pieces and leans on cozy detail; the burger shop is about 680 pieces and the barbecue shop about 920. The stationery shop is the smallest and makes a good starting point for assembling a small shopping street.

**Can I print the stickers myself?**
Yes. The instructions come with a sticker PDF, which prints on ordinary A4 paper or label paper; the sizes are already laid out to fit the parts, and printing them yourself saves the cost of buying stickers separately.
