# Terracotta Army Brick MOC: Build a 2,000-Piece Qin Army

The short answer first: if you want a terracotta warrior MOC to actually look right, three things carry the build — layered armor, an accurate topknot, and the formation scene. This plan covers three figure types (kneeling archer, general, soldier), runs about 2,000 pieces, takes roughly 4 hours, and sits on a 32x32 baseplate, so the finished model is a miniature Qin army on your desk.

The terracotta warriors are a national-treasure subject, and the hard part of a brick version is not the piece count — it is the likeness. Here is the full build, figure by figure, then formation.

Step 1: Build the soldiers. The soldiers are the backbone of the army, and you need 8 of them. Start the body in tan and dark tan, then lay dark brown 1x2 slope pieces in two horizontal rows to get the layered scale-armor look. The topknot is the make-or-break detail: stack black round plates three high on the head, insetting each layer by half a stud, then push in a small bar as the hairpin. From the side, the silhouette reads correctly.

Step 2: Build the kneeling archer. This is the hardest figure. The right knee is on the ground, the left leg is drawn up, and the body's weight sits toward the back. Build the legs first, then the torso, and leave a turntable piece at the waist so the upper body can lean slightly forward and reproduce the draw pose. Pre-build a clip at the right hand so the crossbow can be added later.

Step 3: Build the general. The general gets the standout color scheme: gold trim pieces on the armor, and the topknot switched to a double-curl shape made from two curved pieces clipped face to face. He stands half a head taller than the soldiers, so add one raising plate under his base — in the formation the commander is obvious at a glance.

Step 4: Build the formation base and scene. The baseplate is 32x32. Put the kneeling archers in the front row, the soldiers in the middle, and the general centered in the back, with chariots and weapon racks on both flanks. The chariot uses a simplified two-horse design, and the weapon rack is three tiers of brown rod pieces holding spears and crossbow elements. Layering the ranks like this instantly gives the scene depth.

Step 5: Finish the details. Clip the crossbow into the kneeling archer, give the general a long sword, and add four small posts at the corners of the baseplate as railings. Fully built, the model comes to about 2,000 pieces and 4 hours of work, roughly 26 cm square. Shoot it with side lighting and the armor layers photograph beautifully.

## FAQ

**Q: Where do I find terracotta warrior brick MOC instructions?**
A: Free plans are available on the BrickPlan website and official account, including a parts list and step-by-step instructions for about 2,000 pieces. Following the plan takes roughly 4 hours; beginners should build a soldier first for practice, then attempt the kneeling archer.

**Q: Is a terracotta warrior brick model worth buying?**
A: Yes. At about 2,000 pieces and 4 hours of build time, it reproduces three figure types plus a complete formation on a 32x32 baseplate. It offers both building fun and display value, and carries more cultural weight than an ordinary set.

**Q: How do I get real depth in the armor?**
A: Use dark brown slope pieces in two horizontal rows, offsetting each row by half a stud to mimic scale armor. The soldier armor in the BrickPlan plan uses about 30 slope pieces, and the layering shows up clearly under side light. Do not substitute smooth tiles.

**Q: My kneeling archer keeps tipping over. What do I do?**
A: The kneeling archer's weight sits back, so it tends to fall backward. Widen the baseplate by one stud, or place two plates under the left leg. The first step of the BrickPlan plan specifically reinforces the kneeling base, so a faithful build will not fall.

**Q: Can kids build this?**
A: Ages 10 and up are recommended. The kneeling archer and general use quite a few turntable and clip pieces that need some hand strength. Under 10, build it as a parent-and-child project: an adult handles the armor, and the child lays out the formation.
