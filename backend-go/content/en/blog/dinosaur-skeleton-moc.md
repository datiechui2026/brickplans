# Dinosaur Skeleton MOC: Build a Displayable T. rex Skeleton

Short answer first: if you want a brick-built T. rex skeleton good enough to sit on a shelf, go for a mid-size plan of around 1,200 pieces and about 45 cm long. Parts will run you roughly 300 yuan, a beginner can finish it in 6 to 8 hours, and the finished skeleton has real depth on a desk or in a display cabinet.

Dinosaur skeleton MOCs are a popular theme in the brick community. You use bricks to suggest bone, exposing ribs, vertebrae and skull, and the result is a decorative piece with a scientific feel to it. Below is a workable route from choosing a plan to building it step by step.

## 1. How to Choose a Plan

Check three numbers before anything else: piece count, finished size and difficulty. A count of 800 to 1,500 pieces works best — too few and the detail is thin, too many and the build stalls half-finished. A finished length of 40 to 60 cm is the sweet spot for a display piece. Favor plans built from common bricks so rare parts don't drive up the cost. On a 1,200-piece T. rex plan, the skull is around 180 pieces, the spine and ribs around 500, and the limbs around 400 — scaled at roughly 1:18 to a real T. rex, which reads as visually proportionate.

## Step 1: Gather the Parts from the List

Before you touch a brick, work through the parts list. Export it with brickplan's part statistics feature and sort the pieces by color and type. Skeleton builds usually call for white, beige and dark gray; white is closest to real bone. Buy about 5% extra of the small pieces that go missing easily, such as 1x1 bricks and connection pins, so a shortage doesn't stop the build.

## Step 2: Base and Pelvis

Order matters here. Build the base and pelvis first as the load-bearing structure. Fix the pelvis at the center, then extend the spine upward — that way every rib section added later has a stable anchor, and the finished model doesn't wobble.

## Step 3: Spine and Ribs

Light the spine is the soul of the skeleton. Build it segment by segment from the tail vertebrae up to the neck vertebrae, joining each pair with hinge pieces so you can pose the head in a slight downward tilt. Ribs extend symmetrically outward from both sides of the thoracic vertebrae; keep them all facing the same direction and the ribcage will have proper depth.

## Step 4: Skull and Limbs

Build the skull on its own, then attach it to the neck vertebrae. Use slope bricks plus small white 1x1 bricks for the open jaw and teeth. For the limbs, build the heavy rear legs before the front legs to match real proportions. Finish by checking that every connection point is solid.

## Display and Presentation

A finished skeleton can go into an acrylic display case to keep dust off, and a small nameplate adds a nice touch. A warm spotlight in the evening brings out the layered shadows — it works equally well in a living room or a study.

## FAQ

**Q: Can a beginner build a dinosaur skeleton MOC?**
A: Yes. Pick an 800 to 1,500 piece plan and follow the steps; a beginner can finish in 6 to 8 hours. No special tools are needed — a brick separator is enough. It's easier than you'd expect.

**Q: How is a dinosaur skeleton MOC different from a regular brick set?**
A: A regular set has one fixed official design. MOC plans are designed by builders, the bone structure is more realistic, and you can swap colors or change the pose freely. Both the building experience and the finished look are more distinctive, and the sense of accomplishment is bigger.

**Q: How much does it cost to build a T. rex skeleton?**
A: Parts cost roughly 200 to 300 yuan, and most plans are free or a few dozen yuan each — considerably cheaper than an official set of the same size, which makes it friendly for builders on a budget.

**Q: Can the finished skeleton be used directly as a display piece?**
A: Yes. With the base attached it stands stably, and at about 45 cm long it suits a desk, display cabinet or TV console. A spotlight improves the effect, and a display case keeps the dust off.

**Q: What if some parts on the plan aren't available?**
A: Use brickplan's parts list feature to source everything by color and quantity in one go. Common bricks are all available, and for rare parts you can find substitutes in a different color or size.
