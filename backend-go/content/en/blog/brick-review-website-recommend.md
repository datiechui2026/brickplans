# Brick Review Sites Worth Bookmarking: A Buyer's Guide

Checking reviews before buying a set has become routine for a lot of builders. The problem is that review sites are everywhere: some focus on LEGO, some on Chinese brands, and plenty are little more than paid advertorials. Sorting the useful ones from the noise takes time. This guide breaks the landscape down by platform type, so you know what each kind of site does well and which ones deserve a bookmark.

## Professional review sites: depth is the whole point

Dedicated review outlets are characterised by complete, systematic testing. They pull a set apart category by category — brick precision, clutch power, colour accuracy, build feel, instruction quality and value for money — and often set the model against direct competitors. This type of site is the right stop when you are doing homework before a purchase, especially on an expensive set. One thorough review can save you from a fair amount of impulse buying.

The trade-off is timing. Professional outlets work on long cycles, so a newly released set may not have coverage right away. Some also take brand partnerships, so watch for labels such as "sponsored" or "in partnership with" and read accordingly.

## Player communities: where the honest experiences live

Any serious list of brick review sites has to include player communities. On forums and discussion boards, ordinary builders post unboxings, describe how a set actually felt to build, and write dedicated posts listing the weak points of specific models. Community reviews are less structured than professional coverage, but that is exactly the strength — nobody is paying a regular builder to be flattering.

When you browse a forum, look for long-form reviews and follow-up posts. The impression on day one and the impression a month later are often very different. Whether bricks yellow over time or whether a mechanism loosens up are the kind of details only experienced builders think to mention.

## Video and written-post platforms: fast and visual

Video platforms and image-and-text communities are where most reviews live now. Video lets you watch clutch power and turning mechanisms in action, which beats any written description for sheer clarity. Written posts, meanwhile, are ideal for skimming a quick pros-and-cons summary. Cross-checking a few platforms will build up a reasonably complete picture of a set.

The weakness is fragmentation. You may need to sit through several videos on the same set before the full picture emerges, and quality varies wildly, so some filtering is on you.

## Instruction-sharing review sites: read the review, then build it

One more category deserves attention: communities that combine reviews with building instructions. Take brickplan.cn as an example — the site hosts instruction resources for a wide range of models alongside build write-ups and reviews shared by builders. If a review convinces you, you can go straight to the matching instructions and start building, which makes the path from reading to making unusually short. These platforms suit MOC builders particularly well, because the reviews tend to centre on instruction quality, parts lists and build difficulty — precisely what MOC builders care about most.

## Which one should you use?

There is no objectively best review site; it depends on what you need. Doing homework before buying a set? Go to professional media. Want genuine word of mouth? Browse player communities. Want the quick version? Watch video platforms. Building MOCs? Spend time in instruction-sharing communities. Bookmark one site from each category and you will dodge a few bad purchases along the way.
