# How to Choose Your First MOC Theme: A 5-Factor Scoring Method

Short answer first: there is no single correct MOC theme, but there is a reliable method. Score each candidate theme on five dimensions — interest, difficulty, parts, budget and display — and the highest total becomes your next build. The whole exercise takes about 10 minutes.

Plenty of builders post in the BrickPlan community saying they have no idea what to build. The problem usually isn't a shortage of ideas; it's having too many and being afraid to commit. Choose the wrong theme and every step that follows feels like a grind. Here's how to break the decision down dimension by dimension.

**Dimension 1: Interest fit (30% weight).** Choose a subject you'd happily stare at for 100 hours. Into sci-fi? Build a mech or a spaceship. Into everyday life? Build a street-corner shop or a small house. Write down three candidate themes and list five details about each that genuinely excite you — the one with the longest list wins.

**Dimension 2: Difficulty ladder (25% weight).** Sort by part count: 200-500 pieces is entry level and the right range for a first MOC; 800-1500 pieces is intermediate; 3000+ is master level. Beginners who start at entry level finish their build roughly 50% more often.

**Dimension 3: Part availability (20% weight).** Search the BrickPlan parts library for the pieces your theme uses most. Architecture builds need plenty of standard bricks and window frames; mechs need joints and slopes. If special parts make up more than 10% of the total part count, switch themes or adjust the design.

**Dimension 4: Budget headroom (15% weight).** Estimate material cost at roughly CNY 8-12 per 100 pieces: a micro build lands around CNY 30-60, a mid-size build around CNY 100-200, and a large build CNY 500 or more. On a tight budget, favour "single colour scheme plus geometric shapes" themes — fewer colours cost less.

**Dimension 5: Display context (10% weight).** Decide where the model will actually live. A desk piece should stay within 20 cm; a statement build for the shelf wants 40 cm or more. If you're entering BrickPlan's monthly theme contest, also think about legibility from a judge's point of view — the more focused the theme, the easier it is to stand out.

**Three beginner-friendly themes to start with:**

1. Corner café — about 600 pieces, reference cost around CNY 80, widely available parts
2. Mini mech — about 400 pieces, CNY 50, joints easy to source
3. Pet house — about 300 pieces, CNY 40, great for building together with kids

**Common mistakes:** chasing a trending theme when your parts and skills don't match; picking a theme so grand that one MOC is meant to hold an entire city; and judging only by looks, not structure — for example stockpiling curved parts when you can't yet build a curved surface. The five-factor score exposes all of these before you spend a single yuan.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I choose a MOC theme I'll actually finish?**
A: Pick one under 500 pieces, with special parts below 10% of the total, that you can sketch roughly on paper. Themes that pass all three tests are usually finished more than 80% of the time — the lowest risk of abandonment.

**Q: Which MOC themes suit beginners?**
A: Street-corner shops, mini vehicles and small animals work best — common parts, simple structure, forgiving tolerances. Avoid pure mechanical linkages and large buildings until you've completed 3-5 builds.

**Q: Should I work in an official set theme or my own MOC theme?**
A: Official sets are good for practice and collecting; MOC themes are for expressing yourself. On a limited budget, go MOC first — the parts are reusable, and a mid-size build costs about CNY 100-200, which is better value.

**Q: Is it worth jumping on a trending MOC theme?**
A: Borrow, don't copy. Trending themes demand a lot of parts and a lot of builders produce the same thing. Keep the framework, then swap in your own colour scheme and details — recognisability goes up by roughly 40%.

**Q: How do I balance MOC theme against budget?**
A: Set the budget first, then choose the theme. Under CNY 300, go for a single-colour micro build; under CNY 500, a mid-size street scene; only above CNY 500 should you consider a large build. Write the budget ceiling on the first line of your build plan.
