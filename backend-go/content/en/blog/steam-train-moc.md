# Steam Train MOC: Build a 1.6-Meter Locomotive and 8 Cars

The short version: this steam train MOC uses roughly 6,800 pieces and consists of one steam locomotive, one tender and eight passenger coaches. Coupled together it stretches about 1.6 meters, takes around 28 hours at a normal pace, and costs about 1,400 yuan in parts. The locomotive runs on a motor and can pull the whole consist at low to medium speed on standard track, while every coach has an interior and lighting, so the finished train works as a display line in a study or a kid's room. The parts list and step-by-step instructions for this design are available on brickplan.cn — download them and you can build straight from the plans.

**Step 1: Chassis and drivetrain**

Start with the locomotive chassis, building the frame from 2x8 plates. Mount one M motor and a gearbox in the middle of the frame, with the gearbox output driving the driving wheel axle. The driving wheels are large-diameter wheel pieces, with coupling rods fitted on both sides so all three driving axles turn in sync — this is the mechanical detail that makes a steam train MOC worth looking at. Once the chassis is complete, power it up and test that it rolls forward smoothly before you go any further, so you don't have to tear work apart later.

**Step 2: Boiler and cab**

The boiler is built from cylinder pieces laid horizontally, wrapped in dark red curved panels, then detailed with gold rivet pieces. Fit the smokestack and headlamp at the front, and add a whistle on top. The cab sits directly behind the boiler, with a steering wheel, instrument panel and two minifigures inside, windows in the side walls and a curved roof piece closing it off. This module eats about 1,800 pieces and is the most labour-intensive part of the locomotive.

**Step 3: Tender and the eight coaches**

Build the tender's coal bunker from dark grey bricks and fill it with black round plates and studs to suggest coal. The eight passenger coaches share a green-and-white livery and each measures about 20 cm long. Curved pieces give the roofs their rounded profile, and two bogies under each coach keep it tracking cleanly through curves. Inside there are opening doors and seats, and two of the coaches also get ceiling lights — switch them on and the whole train reads as one long glowing line.

**Step 4: Coupling up and track testing**

Connect the locomotive, tender and coaches in order using standard couplers, leaving one stud of clearance between each vehicle so nothing binds in the curves. Put the whole train on a loop with a circumference of about 3 meters and test it: run two slow laps first to check whether the wheelset slips, then build up speed gradually. While testing, place a few buffer bricks along both sides of the track so a derailment doesn't damage parts.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this steam train MOC worth building?**

A: Yes. At 6,800 pieces and 1.6 meters long it's good value against comparable MOCs at the same price, and it actually runs, so you get playability as well as display presence — one more layer of fun than a purely static model.

**Q: What if the train won't move?**

A: First check whether the gearbox is meshing too tightly, then confirm the motor is getting the specified voltage. Eight times out of ten the problem is a coupling rod fitted backwards or excessive friction between wheels and track; compare the driving wheels and coupling rod orientation against the plans and it will sort itself out.

**Q: Can I swap in ordinary bricks and change the look?**

A: Yes — the curved body panels and decorative pieces can all be substituted. But keep the motor, gears and couplers to the specified types, otherwise you risk mismatched rotational speed or slipping in the drivetrain.

**Q: Are eight coaches too much for one motor to pull?**

A: No. An M motor handles up to ten coaches comfortably on flat, straight track. Eight loaded coaches come to about 1.6 meters, and as long as your curve radius is at least 40 cm the train runs stably.

**Q: What does it cost to build?**

A: About 1,400 yuan in parts, of which the motor, gears and other drivetrain components account for roughly 300 yuan. If you already own standard track and a stock of common bricks, the real outlay can drop to around 1,100 yuan.
