# Bread Van MOC Tutorial: Squared Body and Sliding Door Build

## The Short Answer

A bread van MOC is the best possible step up from beginner builds. The squared body looks simple, but it places real demands on how well your bricks lock together. Following this tutorial you will need roughly 950 parts, about 240 yuan at an average of 0.25 yuan per loose brick, three hours for a first-time builder, and around 1.5 hours once you know the sequence. Three things carry the whole model: the boxy van body, the sliding side door, and an interior large enough for minifigures. With the doors working, the finished van is close to 95% faithful to the real vehicle. The full instructions, including the parts list and a rendered image for every step, can be downloaded at brickplan.cn.

## Why a Bread Van MOC Is Worth Building

The van is the most adaptable vehicle in brick building: it can be a delivery truck, a mobile food truck, a camper, or even a police patrol car. Its boxy shell is perfect practice for wall-building technique — how to turn bricks into a flat surface with no gaps, which is the foundation of every architectural MOC. Compared with engineering vehicles such as a concrete mixer, the van has clean lines and uses common parts, so the loose bricks you already own go further. That makes it an ideal second or third MOC project.

## Step 1: Build the Chassis and Squared Body Frame

The soul of a bread van is squareness. Start the chassis on a 6x14 baseplate, 22 cm long and 9 cm wide, at roughly 1:13 scale. Build the body frame from four corner posts: stack 2x2 bricks vertically at each corner to a height of about 8 cm, then connect the posts with 1x6 beams into a cage-like structure. The key technique is to stagger every course, the way you would lay a brick wall, offsetting each layer by half a brick against the one below. This gives the walls strength and keeps the model from coming apart. Leave two openings in the sides: the right side is where the sliding door track goes, and the rear is reserved for the double cargo doors. Trim the edges of each opening with 1x2 bricks with side studs so the door assemblies have something to attach to later.

## Step 2: Install the Sliding Door and Opening Mechanism

The sliding door is the van's signature feature and the highlight of this tutorial. Fit a 1x8 grooved track brick along the top and the bottom of the right-hand body side, build the door panel from 4x6 plates, and seat its top and bottom edges inside the track channels. Two small handles (1x1 round plates) go on the outside of the panel; push a handle and the door slides front to back along the track with about 5 cm of travel, smooth and without binding. The rear doors use hinge pieces to form a pair of side-hinged leaves, one left and one right, 4x4 each, opening outward to 90 degrees. Once every door is fitted, the van has three movable openings, which doubles the play value. This section has dedicated enlarged diagrams in the instructions at brickplan.cn, so you can follow along without mistakes.

## Step 3: Interior Space and Exterior Details

Interior space is where the van earns extra points. The cargo area measures 14 cm long, 7 cm wide and 5 cm high, which comfortably holds two standard minifigures plus a small bed or a shelving unit. The cab gets two seats (1x2 with backrest pieces), a steering wheel (cross axle plus a round plate) and a dashboard (1x4 slope), and the passenger side can seat a figure. Add a removable roof (6x10 plate): lift it off and you can play straight into the interior, as easy as a dolls' house. For colour, white with blue stripes works well — about 380 white bricks, 200 blue bricks and 180 grey chassis parts. Clear pieces make the windshield; add a licence plate and tail lights, and the little van is finished.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I make the sliding door run smoothly?**
Precision in fitting the track bricks is everything. The top and bottom tracks must be perfectly parallel, with a spacing error of no more than 0.5 mm, and the edges of the door panel should be smooth. After installation, add a drop of lubricant (pencil graphite works as a substitute) and the door will glide with about 5 cm of travel.

**Q: Can the van be converted into a camper or a food truck?**
Absolutely — that is the biggest advantage of a bread van MOC. Replace the interior with a bed, a stove or shelves, add air conditioning and a roof rack, change the colour scheme, and you have a new vehicle. brickplan.cn has three conversion versions on the same chassis; each one needs only 50-80 extra interior parts.

**Q: What is the difference between a bread van MOC and a small truck MOC?**
The van is a single shell with the cab connected to the cargo area, giving more interior room and space for interior detail; a small truck separates the cab from the cargo box, which is simpler to build but leaves the box closed. The van adds a sliding door, so it is slightly harder, but when it is finished you can play with it both as a vehicle and as a small scene house — better value for the parts.
