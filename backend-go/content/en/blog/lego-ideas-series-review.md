# LEGO Ideas Review: Fan Designs and Collector Value

The short version first: Ideas is the most idea-dense theme LEGO makes, and it suits collectors who chase unusual subject matter. It is not a safe bet for anyone hoping to treat sets as an investment with guaranteed returns.

Of all the LEGO lines, Ideas is the odd one out — every set in it started life as an ordinary fan's MOC submission. In this review we look at whether this fan-decided product line is worth your money, and at the selection process behind it, which is strict to the point of being punishing.

## Fan Submissions and the 213xx Numbering System

LEGO Ideas began as the CUUSOO platform, which launched in 2011. The rule was simple: a builder uploads an original design, and once it collects 10,000 supporter votes it goes to an official review. Only projects that clear that review have a chance of reaching mass production. The mechanism naturally filters for ideas with a built-in audience, which is why sets under the 213xx numbering arrive with nostalgia already attached. Be aware that the acceptance rate is extremely low — over a decade, thousands of submissions have turned into only a few dozen retail sets — and that the official version almost always adds a large amount of brick volume and reworks the structure. The final product is usually more polished than the original submission, but also more expensive.

## Standout Sets: 21318 Tree House and Other Classics

21318 Tree House is the milestone no discussion of Ideas can avoid. With 3036 pieces, three detachable treehouse rooms and a modular design that lets you swap foliage between spring, summer and autumn colour schemes, plus lived-in details like bunk beds and a fireplace, it gives "toys for adults" a genuine sense of domestic life. In the same line, 21309 Saturn V uses 1969 pieces to recreate the three-stage structure and can be broken down into two boosters — a benchmark that works equally well as a teaching model and as something to handle. 21335 Motorized Lighthouse and 21343 Viking Village represent two further directions: mechanical interaction and scene reconstruction. Overall, Ideas allows noticeably more design freedom than a regular theme, with SNOT techniques and unusual part combinations turning up constantly, so it suits builders with some experience.

## Collecting and Retired-Set Value

Be clear-eyed about the value of retired Ideas sets. Discontinued models do rise in price — early releases such as 21303 Wall-E and 21310 Old Fishing Store now fetch double — but not every set holds its value, and niche subjects can flatline just as easily. Put "I like it" ahead of "it will appreciate": most Ideas sets are a one-time release, so if you miss one you are waiting for a reissue or buying secondhand. If a set appeals to you and the budget allows, buying while it is still on shelves is the more dependable choice.

## Verdict and Advice

The conclusion of this LEGO Ideas review: it is the most idea-dense LEGO theme, well suited to collectors who love distinctive subjects, and poorly suited to a speculative mindset chasing guaranteed returns. If your budget is limited but you enjoy tinkering, head to brickplan and look for MOC instructions on the same subject — plenty of classic ideas can be rebuilt at home for far less.
