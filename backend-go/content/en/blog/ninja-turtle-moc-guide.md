# Ninja Turtles MOC Review: Four Brothers & Sewer Lair

The Ninja Turtles carry a lot of childhood memories for anyone who grew up with New York's underground heroes: four mutated turtles with very different skill sets, a hidden base beneath the streets, and a rogues' gallery to match. This custom Ninja Turtles instruction set brings the four brothers, the sewer lair and Shredder's hideout into brick form. Here is a breakdown across three angles — accuracy to the source material, the building experience, and what parts you'll actually need.

## The Four Brothers: Minifigure Basics

The minifigures are the heart of the whole thing. Leonardo, Raphael, Donatello and Michelangelo are identified first and foremost by mask colour and weapon pairing: blue mask with twin katana, red mask with twin sai, purple mask with a bo staff, orange mask with nunchaku. This instruction set handles the masks with full-ring curved pieces that close the band neatly — they sit tight on the turtle heads without slipping off. The eyes are done as a white base with black pupil dots, paired with crescent-shaped brow plates to capture that determined turtle expression. The shells use stacked dome bricks to build a raised curve, with alternating light and dark rows forming the scute pattern, and the underside still shows plastron detail when you flip the figure over.

All four weapons are removable designs: the katana and sai use silver blade pieces, the bo staff is a straight rod with studs at both ends, and the nunchaku link two short bars with a flexible hose so they swing freely. The handling feel is genuinely solid.

## The Sewer Lair: The Soul of the Scene

If the minifigures decide whether it looks right, the sewer lair decides whether it feels real. The scene plan here builds its base from large quantities of green, brown and grey bricks, then crosses curved tube pieces with cylindrical bricks to lay out an interlocking pipe network. Along the base of the walls, a few dark-coloured specialised pieces are deliberately set in to simulate years of water staining — from a glance you get that cool, damp underground atmosphere.

The interior is clearly zoned by function. Master Splinter's training room has a wooden practice dummy standing in it, the pizza kitchen holds a pizza built from stacked red round plates, and the lounge TV uses transparent pieces for the screen. Small touches like these are what make you smile when you spot them. Overall scale is roughly two thousand pieces, and the finished model measures close to forty centimetres square — it has real presence.

## Villain Scenes and Parts Requirements

This set doesn't only build the heroic side; adding Shredder and the other villains makes the story complete. The Shredder minifigure covers the torso with silver-grey armour pieces, adds two bladed shoulder plates, and pairs it all with a steel-faced helmet — the sense of menace lands immediately. Beside him, Krang and the mutant duo of Bebop and Rocksteady use exaggerated head moulds to create their unsettling look, a sharp contrast with the four brothers.

On the parts side, this set is very accessible. The main structure relies almost entirely on common plates, bricks, slopes and curved pieces, with no expensive or rare elements required — builders can start straight from their loose-parts stock or a general supplement pack. Only a handful of special pieces, such as Shredder's faceplate and the nunchaku hoses, need to be sourced separately, and matching parts lists are available on brickplan.cn to check and order against.

## Building Experience and Final Thoughts

The instructions are organised into separate booklets for the minifigures, the scene and the villains. Step breakdown within each booklet is fine-grained, with no more than twenty new parts added per step, so even a beginner can work through it steadily. The bends in the pipe runs do need careful checking of the curved pieces' orientation — sort your parts into tubs by colour before you start. Overall difficulty sits at medium-to-high, but once finished it's hugely satisfying, whether displayed on a shelf or paired with the villains for a play fight. This Ninja Turtles instruction set strikes a good balance between accuracy, playability and parts-friendliness, and it's a worthwhile piece for any Turtles fan to keep.
