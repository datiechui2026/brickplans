# How to Print LEGO Instructions: Paper, Settings, Binding

Digital manuals are everywhere now, but plenty of builders still prefer paper: pages spread out on the desk with no screen to tap, a pencil tick next to each finished step, and a physical record filed away with an older set. Printing LEGO instructions looks straightforward until you actually try it — the wrong paper glares under a lamp, a bad scale setting distorts the diagrams, and single-sided printing turns a 60-page manual into a brick-thick slab. This guide walks through the format prep, the printer settings and the binding options so you end up with a manual that is both usable and good to look at.

## 1. Preparing the File Before You Print

The first step is getting a clean, complete PDF. For official sets, the building instructions for a given set number are generally downloadable from the brand's website or app; for MOC instructions, pick the highest-resolution version available on the instructions platform. Once downloaded, check three things: the page count is complete, the images are sharp, and the parts list page is included. If the PDF runs long, split it first and print only the building steps, dropping the cover and any advertising pages — that saves a surprising amount of paper and toner. When an older set has no official PDF, scanning the physical booklet into a digital file works well: it gives you a backup and a source file for printing later.

## 2. Choosing Paper and Print Settings

For paper, ordinary 70–80 gsm A4 copy paper handles everyday building fine. If you want a nicer page-turn feel, step up to a matte stock around 100 gsm. Avoid high-gloss coated paper — the reflections are hard on the eyes.

On layout: if the original manual is A5, printing straight to A4 will enlarge and distort it. Choose "fit to page" or a proportional scale in the print dialog so the diagram sizes stay close to the original.

A few settings worth remembering:

- **Color diagrams need color printing.** Black-and-white output wipes out the color differences that carry meaning in the steps.
- **Duplex saves half the paper**, but set the flip direction to "short edge" so the reverse side does not come out upside down.
- **Set resolution to actual size or 100%.** Do not enable any scaling beyond fitting the printable area, so the stud-to-stud proportions stay correct.

## 3. Three Ways to Bind the Finished Pages

Once the loose sheets are printed, binding decides how the manual feels in use.

**Method one: binder with transparent sleeves.** Slip each page into a clear document sleeve and clip the lot into a ring binder. Pages turn easily and are protected from dirt and splashes, which suits reading while you build; the trade-off is a slightly bulky stack.

**Method two: stapling.** Two or three staples down the left edge is the cheapest option and works well for short manuals. When turning pages, be careful not to press down on the diagrams themselves.

**Method three: thermal or coil binding.** For long manuals you plan to keep, take the pages to a print shop for coil binding. The book lies completely flat and will not spring shut when you weight it against the edge of the desk — the best experience of the three.

Whichever you choose, add a cover page first with the set number and name written on it, so the manual is easy to identify when you file it away.

## 4. Tips for Using and Storing Printed Manuals

A few small details make a difference. While building, hold the current page down with a magnetic clip or a binder clip against the desk or a piece of card — far more comfortable than flattening it with your hand. Tick off completed steps lightly in pencil and erase them when the build is done, so the manual can be reused. If you print several copies of the same file, make one a working copy and one an archive copy; store the archive copy sealed in a document sleeve, away from light.

One note on copyright: printing for your own use is fine, but do not reproduce manuals in bulk for sale. And when you need fresh diagrams to print, MOC instructions are usually easier to obtain as high-resolution PDFs than official set manuals, and cheaper to print too.

## Final Thoughts

A well-printed manual changes building from squinting at a screen and zooming in and out into spreading the pages open and taking your time.
