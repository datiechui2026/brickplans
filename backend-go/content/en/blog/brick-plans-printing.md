# Brick Plan Printing Guide: Get Sharp, Printable Instructions

Blurry pages, colors that look nothing like your screen, and parts that refuse to line up because the scale drifted — nearly every printing problem falls into one of those three buckets. This guide follows the order I actually work in: find a high-resolution file first, then dial in the printer settings, then keep the paper copies organized.

## Why Print Brick Instructions?

Plenty of builders read plans on a tablet or phone, but paper still has advantages that are hard to replace:

- **Easier on the eyes**: long sessions staring at a screen get tiring; paper doesn't
- **Convenient**: the plan sits next to the build, always open, no battery to worry about
- **Easy to annotate**: you can mark progress or note substitutions right on the page
- **Worth keeping**: a well-printed plan is a collectible in its own right

## Step 1: Get High-Resolution Brick Instructions

### Good Sources for Instruction Files

Before you print anything, you need a quality source file. Recommended channels:

1. **BrickPlan.cn**: a dedicated brick plan community with a large library of high-resolution MOC instructions
2. **Rebrickable**: an internationally known MOC platform offering both PDF and image-format instructions
3. **BrickLink**: the largest brick marketplace worldwide, with plenty of instruction files as well
4. **Official instructions**: the LEGO website offers downloads for some official sets

### Pick a Format That Prints Well

Formats differ noticeably in how they come out of the printer:

| Format | Pros | Cons | Print recommendation |
|------|------|------|---------|
| PDF | Vector format, no distortion at any zoom level | Large file size | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Strongly recommended |
| PNG | High resolution, lossless, supports transparent backgrounds | Large file size | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Recommended |
| JPG | Small files, widely compatible | Lossy compression, blurs when enlarged | ⭐⭐⭐ Acceptable |
| SVG | Vector, editable | Limited support | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ For advanced users |

**Recommendation**: favor PDF files for printing to guarantee the best result.

## Step 2: Prep Work Before Printing

### Check the Resolution

Resolution is the core metric for print sharpness. Aim for these standards:

- **A4 printing**: at least 300 DPI
- **A3 printing**: at least 300 DPI, plus a physically larger sheet
- **Zoomed detail views**: 600 DPI or higher

How to check a file's resolution:
- On a computer, right-click the image file → Properties → Details
- In a PDF reader, open the document properties

### Choose the Right Paper

Paper choice directly affects both print quality and how pleasant the build is:

- **Standard A4 (80 gsm)**: everyday use, low cost, fine for simple plans
- **Glossy photo paper**: vivid colors, good for color plans, but highly reflective
- **Matte paper**: no glare, comfortable to read, recommended for complex plans
- **Coated (art) paper**: excellent color reproduction and feel, suited to collector-grade plans

**Suggested pairing**: matte coated paper for color plans, 80 gsm or heavier standard paper for black-and-white plans.

## Step 3: Print Settings in Detail

### Color Mode

This is the step most often overlooked:

- **RGB**: designed for screens; printing directly can shift colors
- **CMYK**: the print-industry standard, with more accurate color reproduction

Most home printers handle RGB fine, but if you're using a print shop, convert the file to CMYK first.

### Print Size and Scale

Make sure the plan prints at the correct ratio:

1. In the print dialog, choose "Actual size" or "100% scale"
2. Turn off "Fit to page" so nothing gets auto-scaled out of proportion
3. If you do need to resize, keep "lock aspect ratio" enabled

### Print Quality

- **Draft mode**: saves ink, good for test prints
- **Standard mode**: everyday use, balances quality and speed
- **High quality mode**: fine output, use it for the final version

### Duplex Printing

For multi-page plans:
- Few pages (2–4): print single-sided so pages are easy to flip through
- Many pages: print double-sided to save paper, and pick a stock that doesn't bleed through

## Step 4: Solutions to Common Problems

### Problem 1: The Printout Is Blurry

**Cause**: insufficient source resolution or a bad print setting

**Fix**:
- Confirm the source file is at least 300 DPI
- Check whether "Draft mode" is selected in the print settings
- Try a different PDF reader (Adobe Acrobat Reader is recommended)

### Problem 2: Colors Are Badly Off

**Cause**: mismatched color mode or a printer cartridge issue

**Fix**:
- In print settings, choose "Color management" → "Printer manages colors"
- Check ink levels and replace cartridges if needed
- Run a color calibration before printing

### Problem 3: Wrong Scale, Parts Don't Line Up

**Cause**: "Scale to fit page" was enabled at print time

**Fix**:
- Print at "Actual size" rather than "Fit to page"
- Before the full run, measure a reference dimension on the sheet with a ruler (for example, a 2x4 brick should measure about 3.2 cm long)

## Advanced Tips: Organizing and Storing Your Prints

### Sort Your Plans

- Group by project: use folders or a ring binder
- Note the difficulty level so it's easy to pick the next build
- Record completion dates as a building log

### Protect the Paper

- Use clear plastic sleeves to prevent smudging and tears
- Keep prints out of direct sunlight to stop the paper yellowing
- Scan important plans as a digital backup

## Wrapping Up

A sharp printout really does make or break a build. Run through the sequence above — pick the file, verify the resolution, match paper to color mode — and you'll stop producing blurry sheets and mis-scaled pages.
