# Brick Succulent MOC Review: A Desk Plant That Never Wilts

The short version: this succulent MOC builds six differently shaped plants plus a pot from roughly 320 pieces, takes about 1 hour 20 minutes to assemble, scores 8.5/10 for accuracy, and sells for around 75 yuan. If every real succulent you buy turns to mush on the windowsill, this is the desktop alternative — good to look at, impossible to kill, and worth the money.

## How Faithful It Looks

The designer zeroed in on what makes a succulent read as a succulent: fat leaves wrapped in tight layers. Slope bricks in several different curves stand in for the rosette shapes of lotus-form, Echeveria and Haworthia cooperi plants, and the green-to-pink-purple gradient palette sits close to the real thing. The pot is built round from brown bricks, and the top surface is finished with "gravel" pieces scattered across it. At a glance it is genuinely hard to tell it is not real, and on a desk it makes a fresh, clean accent. Accuracy: 8.5/10.

## The Building Experience

The build splits into three stages — the pot, the individual plants, and the final assembly. Each plant stacks its leaves a little differently, so nothing feels like a repeat of the one before, and the six shapes stay distinct from each other. The instructions are clearly drawn; a few of the inner leaf positions need a careful second look to get right. The whole model comes together in about 1 hour 20 minutes at a relaxed pace, and finishing it gives you the small satisfaction of having grown a pot of flowers with your own hands.

## Part Quality

The parts feel fine-grained, and the matte surface does not pick up fingerprints. Shade consistency across the green range is well handled — the different depths of green sit together harmoniously instead of clashing. The leaf bricks clutch firmly: a finished plant can be pulled out whole and pushed back in, which makes rearranging the layout easy. The only small complaint is that the tiny outer leaves are easy to mix up while sorting, so keep an eye on the trays before you start.

## Value for Money

320 pieces for 75 yuan works out to roughly 0.23 yuan per piece, a fair middle-of-the-road price. Set that against real succulents, which need light and water and can still die on you, and a brick version is a one-time purchase that never wilts — equally good as a gift or for yourself. Value score: 8.5/10, and one of the steadier picks in the plant-brick category.
