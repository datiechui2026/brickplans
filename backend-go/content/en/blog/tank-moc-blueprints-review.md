# Tank MOC Blueprints Review: From Type 99A to Tiger

## Product Overview

Tank MOC blueprints are one of the most popular categories among military brick builders. Whether you want a WWII-era Tiger or a modern main battle tank like the Type 99A, a well-made blueprint lets you rebuild a piece of armored history out of bricks. On BrickPlans, tank instructions sit permanently near the top of the popularity charts, and they draw a heavily male, military-minded crowd.

The tank blueprints currently on the market span several scales and styles: compact 1:35 diorama tanks, detail-focused 1:28 display builds, and 1:16 or even larger motorized flagship models. Part counts and detail fidelity vary enormously between scales, so pick according to your building experience and how you plan to display the finished model.

## Build Difficulty

Tank MOC difficulty ranges widely. Overall ratings break down like this:

- **Beginner (★★☆☆☆)**: small tanks, 300-600 parts, a good first attempt
- **Intermediate (★★★☆☆)**: mid-size tanks, 600-1500 parts, some building experience required
- **Advanced (★★★★☆)**: large tanks, 1500-3000 parts, full interior structure
- **Expert (★★★★★)**: flagship builds, 3000+ parts, complete interior plus working tracks and linked turret rotation

If this is your first tank MOC, start with a beginner design around 500 parts and work your way up to the more complex structures.

## Part Review

Tank MOCs make specific demands on your parts supply: large quantities of military greens, sand tones and dark grey, plus track links, turntable and hinge parts, and smooth tiles. Good blueprints usually list recommended brands and substitution options.

- **Brick precision**: Gobricks, Double Eagle and other first-tier domestic brands are the usual recommendation. Their parts clutch tightly, which matters for a stable military build.
- **Special parts**: tracks and suspension are the hard part of any tank MOC. Favor blueprints that explicitly state compatibility with LEGO track links.
- **Color consistency**: military color palettes differ noticeably between brands, so try to keep a single build to a single brand of parts.

## Build Experience

Assembling a tank MOC is an unusually absorbing process. You start with the hull and suspension, work up through the engine bay and fighting compartment, add the turret, and finish with the main gun and appliqué armor. It feels like assembling a miniature war machine.

Many higher-quality blueprints also include **interior detail** — engine components, ammunition racks, crew compartments — which makes the finished model far more rewarding to look at. Some go further with working suspension and a rotating turret, adding play value on top of display value. On [brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn) you will find a large library of tank blueprints with detailed build notes and clear step-by-step diagrams.

## Finished Model

A completed tank MOC hits hard visually. A 1:28 main battle tank measures roughly 30 cm long, which makes it an instant focal point on a desk or in a display cabinet. Good blueprints reproduce real equipment features: weld seams, tool boxes, smoke grenade launchers.

Add a terrain base and minifigures and the military diorama atmosphere snaps into place. Plenty of builders display several tank MOCs together to create the effect of an armored column.

## Verdict

**★★★★☆ (Highly recommended)**

Tank MOC blueprints suit any military enthusiast and any builder with intermediate experience or better. The entry barrier is low, the ceiling is extremely high, and there is plenty of room to keep progressing.
