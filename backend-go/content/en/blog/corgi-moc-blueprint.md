# Corgi MOC Blueprint Review: Build a Cute Brick Corgi

Of all the animal MOC subjects that have blown up in recent years, the corgi is firmly on the list. Those big round eyes, the trademark stubby legs and the peach-shaped rear make it a natural fit for the brick world. This review walks through one well-made **corgi MOC blueprint** from start to finish, so you can go from picking the right instructions to having a finished dog on your shelf.

## Why Corgis Are Such a Popular MOC Subject

A corgi's proportions translate to bricks almost too well. The rounded body can be stacked from curved slopes and rounded-corner pieces, while the short legs and chunky rear give builders plenty of room to play with shaping. A good **corgi MOC blueprint** usually lands somewhere between accuracy and buildability: it has to capture the character without demanding so many parts that beginners give up. The blueprint we're reviewing here does a genuinely good job on that balance.

## What's Inside the Blueprint and How the Build Works

The **corgi MOC blueprint** is laid out as step-by-step instructions divided into four modules: head, body, limbs and tail. Each module comes with clear rendered step images and its own parts list.

The build strategy works from the inside out. You start by laying down basic dark brown bricks to form the core structure, then wrap the outside in cream and orange curved slopes to create the corgi's signature two-tone coat. The eyes are handled cleverly with printed round tiles, which saves you the hassle of stickers and gives the face a livelier expression.

## Parts List and Difficulty

If you want to reproduce this **corgi MOC blueprint** yourself, the parts requirement is fairly modest. The full model needs roughly 600 pieces, mostly common 2x2 and 2x4 bricks, a range of curved slopes and a small number of corner pieces. Most builders will already have the bulk of it in their spare-parts bin.

Difficulty sits between beginner and intermediate, and you can expect to finish in about 2 to 3 hours, which makes it a great first animal MOC for a newer builder. The one real challenge is shaping the curve of the rear, where you'll need to make patient small adjustments.

## Tips for Getting the Cute Look Right

There are three key techniques worth sharing if you want the finished **corgi MOC blueprint** build to look as adorable as possible:

1. **Keep the face curves round.** Minimize sharp corners and let the cheeks and muzzle flow into each other.
2. **Make the legs thick and solid.** Stacking height with chunky bricks creates the short-and-squatty illusion that defines the breed.
3. **Point the tail up high.** That perky tail is where the corgi's energy comes from.

Once you have those three down, you can go off-blueprint and still end up with a distinctive corgi of your own.
