# Qiji Space Series Review: Accuracy and Value Tested

Straight to the verdict: Qiji's space line takes a "science education plus display model" approach. Sets like the Long March 5 rocket and the Tiangong space station are accurate at this scale and easy on the wallet, and they ship with a light-up base and a rotating launch tower. If your budget is limited and you want space hardware on the shelf, this is the line to look at first.

Among Chinese brick brands, Qiji has long built its reputation on scene recreation, and the space series pushes that strength as far as it goes. From Long March rockets and space stations to Mars rovers, Qiji has packed a lot of real space-engineering detail into brick form. In this review I'll judge the line on three things: accuracy of the recreation, part quality, and value for money.

## Brand positioning: space hardware for everyone

Qiji's space series targets the "parent-child science plus display" market. The range covers the classic elements: launch pads, carrier rockets, space station core modules and lunar rovers. Unlike brands that chase mechanical functions, Qiji leans toward exterior accuracy and scene interaction, adding electronic parts such as light-up bases and rotating launch towers. On a shelf it works both as a teaching aid and as a display piece.

## Representative sets: accuracy tested

I focused on two sets: the Long March 5 rocket and the Tiangong space station. The Long March 5 uses a multi-stage, segmented structure. The taper of the core stage is reproduced properly, the boosters sit at the correct proportion to the core, and the engine nozzle detail at the tail is a particular highlight. The space station includes a core module, experiment modules and solar panels. The panels fold out and stow away, and the module interfaces connect with standard cross axles, so you can combine them into different configurations freely. In terms of accuracy, this sits in the top tier of Chinese space brick sets.

## Build experience and part quality

Qiji has introduced new moulds in recent years. Parts clutch firmly and the sprue marks are cleanly finished, a clear step up in feel from the brand's earlier output. Transparent elements are nicely clear, printed parts make up a high proportion of the detailing, and most of the markings on the rocket body are printed rather than stickered, with accurate alignment. The instructions are clearly stepped and the difficulty curve is sensible; a teenager can finish a set independently with some guidance. The only small issue is that some of the smaller parts are on the tight side, so disassembly takes a little patience.

## Price and value analysis

Qiji's space sets are priced very affordably, and this is the most surprising part of the review. Mainstream sets sit in the 100 to 300 yuan range, roughly 200 to 300 yuan per thousand pieces, a fraction of what a comparable LEGO space theme costs. Considering how much the printed parts and electronic components add to the look, the value holds up strongly against other Chinese space brick sets. Whether you want a science starting point for a child or a desk model for a space fan, it earns its place.

## Who it suits, and the pros and cons

If you want the conclusion of this review in one line: this line suits families building together, space enthusiasts, and display-focused builders on a tight budget. The pros are the low price, the solid accuracy and the educational angle. The cons are that the variety of part types is narrower than the big international brands, so anything wanting more complex mechanisms, such as linked hatches, will run into limits. ## FAQ

**Q: Is Qiji's space series worth buying?**
A: On a limited budget, yes. The line covers launch pads, carrier rockets, space stations and lunar rovers, accuracy is solid, and individual sets mostly run 100 to 500 yuan. Part precision and clutch feel still trail the top-tier brands slightly.

**Q: What age is Qiji's space series for?**
A: The official recommendation is 6 and up. From about 8, children can complete sets of up to 500 pieces on their own. For multi-stage structures like the Long March 5, which have many steps, parents should sort the parts by module first and then let the child build stage by stage.

**Q: How accurate are Qiji's rocket and space station models?**
A: In testing, the Long March 5 used a multi-stage segmented structure with boosters correctly proportioned to the core stage and well-executed engine nozzle detail. The space station includes a core module, experiment modules and deployable solar panels, and the interfaces can be recombined freely.

**Q: Qiji space or SEMBO space, which should I choose?**
A: Both compete on value. Qiji leans toward scene interaction, with a light-up base and rotating launch tower. SEMBO covers a wider range of subjects and uses more standard colours. Pick Qiji for a display scene, or collect both if you want to cover the whole lineup.
