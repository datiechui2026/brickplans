# Bole Street Series Review: Budget Modular Buildings Worth It?

Modular street buildings have long been one of the hottest corners of the brick hobby, but official street sets that routinely run past 1,000 yuan scare plenty of builders away. Bole's street series attacks that gap at what amounts to pocket money by comparison — so is it actually worth buying? I built my way through Bole's street sets from recent years one after another, and put together an honest report covering part quality, the building process and how the finished models hold up.

## Bole Street Series at a Glance

The Bole street line follows a modular design approach. A single set usually contains 1,500 to 3,000 pieces, and prices sit mostly in the 100 to 200 yuan band — roughly one third of what a comparable official street set costs. The range leans heavily on architecture: a Chinese teahouse, a European clock tower, retro shopfronts and similar subjects. Many are original in-house designs that don't collide with any licensed property.

The module bases share a single specification, which means buying several sets lets you join them edge to edge into one continuous street. That is exactly the play pattern street-scene fans care about most, and it is where the line earns its keep before you even open a box.

## Part Quality Tested

Parts in the Bole street series are largely produced by Gobricks, which puts them in the top tier of domestic manufacturing. In practice the clutch is tight: pushing a part home and pulling it back out both need a little force, and almost nothing comes loose during a build. Color consistency across a batch is high — within one set you won't spot a shade difference — and the transparent elements are pleasantly clear.

The small complaints: a minority of parts show light surface scratches or visible injection points, though they don't affect the overall look. The quality is fair for the price, but there is still a modest gap against LEGO's mold precision. If you demand a flawless feel in hand, go in with realistic expectations.

## Build Experience

The build feel is smooth overall. Bole divides instruction steps sensibly, keeps the part count per step moderate, and never turns the process into a puzzle — a newcomer can work through a set without trouble. Bricks come bagged by step, so you aren't digging through thousands of loose pieces looking for one element, which is a genuine plus at this price.

One thing to watch: a few steps call for closely related colors, dark grey against light grey for instance, and the contrast in the printed instructions is only average. It is easy to grab the wrong part, so separate similar shades on the table before you start. A single set takes roughly four to six hours to finish, with a moderate difficulty level throughout.

## Finished Model and Compatibility

The finished result is the line's biggest strength. Architectural detail is rich — shop signage, roof eaves and tiles, interior furnishings all get proper attention — and the models photograph well once they're on a desk or shelf.

The modular play is the headline. Bases share one spec, multiple sets butt together with tight seams, and you can freely recombine them into a street layout of your own design. The color palette runs bright, which suits photos and display. For MOC work, Bole's bases and wall structures follow standard module specifications, so mixing them with comparable street-scene parts is no problem at all — the freedom here is high.

## Verdict and Buying Advice

Is the Bole street series worth it? As an entry point into street scenes and a value-for-money pick, yes. Among domestic street sets at the same price, its part quality ranks near the front, the design detail is generous and the build runs smoothly; on a limited budget you won't regret picking one up.

That said, if you're a veteran chasing ultimate precision and collectible value, the mold detail still falls short of official street sets. My advice is to buy one basic set first and test the feel in hand, then fill in the rest according to your street plan once you're satisfied. For more real-world photos and mixing examples from other builders, the brickplan.cn community is worth a look — plenty of people have joined multiple Bole street sets into a full street, and the results are genuinely striking.
