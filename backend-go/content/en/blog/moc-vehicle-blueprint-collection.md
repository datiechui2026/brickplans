# MOC Vehicle Blueprints: How to Find and Build Them

For most brick builders, a vehicle is the first thing they try to design themselves. Whether you want a supercar, an off-roader or a piece of construction equipment, the whole project rests on one thing: a reliable set of MOC blueprints. Pick a good one and the build flows; pick a blurry, incomplete one and you will be pulling sections apart all weekend. This guide covers where vehicle blueprints come from, how to judge them before you commit, and what to watch out for while building.

## Where Vehicle MOC Blueprints Come From

There are roughly three sources. The first is international builder platforms, where experienced designers share vehicle MOCs for free — ideal when you are starting out. The second is paid blueprint shops, which usually offer higher completion rates and more detailed instructions. The third is Chinese blueprint sites such as brickplan.cn, which collect a large number of vehicle MOCs complete with parts lists and step-by-step instructions. For a beginner, a clearly structured blueprint matters far more than a flashy cover image; the quality of the file often decides the quality of the finished model.

### What to Check Before You Download

Before downloading anything, confirm two things. First, is the parts list complete for that blueprint, and does it state colours and quantities? Second, are step-by-step instructions included, particularly for tricky areas like wheel arches and doors? Once you have the blueprint, read through the overall structure first so you understand how the model goes together before you pick up a single brick.

## How to Pick a High-Quality Vehicle Blueprint

Judge a blueprint on three dimensions. Start with preview image clarity — if the details are blurry, skip it. Next, check whether the parts list is organised properly; a messy list with inconsistent labelling makes sorting bricks far more expensive in time. Finally, look at whether the building instructions are complete: good blueprints add notes on the critical steps. Build feedback in the comments is worth reading too. A strong blueprint should let you build smoothly without needing to read any accompanying text.

## Key Points for Building a Vehicle MOC

### Wheel Arches and Track Width

Wheel arches are the visual core of any vehicle MOC. Too narrow, and the tyres rub against the body when you steer; too wide, and the whole car looks bloated. Decide the tyre outer diameter and track width first, then design the arch curve — this single step determines whether the blueprint actually works in bricks. Many blueprints devote a separate section to arch detail for exactly this reason.

### Suspension and Shock Absorption

Plenty of sports car brick builds add suspension and shocks to the chassis for playability. Watch how suspension travel matches body weight: too stiff and the model behaves like a brick, too soft and it sags. If the blueprint includes complex drivetrain work, gather the gears and axles from the parts list before you start. Many blueprints also recommend a specific shock stiffness for the model.

### Cockpit and Proportions

Getting proportions right is what makes or breaks a vehicle MOC. Take a typical supercar: body length, wheelbase and track width all have to agree with each other. The steering wheel and seats inside the cockpit are small details, but they lift the finished model considerably. A blueprint made with real care will even specify cockpit details clearly, and that is one of the best ways to judge blueprint quality.

## Classic Vehicle MOC Types to Try

There are many classic vehicle categories. A few worth starting with:

- Sports cars: the emphasis is on clean lines and flow, making them good practice for curved slopes and wheel arch shaping;
- Off-roaders: rugged structure and long suspension travel, ideal for learning chassis and shock design;
- Construction vehicles: excavators and cranes being the obvious examples, with hydraulic structures and detailed cabs that suit more advanced builders;
- Police cars and fire engines: high-recognition liveries and warning lights, and easy to pair with a scene or diorama.
