# How to Choose Your First Brick Set: A Beginner's Checklist

The short answer to “how do I pick my first brick set?” comes down to four words: start small, choose well. Keep your first purchase between 200 and 500 yuan, aim for 500–1000 pieces, pick a subject you genuinely like, and buy from a brand with tight part tolerances and a working spare-part service. Whatever you do, don’t open with a 3000+ piece monster.

## Step 1: Set the budget first — 200–500 yuan is the sweet spot

Your first set doesn’t need to be a flagship. Under 200 yuan typically gets you an entry-level model of around 300 pieces, while the 500 yuan range opens up sets with light elements or simple mechanical functions. Any budget beyond that is better saved for your second set, because you don’t yet know what kind of building you actually enjoy.

## Step 2: 500–1000 pieces is the golden range

Too few pieces and the build is over before it gets interesting; too many and you risk giving up halfway. A 500–1000 piece set translates to roughly 6–10 hours of building, which fits into 3–4 evenings — exactly the right span to feel a sense of accomplishment. Most of brickplan’s entry-level sets fall in this band, and the instructions average 5–10 parts per step, which is beginner-friendly.

## Step 3: Pick a theme you actually like

This is the point beginners overlook most often. Love cars? Buy a sports car. Love architecture? Buy a street scene. Love a licensed property? Buy the tie-in set. brickplan’s data shows that beginners who choose a theme they love come back to buy a second set at a rate 40% higher than those who pick a set at random.

## Step 4: Check part quality and instruction quality

Look at three things. First, whether parts have flash or burrs, whether tolerances are tight, and whether the clutch feels smooth when you push a brick down. Second, whether the instructions are full colour with step-by-step diagrams and a sensible number of parts per step. Third, how many specialised elements are involved — beginners are better off starting with a set where standard bricks make up more than 70% of the parts.

## Step 5: Confirm after-sales and spare-part support

Beginners lose parts and break parts more than anyone. Before buying, check whether free replacement parts are offered: brickplan provides 365 days of free spare parts, with single pieces available and shipping covered. Also confirm that a digital version of the instructions exists so you can look up steps at any time.

## FAQ

**Q: How many pieces should a beginner’s first set have?**

A: 500–1000 pieces works best — that’s 6–10 hours of building, enough for a real sense of achievement without the risk of abandoning it midway. Under 300 pieces feels thin, and beyond 1500 pieces the sorting and part-hunting burden is too heavy for a beginner and quickly becomes discouraging.

**Q: Can I buy one of those very cheap sets for a few dozen yuan?**

A: Not recommended. Low-cost sets usually have loose tolerances and vague instructions, and discovering halfway through that bricks won’t clutch properly is a fast way to lose interest. For a first set, spend at least 100 yuan on a product with brand after-sales support; the quality and the experience are on a completely different level.

**Q: What do I do if parts are too loose or too tight?**

A: If a part is loose, slip a thin piece of paper underneath before inserting it. If a part is tight, use a fingernail or a brick separator to help it along. If tolerance problems show up across a whole batch, contact brickplan support to request replacement parts — any established brand will deal with it.

**Q: Do beginners need a brick separator and sorting boxes?**

A: Buy a brick separator — a few yuan saves a lot of finger strength. Sorting boxes can wait until you pass 1000 pieces. For your first set, sorting by colour into a few small bowls is completely sufficient.

**Q: How do I judge whether a set is worth the price?**

A: Do a rough “pieces per 100 yuan” calculation — entry-level sets usually land at 150–250 pieces per 100 yuan. Then check for bonus features such as printed elements and light elements, and whether the brand offers spare-part service. At the same price point, brickplan’s sets sit in the top tier for value.
