# Countryside Scene Building Tutorial: 5 Zones for a Farm MOC

Here is the short version: build the farmhouse and barn first to establish the main body of the scene, then use wheat fields, roads and fences to tie the zones together, add the well and windmill as finishing touches, and finally bring the whole thing to life with livestock and minifigures.

Many builders who go looking for a countryside building tutorial have no idea where to start. The appeal of the rural theme is that it is made of several independent but connected scenes — farmhouse, barn, fields, roads, well, mill, livestock — and each one is worth polishing on its own. This tutorial breaks the countryside down by scene type and walks through the key points of every part, so you can put together a layered miniature farm world full of everyday life.

## 1. Farmhouse and Barn: The Core Buildings

The farmhouse and barn are the main body that no countryside build can skip. For the farmhouse, start with 2x4 bricks as the base and use warm tones — cream white, brick red, dark brown — to create a rustic feel. Lay the roof in layers with slope bricks or tile bricks so it has a natural pitch. The barn should stress "big" and "empty": leave interior space for hay bales and farm tools. Join the two buildings with a short wooden platform so they read as one compound, and the main structures of your countryside build are standing.

## 2. Fields and Crops: Wheat and Vegetable Plots

Farmland is the part that pays off fastest. For a wheat field, lay 1x1 yellow and gold plates in rows, scatter a few green bricks among them to suggest young shoots, and use brown plates as ridges to divide the plots. For the vegetable patch, use green studs for leafy crops and small red and orange studs as tomatoes and carrots. Stand a small wooden sign at the edge of the plot reading "vegetable garden" and the detail instantly comes alive — the whole field area immediately reads as a harvest.

## 3. Country Roads and Fences

Roads are the thread that links the scenes together. In countryside builds, dirt roads are usually made from gray and brown 1x2 plates laid in a staggered pattern, with dark studs along the edges to suggest trodden earth. Fences can be built from 1x1 posts plus horizontal rails, or you can use ready-made fence pieces in white or natural wood. Run the fence along the road and enclose the fields and buildings inside it, and the countryside scene naturally gains a sense of boundary.

## 4. The Well and Windmill: Finishing Touches

The well and windmill are the finishing touches of a countryside build. For the well, ring round bricks into a well mouth, add a cross-shaped support and a hanging bucket, and drop a few trans-blue studs inside the bucket as water. For the windmill, stack cylindrical bricks into a tall tower and add a set of rotating blades on top, connected with gear pieces so a light touch sets them spinning — genuinely fun. These two small scenes take up very little space but greatly raise the completeness of the whole village, which makes them the best value section of any countryside tutorial.

## 5. Livestock and Farm Minifigures

The last step is "injecting life". Animal pieces such as cows, sheep, pigs and chickens are the bonus that lifts a countryside build — place them beside the barn or inside the fence. Farm minifigures can be kitted out with straw hats, shovels and buckets, posed feeding animals, plowing or driving a cart. Once the minifigures and animals go in, a static scene gains a story, and only then is the build truly finished.

## Final Thoughts

This tutorial splits the countryside into five categories — farmhouse and barn, fields and crops, roads and fences, well and windmill, livestock and minifigures — so you can tackle the scene one module at a time. Start with the part that interests you most, then gradually combine the pieces into a complete village.
