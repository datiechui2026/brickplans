# Brick Pickup Truck MOC Review: Chassis, Steering and Bed

Pickups are the most practical subject in brick vehicle building — you get to shape a body and you get to make it work. A good pickup MOC can carry cargo in the bed, sit on an adjustable chassis and take a whole range of add-on mods. In this review I built one hardcore pickup plan end to end and picked apart the frame, the suspension, the bed structure and the engine details to see how far it goes on both looks and playability.

## 1. Plan Quality and Parts List

The plan is genuinely professional. Every major assembly is numbered step by step, the parts list is split by color across separate pages, and spare-part quantities are noted, so anyone building from loose bulk stock can sort without guesswork. Two complete builds are supplied: a display version clad in smooth tiles for shelf duty, and a functional version that adds steerable front wheels, a bed that tips open and a working rear suspension. Both share the same chassis drawings, so switching between them costs very little — a real plus in this plan's favour. The one annoyance: several hinge pieces are drawn in very similar colors, and after an hour of squinting at the page they start to blur together.

## 2. Frame, Suspension and Steering

Start with the structural core. The frame uses through-running longitudinal beams, with the front and rear axles locked in place by clip pieces, and torsional rigidity is good — a free drop from desk height left it in one piece. On the functional version, the front-wheel steering runs through a set of gears and linkages and swings to roughly 40 degrees. The plan is explicit about the gear ratio and where the limit stops sit, and once built you turn the steering wheel and the wheels follow smoothly, with no binding.

The rear suspension pairs spring shock absorbers with curved swing arms. Press down on the tail and there is obvious rebound; driving it over a strip of tape laid out as a speed bump, the body attitude looks natural and is noticeably more comfortable than a plain rigid-axle setup.

## 3. The Bed and Detail Work

The bed is the highlight of this pickup MOC. All three sides fold down, the tailgate is mounted on hinge pieces, and once it is flat you can use it as a ramp to push small cargo crates up into the load bay. The bed floor is finished with studded plates as an anti-slip texture, so cargo does not slide around.

Small details are handled well too. The hood lifts to show an engine bay where different colored bricks stand in for the engine block and the pipework. The wheel arches are edged with curved pieces, which gives the truck its muscle. Mirrors, bumpers and a tow hook are all present. Three color schemes are provided — military green, desert yellow and classic red — and the plan lists the matching color IDs for each.

## 4. Pros, Cons and Who It's For

On the plus side: the structure is solid, the functions are plentiful and the two-version format is flexible, so one plan covers both display and play. On the minus side: the functional version runs to a lot of parts (around 1,100 pieces), the steering linkages take some patience to tune, and the spare parts are not cheap to assemble.

Who it suits: intermediate and advanced builders who already enjoy vehicle MOCs and want to try functional modification, and it also works well as a hands-on mechanical introduction for a child. Beginners should build the display version first, get familiar with the chassis layout, and then upgrade to the functional build.

## Final Thoughts

A pickup that steers and tips its bed is a collectible on the shelf and a toy in your hands.
