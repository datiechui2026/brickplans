# Brick Mixer Truck MOC: Chassis to Rotating Drum Build Guide

## The Short Answer

A mixer truck MOC is less intimidating than it looks. Three things carry the whole build: a chassis that can take the load, a drum that actually turns, and a cab that looks the part. Following this guide you'll need roughly 1,200 parts at about 350 yuan total (estimated at 0.3 yuan per part from bulk lots). A beginner can finish the main assembly in around 4 hours; an experienced builder gets there in about 2.5. Sort out the chassis load-bearing and the drum's rotation mechanism and you end up with a model you can push across the table while the drum spins by hand, at over 90% likeness. ## Why a Mixer Truck MOC Is Worth Building

Concrete mixer trucks are among the most recognizable vehicles in construction-equipment building. The signature slanted drum and the discharge chute at the rear make them instantly identifiable. Compared with a plain truck MOC, the structure is more demanding: the drum has to sit fixed at an angle and still rotate around its own axis. That tilted-axis rotation is genuinely good practice for spatial thinking. The finished model also displays beautifully — the orange-and-white livery against black rubber tires gives it real visual punch on a shelf.

## Step 1: Chassis and Frame

The chassis is the load-bearing base of the whole vehicle. Once the drum is filled with simulated "concrete," the center of gravity sits high, so the frame has to be solid. Start from a 6x18 baseplate as the foundation, then reinforce both sides with 1x8 bricks stacked three layers high. Total length comes out around 28 cm and width around 9 cm, close to a 1:12 scale of the real vehicle. The front wheels connect to the steering wheel through a steering mechanism; the rear axles carry the weight on double bearings, with 4 wheels per axle and 8 wheels in total, which makes the model very stable to push. Leave a cross-axle hole in the middle of the chassis for the turntable — that is the critical interface for the drum later on, and if the position drifts, the drum's tilt angle will be wrong.

## Step 2: The Mixing Drum and Rotation Mechanism

The drum is the soul of the build. Wrap curved panels (1x3x2 curved bricks) into a tapered drum, narrow at the front and wide at the rear, using about 90 curved pieces for a drum roughly 16 cm long. The rotation uses a tilted-axis approach: fix a cross-axle bracket at a 30-degree angle to the ground on the chassis turntable, and mount the drum on that bracket through gear engagement. A 24-tooth gear drives an 8-tooth gear at a 3:1 ratio, so turning the handle on the side spins the drum through a full revolution smoothly. Inside the drum you can add a spiral pattern — 1x2 slope bricks staggered around the wall — to imitate the pushing action of real mixing blades. Finish with the discharge chute at the rear, attached with two hinge pieces so it swings up and down through 45 degrees.

## Step 3: Cab and Finishing Details

The cab follows the boxy shape of a real mixer: build the body from 1x2 bricks, use transparent pieces for the windshield, and make the doors openable. Add a warning beacon on the cab roof (a red transparent round stud) plus guard rails and a step at the rear. For the color scheme, orange and white should dominate with black as support — roughly 400 orange bricks, 350 white bricks and 250 black pieces. When the build is done, apply a "concrete" decal to the side of the drum and the detail level jumps immediately. ## FAQ

**Q: How do I make the drum actually rotate?**
The key is the turntable on the chassis plus the 30-degree tilted-axle bracket. A large 24-tooth gear drives a small 8-tooth gear at a 3:1 ratio, and turning the side handle spins the drum smoothly. Leave about 0.1 mm of play in the gear mesh — too tight and it jams, too loose and it slips.

**Q: Can I build it from the loose parts I already own?**
Yes, but check your inventory first. Of the roughly 1,200 parts in this build, curved panels and transparent windshields are the scarce ones. If you don't have them, ordinary bricks can replace the curved pieces, though the shape will look boxier. **Q: What's the difference between a mixer truck MOC and a regular truck MOC?**
The big difference is the drum rotation. A regular truck is static cargo; a mixer needs a tilted-axis rotation mechanism, which means gear transmission and angle calculation, so it's a step up in difficulty. If you've already built two or three truck MOCs, a mixer is a very satisfying next challenge.
