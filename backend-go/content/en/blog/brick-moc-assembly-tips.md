# MOC Building Tips: A Faster Workflow for Beginners

Let's start with the conclusion: there are no shortcuts in MOC building, but there is an efficient process — sort by color, build in structural modules, and follow the instructions step by step. Once you have that method down, a 3200-piece MOC can drop from around 12 hours of building time to roughly 7. Below are the most practical techniques, tested by builders in the BrickPlan community.

## Step 1: Unpack and Sort — Build a Parts Map

Don't start snapping bricks the moment you have the instructions. First sort every part by color and type: basic bricks, plates and special pieces each go into their own container, ideally using 6-8 compartment boxes arranged by color family. In practice, spending 30-40 minutes on sorting can raise your overall building efficiency by about 50%. While you sort, count as you go and check the quantities against the parts list in the BrickPlan instructions — a normal missing-part rate is under 1%, and catching it early keeps you from stalling halfway through a build.

Store special pieces (minifigures, animals, printed parts) in a separate compartment so the printed patterns don't get scratched. Transparent parts need their own container too — mixed in with ordinary bricks they scratch very easily.

## Step 2: Build in Modules, Large Structures First

MOC instructions are usually divided into modules, and the correct order is to build the base and main frame (the large structures) first, then fill in the details (the small parts). When a module is finished, store it in its own box with a numbered label. BrickPlan instructions list an estimated time for each module — an 800-piece street scene module, for example, takes about 90 minutes.

The advantage of modular building is that if you make a mistake you only have to take apart a small section instead of starting over. Get into the habit of opening only one bag at a time, returning loose parts to their containers promptly and keeping the table clear. When a step doesn't make sense, rotate the 3D view in the BrickPlan instructions to confirm the placement — that is faster than repeatedly disassembling and rebuilding.

## Step 3: Final Checks and Reinforcement

Once everything is assembled, do three things. First, press down on every connection point and reinforce any loose spots with a cross axle or a reinforced brick. Second, check that left and right symmetric structures actually match. Third, photograph the finished model.

For moving joints on an MOC, replace ordinary bricks with Technic parts where you can — strength improves by roughly 70%. Finally, put the finished build in a display case and keep it out of direct sunlight to prevent the parts from ageing and yellowing. If you plan to display it long-term, add anti-slip pads under the base for better stability, dust it regularly with a soft brush, and check every connection point for looseness once every six months.

## FAQ

**Q: I can never find the part I need while building an MOC. What should I do?**

A: Always sort by color and type before you start, and open the next bag only after finishing the current module. If a part is still missing, check it against the parts list in the BrickPlan instructions — most of the time it has simply been mixed in with bricks of a similar color.

**Q: What if I discover a missing part halfway through a build?**

A: First check whether it is still in a bag or a sorting box. Once you've confirmed it is genuinely missing, contact the instruction designer or the platform's customer service for a replacement. BrickPlan lets you report a missing part directly from the instruction detail page, and the average replacement turnaround is about 5-7 days.

**Q: How long does an MOC usually take to build?**

A: It depends on the piece count and your experience: about 2 hours for 500 pieces, about 5 hours for 2000 pieces, and roughly 8-12 hours for 3000 pieces or more. Using the per-step time estimates in BrickPlan instructions, you can split the work across several sessions and complete one module at a time.

**Q: Which tools make MOC building easiest?**

A: A brick separator is essential. It's also worth adding tweezers for small parts and a soft brush for dust, about 30 yuan in total. Choose a sorting box with six or more compartments — when you have a lot of parts, the efficiency difference is very obvious.
