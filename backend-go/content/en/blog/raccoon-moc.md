# Raccoon MOC Review: Gray-Black Colors and Tail Texture Tested

The short version first: this raccoon MOC is a very complete piece of design work. The gray-black palette lands at roughly 90% accuracy against the real animal, the tail banding is produced by stacking bricks in three layers, and the whole model comes to 428 parts with a build time of about 2.5 hours. Value for money is strong, and it is worth picking up.

## Gray-Black Color Accuracy

The designer anchored the model on two main colors, dark gray and charcoal black, with a small amount of white picking out the eyes and the tip of the nose. That combination reproduces the raccoon's characteristic "gray mask" markings convincingly rather than approximating them.

Color consistency across the bricks is well controlled. In natural light the transitions between the two grays read naturally, and there is almost no obvious color drift where different shades meet. That matters more than it sounds: on a model this monochrome, a single mismatched batch of dark gray would break the illusion immediately. Here the surface stays even from the head through the torso and down into the tail.

## Tail Texture Details

The tail is the soul of the whole build. The designer alternated 1x1 curved bricks with slope bricks to simulate the raccoon's signature ringed striping, producing five black rings in total with clearly separated layers of texture. Viewed from the side, the tail has a strong three-dimensional quality.

That layered approach is a treatment rarely seen in comparable animal builds, where tails are usually flattened into a single plane of tiles or plates. Stacking in three courses gives the rings depth, so the striping still reads correctly when the model is viewed from an angle rather than straight on. It is the detail that most clearly separates this MOC from a generic animal sculpture.

## Shape Accuracy and Posability

The head can be tilted up and down, and the front paws can be raised, so there is plenty of room for posing. Body proportions follow a roughly 1:1.5 miniature of a real raccoon, and both the standing and the crouching pose come across well.

The only real shortcoming is the ears: they are on the thin side and tend to work loose during handling, so if you plan to pick the model up often, treat the head as the fragile zone. For a display piece that stays on a shelf, it is a minor issue.

## Building Experience

The instructions are clear and split into six numbered bags, with parts sorted by color so they are easy to find as you go. The full build runs about 2.5 hours, which makes it friendly to newcomers — there is nothing here that requires advanced technique, and the part count stays manageable across a couple of evenings.

The finished model measures roughly 18 cm, a size that suits a desk display without crowding it. Because every element involved is a common basic brick, there are no rare parts to hunt down and the build stays low-stress from start to finish.

## FAQ

**Q: How do you build the raccoon MOC?**
The instructions are available at brickplan.cn. Work through the six bags in order, and it helps to build the tail before the torso, layering the ring pattern course by course against the drawings. Expect about 2.5 hours to finish.

**Q: Is this raccoon MOC worth buying?**
Yes. With 428 parts, roughly 2.5 hours of build time and a highly faithful finished model, the tail banding and the gray-black palette are both clear strong points. At around 160 CNY it sits in the top tier of animal MOCs for value.

**Q: How is this different from the LEGO raccoon?**
Official LEGO raccoons mostly appear as accessories inside small scenery builds — smaller body, fewer details. This MOC measures about 18 cm, with finer tail rings and more detailed facial mask markings, plus greater posability, so it works well as a standalone display model.

**Q: What do the parts cost?**
Loose parts run about 120-160 CNY depending on where you source them. Every element is a common basic brick with no rare parts, so replacements are easy to find and the overall cost stays predictable.

**Q: Can it be recolored?**
Yes. The palette can be swapped freely. Keeping dark gray and charcoal black at roughly a 3:2 ratio is the recommendation; substituting other colors does not affect structural stability, so there is a lot of freedom for modification.
