# MOC Village Review: Cottage and Farmland Details Worth Buying?

The short answer first: this village MOC is a very complete build, and the roofs and farmland are the standout features. The catch is a high piece count and a high price, so it suits patient, experienced builders. Beginners should read the difficulty section before ordering. This review covers the highly rated "Rural Village" blueprint from the BrickPlan community.

## Cottage Builds and Roof Structure

The core of the model is three cottages with white walls and dark brown timber beams. SNOT technique is used heavily to keep the wall surfaces flat and clean, which is what gives the facades their tidy, plastered look instead of an exposed-stud grid.

The roofs are dual-pitch and share roughly 420 tile and slope pieces, layered course over course to produce genuine shingle depth. Each cottage stands about 18cm tall, and the roof lifts off as a single unit. Inside there is a fireplace, a bed and a dining table, so there is a decent amount of play value once the build is finished.

## Farmland Details and Scene Accuracy

Farmland takes up about 30% of the total scene footprint, and it is where most of the character lives: 6 field rows, 2 scarecrows and 1 wooden handcart.

The wheat is laid with light yellow and olive green bricks alternating row by row. The texture is strong, and from a distance it reads like ripe wheat moving in the wind. The scene also includes a wooden fence, a well and 3 small animals (a chicken, a duck and a sheep). Set together, the composition feels finished rather than sparse, and it photographs very well.

## Build Difficulty and Value

The full model runs about 2,800 pieces and takes roughly 8 hours, which puts it in the medium-to-high difficulty band. The roof and the farmland are the demanding parts, because both rely on long stretches of repeated placement that test your patience more than your technique.

At about ¥1,299, the cost works out to roughly ¥0.46 per piece, which is on the high side. The verdict: detail-focused builders and fans of rural, pastoral scenes will get their money's worth. If your budget is tight, wait for a major sale before ordering.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this village MOC hard to build?**
A: Overall it sits at medium-to-high difficulty. The repeated roof and farmland sections are tedious, but the structure is solid. Bag the parts out separately and set aside around 8 hours. First-time builders should follow the instructions step by step and are unlikely to go wrong.

**Q: Can it be mixed with ordinary brick sets?**
A: Yes, without any problem. This MOC uses standard bricks and is compatible with mainstream brands for mixed building. That said, it contains a lot of roof tiles and SNOT parts, so store the special pieces carefully when you disassemble it so nothing goes missing for a future rebuild.

**Q: Is 2,800 pieces for ¥1,299 worth it?**
A: At roughly ¥0.46 per piece, it sits in the normal-to-high range for a scene MOC of this type. If you love pastoral scenes and care about the farmland and roof detail, it justifies the price. If you mainly want minifigures or just want to build casually, wait for a promotion or choose a smaller model.

**Q: How big is the finished model, and is it easy to display?**
A: The finished build is about 52cm long, 28cm wide and 20cm tall, taking up roughly 0.15 square metres. A bookcase or display shelf is a good fit. Mount it on a baseplate to hold it together, and lift the whole thing when moving it so it does not come apart.
