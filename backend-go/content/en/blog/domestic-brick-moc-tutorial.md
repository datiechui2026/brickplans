# Domestic Brick MOC Tutorial: Build Your First Original Model

If you have been building domestic brick sets for a while, you probably have a stack of finished models and a growing urge to make something that is actually yours. MOC (My Own Creation) means building without instructions — taking your own idea and turning it into a model that exists nowhere else. This tutorial is a practical, start-from-zero guide for builders who have never designed anything before.

## 1. Start With the Right Sets and Parts

Before you design anything, you need raw material. For a beginner, the smart buy is not an expensive flagship set — it is a domestic brick range with **good part versatility and a high proportion of basic elements**. You want lots of generic bricks that are compatible with standard studs, so that taking a model apart does not feel like destroying something valuable. If your budget is tight, buy bulk second-hand parts sold by weight on resale platforms. That is the best value per brick, and it gives you enough stock to experiment through several rounds.

When you sort through parts, check two things. First, clutch power — the connection should be neither too tight (it hurts your fingers to pull apart) nor too loose (the model falls apart when you touch it). Second, the surface should be clean and free of grease. Domestic brick manufacturing has matured considerably, and both clutch and colour consistency are now good enough for beginners to practise on without frustration.

## 2. Learn to Take Apart Before You Learn to Build

Many beginners jump straight into "pure original design", then discover halfway through that they are short of parts or that the structure will not stand up — and the project stalls unfinished. The right approach is **teardown first, creation second**:

1. Build one instruction-based set completely, and study its structural logic — how the base is laid, how corners are handled, how walls are locked together and reinforced.
2. Then take it apart and sort the parts into storage boxes by colour and function. You will quickly learn how each type of part behaves, and you will find parts twice as fast later.
3. Finally, try modifying sections: add a cargo bed to a small truck, a chimney to a house, a different arm to a mech. This is the most natural way into MOC building.

Remember one line: **teardown is the best teacher**. It takes you beyond following instructions and shows you *why* a model is put together the way it is.

## 3. Build Order: From Baseplate to Detail

Once you have a parts stock and some teardown experience, work through the following steps:

1. **Decide the subject and size.** Is it a building, a vehicle or a scene? Mark out the footprint on the ground with bricks so the model cannot creep larger and get out of control.
2. **Lay the base.** Use large plates, or a combination of beams and plates, with the layers running crosswise for reinforcement. This is the most stable foundation of the whole model — if the base holds, everything above it holds.
3. **Build the main structure.** Work from bottom to top and from outside in. Make sure the four walls can lock together first, then think about interior space. A solid skeleton matters far more than a good-looking surface.
4. **Add details.** Once the body is done, dress it with windows, doors, railings, lighting elements and other specialised parts to bring the build to life.

Check the clutch at every step as you go. Do not wait until an entire wall collapses before you go back and redo it.

## 4. Common Mistakes to Avoid

A few traps catch almost every beginner:

- **Trying to build too big.** Keep your first model within roughly 20×20 studs. Anything larger and you risk losing patience halfway and leaving it half-finished.
- **Ignoring structural strength.** Chasing looks without reinforcing load-bearing areas means the model falls apart at the first touch. Use staggered, offset connections at corners instead of long straight seams.
- **Mixing all parts together.** Sort by colour and type. It saves you half your search time and keeps the session enjoyable.
- **Being afraid to dismantle and change things.** The greatest pleasure of MOC is rebuilding over and over. Taking a model apart and starting again is nothing to be embarrassed about — the more you rebuild, the more experience you gain.

## Final Thoughts

After reading this tutorial you already have what you need for a first original build: choose the right parts, learn by taking models apart, follow a sensible build order, and avoid the usual beginner traps.
