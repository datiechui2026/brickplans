# DCMOC Blueprint Review: Batmobile and Hall of Justice Plans

After three years of building DC-themed brick models, I have bought plenty of **DCMOC blueprints** — and wasted money on a few. This review comes from actual bench time, not cover photos. I break down the two most representative blueprint types, the Batmobile and the Hall of Justice, and I give credit where it is due and call out the problems directly.

## Batmobile Blueprint: Strong Accuracy, Expensive Parts List

Start with the 1989 Batmobile blueprint I finished most recently. I would give the shape accuracy a 9 out of 10 — the streamlined body, the angle of the rear fins and the curve of the bat emblem are all handled correctly, and the aggressive nose-down stance is reproduced faithfully. The step breakdown is also reasonable: the viewing angle changes roughly every 30 pieces, so it is hard to lose your place.

There are two real drawbacks. First, the part count is far higher than you would expect. The finished model fits in your palm, yet it needs over a thousand pieces, with a heavy share of dark black elements and curved slope parts. Gathering those from loose stock is genuinely painful; buying a set to part out works out cheaper. Second, the chassis assembly order is unfriendly to newcomers: the front wheel steering and the folding rear fins require you to adjust gear meshing at the same time, and the instructions devote only two pages of diagrams to this section. On my first build I had to tear it back down and redo it three times. Overall this is a "great blueprint, high barrier to entry" situation.

## Hall of Justice Blueprint: Stunning Structure, Instructions Let It Down

The other blueprint that stuck with me is the Hall of Justice diorama. This version turns the Justice League's iconic hall into a three-level cutaway, with each level devoted to one hero's space — the Batcave monitor wall, elements of Superman's Fortress of Solitude, and Wonder Woman's weapon display. The detail density is high, and once built it looks superb on a shelf. Among **DCMOC blueprints**, this kind of scene design sits at the top tier.

The complaints are equally clear. The colour legend in the instructions is printed far too light, so dark blue and black plates are almost indistinguishable on the page. I mis-built several times and eventually had to work backwards from the parts list. Separately, no cable channel is provided for the lighting wires: you only discover at the top level that the light cable has to exit through the door gap, which badly hurts the look. If you are a detail obsessive, plan your lighting route before you start building.

## Three Practical Rules for Choosing a Blueprint

- **Judge accuracy before reputation**: shape accuracy comes first. If the photos look awkward, the built model will look worse.
- **Check the part difficulty in advance**: scan the parts list. If the share of special elements (curved pieces, large plates, printed parts) is high, double both your budget and your patience.
- **The instructions are the hidden variable**: step viewpoints, colour legends and close-ups of key structures decide whether the build goes smoothly. Do not order based on the cover image alone.

## Wrap-Up

Honestly, DC-themed **DCMOC blueprints** split sharply into two camps: some have top-tier accuracy and weak instructions, others look beautiful on paper but drive you away with the parts list. The good news is that once you know your own parts pool and your patience limit, and you apply the three rules above, your odds of hitting a dud drop considerably.
