# Suspension Bridge MOC: Towers, Cable and Deck Guide

Here is the short version: a suspension bridge MOC works because of a three-part load path — the towers carry the weight, the main cable transfers the force, and the deck hangs from it. This plan uses technic chain links to imitate the main cable, stands the towers about 20 cm tall and stretches the deck to 60 cm. It takes roughly 1,800 parts and a budget of around 500 CNY, and in testing it held 2 kg once finished.

A suspension bridge is one of the best subjects for showing structural elegance in a MOC, and almost all the difficulty sits in two places: getting the main cable taut and getting the deck to hang evenly. Below is the build broken into stages, one step at a time.

**Step 1: Build the towers.** Raise the two towers with brown technic bricks until each reaches about 20 cm. Every tower is made of 4 columns and 3 cross beams, and the base of each column gets reinforced with plates so the tower stays perfectly vertical. The towers are the load-bearing core of the whole bridge, so check each one against a flat plate as you go — a tower that leans will put uneven tension on the main cable later.

**Step 2: Build the deck frame.** The deck is 60 cm long and splits into 3 sections of 20 cm each, built from gray plates with railings along both sides. Building it in sections makes adjustments much easier, and you join everything into one piece with connectors at the end. Leave two longitudinal channels running down the middle of the deck — those are where the hangers attach.

**Step 3: Run the main cable.** The main cable is simulated with technic chain links. It passes over the tops of the towers and anchors at the abutments on both ends. The key technique is "slack first, tighten later": hang the chain loosely first, then pull it tight from both ends at the same time so the cable settles into a natural catenary curve. A sag of about 3 cm looks best.

**Step 4: Add the hangers and hang the deck.** Use short chain links or cord for the hangers, one roughly every 4 cm, dropping from the main cable down into the channels in the deck. Install them symmetrically — middle first, then outward toward both sides — so the deck does not end up loaded off-center. Once everything is attached, the deck will arch upward very slightly. That is normal and shows the structure is under load.

**Step 5: Load test and reinforce.** Start adding bricks or weights at the center of the deck. In testing this bridge carried 2 kg, roughly 50 standard bricks. If the deck starts to sag, add two technic bricks of counterweight at each main cable anchor point — that solves the problem in nearly every case.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I get the main cable on a brick suspension bridge taut?**
A: Hang the chain slack first, then tighten from both anchor points at the same time so the cable falls into a natural catenary, with sag held at around 3 cm. The plans include a specific tightening order in step four — just follow it.

**Q: How much does a suspension bridge MOC cost?**
A: This build is about 1,800 parts and around 500 CNY, covering the towers, the main cable chain and the full deck assembly. To save money you can build a half bridge with a single tower, which cuts roughly a third of the parts.

**Q: What if the deck collapses?**
A: First check that the towers are vertical, then confirm the hangers are installed symmetrically. Most collapses come from anchor points that are too loose — add two technic bricks of counterweight at each end of the main cable and load capacity returns to about 2 kg.

**Q: What is the difference between suspension bridge and cable-stayed bridge MOCs?**
A: On a suspension bridge the main cable runs continuously over the tower tops and the deck hangs from hangers, which suits long spans. On a cable-stayed bridge the angled cables pull the deck directly and the towers are shorter. Both types are covered, and the suspension bridge is the more classic structure.

**Q: Can I replace the technic chain with string?**
A: Yes — cotton cord with hooks can imitate the main cable, but the sag is harder to control. The original chain runs about 2 cm per link, which lets you tune the curve precisely, so beginners should start with the supplied parts.
