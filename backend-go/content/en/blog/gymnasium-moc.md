# How to Build a LEGO Gymnasium MOC: Stands, Track and Dome

A brick gymnasium is one of the most popular subjects among architectural builders. A convincing arena anchors an entire city layout, and it doubles as a real test of your building skills. Below we break the project down piece by piece, from the main structure and the seating tiers to the running track and the dome overhead, so you can put together a sports venue of your own.

## 1. The Main Structure of the Venue

The skeleton of the gymnasium is the soul of the whole MOC. Start by laying out a base from standard plates and dividing it into functional zones: the central court, the stands on all four sides, and the entrance and exit passages. For the exterior walls, alternate light grey and white bricks to create the airy, translucent look of a modern sports building. Remember to leave openings for a spectator entrance and a VIP passage, which gives the model a layered, genuinely walkable feel rather than a sealed box.

## 2. Designing the Stands

The stands are the most recognisable part of any gymnasium. Stack slope pieces in steps to form the tiered seating, keeping the rake at roughly 30 degrees, which reads as the most natural angle. You can colour-code the seats by zone: red for the home supporters and blue for the away section, which looks sharp and makes the crowd's allegiance easy to read. Add a ring of transparent pieces along the top of the stands as a barrier. It keeps the seating safe without blocking the view into the arena.

## 3. The Court and Running Track

For an indoor arena, build the floor from large smooth plates and mark out a basketball or badminton court, then use thin tiles to trace the sidelines and centre line. If you are going for a multi-sport venue, lay a ring-shaped running track as well: assemble it from multiple curved plates, and place the field events area on the inside of the track. The floor colour at the centre of the court should contrast with the stands so the visual weight lands on the competition area.

## 4. The Roof and Dome Structure

The dome is the technical challenge of a gymnasium MOC, and also the part with the most impact. A truss-plus-transparent-panel approach works well: first build a radial or grid-shaped load-bearing frame from small pieces, then cover it with transparent curved elements as skylights, mimicking the retractable roof of a modern arena. Keep the support columns symmetrical so the structure stays stable and you avoid the embarrassment of a collapsed roof.

## 5. Staging the Sports Scene

Finally, bring the venue to life with minifigures and accessories: sprinting athletes, a referee at the sideline and spectators waving flags all raise the tension of the scene instantly. You can also add a scoreboard, spotlights and awnings over the stands. If your collection includes light-up elements, fit an LED inside the dome. A gymnasium at night is far more striking.

Building a brick gymnasium MOC is not complicated; the key lies in symmetrical structure and care over the details.
