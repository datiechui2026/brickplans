# Underwater LEGO Scene for Kids: A 300-Piece Build Plan

Short answer first: an underwater LEGO scene does not need a 1,000-piece flagship set. Roughly 300 pieces and a budget under RMB 200 is enough to assemble a 25 cm square scene that a parent and a child can build together. The plan below splits the work into three modules — coral reef, school of fish, mini submarine — sorts the parts by color, and can be finished in about two hours by a child aged 5 and up working alongside an adult. Value for money and a sense of accomplishment are both high. Here is the breakdown, in four parts.

## 1. Scene design: three modules keep the entry barrier low

The biggest risk with an underwater build is trying to do everything at once. Split it into a coral reef base, a school of fish and a mini submarine instead — each module uses 80–120 pieces, so the child only has to focus on one goal per sitting. The reef gets its depth from stacked curved bricks and slope bricks, the fish are built from 2x4 tiles plus eye stickers, and the submarine pairs cylinder pieces with a transparent canopy. The structures stay simple, but each one is instantly recognizable, which matters when the builder is five.

## 2. Parts list: stock by color and you save the most time

The number one pain point for beginners is never being able to find the piece they need. Sorting parts by color before you start is the fix. Here is the working list:

- **Blue (seabed and water base):** 2x4 plates ×40, 2x2 bricks ×30
- **Orange/red (coral):** curved bricks ×25, slope bricks ×20
- **Yellow (fish bodies):** 2x4 tiles ×15, 1x1 round plates ×10
- **White/transparent (submarine):** cylinder pieces ×8, transparent canopies ×2, propellers ×4
- **Green (seaweed):** 1x1 bricks ×20, leaf pieces ×10

Altogether that comes to about 300 pieces. Priced at the average for comparable loose parts on brickplan, the cost lands at roughly RMB 150–200 — far below the RMB 500–600 that official scene sets typically ask for.

## 3. Build order and how to split the work

Work in this order: base, then coral, then fish, then submarine. The parent reads the instructions and talks through each step; the child does the clicking and chooses the colors. Photograph each module when it is finished, then join all three onto the baseplate at the end. One module per session is the practical rhythm — it keeps the build moving without stretching anyone's attention. Done this way, the build is itself a complete piece of parent-child collaboration, with some counting and color recognition folded in for free.

## 4. Play value and room to expand

The finished model is about 25 cm long and 12 cm tall, small enough to sit on a desk without taking it over. The modular design makes later expansion easy: add an octopus or swap in a bigger shark by stacking a new module onto the existing baseplate — nothing has to be torn down first. Compared with a single large set, this "grow the scene" approach fits the way children actually keep returning to a build over weeks rather than hours.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I choose parts that suit a child for an underwater LEGO build?**
A: Favor basics such as 2x4 plates, curved bricks and tiles, and bag them by color — blue, orange, yellow, white. 300 pieces and a RMB 200 budget are plenty. Pieces that are too large or too small are both awkward for a 5-year-old to grip.

**Q: Is this parent-child scene worth buying as an official set, or should I source the parts myself?**
A: Sourcing it yourself is the better deal. Official scene sets usually start at RMB 500 and up, while the parts list above comes to roughly RMB 150–200 in loose parts. You can also swap colors freely, and the child gets more of a stake in the result — clearly better value.

**Q: How long does the underwater LEGO build take?**
A: About 1.5–2 hours for a child aged 5 and up with a parent alongside. Split it into two sessions, finishing one module each time. For younger children, stretch it to three sessions so attention doesn't run out.

**Q: How big is the finished model, and will it take up space at home?**
A: Roughly 25 cm long, 12 cm tall and 15 cm wide — about the size of a picture book, so a desk or a shelf will hold it. The modular design also makes it easy to break down for storage in a standard brick box.
