# How to Choose a Supermarket Brick Set (2026 Brand & Price Guide)

Short answer first: the supermarket sets worth your money are mid-range kits of 1,000–1,500 pieces that include a checkout counter and minifigures with a shopping cart, priced in the 150–400 RMB band. "Mini supermarkets" under 300 pieces are display pieces only, with limited play value. The five points below will help you pick the right one.

## 1. Start With Piece Count and Footprint

Piece count decides how much building you actually get. Entry-level sets run 300–600 pieces (about 30–40 RMB) and only make a single-storey corner shop. Mid-range sets of 1,000–1,500 pieces (about 150–300 RMB) can reproduce three zones — checkout, shelving and chilled cabinets. Flagship sets above 2,000 pieces (500 RMB and up) usually add light bricks and an escalator. For most builders a set around 1,200 pieces is the best value: the finished model is roughly 38×25 cm and fits on a shelf without eating space.

## 2. Then Check Minifigures and Accessories

The character of a supermarket set lives in the small accessories: checkout counter, barcode scanner, shopping carts, shopping baskets, drinks cooler, bread rack. A good set ships with 3–5 minifigures, including at least one cashier, one customer and one stock clerk. Two or more shopping carts is the pass mark — plenty of budget sets include only one, which cuts the interactivity sharply. Look at the official accessory list before you order rather than trusting the box render.

## 3. Brand Comparison

Four brands dominate the market right now. LEGO City supermarket sets have the tightest part precision, but 600 pieces already costs over 500 RMB, a clear premium. SEMBO's supermarket line is the value standout: 1,200 pieces for roughly 180–220 RMB, with parts that feel close to LEGO. JAKI specialises in street-scene style supermarkets where the light bricks are the selling point, at about 300 RMB for 1,500 pieces. Qiji suits younger children — larger parts, safer, but fewer details. Overall the three domestic brands cost one-third to one-half of LEGO.

## 4. Traps to Avoid

First, beware the "high piece count, low price" trick: some brands pad the count with piles of 1×1 bricks, which makes for a poor build. Second, check whether the instructions list part numbers — BrickPlan hosts plenty of set instructions you can preview to study the structure in advance. Third, confirm the set is compatible with standard bricks; an incompatible kit can't be extended into a street scene later. Fourth, favour brands that offer a replacement-part service, so missing pieces can be replaced free of charge.

## 5. Where and When to Buy

During major e-commerce sales (618 and Double 11), supermarket sets commonly sell at 50–70% of list price, and SEMBO and JAKI kits can drop to around 120 RMB. You can also hunt discontinued supermarket sets on the second-hand market at Xianyu, but watch for missing pieces and ask the seller for photos of the full part inventory before paying. If you just want to try a supermarket scene without spending, download the free instructions on BrickPlan and build it from the loose parts you already own.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How much should a supermarket brick set cost?**
A: A mid-range set of 1,000–1,500 pieces sits at a fair 150–300 RMB, and domestic brands can reach around 120 RMB during big sales. Mini versions under 300 pieces cost 30–40 RMB and are display pieces only.

**Q: Is there a big difference between LEGO and domestic supermarket sets?**
A: LEGO has better precision and feel, but the same piece count costs 2–3 times more. Domestic brands such as SEMBO and JAKI are strong value and compatible with standard bricks, so they are the better recommendation on a limited budget.

**Q: How many pieces does a supermarket brick set usually have?**
A: Entry-level 300–600 pieces, mid-range 1,000–1,500 pieces, flagship 2,000 pieces and up. Around 1,200 pieces is the most balanced choice, at about 38×25 cm finished, with detail and price both reasonable.

**Q: How do I judge whether a supermarket set is worth buying?**
A: Check three things: whether it has 3–5 minifigures including a cashier, whether it includes two or more shopping carts, and whether the accessories cover a checkout counter and a cooler. Then preview the instructions on BrickPlan to see the interior structure before ordering.

**Q: What if my supermarket set is missing pieces?**
A: Prefer brands with free replacement-part service — contact support with the set number and the missing part number and replacements typically arrive within 7–15 working days. For second-hand sets, ask the seller for photos of all the parts before you buy.
