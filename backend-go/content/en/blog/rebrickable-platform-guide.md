# Rebrickable Guide: Find MOC Instructions for Sets You Own

Anyone who builds MOCs eventually lands on Rebrickable. The platform hosts tens of thousands of player-designed instructions alongside a parts database covering almost every retail set ever produced. For builders outside the US and Europe it doubles as a money-saving tool. This guide starts from zero — you can be up and running in about five minutes.

## Registration and the basic interface

Signing up is free. The one step that matters most comes right after: enter your sets into **My Sets**. Type a set number into the search box — LEGO 42110, for example — and click to add it. Every set you log is folded into your parts inventory automatically, and from that point on the platform can recommend instructions based on *what you already own*. That is the core logic that makes Rebrickable worth the setup time.

## Finding MOCs with sets you already have

Search the main site by keyword, then pay attention to the filters. Under **Filters**, tick **Buildable with my inventory** and the results shrink to MOCs your current parts can actually complete. Each result also shows how many pieces you are short.

If a model you want to build is only missing a handful of parts, open its **Parts List** and compare it against your inventory in one click. The site generates a missing-parts list you can take straight to an online seller — far cheaper than buying a whole set for a few dozen pieces.

## Parts lists and alternate builds

Every MOC ships with a complete parts table and the designer's official color scheme. You can export the whole thing to CSV in one click, which makes it easy to check brick counts and color choices before you commit.

**Alternate Builds** are the other big draw. Plenty of sets have exactly one official model, yet on Rebrickable you will find dozens of variations built from the same box of parts. Search a set number and switch to the **Alternate Builds** tab to see them all — one purchase, several finished models.

## Practical tips for builders outside the US and Europe

First, the interface supports multiple languages, and browser translation extensions work well on top of it. Second, check a MOC's parts rating and the designer's profile page before downloading; designers with high ratings and frequent updates tend to be the reliable ones. Third, verify your parts gap before you pay — buying loose pieces against a missing-parts list beats buying a full set you half need. Fourth, the community comments are worth reading: builders often share substitute-part solutions for pieces that are expensive or hard to source in their region. Get these habits in place and Rebrickable stops being a browse-only site and starts working for you.

## The short version

Rebrickable is three tools in one: a set database, an instruction marketplace and a parts inventory manager. Log your collection once and the whole workflow — finding instructions, checking parts, building alternates — runs from that single inventory.
