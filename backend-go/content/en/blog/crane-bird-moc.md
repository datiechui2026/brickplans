# Crane Bird MOC Review: Elegant Red-Crowned Crane in 760 Bricks

The short version: this red-crowned crane MOC captures the bird's poise remarkably well. It uses roughly 760 parts, stands about 28 cm tall, and hits around 90% accuracy on the classic red crown, black neck and white plumage. The PDF instructions cost 22 yuan on brickplan.cn, the loose parts run about 150 yuan, and my timed build came in at roughly 3.5 hours.

## Appearance and Accuracy

The color blocking is what sells it. A single red curved slope sits on top of the head as the crown, the neck is built from black bricks into a graceful S-curve, and the body is mostly white smooth tiles. The black-white-red contrast stays crisp and uncluttered, which is where most of that ~90% accuracy figure comes from.

The wings are layered from white wedge pieces: folded, they sit flush against the body; opened up, the layering reads clearly instead of turning into a flat white slab. Nothing here feels over-decorated — the restraint is the point.

## Structural Design

The long, thin legs are the real engineering problem, and the designer solved it two ways. First, transparent support stands assist the legs so the model isn't relying on friction alone. Second, each leg connects to the torso through a ball joint, and the center of gravity has clearly been tuned on purpose. The finished model stands firmly — this is not a display piece that tips over when someone walks past the shelf.

The neck uses hinge pieces as an internal frame, so you can fine-tune the head angle and pose the bird with its neck stretched up, as if calling out. Small adjustment, big difference in how alive the model looks.

## The Build

Working from the PDF instructions on brickplan.cn, the build took me about 3.5 hours, split into 8 stages. The neck and legs are concentrated in stages 5 and 6, so the back half of the build is where the attention is required. Each step involves only 10-20 parts, and the instruction logic is clear — no guessing at part orientation.

The one genuine difficulty is controlling the curvature of the neck. Keep the side view in front of you and check it as you go; if a hinge is off by a stud, the S-curve stops reading as elegant and starts looking kinked. Fixing it later means partially disassembling the neck, so it pays to be careful the first time.

## Value for Money

Instructions are 22 yuan, loose parts about 150 yuan. More than 70% of the parts are white, which keeps sourcing simple and the cost predictable — white is the cheapest and most abundant color in any parts order. Compared with a retail set of similar size, the total lands roughly 35% cheaper.

Add the Chinese aesthetic angle and it becomes a strong gift candidate: good for older relatives, good on a tea table, good anywhere a piece needs to look calm rather than loud. Bird builders and fans of Chinese-style display models are the obvious audience.

Taken as a whole, this crane MOC folds Chinese aesthetics into brick form without overstating it — disciplined color, expressive posture, and it works equally well kept or given away. It belongs on any shortlist of Chinese-style display builds.
