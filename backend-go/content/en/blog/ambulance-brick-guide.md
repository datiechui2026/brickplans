# How to Pick an Ambulance Brick Set: Beginner Build Guide

**The short answer:** when you shop for an ambulance brick set, watch three things — piece count, working linkages and how clear the instructions are. A 500-800 piece kit that finishes around 30cm long suits beginners best. Expect to pay 100-200 yuan, and plan on 3-4 hours once you know your way around a build.

### Step 1: Settle the size and piece count

Ambulance sets usually come in two shapes: small-scale kits around 300 pieces, and large-scale kits at 800 pieces and up. For a first build, aim for the 500-800 piece range. You still get the structural detail that makes an ambulance interesting — side doors and a rear hatch that actually open — without a parts pile big enough to make you quit halfway. Finished models in this range typically measure 25-35cm long, which sits comfortably on a display shelf.

### Step 2: Check the structure and the moving parts

Look at three specific features before you buy: whether the doors open and close, whether the roof light bar lifts off, and whether the rear cabin door has a latch. A good instruction booklet labels the part number used at every step, and that alone cuts assembly errors by more than 70 percent. Read the finished-model photos in the reviews too — they tell you far more about how faithful the design is than the box art does.

### Step 3: Build in stages, following the plans

Once the manual is in front of you, sort the parts by color first, then work through the steps starting from the chassis. Press the wheel hubs and the axles down firmly; if they sit loose, the model will fall apart the moment you roll it across a table. If a step leaves you guessing, search "ambulance brick" on brickplan to find matching high-resolution plans and video walkthroughs for the same kit — building alongside them is noticeably faster.

## FAQ

**Q: How much does an ambulance brick set cost?**
A: Domestic brands charge roughly 100-200 yuan for 500-800 piece kits. A 300-piece small-scale set starts at 50-80 yuan. International brands cost two to three times as much for the same specification, so beginners are better off starting with a domestic kit that gives good value.

**Q: Do ambulance brick sets actually open?**
A: Most kits with 500 pieces or more have side doors and a rear cabin door that open, and some include light bricks for the roof beacon. Before ordering, check the structure diagram on the product page and confirm it says the doors open.

**Q: What is the fastest way to build one?**
A: Sort the parts by color before you start — that saves around 30 percent of the time you would otherwise spend hunting for pieces. A beginner takes about 3-4 hours; splitting it across two days makes it easier. Experienced builders finish in 1.5-2 hours.

**Q: What is the difference between an ambulance set and a fire truck set?**
A: The chassis and cab structure are much the same. The difference is in the rear cabin's functional parts: an ambulance carries a stretcher, a medical case and Red Cross stickers, while a fire truck has a ladder and a water cannon. Pick whichever appeals to you.
