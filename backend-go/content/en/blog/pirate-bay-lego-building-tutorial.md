# Pirate Bay LEGO Build Tutorial: Island, Cave, Ship, Lighthouse

Pirate Bay is one of those scenes almost every brick builder ends up attempting: steep rock walls, a dark treasure cave, a moored sailing ship, and a lighthouse keeping watch over the water. Put together, they form a pirate story you can actually touch. This tutorial breaks the build into modules and works from the foundation up to the final details, and it works whether you are building with LEGO parts or compatible bricks.

## 1. Splitting the Modules and Planning the Baseplate

Start by dividing the whole scene into four independent modules: **the island rock wall, the treasure cave, the sailing ship, and the lighthouse**. Building this way lets you work zone by zone, makes the model easier to move, and leaves room to expand later. A 48x32 stud baseplate is the recommended main base: put the rock wall and cave in the lower left, save the right side as a harbor for the ship, and stand the lighthouse in the upper left corner. Before you lay any real bricks, outline each module with plain bricks on the baseplate to lock in the footprint proportions, so nothing ends up without space at the end.

## 2. Building the Island Terrain

The island terrain is the skeleton of the scene, and the goal is **rock walls with uneven, stepped texture**. Build the main rock mass 2 to 3 layers high from dark gray bricks at the bottom, then mix light gray, sand green and brown into the upper layers to break up the monotony. At the corners, use 1x1 slope bricks and wedge bricks as transitions so the outline reads more naturally. If you want visible wave erosion, insert a few protruding bricks at intervals along the foot of the rock wall to mimic cracks in the stone. **Keep the inside of the rock wall solid** — it stays stable and it carries the weight of the platforms above.

## 3. Treasure Cave and Interior Details

The treasure cave usually sits at the base of the rock wall, facing the sea. Build the opening first with **arch bricks (a 1x6 arch and a 1x8 arch, for example)**, then stack irregular bricks around the opening to get that natural rock cave feel. Inside, lay gold plates to suggest piles of treasure and add a chest, coins and skull parts — the story lands immediately. To make the cave feel deeper, stack one more layer of bricks above the opening as an overhang and use dark parts inside to create a shadow transition. This is one of the highest-impact steps in a Pirate Bay build.

## 4. Sailing Ship and Dock

Build the ship as a small, detachable two-masted vessel so you can take it off and play with it. Shape the hull by stacking black and dark brown plates, taper the bow with slope bricks, close both sides of the hull flat with SNOT techniques, then hang the white sails. **Connect the mast base with hinge pieces** so the mast can lean slightly and give the ship the sense of coming into port. For the dock, lay a plank walkway from brown 1x6 and 1x8 plates, run it 4 to 6 studs out over the water, support the far end with two rows of brick pillars, and leave a gap in the deck so the bow can pull alongside.

## 5. Lighthouse and Finishing Touches

Put the lighthouse at the highest point of the island — it makes sense physically and it becomes the visual anchor of the whole scene. Step the white brick tower inward layer by layer, add a red or black lamp top on the platform, and set a transparent light element in place as the lamp itself. **Blend the lighthouse base into the rock wall**, filling the gaps between tower foot and stone with dark gray bricks. Finish the water in one pass: transparent light blue plates for the sea surface, a layer of white wave pieces for foam, then a few palm trees along the shore and some minifigures (a captain, a sailor, a guard) to bring the whole bay to life.

Once those modules are done, you have a Pirate Bay with clear layers and a real sense of story standing in front of you. From terrain to cave, from ship to lighthouse, every stage leaves room for your own ideas.
