# Brick Giraffe MOC Blueprint: Parts List and Build Tips

Animal MOCs are one of the most popular corners of the brick hobby, and the giraffe — long necked, oddly patterned, elegant in stance — is often the first large animal build a fan attempts. The hard part is exactly the part you'd expect: making a neck only two or three studs thick stand up on its own while still curving naturally. Below is what I've learned across several versions of my own brick giraffe MOC blueprint, covering both the design thinking and the assembly technique.

## Decide the Scale Before You Pick a Blueprint

The first decision when choosing a brick giraffe MOC blueprint is scale. Three tiers are common. **Miniature** (roughly 15 cm tall overall) makes a good desk display, uses few parts and is beginner friendly. **Standard** (20–30 cm) balances detail against structural strength and is what most blueprints target. **Large** (40 cm and up) gives the most dramatic pose but demands a serious parts inventory — the legs alone often call for dozens of hinge pieces. Beginners should start at standard scale: you get a real sense of achievement without drowning in part sourcing.

## The Neck Structure Is the Whole Build

The giraffe's neck is roughly half its body length, which is why it's the most demanding design problem in any brick giraffe MOC blueprint. Three approaches are common:

1. **Stacked plates with lateral connectors** — stable, but the neck is rigid and can only stand straight up.
2. **Segmented ball joints** — each section connects through a ball-and-socket, allowing an S-curve and a lively pose. This is what most strong blueprints use.
3. **Geared neck** — a gear train gives adjustable pitch, ideal for a "bending down to drink" scene.

Judging a blueprint is easier than it looks: count how many sections the neck is split into and check which joint parts it uses. That alone usually tells you how much the designer knows.

## Pattern and Color Technique

The giraffe's signature blotches decide whether the finished model reads as a giraffe or as a brown horse. The mainstream approach in brick giraffe MOC blueprints is to lay dark brown small elements — commonly 1x1 and 1x2 plates or bricks — as irregular patches over light tan or beige base bricks, then scatter a few dark red or black accents to add depth. Don't chase symmetry: real giraffe patches are unique to each animal, and asymmetry actually looks more convincing.

For the eyes, use a black 1x1 round plate with a 1x1 transparent piece as the highlight. The ossicones (horns) and ears come from cone elements plus light tan plates — a handful of parts is enough to bring the head to life.

## Build Order and Structural Reinforcement

For builds like this, work in the order **legs → torso → neck → head → pattern**. Start with the four legs, noting that the front legs are longer and straighter than the rear ones — that's the defining feature of a giraffe's stance. Inside the torso, leave room for cross axles or pins running through as reinforcement; otherwise the neck will make the model fold at the waist. When assembling the neck, work bottom to top and test the load after each section — if something feels loose, add another connection point. Apply the pattern last, so you aren't repeatedly disassembling and stressing parts.

## Where to Find Blueprints

Sources for brick giraffe MOC blueprints are plentiful today: official building instructions, player forum threads, blueprint download sites, and plenty of authors who publish the blueprint bundled with a complete parts list. My advice is to favor complete versions that include both a parts list and step-by-step instructions — it saves you the work of sourcing everything yourself. The BrickPlans community (brickplan.cn) has a good number of animal blueprints plus real photos of finished builds from members, so color schemes and structures are directly referenceable. Once the giraffe is done, pair it with a zebra and a lion for a savanna scene — the payoff multiplies.
