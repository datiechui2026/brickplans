# 10 Brick Beginner Mistakes to Avoid (and How to Fix Them)

Short version first: nine out of ten beginner mistakes come down to three habits — buying too big, buying too cheap, and ignoring the instructions. The worst of the ten is buying a 5,000-piece set and sub-RMB 50 no-name loose parts in your first month. The first will make you quit; the second will arrive with missing pieces. One rule keeps you out of roughly 80% of these traps: make your first set 300–800 pieces at RMB 100–300.

## Mistake 1: Starting with a 5,000+ piece set

**The fix:** keep your first set under 800 pieces. A 5,000-piece build usually takes more than 30 hours, and most beginners abandon it halfway — money wasted and the hobby ruined before it starts. Build up experience on smaller sets first.

## Mistake 2: Chasing bargains under RMB 50

**The fix:** cheap off-brand parts often have tolerances beyond 0.1 mm, so connections are loose, the missing-part rate is high, and there is no after-sales service. Choose a set from a real brand that promises replacement parts. Paying a few dozen yuan more changes the whole experience. Before ordering, check brand reputation and replacement policy on the BrickPlan site (https://brickplan.cn).

## Mistake 3: Ignoring the instruction manual

**The fix:** sort parts by bag number first, then follow the steps exactly. Skipping steps or mixing bags almost always means rework later — and disassembling does more damage to parts than building does. Tear a joint apart twice and it goes loose.

## Mistake 4: Skipping part sorting

**The fix:** spend 15 minutes before you start sorting parts by color and shape. On an 800-piece set, hunting for parts without sorting takes longer than the build itself and the experience drops off a cliff. Sorting at least doubles your efficiency.

## Mistake 5: Building in poor lighting

**The fix:** dark parts are easy to misread in dim light, and color mix-ups mean a high rework rate. Use a desk lamp, or pick a light-colored theme for your first set to reduce color confusion. It makes the whole build more relaxed.

## Mistake 6: Pressing too hard and stressing parts

**The fix:** when a connection feels tight, use a brick separator instead of forcing it. Joints that go white at the stress point become brittle and snap on the second disassembly — a particular risk with Chinese sets that run on the tight side. A separator costs a couple of yuan and is worth keeping on the table.

## Mistake 7: Not checking piece count and finished dimensions

**The fix:** read the finished dimensions listed on the product page. Many photos make a model look big when the finished build fits in your palm; conversely, a 1,000-piece model can be 50 cm long with nowhere to go. Measure your display space before you buy.

## Mistake 8: Overlooking the number of sub-bags

**The fix:** choose sets with 8 or more sub-bags. More bags means you open them step by step instead of dumping the whole box at once. Sets with few sub-bags mix everything together, and beginners waste enormous time hunting for parts.

## Mistake 9: Finding missing parts only after the build

**The fix:** check the parts list in the manual against the box right after opening, and contact customer service the moment something is missing — most brands ship free replacements within 7 days. Discovering the gap after you finish means a long wait and a bad mood. Count first.

## Mistake 10: Blindly chasing new releases and limited editions

**The fix:** limited sets typically carry a 20%–50% premium, and beginners do not need them. Build 2–3 regular sets first, work out your own preferences and display needs, then think about collecting — otherwise you pay a high price for something that sits on a shelf gathering dust.

## FAQ

**Q: What are the most common beginner mistakes in brick building?**
A: Buying too big and buying too cheap. A 5,000-piece set as your first build often ends in abandonment, and sub-RMB 50 off-brand parts tend to be loose or missing pieces. A first set of under 800 pieces at RMB 100–300 is the safest bet.

**Q: What should I do if I find missing parts halfway through a build?**
A: Check the parts list in the manual to confirm the part really is missing, then contact the brand's customer service to request a replacement. Most brands ship free replacements within 7 days. Counting the parts when you open the box prevents the problem at the source.

**Q: How do I avoid buying counterfeit bricks?**
A: Stick to official flagship stores and authorized brand markings, and be suspicious of sets priced well below market rate. Counterfeit parts have poor tolerances, printed details fade, and you may end up with missing pieces and no support — the money you save is not worth the hassle.

**Q: How many pieces is right for a beginner?**
A: 300–800 pieces is the sweet spot, roughly 3–8 hours of building. That range gives you the full building process without the risk of quitting halfway, and it fits comfortably into a weekend.
