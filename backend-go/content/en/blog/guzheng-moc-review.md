# Guzheng MOC Review: 1,180-Part Chinese Instrument Build

Verdict first: this guzheng MOC is one of the most distinctive Chinese-style instruction sets on brickplan.cn. It runs to roughly 1,180 parts and measures 45 cm long once assembled, reproducing the instrument's movable bridges, strings and nut structure. The red-brown body paired with gold strings makes it a genuine focal point in a modern-Chinese interior. It suits builders aged 15 and up.

## 1. Structural Accuracy

The designer builds the soundboard from long tiles, then edges both sides with dark red-brown bricks to define the frame. The 21 strings are drawn straight using cord elements, and beneath them sits a row of movable white bridges. The nut and front nut are picked out in lighter-colored parts so the two ends read differently at a glance. Overall accuracy lands at roughly 85%. The dragon pattern on the tail end is done with printed parts, and the detailing holds up close.

## 2. Parts and Color Scheme

Red-brown accounts for about 60% of the roughly 1,180 parts, backed by a black instrument-case base and gold strings — a restrained, weighty palette. The wood grain of the soundboard is laid with light and dark bricks alternating across the surface, producing a subtle gradient in the texture rather than a flat block of one color. Every bridge is placed as a separate small part, so you can nudge them left or right the way you would on a real guzheng. That adjustability adds a lot of hands-on play value after the build is finished.

## 3. Building Experience

The instructions are split into 6 stages, and our test build took about 5 hours. The flat soundboard stage involves a lot of repeated brickwork — roughly 300 parts are straightforward tiling — so it drags slightly, though many builders find that kind of repetition relaxing. Stringing and positioning the bridges are the highlight saved for last: 21 strings and 21 bridges go into place one at a time, and the moment the final piece seats has a real sense of ceremony to it.

## 4. Value and Who It Suits

Sourcing the individual parts costs about 200-280 CNY, which is mid-range for instrument MOCs. It is a good fit for Chinese-style enthusiasts, guzheng students and teachers — as music-room decor or as part of a Chinese-themed display it works well — and it also makes a thoughtful gift for older relatives who appreciate Chinese aesthetics.

## FAQ

**Q: How does this guzheng MOC differ from a real guzheng?**

A: A real guzheng is 163 cm long and playable. This MOC is 45 cm long and is purely a display model — the strings cannot be plucked to produce notes. Its strengths are the movable bridges and the accuracy of the silhouette, which make it useful for display and for teaching demonstrations.

**Q: Can the bridges on the guzheng MOC be moved?**

A: Yes. Each bridge is an independent small part that slides left and right like the real thing, so you can show different string positions. This is one of the most enjoyable design touches in the whole model.

**Q: Is the guzheng MOC a good gift for an older relative?**
