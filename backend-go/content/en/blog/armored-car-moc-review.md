# Brick Armored Car MOC Review: 1:18 Six-Wheel Build

## Product Overview

Military armored vehicles, with their hard edges and heavy protection, have long been a favorite subject in the brick MOC community. This **brick armored car MOC blueprint** comes from BrickPlans community designer Armored Division Studio, an original work that recreates a classic wheeled infantry fighting vehicle at 1:18 scale. The blueprint package includes a complete parts list, step-by-step building instructions and multi-angle reference photos. It downloads free on [BrickPlans](https://brickplan.cn), runs 218 building steps, and comes as a clean, easy-to-read PDF.

The design's strength is how it merges the ballistic silhouette of a real armored vehicle with brick structural logic, keeping the hard military character while making the build actually workable. Instructions are labeled in multiple languages, and the parts list is sorted by part number so you can gather everything before you start.

## Build Difficulty

**Difficulty rating**: ★★★☆☆ (medium)

Overall difficulty is moderate, a good challenge for builders with roughly 1 to 3 months of MOC experience. The main sticking points are the sloped armor along the hull sides and the structural design of the roof weapon station. Expect **4 to 6 hours** of building time; if you tweak the color scheme as you go, that can stretch to 8 hours.

The step breakdown is sensible, with each step adding only 5 to 8 new parts, so you never feel buried in loose pieces. New builders should read the [BrickPlans beginner's building guide](/en/blog/moc-beginner-guide) first and get comfortable with the basics before taking this on.

## Parts Review

The **brick armored car MOC blueprint** calls for roughly **1,650 parts**, with the practical range sitting between 1,400 and 1,900 pcs. That puts it in the medium-size MOC category. Recommended brands:

- **Gobricks**: firm, tight clutch that holds up in the load-bearing armored hull frame
- **Double Eagle**: precise technic parts, smooth-running gears and drivetrains for the wheel and suspension assemblies
- **SEMBO**: wide color range, plenty of options when matching military green and sand liveries

Key parts list:

| Part type | Quantity | Recommended brand | Use |
|---------|------|---------|------|
| Technic beam (15L) | ~30 | Gobricks / Double Eagle | Chassis frame |
| Shock absorber springs | 8 | Double Eagle | Independent suspension |
| Off-road tires + hubs | 6 | SEMBO | Full-time six-wheel drive |
| Transparent parts | ~12 | Gobricks | Lights and vision ports |
| Wedge bricks / slopes | ~60 | Gobricks | Sloped armor shaping |

On parts quality: use Gobricks technic elements for the main load-bearing areas of the hull, since their tolerance control is excellent and the finished model stays rigid instead of loosening up. Cosmetic and exterior parts can be sourced from whichever brand matches your preferred color scheme.

## Build Experience

The build starts with the chassis, the skeleton everything else hangs on. First comes the six-wheel independent suspension: every wheel gets its own shock absorber spring, so the body sits and moves convincingly when you roll it across a table. Once the chassis is done, you move on to the main hull.

The hull goes together very smoothly. The designer leans heavily on SNOT (studs not on top) techniques to reproduce the vehicle's sloped ballistic armor. Side armor panels combine wedge bricks and slopes so the model picks up the sharp, faceted look real armored cars have. The nose is modular: the front bumper, crash bars and light cluster all detach independently, which makes later modification easy.

The discussion section for this blueprint on [BrickPlans](https://brickplan.cn) is lively, and builders share useful tweaks, such as adding a rubber band to give the hatch a damped closing feel, or fitting magnets underneath the hull so the model locks onto a display base. That community knowledge makes the build smoother and more fun.

The roof weapon station is the highlight of the whole vehicle, with a rotating machine gun turret and an elevating barrel. The structure is relatively complex, but the instructions are clearly labeled and no step is hard to follow.

## Finished Model

The finished model measures about **38cm × 16cm × 14cm** and weighs roughly **0.9kg** — a scale that shows plenty of detail without eating up your display space.

Working features:

- Six-wheel independent suspension, each wheel moving on its own as you roll it
- Roof weapon station rotates 360 degrees
- Machine gun barrel elevates up and down
- Front and rear doors open and close
- Front winch turns

On accuracy, the sloped armor, armored vision ports, external stowage boxes and roof antenna are all present. The military green body with sand and black accents gives a restrained, long-lasting look. If you want a personal touch, you can adjust the palette on top of the original blueprint — desert camouflage or urban grey, for example.
