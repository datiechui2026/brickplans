# Phone Booth MOC Review: Is This British Street Scene Worth It?

Short version first: this phone booth MOC recreates the classic British red telephone box from roughly 800 parts, builds at a comfortable middle difficulty, and costs about 120 yuan. As a small accent piece for a street diorama, it is well worth picking up.

## 1. Exterior Accuracy

The iconic British red is the first thing you notice, and the details hold up close inspection: the domed roof, the glazed window grilles, and the door frame are all handled properly. The curved top is shaped with sideways building rather than a single moulded piece, which gives it a much better silhouette than most small-scene kits. A printed 2x4 nameplate brick sits above the door, so there is no sticker to worry about. Dropped into a street layout, it is instantly recognisable.

Finished, the model stands about 12 cm tall and 8 cm wide, which keeps it in proportion with mainstream street-scene buildings — tall enough to read as a phone box, small enough to sit on a pavement plate without crowding the neighbours.

## 2. The Build

Expect around 40 minutes from opening the box to finished model. The parts come in 4 numbered step bags and the sequence flows logically, with no backtracking to hunt for pieces you should have placed earlier.

The dome is the only genuinely technical section: it uses a small amount of SNOT (studs-not-on-top) building to turn the bricks upside down and build the curve outward. That is a little more demanding than the rest of the model, but the instructions are clear enough that a first-time builder can follow them without guessing. Just pay attention to the direction the hinge parts face when you install them — there are no other tricky structures and nothing that is easy to get wrong.

The door opens and closes, and there is a miniature telephone inside. That is a nice touch of play value for a display model; the only niggle is that the door hinge is on the tight side, so opening it takes a bit more force than it should.

## 3. Part Quality

The kit uses high-quality domestic aftermarket bricks. Clutch power is firm, the sprue marks are clean, and there is no colour mismatch across the red, black and grey elements — which matters a lot on a model this monochrome, since a single off-tone red panel would be obvious.

Across the 800 parts, special elements account for roughly 15%, so this is not a kit padded out with basic bricks. Nothing was missing from the box. The transparent window elements transmit light well, which is what makes the glazed sides read properly instead of looking cloudy. Overall the texture and finish come close to original brand parts.

## 4. Value for Money

Instructions plus the parts pack come to about 120 yuan. Comparable street-scene sets of the same footprint usually run 200 to 300 yuan, so the value here is strong. If you already have a parts library, you can buy just the instructions for about 20 yuan and source the pieces from your own stock.

Judged on the finished model, I would give it 8.5 out of 10.
