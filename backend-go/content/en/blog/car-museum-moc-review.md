# Car Museum MOC Review: 9,800 Pieces at ~CNY 1,600

The short answer first: this brick car museum MOC is worth the money. Roughly 9,800 pieces turn into an 80×54 cm two-storey exhibition building, the build runs about 34 hours, the parts budget lands near CNY 1,600, and difficulty sits at medium-to-hard. It is aimed at modular builders with some experience behind them, and the finished model's presence and detail justify the outlay.

## 1. How well it captures the subject

The character of a car museum lives or dies on three things: the display hall, the rotating turntable and the glass curtain wall. All three are here.

The display hall is the heart of the building. It holds six or more small-scale display cars at 8 studs wide, and the floor is laid with grey baseplates printed or tiled with lane markings, recreating the traffic flow of a real motor show. In the centre of the hall sits the rotating turntable, built as a hand-cranked turntable mechanism. Rotation is smooth, and dropping a car onto it immediately gives you that motor-show turntable effect. It is the single biggest highlight of the build.

The front facade is one full run of glass curtain wall, assembled from a large quantity of trans-clear pieces into full-height floor-to-ceiling windows. Combined with the warm-toned light bricks inside, the effect at night is especially striking. I would score the subject fidelity at 9 out of 10.

## 2. Build experience

The ~9,800 pieces arrive split across 14 numbered bags, and the sequence runs foundation, hall, rotating turntable, glass curtain wall, then the upper floor. The logic is clear and easy to follow.

The slowest stages are aligning the trans-clear pieces of the glass curtain wall and calibrating the axle centre of the turntable. The first means repeatedly checking pieces against their slots; the second, if it is off, produces a turntable that binds as it turns. Follow the instructions and fix each layer in order before moving up. Overall the clutch is tight and nothing was missing. Of the 34 hours, roughly 6 go into the finishing decoration, which makes this a classic front-half-flows, back-half-fiddly build.

## 3. Value for money and verdict

At about CNY 1,600, the cost works out to roughly CNY 0.16 per piece, which is mid-to-upper value among large MOCs of this class. Compared with the official LEGO City car-show sets, this one adds the rotating turntable and a complete glass curtain wall, so the scene is far more complete, and the 80×54 cm footprint is big enough to be the centrepiece of a display.

The drawbacks are real but predictable: it needs a lot of display space, and the turntable is manual, so you cannot simply drop in a motor for automatic rotation. Taken as a whole, car fans and modular street builders can buy this one without much agonising.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this car museum MOC worth buying?**

A: Yes. Around 9,800 pieces, an 80×54 cm footprint, plus a complete scene built around the rotating turntable and glass curtain wall, is a strong display result for a budget of roughly CNY 1,600. Among comparable MOCs it competes well.

**Q: Does the turntable actually turn?**

A: It does. It is a hand-cranked turntable mechanism and it rotates very smoothly, so putting a display car on it recreates the motor-show turntable effect. It does not accept a motor, though, so full automatic rotation means modifying it yourself.

**Q: How is this different from a modular street series set?**

A: Modular street sets are about the building facade and domestic interiors. This one is about display function: the core is the exhibition hall, the rotating turntable and the glass curtain wall. It reads more like a purpose-built exhibition venue, and it pairs well with garage and repair-shop MOCs.

**Q: How much money and time will it take?**

A: Budget about CNY 1,600 in parts and about 34 hours of building. Splitting it over three or four evenings is sensible; the numbered bags are clear, so pausing mid-build does not create chaos.

**Q: Can a beginner build this?**

A: Not recommended as a first project. Aligning the glass curtain wall and calibrating the turntable both demand patience. Build a MOC of 3,000 pieces or more before taking this one on.
