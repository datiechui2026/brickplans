# LEGO Mario Blueprint Guide: Build Pipes, Mushrooms & Levels

Any brick builder who grew up with the red-capped plumber in overalls eventually wants to rebuild that pixel world stud by stud. The green pipes, the red-and-white mushrooms, the gold question blocks and the block-by-block platforms you jump across are the most appealing design elements in any Mario blueprint. This guide starts from the parts themselves and walks you through building your own small Mario brick world.

## What Makes Mario Blueprints Good for Brick Builders

The Mario theme has stayed popular in the brick hobby because its visual language suits bricks unusually well. The characters are simple and bold, the scenes are built from grid-like platforms, and that fits the logic of how bricks clutch together. A good Mario blueprint turns the classic pixel elements into a clear parts list and step-by-step instructions, so even a beginner can follow along and reproduce a familiar screen. It also leaves room to play: swap the color scheme, rearrange the layout, add small mechanisms, and the build still feels fresh.

## Pipes: Getting Those Rounded Curves Right

The green pipe is the signature of the Mario world, and it is the most technically demanding part of the blueprint. The usual approach is to build the pipe body from semicircular or curved pieces so the cross-section reads as round, then wrap narrow plates around it to close the shape and create the two stacked sections with visible depth. The pipe mouth can taper slightly inward to suggest a dark opening you could drop into. If you want a taller pipe, stack more sections and leave a seam between each one; if you want the pipe lying horizontally, watch how the opening faces and how the body engages the baseplate. When building pipes, sort the dark green and light green parts into separate piles first, so a color mix-up does not flatten the layering of the finished model.

## Mushrooms and Question Blocks: Recreating the Iconic Elements

The red-and-white mushroom and the gold question block are the accents that make a Mario level read instantly. The mushroom is usually built with a red dome piece for the cap, then white round pieces and short cylinders for the stem, with small light-colored round plates dotted across the cap for the spots. The question block is a balance between a crisp square silhouette and surface texture: build the body from tiles, set a printed tile with the question mark into the front face, then wrap a dark border around all four corners, and it immediately has that "one bump from above and something good happens" feel. Place the question block next to a pipe or at the edge of a platform and the scene starts telling a story.

## Level Scenes: Layering Platforms and Obstacles

The core of a Mario level scene is staggered platforms and a sense of rhythm. Start by using a large baseplate to fix the direction the level runs, then offset platforms at different heights like steps, connecting them with slope pieces to mimic the jump-and-land path of the game. Brick staircases, small passages that flip open, and corners with a hidden star are all popular details. For extra impact, run a border of green parts along the edge of the scene as grass, and use transparent blue pieces for sky or water accents, so the whole model feels like a game in progress.

## Build Tips and Choosing a Blueprint

Before you start, sort the parts by color from the blueprint's parts list, keeping baseplates, plates, bricks and special pieces in separate groups; the build goes much more smoothly that way. Mario-themed models use a lot of curved pieces and printed elements, so check the orientation and color of the parts at every step. If this is your first attempt, pick a blueprint with finely broken-down steps and clear rendered images; once you are comfortable, move on to larger scenes with mechanisms or lighting.
