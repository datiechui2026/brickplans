# Where to Find Digital LEGO Instructions and Save Them

Most builders know the pain: the paper booklet that came with your set picks up deeper creases with every fold, and one spilled drink can leave you squinting at a blurred step with no way to continue. That is why more and more builders now work from **digital building instructions** instead, flipping pages on a phone, tablet or laptop whenever they like.

## Why Digital Instructions Beat Paper

The biggest complaint about paper booklets is creasing and glare, and the biggest advantage of digital instructions is simple legibility. A high-resolution scan can be zoomed to any level on screen, so even the smallest part detail stays sharp — no more squinting at coated paper under a lamp.

Cost comes second. Whether you are after MOC instructions or a set manual, plenty of free digital files are available online, and the savings go beyond the price of one printed booklet. Digital files also take up no shelf space: store thousands of them on your phone or cloud drive and you can build wherever you are, with no risk of losing a booklet.

One benefit gets overlooked: digital files let you step backward. If you make a mistake and have to disassemble, paper instructions make it hard to return precisely to the right step, while a digital file with step markers makes review much easier — especially helpful for beginners.

## Where to Get High-Resolution Digital Instructions

There are three main sources.

**Official brand channels.** Large makers such as LEGO publish some manuals as PDFs on their own websites, and MOC design platforms like Rebrickable attach the designer's instruction download links. These are the most reliable sources.

**Community sharing.** Brick hobby communities often host high-resolution collections scanned and organized by helpful builders. Quality is usually decent, but check whether the version you downloaded is complete.

**Dedicated instruction sites.** Communities focused on brick instructions, such as [brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn), gather scattered files into one organized, categorized library that you can search by set number or theme. That is far more efficient than scrolling through forum threads, and it is one of the first places to check for digital instructions.

## How to Download and Store Digital Instructions

Before downloading, check the resolution. Scans of 300 dpi or higher are generally recommended, with image dimensions of at least 2000 pixels on the long edge — anything lower turns to mush when zoomed.

For storage, create folders using a "theme – set number – file name" pattern, for example "Star Wars-75054-AT-AT.pdf", so you can locate a file quickly later.

Backups matter as much as naming. Keep one copy on a local drive and one in a cloud drive so a failed device or a cloud cleanup cannot wipe out your library. On mobile, import PDFs into a reader app and switch on night mode so late-night building sessions do not strain your eyes.

## Common File Formats: PDF or Image?

Digital instructions usually come as either PDF or image files. PDF is the most universal: smooth page turning, bookmark support and zoom, which makes it right for complete booklets. Image formats (JPG/PNG) suit individual step photos and are easy to preview and share quickly. The rule of thumb is simple: choose PDF for a full booklet of dozens of pages, and high-resolution images for a specific sub-step you need to check again and again.
