# Planetarium MOC Blueprint Review: Dome, Orrery and Observatory

A planetarium is the most romantic building you can put on a street layout: creamy white dome, spinning orrery, a telescope on the roof terrace — one structure that holds an entire night sky. For this **planetarium MOC blueprint review**, we built the model exactly as drawn, with the openable dome, and scored it section by section so you know whether it deserves a place in your district.

## 1. Blueprint and Parts: Pure White with Starry Blue

The blueprint is drawn at 1:48 street-scene scale. The full building runs about 2,000 pieces, split across 7 numbered bags. The dome and vault are the hard part, and the instructions specifically call out the order in which the curved panels interlock. The palette is dominated by cream white, dark blue and transparent starry blue, which keeps the colour scheme clean and uncluttered. Printed parts are generous: the dome star pattern, the museum nameplate and the star chart are all printed rather than stickered. Missing-piece rate was low. **Blueprint and parts: 9/10.**

## 2. The Openable Dome: The Best Structure in the Build

### Dome frame

The dome measures roughly 12 studs across. Curved elements are layered inward to form the hemisphere, capped with a round top piece. The real highlight is that it opens: hinge elements plus slide-rail elements along the centreline create two dome halves that slide apart. Open, you can see the orrery inside; closed, the seams meet cleanly. Opening structures like this are rare in street-scene builds.

### Star chart inside the dome

The inner surface of the dome uses printed star-pattern plates to form constellations, backed by a built-in light strip. When the dome is closed, points of light show through — the effect is genuinely striking. **Dome structure: 9.5/10.**

## 3. The Orrery and the Main Hall

### Orrery

A single orrery sits at the centre of the hall: column elements plus ball elements form the body, with four projection arms extending outward (thin rod elements tipped with transparent lamp heads). The base rotates a full 360 degrees on gear and turntable elements. Pose a minifigure with a hand on the switch and the main hall instantly reads as a lecture in progress.

### Exhibit hall displays

The main hall is laid out with planet models, Mercury through Saturn, lined up on stands using ball elements in different sizes. Saturn gets a ring made from a flat ring element. Each planet has its own nameplate, which gives the room real scientific credibility and makes it easy to tell astronomy stories to kids.

## 4. Observatory Terrace and Courtyard Details

### Observatory terrace

The second floor opens onto an outdoor observatory terrace. The railing is built from thin rod elements, and a large telescope sits at the centre — tripod elements, a long tube element and an eyepiece — with an adjustable pitch angle. A folding chair completes the scene, so the minifigure's late-night stargazing route works from start to finish.

### Courtyard atmosphere

In front of the building, the courtyard uses a dark blue baseplate scattered with star dot elements (small yellow round studs arranged into constellations), plus a low tree and a streetlamp. Add a light strip at night and the courtyard answers the glowing dome across the plaza — the romantic mood is complete.

It is a particularly good fit for builders who want to add a romantic focal point to a street layout, for players who love movable parts and opening mechanisms, and for collectors drawn to astronomy themes. The one weakness is that the dome hinge elements are fine and delicate, so opening and closing needs careful handling — but that does not outweigh the strengths.
