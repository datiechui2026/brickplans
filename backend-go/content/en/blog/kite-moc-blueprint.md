# Swallow Kite MOC: 400-Piece Beginner Brick Build Guide

The short version: this swallow-shaped kite MOC blueprint is a genuinely good starting point for a first-time builder. It uses a three-layer structure, a swallow frame, a skinned surface and tail streamers, and the finished model measures roughly 28 cm across the wings. You need about 400 parts, a budget of around 120 yuan, and roughly two hours of building time. The cross-locked frame joints are the main load-bearing point of the whole kite, so treat them as the heart of the build.

## Step 1: Sort the parts and set up the base

Work through the official brickplan blueprint bag by bag. Dark gray long bricks are for the main beam, light gray short bricks are for the cross ribs, and about 60 plate bricks are set aside specifically for the skinning layer. Sorting by color before you start raises your building speed by roughly 30 percent, which matters more than it sounds when you are 300 parts in.

## Step 2: Build the swallow frame

Start with the central main beam, about 20 cm long, then attach three cross ribs on each side to form the spread-wing outline of a swallow. The critical detail here is the cross joint: where the main beam meets a cross rib, lock the two together with a double connection, studs plugged into studs plus a pin through the side. That combination keeps the wingspan steady at around 28 cm once the wings are spread, with no sagging in the middle.

## Step 3: Skin the surface

Cover the frame with plates, working row by row from the wingtips toward the center, the way you would paste paper over a bamboo frame. Stagger the joints between each row of plates; continuous seams running across the wing will weaken the whole assembly. The skin should cover about 85 percent of the frame area, leaving open space at the tail end for the streamers.

## Step 4: Fit the tail streamers

String chain pieces and 1x1 round bricks into three streamers measuring roughly 8 cm, 10 cm and 8 cm, and hang them from the rear cross rib. They reproduce the tail feathers of a swallow, and they add a sense of motion when the model is on display.

## Step 5: Check and display

Go over every cross joint and confirm it is tight. Mount the kite on a display base tilted at about 15 degrees to suggest flight. Pair it with a white baseplate and a few small blue wave pieces and you have a 28 cm wide "kite in the sky" scene.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this kite MOC blueprint suitable for beginners?**

A: Very much so. It runs about 400 parts at a 120 yuan budget, the cross joints are marked step by step in the blueprint, and it takes around two hours, which makes it a solid choice both as a first brick project and as a parent-and-child build.

**Q: Which parts work best for the skin?**

A: Use 2x4 and 2x6 plates laid in staggered rows. They follow the curved wing surface better than a single large plate and they increase overall rigidity, and at roughly 85 percent coverage the surface looks fullest.

**Q: The cross joints on my frame keep coming loose. What should I do?**

A: Add a 1x2 brick underneath the joint to raise and support it, or use a brick with side pin holes together with a pin to lock it down. Step 12 of the brickplan blueprint shows a dedicated reinforcement example.

**Q: How big is the finished model, and can I hang it up?**

A: The wingspan is about 28 cm and the thickness about 6 cm. It is light enough to hang from clear fishing line, but check that every cross joint has been reinforced first so it does not come apart while suspended.
