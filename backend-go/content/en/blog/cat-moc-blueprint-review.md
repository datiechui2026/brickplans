# Cat MOC Blueprint Review: 5 Brick-Built Cats Compared

Cats may be the hardest animal to capture in brick. The body is simple enough — the whole character lives in the face. Swap one curved slope and the build stops reading as a cat and starts reading as a dog. Cat MOC blueprints have multiplied over the past couple of years, so I picked five to compare side by side: a sitting cat, a stretching cat, a sleeping cat, a walking cat and a head bust. The focus throughout is on facial expression and how each design handles coat colour.

## Design Fundamentals of a Cat MOC

### Feline Body Proportions

A cat's silhouette is elegant and light, and different breeds carry distinctly different proportions in brick:

- **Head**: round or wedge-shaped, depending on breed
- **Ears**: triangular; position and size are the main breed tells
- **Eyes**: large and round — the core of any cat's expression
- **Body**: supple and long, with smooth muscle lines
- **Tail**: long and flexible, the main channel for mood

### Breed Differences

A cat MOC has to commit to a breed before the first brick goes down:

- **British Shorthair / American Shorthair**: round face, round eyes, short nose, sturdy build
- **Siamese**: wedge face, blue eyes, slender body
- **Persian**: flat face, long fur, rounded body
- **Orange tabby**: rounded build, warm colour palette
- **Black cat**: mysterious and elegant, all black

## Cat MOC Building Techniques

### Shaping the Face

The expression makes or breaks a brick cat:

1. **Eyes**: printed parts or stickers for different looks — round (surprised), half-lidded (lazy), narrow (alert)
2. **Nose**: a small pink triangle piece or a 1x1 pink round plate
3. **Mouth**: curved bricks arranged in a "W" for the classic cute cat mouth
4. **Whiskers**: bar pieces or thin rods inserted on both sides of the muzzle

### Coat Colour and Texture

- **Solid colours** (black, white, grey): use several shades within the same family to create depth
- **Two-tone cats** (black and white, orange and white): watch the boundaries and transitions between colour patches
- **Calicos**: mix three colours at random to imitate the patchy pattern
- **Tabbies**: use striped bricks, or arrange bricks to create a stripe effect

### Articulation

- **Neck**: ball joints for head rotation and tilt
- **Limbs**: Technic pins for forward and back swing
- **Tail**: bead pieces or flexible tubing for curling and swishing
- **Ears**: hinge pieces so the ears can rotate and signal mood

## How to Choose a Cat MOC Blueprint

### By Breed

- **British / American Shorthair**: pick a round-faced design
- **Siamese**: pick a wedge face and slender body
- **Orange tabby**: pick a rounded design
- **Black cat**: pick a long, elegant design

### By Purpose

- **Display piece**: a sitting or standing cat at a moderate size
- **Play and posing**: a blueprint with more moving joints
- **Gift**: a mini keychain or small cat design
- **Family build**: a low part count and low difficulty

## Final Thoughts

Half the fun of cat MOC blueprints is matching the build to your own cat. Keep a British Shorthair? Build the sitting one. Siamese person? Go for the stretch. Orange tabby devotee? Straight to the sleeping pose. Across all five, the thing that really separates them is the face, not the body.
