# How to Build a Whale Shark MOC: Parts, Steps and Display

The short answer first: a whale shark MOC is a realistic home build. At roughly 1,200 pieces the finished model measures about 45 cm long, costs around 300 RMB in loose parts, and comes in at about half the price of a comparable retail set. A first-time builder working through the steps below can finish in 6 to 8 hours. The one decision that shapes everything is scale — settle the proportions before you build the skeleton.

**Step 1: Fix the scale and sort the parts list.** A 1:60 scale gives you a 45 cm shark. Build the color scheme around dark gray and light gray, with a cream-white belly, for a total of about 1,200 pieces. Basic bricks make up roughly 70 percent of that count. Only three kinds of specialized parts are needed: side-mounted connectors, curved panels, and trans-blue elements. Keep the budget under 300 RMB and sort the parts against your list on a marketplace such as BrickPlan before you start.

**Step 2: Build the internal spine.** Use technic elements for the spine, adding a connection point every 5 bricks so the body can flex as a whole. Once the frame is together, test the curve before you cover anything. A whale shark's body is streamlined — head and tail lifted, midsection nearly flat — and a wrong curve will show through no matter how good the shell looks.

**Step 3: Layer the outer shell.** Start from the cream-white belly, then wrap outward on both sides with dark gray curved panels. Offset every layer, the way you would lay bricks in a wall, so the shell gains strength as it grows. Leave slots in the light gray back sections for the dorsal fin, and build the tail fin separately before locking it in place with side-mounted connectors.

**Step 4: Add the details and the eyes.** Each eye is two black 1x1 round plates plus a trans element for the highlight. Space dark gray plates in alternating rows to make the gill slits — 5 in total. The mouth should tilt up slightly; press a gentle curve into it with curved elements. This is the step that sets the shark's expression, so take your time here.

**Step 5: Display and photograph it.** Mount the model on a trans-blue baseplate to suggest ocean water and scatter a few small white elements as spray. For photos, light it from 45 degrees above and to the side — that angle picks out the ridge along the back.

## FAQ

**Q: Can a beginner build a whale shark MOC?**
A: Yes. At 1,200 pieces it sits in the medium range. Work through it as three passes — spine, shell, details — and a beginner can finish in 6 to 8 hours. Check your parts list against the instructions before you start rather than hunting for pieces mid-build.

**Q: How much do the parts for a whale shark MOC cost?**
A: About 300 RMB if you source loose bricks yourself. Using domestic compatible parts can bring it down to around 200 RMB, which is roughly half the price of a retail set in the same size — strong value for what you get.

**Q: Is a 45 cm whale shark worth displaying at home?**
A: Yes. At 45 cm it has the visual impact of a scaled-down real animal, and it reads as a focal point on a bookshelf or in an entryway. You can also pair it with a small school of fish MOCs to build out a full ocean scene.

**Q: How do you build one without instructions?**
A: Find a side-on photo of a whale shark, sketch a simplified outline at 1:60 scale, and let that drawing decide the spine curve. The BrickPlan community has plenty of player-shared step photos you can reference along the way.

**Q: What separates a whale shark MOC from a normal brick set?**
A: A set locks you into the instructions. A MOC is open — the same 1,200 pieces can be reworked into a different pose, or mixed with other ocean animals, so there is far more to keep playing with.
