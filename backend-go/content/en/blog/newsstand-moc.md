# Brick Newsstand MOC: Retro Street Corner Build Guide

Here is the short version first: this newsstand MOC uses roughly 700 pieces, takes about 2.5 hours to assemble, and stands around 18 cm tall on a 16x16 footprint. It has three-sided shelving, flip-out newspaper boards and a retro canopy, it photographs beautifully when dropped into a street-scene block, and parting it out costs about 160 to 210 yuan. The instructions are available on brickplan.cn.

## Step 1: Base and Pillars

The base accounts for about 120 pieces. Lay grey-white bricks across a 16x16 baseplate, then stand four dark brown pillars at the corners to carry the body of the kiosk, leaving counter space in the middle where the vendor stands. Fix the pillars to each other with cross beams so the structure stays solid and does not wobble. Add two small steps at the front of the base so a minifigure can climb up and buy a paper — a small detail that reads clearly. Edge the sides of the steps with dark grey bricks to set up a light-and-dark contrast against the base and give the whole thing more depth.

## Step 2: Three-Sided Shelving and Newspaper Boards

The shelving accounts for about 320 pieces. Build shelves out of plate bricks on three sides of the kiosk, then hang flip-out newspaper boards on the outside, using printed tiles or stickers to suggest magazine covers. Under the front counter, fit two drawers for loose change and lighters. Stack newspapers from small plates, bind them with rubber bands, and hang a newspaper clamp on the side of the kiosk — that is where the retro newsstand look really lands. You can also hang a small blackboard on the side of the counter and chalk the day's headline on it for extra street-scene storytelling.

## Step 3: Canopy and Signage

The canopy accounts for about 260 pieces. Build a wave-shaped awning from curved slope bricks — dark green with beige stripes is the most retro combination. Add a newsstand sign on top, either lettered with alphabet tiles or made from a printed piece, and fit a light brick for night-time illumination. Place a mailbox and a few leaf pieces in the corner and the street-scene storytelling is immediately complete.

## Colour Plan and Scene Pairing

- Main colours: about 200 dark brown pieces, about 180 beige, about 120 dark green and about 100 white, with the rest made up of accent pieces.
- Budget: 700 pieces runs about 160 to 210 yuan. There are a lot of wood-tone elements, so buying loose parts by weight works out cheaper.
- Pairing: line it up with a post office, a vendor cart and an old-style street lamp to form an 1980s-style old street — the photo setup is excellent.
- Snow version: swap the canopy for white and light blue stripes, then lay a thin layer of snow bricks over the roof and the winter mood is complete.

## FAQ

**Q: How many pieces does the brick newsstand MOC need?**

A: The basic version is about 700 pieces — roughly 120 for the base, 320 for the shelving and 260 for the canopy. Loose parts cost about 160 to 210 yuan, and you can split it into small bags and build in stages. If you already have wood-tone bricks at home you can save a bit more.

**Q: Can the newsstand MOC go into a street-scene block?**

A: Yes. It occupies 16x16 studs and is compatible with standard street-scene modules, so you can drop it straight into the corner of a 32x32 baseplate, combine it side by side with shops to form a complete street, or swap its position without affecting the overall layout.

**Q: How long does it take to build a brick newsstand?**

A: About 2.5 hours for a beginner and around 1.5 hours for an experienced builder. The shelving and newspaper boards are the most repetitive sections, and the curved canopy needs careful alignment. Overall difficulty is medium, and it suits builders aged 12 and up.

**Q: Is the brick newsstand worth building?**

A: Yes. 700 pieces are enough to recreate a complete newsstand with shelving, signage and a canopy. The retro colour scheme looks great on a shelf, it works as a backdrop for minifigure scenes, and its value for money is high among street-scene MOCs.
