# Saber-Tooth Tiger MOC Review: 520 Parts, 10cm of Menace

## The Verdict Up Front

**Verdict first: this saber-tooth tiger MOC looks stunning as a finished model, and scores 8.8/10 overall.** About 520 parts build into a 10cm-tall predator, with two exposed canine fangs around 4cm long; the muscle definition and standing pose give it serious presence. The instructions come from brickplan.cn, the build takes roughly 1.5 hours, and it is a piece animal-brick fans should not skip.

## 1. Shape and Design

The first thing anyone notices is the pair of signature fangs. The designer stacked white curved elements and slope bricks to build them, each about 4cm long — close to a third of the head's height — angled slightly forward, so the whole tiger reads as a low, wary growl. The colour scheme is sand-tan paired with off-white, with the lighter tone carrying through the belly and the inside of the limbs. That pulls the visual weight downward, making the stance look planted and powerful. From the front, broad shoulders narrowing into the waist form an inverted triangle, and the sense of a predator bearing down on you arrives immediately.

## 2. Detail Work

The muscle sculpting is the strongest part of the build. The hind legs use three stacked layers of curved bricks to raise the thigh mass, while the shoulder blades on the forelimbs get angled cuts from wedge elements. Combined with articulating limb joints, the muscle contours stay continuous even in a pouncing pose. On the side of the head, a few small dark bricks are set in with SNOT technique to suggest the eye socket and the muzzle crease, and the base of each fang is deliberately raised by one plate to recreate how the upper canine passes through the lip. The tail is a chain of 8 ball joints, bendable into an S curve that adds motion to an otherwise static display piece.

## 3. Build Experience

The parts are bagged sensibly, split into four numbered stages — head, torso, limbs — at roughly 130 parts per bag. The instructions are brickplan's full-colour PDF, with clear steps and no skipped stages. The part that tests your patience is the neck-to-torso connection, where you have to seat 6 pins at once, but the structure is solid: once it is together, shaking the model will not loosen it. Total build time is about 1.5 hours, which lands at medium-easy difficulty — a newcomer can finish inside 2 hours.

## 4. Value for Money

At roughly 520 parts, with a 10cm finished height and dense detail, this is very much a display piece. Against official animal sets of the same size, this MOC wins on both shape expressiveness and posability, while the parts cost stays manageable. The downside is the absence of printed parts: the eyes and the stripe markings need stickers or parts from your own bins, so add a point or two if plain, unpatterned colour is not your thing. Overall, it is one of the most "museum-grade" animal MOCs you can get inside the hundred-yuan price bracket.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the saber-tooth tiger MOC worth buying?**

A: Yes. At an 8.8 score, 520 parts get you a highly detailed 10cm display model whose shape has far more tension than official sets at the same price. The instructions are clear, the build runs about 1.5 hours, and the value is strong.

**Q: Are the fangs sturdy, or do they break easily?**

A: The fangs are built from curved elements and slope bricks stacked together and secured with pins, so the structure is stable and normal handling will not knock them off. They are purely decorative, though — repeated disassembly is not recommended, and forcing them to bend still risks breakage.

**Q: Where should the finished model go?**

A: A middle shelf on a bookcase, the corner of a desk, or either end of a TV cabinet all work — at 10cm tall it takes up very little space. Keep it out of direct sunlight; the white fangs will yellow over long exposure.

**Q: Can you source all the parts, and are any special?**

A: The vast majority are common basic bricks and curved elements. The brickplan instructions end with a complete parts list, so you can order everything by part number from online loose-part shops. Fewer than 10 part types are unusual.

**Q: If I make a mistake, is it easy to take apart?**

A: The limbs and tail connect with pins, so they come apart easily. The inside of the torso uses a locking structure with 6 pins, which needs a brick separator to open up. Follow the instruction order and you will avoid rework.
