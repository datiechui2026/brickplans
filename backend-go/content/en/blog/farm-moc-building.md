# How to Build a Dream Farm MOC: Step-by-Step Guide

## Introduction

Farm scenes are one of the most popular subjects in brick building. A red barn, a field of wheat and a few animals never fail to feel calm and welcoming. In this tutorial we build a complete farm MOC from scratch, starting with a flat baseplate and finishing with props and decoration.

## 1. Overall Planning

Start with a **32x32 stud baseplate**. Suggested layout: the red barn in the lower left (20x16), the animal pen in the lower right (12x10), the wheat field in the upper left, the farmhouse cottage in the upper right, and a courtyard path through the middle. Sketch a top-down plan first and the build will go much more smoothly.

## 2. Base and Terrain

Use 1x2 and 2x4 bricks to raise parts of the ground by 1-2 layers so the plot has gentle slopes. Lay a winding path with brown or gray tiles. In the wheat field area, alternate yellow and orange round plates (stud shooters / dots) with green tiles to suggest ears of wheat against bare soil. Add a few 1x1 green leaf elements along the edge of the baseplate to break up the straight border and make the farm look more natural.

## 3. Building the Barn

The barn is the visual anchor of the scene. Work in red and white with a dark gray gabled roof.

- **Walls**: alternate 2x4 red bricks, 8-10 layers high
- **Main door**: leave a 4-stud-wide opening and build the door itself from dark brown plates
- **Windows**: two 1x2 windows on each side, using transparent blue glass panes
- **Roof**: pair two 2x8 slope bricks against each other, then top the ridge with a round brick or a weather vane
- **Interior**: optional hay bales and tools (a 1x1 brick with handle plus a cross axle makes a pitchfork)

## 4. The Animal Pen

Enclose a rectangle with 1x4 dark brown plates, 2-3 bricks high. Use 2x2 bricks with holes at the corners as posts. Build the gate from 1x6 plates and hinge elements so it actually opens and closes. For livestock, a chicken is simply a 1x1 white round brick plus an orange triangle (slope); a cow can be simplified into a combination of 1x2 bricks. A feeding trough is a 1x4 brown brick topped with a 1x2 transparent blue brick for water.

## 5. Fields and Vegetable Garden

**Wheat field**: push 1x1 green plant stems into bricks with holes, add a yellow round plate on top, and space the clusters every 3-4 studs. **Vegetable garden**: frame small beds with dark brown bricks, use red and green round plates for tomatoes and cabbages, and add a small scarecrow. **Trees**: stack dark brown bricks for trunks and combine green plates with round plates for the canopy — two or three trees are enough to break up the scene.

## 6. Farmhouse and Tool Shed

The cottage uses white walls with a dark brown roof, measures 12x10 and stands 6-8 bricks tall, with a small chimney and steps at the front door. The tool shed is an open three-wall structure holding a broom (1x1 round brick plus a long bar), a bucket and similar small props.

## 7. Assembly and Detailing

Once every module is fixed in place, do the final pass: a wooden fence of dark brown bricks around the perimeter; 1x1 flower elements along the path; a wheelbarrow built from wheels and plates; an old-fashioned hand pump assembled from round bricks; and a signpost pointing toward the barn.

## 8. Common Questions

**Q: Not enough parts?** Build the core buildings first and add details over time — mixing colors also gives the model a nice patchwork character. **Q: Want it bigger?** Expand to a 48x48 baseplate, or join several baseplates together and add a pond or a windmill. **Q: How do I make it stand out?** Add a string of warm LED lights, or build a seasonal version — orange and brown for autumn, or a white snow-covered roof for winter.

## Closing Thoughts

The appeal of a farm MOC is that it works as a self-contained miniature world and as a piece of a larger town layout. From the first brick to a farm full of life, the building process is the real reward. When yours is finished, share it with the BrickPlans community.

**Happy Building!** 🧱🚜

*Note on part naming: this tutorial uses standard LEGO terminology (brick, plate, tile, slope, round plate) so the parts are easy to look up in any inventory or BrickLink-style catalog.*

*Working at minifigure scale? Keep a minifigure nearby as a reference — doors, gates and fence heights read correctly when the proportions match a figure.*

*Budget tip: the barn eats the most red bricks. Build it first, then decide how much detail the fields and pen can afford with what is left in your drawers.*

*Storage tip: keep the four modules (barn, pen, field, cottage) as separate removable sections so the farm can be transported and rearranged without rebuilding the terrain.*

*Color tip: red, white and dark gray for the barn, dark brown for wood and fences, sand green or bright green for planting. Sticking to that limited palette is what makes a small farm MOC look cohesive.*

*Scale tip: the 20x16 barn footprint fits comfortably on a 32x32 baseplate with room for a 12x10 pen and a 12x10 cottage, so measure your modules on the plan before you start stacking.*

*Detail tip: a single minifigure-scale pitchfork, bucket or chicken draws the eye more than a dozen extra studs, so spend your remaining parts on a handful of props rather than filling every gap.*
