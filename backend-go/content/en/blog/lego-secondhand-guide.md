# Secondhand LEGO Guide: Inspection, Pricing and Haggling

The short version: buying and selling used LEGO works, and a set in good condition typically costs 50-70% of retail. What you cannot skip is inspection. Confirm the parts are complete, verify the minifigures are genuine, then cross-check the whole box against its weight and piece count. Do those three things in that order and you can trade with confidence.

## Is secondhand LEGO worth buying?

Price is the main draw. Retired sets can hold their value at retail or even sell above it, while sets still in production commonly change hands at 50-70% of retail. That gap is what makes the secondhand market useful rather than a compromise.

Three groups get the most out of it: beginners on a tight budget, veteran builders hunting down retired sets they missed, and MOC makers who only want raw material. For that last group, buying loose parts by weight is the better deal — figures in the source work out at roughly 0.3-0.5 yuan per gram, which undercuts buying new sets purely to harvest their pieces.

## The five inspection checks

1. **Box and instructions.** Confirm the outer box and the instruction manual are both present and in reasonable shape before you agree on a price.
2. **Spot-check parts against the parts list.** Pull a sample of key pieces and pay particular attention to minifigures and any special or rare elements, since those are the hardest and most expensive to replace.
3. **Authenticate the minifigures.** Genuine minifigure torsos and legs carry a brand stamp molded into the plastic. A missing or blurry stamp is your first warning sign.
4. **Look for yellowing and scratches.** Yellowed white parts do the most damage to a set's value, so check light-coloured elements in daylight rather than under warm indoor light.
5. **Weigh the full box.** Compare the total against the official weight for that set. A deviation above 5% is a warning that parts are missing.

## Transaction safety

- Stick to platforms that hold the money until delivery is confirmed, and refuse private transfers — escrow is what gives you a route to a refund.
- For large sets, ask the seller to bag the contents in stages and wrap everything in bubble wrap, so loose parts do not rattle around in transit.
- Record an unboxing video within 48 hours of delivery. After-sales claims only go smoothly when your evidence for missing parts is complete.

## Haggling and selling

A workable anchor when negotiating is 60% of retail minus shipping. Do not push hard on rare retired sets, though — those sellers know exactly what they hold, and a lowball offer usually just ends the conversation.

Before you list a set, photograph the parts spread out and take close-ups of every flaw. State missing parts honestly; an accurate description usually sells faster than an optimistically vague one, because buyers can bid with confidence instead of asking questions. If you need a quick sale, list at 60-70% of retail. If you can wait, list at 80% and be patient. You can also post photos in the brickplan.cn community first and ask what people would realistically pay before you commit to a number.

## FAQ

**Is secondhand LEGO reliable, or will I end up with fakes?**
Go through an escrow platform and inspect the set properly and it is reliable. Check the minifigure stamps, the part colour and the total box weight; when all three line up, you can generally buy with confidence.

**How do I tell a genuine LEGO minifigure from a fake?**
Genuine torsos and the inside of the legs carry a brand stamp, the printing edges are crisp, and the head and arms connect to the torso with the right amount of friction. Counterfeits have a blurred or missing stamp and feel loose at the joints.

**What if parts are missing from a secondhand set?**
First check the parts list to confirm the parts really are absent rather than misplaced in another bag, then contact the seller with your unboxing video and photos to arrange a replacement or refund. Under an escrow transaction this is usually resolvable.

**What is a fair price for secondhand LEGO?**
For sets still in production, 50-70% of retail is reasonable. Popular retired sets can justify retail or a small premium, but once you are above 80% of retail you are better off waiting for new stock.

**How should I price a set I am selling?**
Check what the same set has actually sold for in the last 30 days. In good condition, list at 70-80% of retail; with missing parts or yellowing, drop proportionally. Clear photos of the parts and close-ups of the flaws will move it faster.
