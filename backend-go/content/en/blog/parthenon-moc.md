# Parthenon MOC Tutorial: Colonnade, Roof and Stepped Base

Bottom line first: the Parthenon is the textbook multi-column building, and you can build a complete 28 cm-tall temple from roughly 2,400 pieces on a 48x24 baseplate. The colonnade wraps all the way around, the proportions are long and low, and the full build runs about 8 hours — a good fit for builders with intermediate experience who like classical architecture.

## Step 1: The Stepped Base

The temple sits on a three-level platform. Start with the 48x24 baseplate as your footprint, then inset each step as it rises, with every level about 1.5 cm tall. Lay the steps in off-white plates and keep a step margin on all four sides — front, back, left and right — so you get the classic three-tier base.

Before you go further, mark the column positions at the four corners of the base. Spacing has to be even; one column every 8 studs is the working rule. That gives you 16 outer columns plus 6 inner columns.

## Step 2: Building the Colonnade

This is the slowest part of the Parthenon and the one that sells the look. Stack 2x2 round bricks for the shafts, each column about 18 cm tall, and add fluted elements at intervals along the shaft to suggest the vertical grooves. Set the four corner columns first to lock in your footprint, then work along the long sides and the short sides column by column. Cap each shaft with a plate for the capital, then run beam bricks across the top to tie all 16 outer columns into one continuous colonnade. Watch your left-right symmetry as you go — small errors here are what make the whole thing lean.

## Step 3: The Inner Chamber and Roof

Inside the colonnade, build the inner chamber walls from 1x4 bricks and leave a seat for the tie beams at the top of the wall. For the roof, use slope bricks to form the gabled pitch, with one gable group at the front and one at the back; fill the gable fields with stickers or small bricks for the relief pattern. The roof pitch is about 30 degrees, and hinge pieces at the transition make the sloping surfaces run smoother. Once the roof is on, the model stands about 28 cm tall — and with the colonnade wrapping the stepped base, the presence arrives all at once.

## Step 4: Details and Display

Finish by adding white detail bricks to the capitals and the beams, and put three small minifigure-style figures on the gable (or use stickers instead). The all off-white palette keeps it clean and grand. Figure on about 2,400 pieces and a budget of roughly 200 yuan. The finished model measures 46 cm long, 22 cm wide and 28 cm tall, so a dark display case is the right call: white against a dark backdrop gives you clear layering, and it holds attention in a study or living room.

## FAQ

**Q: Where do I download the Parthenon MOC instructions?**
A: Search "Parthenon" on brickplan.cn. The PDF is about 35 yuan and includes the parts list, column-position drawings and step-by-step instructions, so you can source the bricks yourself.

**Q: How hard is the Parthenon MOC?**
A: Medium-to-high. There are a lot of columns and the symmetry tolerance is tight, and stacking shafts gets repetitive. Budget around 8 hours for 2,400 pieces, and it helps to have a street-scene or architecture MOC under your belt first.

**Q: How many parts does the Parthenon MOC need?**
A: About 2,400. Roughly 90 of those are 2x2 round bricks, and off-white plates make up most of the rest; curved bricks are few. The color count is low, so sourcing is easy and rare parts are only a small share.

**Q: Why a 48x24 baseplate?**
A: The Parthenon is a long, low building. A 48x24 baseplate holds the three-level platform and the surrounding colonnade at roughly 1:100 scale — enough presence without eating up your display space.

**Q: How is this different from a Roman Pantheon MOC?**
A: The Parthenon is rectangular and multi-column, with a colonnade running around the exterior; the Pantheon is a circular structure under a dome. The Parthenon means more columns and more repetitive but very regular work, while the Pantheon lives or dies on the dome curvature. Both suit classical architecture fans.
