# Dumpling MOC Review: Steamer and Dipping Saucer Details

The short version first: this dumpling MOC runs about 150 yuan for all three pieces — steamer, dumplings and dipping saucer — and the shape fidelity is high across the board. The pleat curvature on the dumplings and the exposed filling cross-section are the standout details, each dumpling uses roughly 40 parts, the clutch is firm with no obvious burrs, and the set works beautifully as an accent next to a street-scene restaurant. Worth picking up.

**Shape accuracy.** The steamer is built from brown round ring bricks stacked into two tiers, with printed bamboo-texture pieces that land at roughly 90% fidelity to the real thing; the handle on the lid has a natural, believable curve. The dumpling pleats are the biggest highlight of the build: 1x1 curved bricks are offset one by one around the edge to produce 12 pleats. The filling cross-section uses light yellow bricks to suggest minced meat, with small dark green round dots standing in for chopped scallion — the visual effect is remarkably convincing. The dipping saucer is small, but the inward-curving line of the rim is handled properly.

**Part quality.** The whole set is about 150 yuan for roughly 500 parts. Clutch is moderate to slightly tight, and disassembling a mistake does not damage the bricks. Edges and corners are clean, with no obvious burrs or flash, and the printing on the patterned pieces is aligned precisely. After 20 repeated builds the clutch is still stable. Build quality sits in the upper-middle range for MOCs of this type.

**How it works in a scene.** A single dumpling is about 40 parts and measures roughly 6cm square, so it scales correctly against restaurant tables and steamer stalls in a street scene. Stack the steamer by the restaurant door and set the saucer at the corner of a table, add warm lighting, and the lived-in street-food atmosphere arrives instantly. In testing it connected seamlessly with the restaurant and noodle shop builds in the brickplan street scene series.
