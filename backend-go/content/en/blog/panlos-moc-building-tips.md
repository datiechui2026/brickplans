# Panlos Building Tips: Parts, Teardown and Scene Modding

Among Chinese brick brands, Panlos has held a steady reputation, with solid kits across mech, vehicle and architecture lines. The problem is that most builders close the box the moment the last piece clicks, which throws away the real strength of these sets: how well they take modifications. These Panlos building tips are not about following the manual. They cover how to identify the parts, how to take a model apart, and how to change it, so the set you own builds tighter and can be reworked on your own terms.

## Know the Parts First: How Panlos Differs from LEGO

The first step in any Panlos building tip is getting a feel for the parts system. Panlos is broadly compatible with the standard stud-and-tube interface used by mainstream brick brands, so plates, bricks and axles all interchange — a Panlos plate snaps onto a LEGO brick and the other way round. Two things stand out, though. First, the share of special pieces is high: weapons, mech joints, trans-clear effect parts, many of them original molds you will not find an identical match for elsewhere. Second, some parts clutch tight, especially fresh out of the bag, so you cannot muscle them apart. Before you start, flip through the parts list and pull the special pieces out separately. Knowing what you have on hand makes the rest of the build go far more smoothly.

## Sorting and Bag-by-Bag Building: The Key to Easier Mods

If you plan to modify later, the assembly order matters now. Panlos manuals usually break the build into sub-assemblies. Do not skip that — sort same-color, same-type parts into sorting boxes before you start. Then build with one habit: leave joints and posable sections unseated. Click them into place but do not press them home, and tighten everything in one pass once the model is complete. That makes tuning easier now and saves effort when you take it apart again. On any part that has been pressed in hard, use a brick separator and pry gently in the direction of the stress. Do not force it — forcing leaves stress marks on the plastic and can snap a pin.

## Taking It Apart Properly: Disassembly Without Damage

Modding only works if you can get the model apart without wrecking it. The most useful rule: disassemble in the reverse of the build order. Start with the outermost decorative pieces, work inward layer by layer, and leave the core structure for last. On plates that are pressed tight, insert the separator into the seam and alternate between both ends — a distinct click tells you it has released. Trans-clear parts and thin, long pieces are the most fragile, so pad the stress point with a cloth while prying. And if something does break, don't panic: Panlos's common parts are easy to source as replacements on Taobao.

## Scene Modding: Three Ways from Set to MOC

Once you know the parts and can take things apart cleanly, you have room to modify. Three approaches, from shallow to deep. The first is a reskin: change only the color scheme and stickers, repainting a mech in your own palette — zero risk, and a good first attempt. The second is adding structure: mount weapons on a vehicle or add balcony lighting to a building, sourcing what you need from the spare parts left in the kit. The third is rebuilding: break down the parts from two or three sets and design something new. That is MOC modding in the real sense, and it is worth waiting until the first two feel comfortable.

## Connection Logic When You Modify

Whether a mod holds up comes down to the connection design. A structure you add needs at least two points of clutch; a part held at a single point will drop off under the slightest load, and that is the most common reason a mod looks fine on the bench and falls apart in your hand. To put a weapon on a mech's back, use a socket piece with a cross axle as the mount and match it with the corresponding axle on the weapon — that stays solid and still rotates. To add a second floor to a building, use a studded top plate and standard bricks in a staggered bond, which is far stronger than simply stacking. Test the structure with spare parts from your own collection first, and only install it in the model once the clutch is confirmed stable.

## Quick Troubleshooting

A short reference to finish. Parts too tight: pry with a brick separator, alternating ends. Special part missing: look up the number on the parts list and order a replacement. Model wobbles after modding: check for single-point clutch. Joint too loose: add a 0.5-thick shim piece on the axle. Turn these into habits and you have largely mastered the Panlos system.
