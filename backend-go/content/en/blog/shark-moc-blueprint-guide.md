# Brick Shark MOC Blueprint: Build a Streamlined Ocean Predator

Sharks are the ocean's most intimidating predators, and getting that menace onto your desk starts with a reliable MOC blueprint. Compared with following a retail set's instructions, MOC (My Own Creation) building is about free design and personal expression, which suits a fast, dynamic animal like a shark. Here is how to break the job down: body shape, fins, teeth, and finally the scene.

## Streamlined Body: The Soul Curve of a Shark MOC

The first thing anyone notices about a shark is that aggressive, streamlined body. When you design a brick shark MOC, the body's curve is the hardest and most important part. Straight, square bricks quickly produce a "blocky fish," so lean on wedge bricks, slope bricks, and curved plates to transition between the head, belly, and tail.

Seen from above, a shark's body is a clear spindle: wide at the head, narrow at the tail, thickest in the middle. A good approach is to lay down a longitudinal spine with basic plates first, then build outward layer by layer so both sides gradually swell into a natural sense of muscle. Lower the head slightly and push the snout forward, stacking 1x2 slopes to sharpen the tapered snout profile. Do not forget a slight downward bend in the body — it makes the model look like it is cruising when it sits on a desk, rather than reading as a stiff, straight rod.

## Dorsal, Pectoral and Tail Fins: Three Layers of Shape

Fins are among the shark's most recognizable features and the easiest place for a brick shark MOC to shine. The dorsal fin is usually built from triangular plates or fan pieces, standing at the center of the back and tilted slightly backward, narrowing as it rises. Pectoral fins stretch out from both sides of the body; hinge pieces let them swing a little, which adds play value.

The tail matters most: a shark's caudal fin is a classic fork, with the upper lobe clearly longer than the lower one. Build the tail fin as two separate units, fix them to either side of the tail stock with hinges, extend the upper lobe by a stud or two and splay it outward, and you get that signature asymmetrical tail. When all three fin groups point in directions that answer each other, the model instantly gains the three-dimensional layering of a shark cutting through waves.

## Teeth and Mouth Details: Where the Menace Comes From

A streamlined body alone is not enough — the shark's rows of overlapping, razor teeth are what really sell the "predator" read. In brick shark MOC designs, the mouth is usually handled one of two ways. The first is to line the lower jaw's edge with small white parts or tooth elements, simple and direct. The second is to attach two staggered rows of teeth upside down using 2x1 plates, creating an uneven, jagged effect.

To add tension to the expression, tilt the upper jaw up slightly and pull the lower jaw back, then add a single pointed brick at each corner of the mouth like a canine tooth. For the eyes, choose small dark round parts and set them slightly recessed so the stare looks cold. A few gray bricks around the mouth suggest skin texture and keep the head from looking flat. Remember: the number and orientation of the teeth decide how fierce the shark looks, so try several arrangements — the results often surprise you.

## Underwater Scene: Make the Shark Actually Swim

Once the model is finished, the scene is the last step that brings it to life. Build a small base of seabed rock, use transparent blue parts to suggest waves and bubbles, and add a few small fish, coral, or seaweed as supporting cast for an instant ocean ecosystem. Transparent support columns can suspend the shark in midair, as if it were gliding overhead — great for photos or as a desk piece.

If you want something bigger and more detailed, try a poseable version — gears and linkages can make the dorsal fin or tail sway gently — or build it in detachable sections so you can open it up and inspect the internal structure anytime. Whichever route you take, studying other builders' work and iterating on your own are the most reliable ways to improve your MOC skills.
