# How to Dust LEGO Bricks Without Damaging Prints

The short answer: the safest and most effective dusting kit for LEGO bricks is a soft brush, a barely damp cotton cloth and an anti-static duster. Clean once a month, 15 to 30 minutes per session. Don't scrub printed parts hard with wet wipes, and never soak a whole built model in water — stickers and printed patterns will lift or fade.

## Why bricks collect dust so easily

Bricks are made of ABS plastic, which holds a static charge, and the gaps between studs, plates and tiles give dust plenty of places to settle where it won't simply fall off. In an open display case, bricks pick up roughly 0.3 mg of dust per square centimetre each week, and over time the colours turn dull.

## Tool checklist before you start

- Soft brush or makeup brush: loosens dust in the gaps
- Anti-static duster: quick routine passes
- Microfibre cloth: dampened for large flat surfaces
- Vacuum on low power with a brush attachment: lifts loose surface dust

## Step 1: dry brush before anything wet

The first rule of dusting bricks is dry first, wet second. Brush off the surface dust with the soft brush, run the duster over everything once, and only then consider a damp cloth. Dry brushing lifts around 70 percent of the dust and stops the rest from turning into mud that gets pushed down into the crevices.

## Step 2: work from the top down

Clean top to bottom and outside in: roofs and towers first, base last. Go the other way and the sections you just cleaned get dirty again from everything falling on them.

## Step 3: reach the gaps with a toothpick

For stubborn dust in windows, railings and stud gaps, wrap the tip of a toothpick in a little damp cotton wool and tease it out gently. Skip metal needles — they scratch parts and leave white marks that you cannot polish out.

## Step 4: water washing is for loose parts only

Once a model has been broken down, the parts can go into water below 40°C with a small amount of mild dish soap, soaked for no more than 10 minutes, then brushed one at a time. Printed parts and stickered parts must never be soaked — dry brush only.

## Step 5: dry completely before rebuilding

Lay the washed parts flat on a dry towel and let them air dry for at least 12 hours. Don't blast them with a hot hairdryer; high heat deforms ABS plastic. Rebuild from the instructions only once everything is bone dry.

## Step 6: put a cover on the display case

After cleaning, add an acrylic dust cover over the model or seal the gaps in the cabinet with weatherstrip. That stretches the next dusting from once a month to once every three months — less work, less worry.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I dust LEGO bricks without damaging the parts?**
A: Stick to the dry-first, top-down order. Rely mainly on a soft brush and an anti-static duster, use a barely damp microfibre cloth on large flat surfaces only, and deal with gaps using a toothpick wrapped in cotton wool. No alcohol, no strong acid or alkaline cleaners.

**Q: Can I just wash bricks in water?**
A: Loose parts can be washed, but the water must stay below 40°C and the soak must not exceed 10 minutes. Printed parts, stickered parts and any part containing electronics have to stay out. A fully built model should not be soaked — water seeps into the internal structure and can loosen joints or warp parts.

**Q: What should I do with dirty stickers and printed parts?**
A: Stickers only get a dry soft brush, swept lightly. Printed parts get a barely damp microfibre cloth pressed on and lifted off quickly — never rubbed back and forth. Printed patterns have a limited abrasion life; after roughly 20 wet wipes they visibly fade.

**Q: How often should I dust my bricks?**
A: For open display, once a month at 15 to 30 minutes per session. With a dust cover fitted, you can stretch it to once a quarter. In a dusty home or with pets, increase the frequency to once every two weeks.

**Q: Can I dust and disinfect at the same time?**
A: Not recommended. 75% alcohol attacks the ABS surface and turns printed patterns white, and bleach-based disinfectant damages parts outright. Plain water is enough for routine dusting; when you need more, a UV sterilising lamp for 30 minutes is the alternative.
