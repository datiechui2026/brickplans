# How to Build a Brick Pen Holder: 80-Piece DIY Desk Organizer

Short answer first: a brick pen holder is one of the most genuinely useful beginner MOCs you can build. It takes about 80 basic bricks and roughly 30 minutes. The version worth building is a square holder on a 16x16 baseplate with walls 8 layers tall, split inside by a divider into two zones — the big bay for pens and a ruler, the small bay for erasers and sticky notes. No glue, and nothing falls through the bottom.

Compared with a phone stand, a pen holder asks more of your structure, because it has to carry the weight of the pens plus the friction of pulling them in and out every day. Build the four walls solidly and seal the floor with a layer of tiles, though, and it holds up fine. When it gets dusty you can take it apart and wash it — that is the real advantage over a plastic desk cup.

## Step 1: Gather the Parts

Parts list: one 16x16 baseplate, thirty-two 2x4 bricks, sixteen 1x4 bricks, twenty 2x2 tiles, and twelve 1x2 bricks — about 80 pieces in total. Pick two or three colors that work together; a white body with a dark blue divider, for example, reads clean on a desk. Everything is standard stud-and-tube brick, and compatible Chinese-made bricks run about 0.1 yuan per piece, so the whole build comes in under 10 yuan.

## Step 2: Build the Base and Walls

Lay the 16x16 baseplate flat and cover the whole surface with 2x2 tiles to seal the floor so small items cannot slip out. Then run the walls around the edge: at each of the four corners, interlock 2x4 bricks horizontally and vertically, rotating the direction 90 degrees every layer as you stack. That cross-tied corner is what makes the walls strong. Build the four walls up to 8 layers — about 8 cm tall — which is enough to swallow a 15 cm ruler.

## Step 3: Add the Inner Divider

Six studs in from the left edge, build a divider wall by stacking 2x4 bricks upright to the same height as the outer walls. That splits the interior into a roughly 10x16 bay and a 6x16 bay. The large bay holds gel pens, pencils and a ruler; the small bay takes erasers, paper clips and sticky notes. Make sure the divider's bricks are staggered and tied into the walls on both sides, otherwise the whole panel can work loose as one piece.

## Step 4: Decorate and Use

On the outside of the walls you can add a trim band of 1x2 bricks, or build a small motif on the front — a little house, a heart. Once it is finished, actually push pens in and pull them out to test it; if nothing wobbles, you are done. When dust builds up on the desk, break it back down into bricks, rinse them in water, let them dry and rebuild.

This is also a great parent-and-child project: an adult plans the dimensions, the child finds the bricks and stacks the walls, and in 30 minutes you have a desk organizer a kid made themselves — the sense of achievement is the point. ## FAQ

**Q: How do you build a brick pen holder, and will things fall out of the bottom?**
A: Cover the base with a full layer of 2x2 tiles first and nothing will fall through. Build the walls to 8 layers (about 8 cm) and cross-tie the bricks at all four corners for strength. The whole thing is about 80 bricks and 30 minutes.

**Q: Do you need glue to build a brick pen holder?**
A: No. As long as the wall bricks are staggered and the seams pressed tight, everyday pen handling will not pull it apart. Glue only costs you the ability to take it apart, wash it and rebuild it differently later.

**Q: How many pens will a brick pen holder hold?**
A: On a 16x16 baseplate with a divider splitting it into two bays, the large bay takes roughly 25-30 pens plus a ruler, and the small bay handles erasers and sticky notes. If you need more capacity, raise the walls to 10 layers.

**Q: What age is this suitable for with kids?**
A: Around 5 and up. An adult plans the size and structure while the child finds bricks, builds the walls and decorates. It takes 30 minutes, it builds fine motor skills, and the finished holder goes straight onto the desk.
