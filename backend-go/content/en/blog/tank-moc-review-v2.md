# Tank MOC Instructions Reviewed: Design, Difficulty and Parts

![Completed brick tank MOC model](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/tank-moc-review-v2/tank-moc-review-v2-1.jpg)



## Why tank MOCs are so demanding

![Brick tank MOC build steps](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/tank-moc-review-v2/tank-moc-review-v2-2.jpg)


Armor has always been one of the hard subjects in the MOC scene. The sharp, angular lines of a modern main battle tank and the historical weight of a WWII classic are both within reach of bricks. Over the past couple of years, domestic parts makers and instruction-sharing communities have grown up together, and **tank MOC instructions** have changed enormously in both quality and price. A full-interior build used to require a deep breath and a serious budget; today the entry tier is genuinely playable. Below is a hands-on look at four areas: design quality, building experience, parts sourcing and display.

## 1. Design quality: balancing accuracy and playability

A good tank instruction set has to find the balance between fidelity and playability. Current tank MOC designs fall into three broad schools:

**High-fidelity static display** focuses on precise exterior detail, from turret slope angles to the exact position of skirt attachment points. These designs lean heavily on SNOT (Studs Not On Top) techniques and deliver enormous display value. A popular Type 99A tank MOC, for example, reproduces the turret bustle stowage bin, the remote weapon station and the smoke grenade launchers at 1:35 scale, and the detail is remarkable.

**Functional and posable** designs put the emphasis on internal mechanics and moving parts: rotating turret, elevating gun barrel, working suspension and track running gear. Some high-end instructions even include a firing mechanism, which makes them extremely playable.

**Standardized scale** designs follow a fixed ratio such as 1:35 or 1:48, which makes them easy to pair in dioramas and compare side by side. These suit experienced MOC builders.

## 2. Difficulty tiers and how to choose

Builders should pick instructions that match their experience. We split difficulty into three tiers:

**Entry (★☆☆)**: for less experienced builders, 500-1000 pieces, built from large bricks and simple stacking, roughly 2-4 hours of building. An eight-wheel armored car or a light scout vehicle is the recommended starting point.

**Intermediate (★★☆)**: for builders with some experience, 1000-2500 pieces, including SNOT techniques, moving joints and sticker detailing, roughly 4-8 hours of building. Most modern main battle tank MOC instructions sit at this level.

**Challenge (★★★)**: for veteran builders, 3000-5000 pieces and up, with complex internal structure and plenty of specialized parts, and more than 10 hours of building. Examples include a 1:28 scale M1A2 SEPv3 Abrams or a 1:24 scale Tiger heavy tank MOC.

On the BrickPlan community ([brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn)), every **brick tank instruction** listing carries a detailed difficulty rating and piece count, so builders can pick to their own level.

## 3. Parts sourcing: the rise of Chinese brands

![Brick tank MOC part detail](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/tank-moc-review-v2/tank-moc-review-v2-3.jpg)


Parts sourcing used to be the single biggest obstacle for MOC builders. Early tank instructions were mostly built around LEGO parts, and many specialized elements were expensive and hard to find. In recent years, the rise of Chinese brick brands has changed that picture completely.

Mainstream brands such as Gobricks, SEMBO, Double Eagle and Qiji have reached a genuinely high standard in both part quality and compatibility. Gobricks elements come close to LEGO in clutch power and surface finish at a third to a fifth of the price. More importantly, Chinese makers are actually stronger on military-specific elements: road wheels in a wide range of sizes, track links, gun components and more, all things the LEGO system lacks.

We recommend tank MOC builders assemble their own "military parts bin": wheel sets and tracks in multiple sizes, slope and wedge bricks for building sloped armor, round tiles and curved bricks for smooth turret transitions, connectors and joints for suspension and turret mechanisms, plus military color elements in dark green, sand and olive drab.

## 4. Building tips and pitfalls

**Chassis reinforcement**: a tank model is large and its weight is concentrated, so the chassis has to be rigid. Build an internal frame from Technic beams and pins, then skin it with decorative bricks. This is currently the most common structural approach.

**Suspension design**: for a display model, a fixed torsion-bar setup is fine. If playability is the goal, go for independent spring shock absorbers, which simulate off-road performance more realistically.

**Color scheme**: reference real vehicle paint. Start from military green, sand and olive brown, then add small amounts of black and dark gray as accents. Too many bright colors will break the military mood.

## 5. Display and diorama pairing

**Building a base**: use gray and dark gray baseplates for a simple groundwork, add brick-built terrain undulation, and work in trees, stone walls or ruins to set the battlefield mood.

**Scene pairing**: display tank MOCs in different scales together, or combine them with support vehicles such as an infantry jeep or a supply truck to form a complete military scene. Many tank MOC designers also create matching vehicles and scenery modules, and you can find complete series on [brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn).

**Lighting and photography**: shoot with warm side light to bring out the model's depth and the texture of the armor. Use dark camo cloth as a backdrop and place miniature sandbags or barricades in the foreground to give the photo a sense of story.

## Wrapping up

Tank MOCs pay off twice: military fans get the accuracy, brick fans get the structure. From entry-level armored cars to full-interior main battle tanks, **brick tank MOC instructions** offer a way in for every skill level. The supply chain is still maturing and the catalog keeps growing, so now is a good moment to start. When your build is done, post it in the community and share your notes. Every tank has its own set of solutions behind it.
