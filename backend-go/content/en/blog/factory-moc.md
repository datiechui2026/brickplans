# Brick Factory MOC Review: Conveyor Belts and Workshop Machinery

The short version: one working conveyor line runs the full length of this factory MOC and ties the whole build together. The mechanical detailing is dense, the difficulty sits firmly in the advanced range, and the model is aimed at builders who want mechanisms they can crank by hand rather than a static display piece.

## Exterior Design

The factory is styled as a grey steel-frame industrial hall. The facade relies on beam pieces to suggest structural steelwork, and the roof carries a run of sawtooth skylights that let light through very effectively. That roof line does a lot of the visual work: it reads as a real production hall before you even look inside.

Inside, the workshop is split into functional zones. The raw material bay is stacked with cargo crates, the machining bay holds a gear linkage assembly, and the packaging bay sits at the exit end of the conveyor, so the layout follows the actual flow of material through the building. Gauges and pipework on the walls are built from technic pieces, which is where most of the mechanical character comes from — the walls are never flat panels, they are always doing something.

## Structural Stability

The hall frame is built from technic beams and pins, and it carries load well: the second-level platform stays rigid even with a person standing on it. That matters here, because a factory scene invites you to reach in and move things around, and a flexing frame would ruin the effect.

The conveyor itself is assembled from track links, and the gears at each end mesh tightly enough that the belt does not skip teeth as it turns. The large gear train is linked through cross axles and runs smoothly through the whole rotation. Overall the structure is solid and takes frequent handling and play without loosening up.

## Playability and Value

Turn the crank on the side and the conveyor carries crates from the raw material bay all the way through to the packaging bay, passing a lift platform that raises them automatically on the way through. The linkage is genuinely satisfying to operate, and the fact that it runs entirely off the crank means there is no motor, no wiring and nothing to charge.

The model totals about 2,600 pieces. Sourcing the parts against the brickplan.cn parts list comes to roughly 900 CNY, and the build runs about 10 hours — a good weekend project to take slowly rather than rush.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the conveyor on this factory MOC actually move?**

A: Yes. Turn the crank on the side and the conveyor drives the cargo crates along through a gear linkage, with a lift platform raising them automatically partway through. The whole mechanism runs without a motor, and the design is cleverly put together.

**Q: Is this factory MOC hard to build?**

A: Yes, the difficulty is on the high side and suits experienced builders. At roughly 2,600 pieces and about 10 hours of work, the gear linkage section is the real test of patience. Follow the instructions in order — frame first, then the conveyor — to avoid having to take things apart again.

**Q: How much does it cost to source all the parts?**

A: Against the parts list on brickplan.cn it comes to around 900 CNY, since the model uses a lot of technic beams and gears. If you already have technic spares in your collection you can reuse them and bring the cost down to roughly 600 CNY.

**Q: How is this different from an official Technic set?**

A: Official Technic sets lean heavily toward vehicles and construction machinery. This factory MOC is a static production-line scene instead, and the focus is on process mechanisms such as the conveyor and lift platform plus the workshop layout — the subject matter is much closer to an industrial process display.

**Q: How do you maintain the conveyor?**

A: Track links and gears pick up dust over time. Every six months or so, take them off and clean them with a soft brush, then put a drop of silicone oil on the cross axle connections. It will turn more smoothly and you will avoid gear wear and skipped teeth.
