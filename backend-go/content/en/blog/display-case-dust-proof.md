# How to Dust-Proof LEGO Display Cases: A Full Storage Guide

A large street-scene model with hundreds or thousands of pieces can take several evenings to build. Without protection, dust settles into the gaps within weeks: the crisp colour scheme turns grey, the part surfaces lose their sheen, and dark bricks in particular start to look chalky and faded. Dust-proofing your display case is a lesson every builder eventually has to learn.

## Why brick builds are so vulnerable to dust

Brick surfaces are covered in studs, recesses and connecting seams, and those structures are natural dust traps. Once dust works into the joints between parts, a normal wipe rarely clears it out, and scrubbing with a cloth can scratch the parts. Worse, dust that sits on the model for a long time makes the plastic feel tacky and tightens the fit between bricks, which complicates later disassembly. Dust-proofing is therefore not just about looks: it affects how long a build lasts and how pleasant it is to handle.

## Choosing the right display case

The first step in any dust-proofing plan is picking the right container. Display cases generally fall into three types: acrylic cases, glass cabinets and wooden cabinets with doors. For dust control, the seal matters more than the material. The smaller the gap between lid and body, the better, so look for products with a silicone gasket. On size, leave roughly five centimetres of clearance above, below and on all four sides. That makes the model easy to lift in and out, and prevents a tight fit from knocking parts loose. If your budget is limited, a plain storage bin works too: turn it upside down over the model and it will block most airborne dust.

## Sealing the case and everyday upkeep

The seams, hinge points and cable cutouts on a case are the entry points dust exploits. Seal them with soft weatherstripping or paper tape. Before a model goes inside, clear the loose dust from its surface with a soft brush or an air blower, so you are not locking dust into the box. Done properly, this routine pays off immediately, and you may get through an entire year without a deep clean.

## How to clean bricks correctly

Even with a sealed case, occasional cleaning is still needed. Start with a soft brush, a makeup brush or an air blower to lift dust off the surface; these tools will not scratch the parts. If dust has already worked into the seams, run a slightly damp cotton swab along the gaps, then draw the moisture away with a dry cloth. Never immerse a finished model in water or spray it with an alcohol-based cleaner, since that can damage printed patterns and the plastic itself. Once cleaning is finished, let everything dry completely before it goes back in the case.

## Placement matters more than you think

A good location makes dust-proofing far easier. Keep cases away from windows, doorways and air-conditioning vents, so that dust stirred up when a window opens does not land straight on the lid. High spots such as the top of a bookshelf or on top of a TV cabinet usually collect less airborne dust and make ideal display positions. If you have pets, or the case sits near a kitchen, add a dust mat under the base to reduce the intrusion of hair and cooking particles. Wiping the outside of the case with a dry cloth regularly keeps it looking clear for the long term.

## Display and storage as part of the hobby

Dust-proofing should not feel like a chore; it is a way of taking care of a build you care about. A properly sealed case, sensible cleaning habits and a well-chosen position will keep a model looking freshly built years later.
