# LEGO MOC Instructions Compared: Rebrickable, BrickLink, BrickPlan

## Introduction

LEGO MOC (My Own Creation) building is the ultimate expression of the hobby — setting the official set instructions aside and building a world of your own design. The instruction file is the treasure map for that journey. Whether you are just getting started or already have a shelf full of originals, a good MOC instruction set saves hours of trial and error and a meaningful amount of money on parts.

This review looks at the main sources of LEGO MOC instructions today across three dimensions: platform resources, instruction quality and user experience.

## 1. Rebrickable: The Encyclopedia of MOC Instructions

**Overall rating: ★★★★★**

Rebrickable is the largest MOC instruction community in the world, with more than 100,000 instructions covering mechs, architecture, vehicles, dioramas and almost every other theme.

**Strengths:**
- **Automatic parts list export**: upload your brick inventory and the system works out which parts you already own and which ones you still need to buy. No other platform matches this core feature.
- **Multiple formats**: instructions come as PDF step-by-step guides, Studio digital model files (.io) and some LDD/LDraw files, so you can work in whichever way suits you.
- **Community rating system**: every instruction has ratings, comments and a gallery of finished builds, which makes it quick to filter for quality.

**Weaknesses:**
- Some of the best instructions are paid, typically $5–$20, which adds up.
- The interface is English-only, which is a barrier for some builders.

**Best for:** builders with reasonable English who want a professional-grade MOC experience.

## 2. BrickLink Studio: One-Stop from Design to Instructions

**Overall rating: ★★★★☆**

Since LEGO acquired BrickLink, Studio has become the de facto standard for MOC design. It is primarily a design tool, but its built-in Gallery and instruction export features deserve a review of their own.

**Strengths:**
- **Studio integration**: browse and download community builds inside the software, generate a parts list with one click and order the parts.
- **Official parts library**: precise 3D models of every LEGO part, which keeps instructions physically buildable.
- **Free**: every feature is free, and Studio itself is a high-quality MOC design tool.

**Weaknesses:**
- Gallery instructions vary widely in both quantity and quality, with little moderation.
- Most files are .io, and relatively few creators publish plain PDF step instructions.

**Best for:** builders who like designing their own models, or who want to learn MOC building from scratch.

## 3. BrickPlan: A Community for Sharing Brick Instructions

**Overall rating: ★★★★☆**

BrickPlan is a newer community built around sharing MOC instructions, aimed primarily at Chinese-speaking builders, and it is growing quickly.

**Strengths:**
- **Fully localized interface**: from instruction descriptions to tag categories, everything is localized, so there is no barrier to getting started.
- **Multiple upload formats**: creators can post step images, cover images and the original PDF together, and users pick whichever suits them.
- **Community interaction**: likes, favourites, comments and follows; the building atmosphere is active and creators respond quickly.

**Weaknesses:**
- The total library is still smaller than Rebrickable's, and some niche themes are thin.
- There is no automatic parts comparison yet, so you have to check the parts list by hand.

**Best for:** Chinese-speaking builders, newcomers, and MOC fans who want to interact with creators directly.

## 4. YouTube & Bilibili: The Unique Value of Video Instructions

**Overall rating: ★★★☆☆**

More and more MOC designers present their builds on video, which is effectively a moving instruction sheet.

**Strengths:**
- **Easy to follow**: video shows the small handling tricks and techniques that a static instruction sheet cannot convey.
- **Free and plentiful**: a huge amount of good content costs nothing — searching for LEGO MOC tutorial videos turns up endless material.

**Weaknesses:**
- No parts list is generated, so you have to write the parts down yourself, one by one.
- Video quality and step clarity vary, and some creators skip key steps.

**Best for:** visual learners who like building along with a video.

## 5. How to Choose the Right MOC Instruction Source

1. **If you are new** — and especially if you read Chinese — start with BrickPlan or Bilibili, where the localized environment lowers the learning curve. Begin with simple street scenes and small dioramas.
2. **If you want professional results**: Rebrickable is essential. Its inventory matching can significantly cut your parts bill.
3. **If you like designing**: BrickLink Studio is the first choice. Design as you learn, and the finished model can be exported as instructions to share with others.
4. **Combine platforms**: the smartest workflow is to find inspiration on Rebrickable, adjust the design in Studio, then share and discuss it on BrickPlan.

## Final Thoughts

MOC instruction resources are becoming richer and easier to use every year. From the early days of blurry photos shared in forum threads to today's mature ecosystem of Rebrickable, BrickLink Studio and BrickPlan, the barrier to MOC building has dropped a long way.

Whichever platform you choose, what matters most is the urge to start building. Pick up your bricks, find an instruction set you like, and begin your MOC journey.
