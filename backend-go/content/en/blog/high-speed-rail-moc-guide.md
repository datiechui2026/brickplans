# High-Speed Rail MOC Guide: Planning to Finished Train

MOC stands for "My Own Creation" — a model a builder designs from scratch rather than following a set's instructions. Among the many subjects builders return to, the high-speed rail MOC is one of the most popular. A train's long, unbroken bodywork, the aerodynamic nose, and the futuristic liveries make it a challenging subject with a big payoff. Whether you want to recreate a Fuxing or Harmony unit or design your own concept train, this guide walks you through the whole process, from first sketch to finished model.

## High-Speed Rail MOC Project Overview

Before you pick up a brick, decide what kind of model you are actually building. High-speed rail MOCs generally fall into two camps: static display models and running models. A static build lets you chase exterior detail and accurate proportions freely. A running build has to reserve space for motors, wheels and the steering mechanism, which makes the build noticeably harder. New builders should start with a static model or a simple "push-along" version, then upgrade to a motorized one once they understand the structure.

Scale choice affects the difficulty just as much. Common scales such as 1:48 and 1:64 are practical at mainstream brick sizes: they leave enough room to reproduce the streamlined nose without shrinking the bricks so far that nothing holds together. Once the scale is locked in, sketch the side profile first and mark the body length, the curve of the nose and the window positions. That single step saves a lot of rebuilding later.

## Key Building Techniques

The hardest part of any high-speed rail MOC is the nose. Straight rectangular stacking will not produce a rounded aerodynamic curve, so use slope bricks, wedge bricks and curved slopes to create a stepped transition. Narrow the profile layer by layer from the base up to the roof, offsetting wedges at different angles as you go, and a smooth cab curve appears.

Windows are the detail that sells the realism. Line both sides of the body with transparent elements or 1x2 transparent window bricks, then run dark bar bricks above and below them to pick out the waistline — the whole train instantly looks more solid. Give the wheels and bogies enough clearance so nothing binds when the model runs through a curve. If you plan to couple several cars, use hinge pieces, or Technic beams with pins, so the cars articulate without an obvious gap.

## Where to Find Instructions

If you would rather not design from zero, plenty of good resources already exist online. BrickPlans (brickplan.cn) collects high-speed rail brick MOC instructions shared by community builders, covering everything from classic electric multiple units to futuristic concepts, and each set of plans comes with a parts list and step-by-step build notes. Brick forums at home and abroad, build videos on social media and dedicated instruction sites are all good places to look for inspiration and reference.

After downloading a plan, check the parts list against your own stock first, and sort the various colours into compartment boxes ahead of time — the build goes much faster. If a few parts are missing, order them through a single-parts seller or substitute a brick in a close colour; it will not affect the overall look.

## Building and Refinement Tips

During the build, work "inside out, bottom up": finish the chassis and wheels first, then the body side walls, and handle the roof and decorative details last. Check symmetry and rigidity after each module so you catch errors early. If a particular joint keeps working loose, add a reinforcement beam inside or swap in a brick with more friction.

Finally, give the model some personality: follow the livery and colour blocking of a real high-speed train, add a custom logo, or even fit lighting elements to simulate the headlights. When it is done, photograph it from several angles and share it with the BrickPlans community to compare notes with other builders. The pleasure of a high-speed rail MOC is not only in copying the real thing — it is in the creativity that comes out of every attempt and revision along the way. May every builder end up with a high-speed train of their own, heading for wherever their imagination goes.
