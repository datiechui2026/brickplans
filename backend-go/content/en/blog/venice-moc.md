# Venice Canal MOC: 12,500 Parts, Gondolas and Bridge of Sighs

The short version: this Venice canal MOC uses about 12,500 parts, measures 92 × 58 cm when finished, and takes roughly 36 hours at a pace of around 3 hours a day. Total part cost lands near 1,800 yuan. The layout is a classic "one canal, two banks" arrangement — a central waterway runs the length of the city, with the Bridge of Sighs, St Mark's Campanile, the Doge's Palace and a gondola dock lined up along the two shores. The finished build has real depth and photographs beautifully in a display case. The instructions come from an architecture MOC plan published on brickplan.cn, where both the parts list and the step-by-step manual can be downloaded directly.

**Step 1: Build the canal bed and foundation**

Start with 48×32 stud baseplates to lay out the city's foundation, leaving a 4-stud-wide channel down the middle for the canal. Line the canal bed with dark blue plates, then build the embankments on both sides from light gray bricks, kept to a uniform height of 6 bricks so there is a clean dividing line between water level and buildings. Get the foundation right in one pass — every building afterwards is anchored to those embankments, and reworking them later is expensive.

**Step 2: Assemble the Bridge of Sighs and St Mark's Campanile**

The Bridge of Sighs is the visual focal point of the whole city. The span crosses the canal, curved pieces form the enclosed walkway on top, and the bridge body carries two small windows per side — each one can hold a warm LED, which looks striking once lit. St Mark's Campanile stands about 48 cm tall, the highest structure in the build; the tower body steps in one ring every 4 brick layers and finishes with a gold spire piece. These two buildings alone consume roughly 4,200 parts, making them the most brick-hungry part of the plan.

**Step 3: Finish the Doge's Palace and waterfront shops**

The Doge's Palace uses a pink-and-white facade, with a continuous run of arcade pieces across the second floor to create the signature Gothic front. The waterfront shops are 2-story buildings: a full-height window wall facing the canal on the ground floor, a balcony above, and 1×1 railing pieces for the balustrade — small details that read strongly at this scale. This module takes about 3,500 parts. Work walls first, roofs second, otherwise the structure gets wobbly.

**Step 4: Make the gondolas and water effects**

Build 6 gondolas at roughly 40 parts each: black hull, gold bow ornament and a canopied seat. Simulate the water with transparent light blue plates, scatter a layer of 1×1 transparent studs on the surface for ripples, and add white foam pieces at the sterns so the scene still reads as moving when it sits still. Fix the gondolas into the canal last, varying the bow directions to give the scene a bit of narrative.

## FAQ

**Q: How much does this Venice MOC cost?**

A: Parts run about 1,800 yuan, with transparent water pieces and gold decorative elements taking the largest share. Sourcing everything as loose parts from the list keeps the budget between 1,600 and 2,000 yuan; the instruction page on brickplan.cn gives a reference price.

**Q: How do I make the water look more realistic?**

A: Start with transparent light blue plates, lay a layer of 1×1 transparent studs on top for ripples, then accent with white foam pieces. For a night scene, bury two warm LED bricks under the canal bed — the light diffuses through the transparent pieces for a shimmering effect.

**Q: Can a beginner build straight from this plan?**

A: Yes, but finish a small architecture MOC of around 1,000 parts first. The full plan is split into four modules of roughly 3,000 parts each, so building one module a week gets a beginner to completion within two months.

**Q: How is this different from the official Venice set?**

A: The official set is around 1,000 parts and about 30 cm across, built for portability. This MOC is 12,500 parts and 92 cm wide, with far more detail and a more complete scene — better suited to builders who have display space and want immersion. It also costs considerably more.

**Q: Can I substitute parts I already own?**

A: Absolutely. Standard bricks in the embankments and walls can be swapped for the same color from another brand. Only the curved bridge roof, the gold spire and the transparent water pieces should be bought to the list — substitutes in those categories change the look noticeably.
