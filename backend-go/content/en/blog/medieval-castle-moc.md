# Medieval Castle MOC Review: 3,600 Pieces, Working Drawbridge

## Product Overview

This review covers a brick MOC instruction set built around a medieval castle theme. The entire design is organised around a single goal: recreating the age of knights. It packs a towering outer curtain wall, two high corner towers, a drawbridge that actually raises and lowers, and a main keep hall, at roughly 3,600 pieces overall. The instructions come from BrickPlans community author Lionheart Knight and are delivered as a step-by-step PDF build guide plus a numbered parts list, downloadable directly at https://brickplan.cn. Both the exterior silhouette and the internal structure aim at authentic medieval defensive architecture rather than a fantasy castle look, which makes it a hard one for castle fans to skip.

## Build Difficulty

Overall difficulty sits at medium-to-high, aimed at builders who already have some MOC experience behind them. Three spots are the real challenges: the interlocking at the wall corners, the curved-surface stacking that forms the cylindrical tower bodies, and the drawbridge linkage mechanism. The instructions give clear step notes at all three, so the difficulty is in the part count and the precision, not in guesswork. Built strictly to the drawings, expect 8 to 12 hours of work. Beginners should split that across two or three sessions rather than pushing through in one sitting, since fatigue is where mistakes creep in on a model this size.

## Parts Review

The parts are compatible with mainstream brick brands, so you can pull most of the inventory from what you already have sorted. Key structural pieces are mostly grey bricks and brown-tan plates, and the colour consistency across the build is strong. The walls use stud-textured bricks to imitate stone masonry, and the effect is convincing at normal viewing distance; the conical dome pieces on top of the towers mate well, with no looseness or tolerance problems showing up during the build. The one thing to watch is the drawbridge hinge: use original-brand parts there, because third-party substitutes can affect how smoothly the bridge raises and lowers. The parts list flags those pieces separately, so you can plan the order before you start digging through bins.

## Build Experience

The highlight of the whole build was the drawbridge linkage. Turning a winch inside the keep raises and lowers the bridge, the gears mesh tightly, and testing showed smooth travel with no catching or slipping. Hidden storage compartments at the wall corners add play value beyond the display side of the model. The instruction logic is clear throughout, step numbers line up with the parts list, and there was almost no teardown and rework halfway through, which is rare on a build of this scale. Sort the grey bricks and the brown-tan plates into separate trays before you begin, and test the winch mechanism as soon as that subassembly is finished, before the keep walls close over it.

## Finished Model

The completed castle measures about 45 cm square and 38 cm tall, and it looks striking on a desk. Grey stone walls set against the dark brown timber drawbridge and banners give clear visual layering, and the two corner towers keep the silhouette from reading as a flat box. The interior can hold several minifigures posed for a siege defence or a banquet scene, and a lighting kit makes the display stronger still. The only small letdown is the roof banner, which feels thin next to the mass of the walls; builders can swap in a sturdier one or reinforce it themselves. Photos of the finished model and close-up detail shots are in the author's original post at https://brickplan.cn.

## Verdict

Overall score: 4.5/5. This medieval castle brick MOC delivers on structural accuracy, mechanism design and build experience, and it is a strong addition for castle enthusiasts. The higher difficulty is a real barrier for newcomers, but the sense of accomplishment along the way more than compensates for the extra hours. If you are looking for a medieval castle instruction set that is both playable and display-worthy, this one is worth picking up.
