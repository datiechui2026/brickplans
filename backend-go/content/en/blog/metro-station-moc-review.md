# Metro Station Brick MOC Review: A Subway That Arrives

Once a street scene reaches a certain size, shops alone stop carrying the "city" feeling — you need public transport to light up the skeleton of the block. For this **metro station brick MOC review**, we built a station with an underground platform from the instructions, and we're walking the whole route in one pass: concourse, turnstiles, platform, train arrival. If you're thinking about adding a transit node to your city, here's the straight answer.

## 1. Instructions and Parts: A Standard Public Transport Build

The model is drawn at **1:48 street scene scale** and comes in at roughly **2,400 pieces**, on the larger side for a public transport subject, split across 8 numbered bags. The manual runs two main threads — "above-ground concourse" and "underground platform" — and highlights the current part at every step, so the page-to-page rhythm stays comfortable. The palette leans on **metro white, dark grey, warning yellow and glass blue**; it reads clean and instantly like civic infrastructure. The best surprise is the printed elements: the line map sign, the ticket machine screens and the ceiling light boxes are all printed rather than stickered, and the platform signage sits flat on the walls without curling at the edges. Missing-part rate was low. **Instructions and parts: 8.5/10.**

## 2. Structure and Concourse: The Turnstiles Really Let You Through

The biggest risk with a metro station is ending up with "a sign above a doorway." This **metro station brick MOC** avoids that — the ground-level concourse is a real build:

- **Ticket hall and gates:** a ticket machine stands on each side of the entrance, with a row of three-lane turnstiles between them. The gate doors are built from holed pieces plus thin plates, so **a minifigure can slide a "ticket" card through the reader and the gate swings open for them to pass** — the swipe-in flow is genuinely reproduced.
- **Security and lift:** just inside the gates sits a security desk (conveyor belt plus scanner arch), with a glass elevator beside it that lifts minifigures up and down.
- **Separated flows:** dark and light grey plates on the floor divide the entry lanes from the exit lanes.

The structure is compact and the clutch is firm — we opened and closed the gates repeatedly with no loosening. **Structure: 8.5/10.**

## 3. Playability Core: A Train That Pulls Into the Underground Platform

This is the part of the **metro station brick MOC** that wins people over. Lift off the concourse floor plate and the **underground platform level** is revealed: two tracks run beneath the platform, yellow warning plates form the **safety line** along the edge, and platform screen door posts stand on both sides. Push the included **6-stud-wide** carriage onto the rails, ease it in from the platform edge, stop it with the doors lined up against the screen doors, and let minifigures get on and off — the whole "train arrives" story plays out. Benches, bins and wayfinding signs fill in the small scenery, and a few minifigures queuing for the train instantly give the scene its commuter bustle. The letdown: the train can only be pushed by hand, and the track cannot be extended. **Playability: 9/10.**

## 4. Accuracy and Lighting Atmosphere

On accuracy, nothing is missing — line map, turnstiles, security arch, safety line, screen doors — and it reads as a standard metro station at a glance, with very high recognisability. Add a cool white light strip and the underground platform gets a tunnel feel that photographs beautifully; pair it with warm light inside the carriage and the contrast lands immediately. The small regrets are a rather flat roof and the fact that the concourse and platform can't be lifted together as one unit for play. **Atmosphere: 8.5/10.**

**Verdict: worth buying, especially if you want to add a transit hub to your city block.** The difficulty sits at medium-high (roughly 6–7 hours); a newcomer can finish it with guidance from an experienced builder. It suits players who want a public transport node that gives their street scene a commuter rhythm; players who enjoy underground structures and liftable or hinged mechanisms; and players who already own minifigure commuter scenes and want to stage the "exit the station, cross the road" story. The main shortcomings are the fixed track and the hand-pushed train, but for most transport fans the strengths outweigh them.
