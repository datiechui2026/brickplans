# Brick Taxi MOC Guide: Streamlined Body and Roof Sign

Short version first: judge a brick taxi on three things — curved elements shaping the roof and waistline into smooth transitions, a TAXI roof box built from transparent parts over a reflective base, and doors pivoting on 1x2 hinge plates with pins.

In any city street scene, the taxi is one of the most recognizable elements. Park a yellow-and-black brick taxi MOC in front of a modular block and the whole street suddenly feels alive. But a taxi with clean lines and convincing detail is not easy to pull off: body curvature, roof sign and door movement each test how well you understand your parts. This guide breaks down the key techniques, from body shell to interior.

## Streamlined Body: Curved Elements and SNOT Working Together

The most appealing thing about a taxi is the rounded roof and the smooth line running along the sides. To get that effect, don't rely only on standard bricks — stock several sizes of curved elements (curved bricks, curved panels and wedge plates). For the roof, pair two large curved pieces symmetrically and bridge them with tiles, which keeps the curvature while hiding the studs. The side skirts are a natural place for SNOT: turn bricks 90 degrees so the studs face outward, then attach a row of tiles for a flat waistline that looks far more refined than simply stacking bricks. At the front, sloped wedge plates can carve the pressed-down nose line, giving the whole car a forward, diving stance.

## Roof Sign Details: The Soul of the Taxi

The TAXI light box on the roof is the soul of the vehicle and should never be skipped. The recommended approach: frame the box with transparent parts (transparent 1x2 bricks or transparent panels), lay a white tile inside as a reflective base, then add yellow or orange transparent elements to suggest bulbs. Raise the base on two small bracket elements so a gap opens between box and roof, which gives the lighting more depth. If you own a printed transparent TAXI element, use it; if not, a tile plus a sticker works well too. Remember to cap each side of the box with a small curved piece so the sign blends naturally into the roof curvature.

## Doors That Open: Using Hinges Correctly

Hinges are the key to doors that actually open and close. Use a 1x2 hinge with pin together with a 1x4 plate to make the door pivot: fix the hinge to the chassis frame inside the rear wheel arch, then hang the door panel on the pins. This gives a wide opening angle and the door will not drop off. The door panel itself can be built from door frame elements or tiles, with the window area hollowed out and a transparent part set in to simulate glass. Place a 1x2 brick between front and rear doors as a B-pillar — it stiffens the structure and keeps the two doors from interfering with each other. Note that the hinges must be installed as mirrored pairs, otherwise the doors will fight each other over which way to swing.

## Interior and Color Scheme: Big Details in a Small Space

A taxi is cramped, but the interior still deserves attention. Build the dashboard and center console with SNOT, and fix the steering wheel — a 1x1 round plate on a short axle — to the side. For the rear bench, use dark gray tiles, with the backrest tilted slightly rearward for a more convincing seating position. On color: bright yellow for the main body, black or white to divide the roof and waistline, dark gray inside the wheel arches as a transition — eye-catching yet stable overall. Keep the interior mostly dark gray and black so it contrasts with the bright shell and reads clearly in photos. Try to keep all transparent parts within one color family so the windows don't look muddy.

## FAQ

**Q: What if the roof curvature sags?**
A: Add cross-bracing beams inside the curved elements. Pair two large curved pieces symmetrically with tiles bridging the middle so the roof holds its shape under pressure, and avoid pressing hard on the curved surface while assembling.

**Q: How do I fix doors that won't close flush?**
A: The hinge position is usually off. Use a 1x2 hinge with pin and a 1x4 plate as the pivot, fix the hinge to the frame inside the rear wheel arch, then mount the door panel and fine-tune the pins until the panel sits flush with the body.

**Q: What do I do about foggy transparent parts?**
A: Wipe them with a soft cloth before building and avoid handling them repeatedly. Do not clean them with alcohol or similar solvents, which can leave the surface hazy white. The white tile inside the light box doubles as a reflective base and makes the lighting read stronger.

**Q: What colors should a taxi use?**
A: Classic yellow and black is the safest choice: bright yellow body, black roof and waistline, dark gray wheels. For the light box, add yellow or orange transparent elements inside with a white tile underneath as a reflector.

- **Doors won't close flush**: the hinge position is usually off — mount the door first, then fine-tune the pins until the panel is level with the body.
- **Roof curvature sags**: add cross-bracing beams inside the curved elements so they don't deform under pressure.
- **Foggy transparent parts**: wipe them with a soft cloth before building; fingerprints are the biggest enemy of clear elements.
- **Off proportions**: work from a standard 6-wide body, set the wheelbase first, then pile on detail — much harder to get wrong.
