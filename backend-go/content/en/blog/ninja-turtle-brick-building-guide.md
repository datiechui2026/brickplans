# Ninja Turtles Brick Build Guide: Figures, Van and Sewer Lair

The Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles are a childhood memory for a whole generation, and rebuilding Leonardo, Raphael, Donatello and Michelangelo out of bricks is exactly what makes a Ninja Turtles build so appealing. The hard part of this theme is not technique, it is planning. Decide first whether you are building figures, the van or the lair, then start cutting parts — that alone saves a lot of rework. Here is a complete plan you can follow.

## Four Turtle Figures: Three Identification Points

The figures are the soul of the whole build. All four turtles share the same body template and are separated only by mask color, weapon and expression, so lock down those three identification points and nobody will look alike:

| Turtle | Mask color | Signature weapon | Suggested expression |
|--------|------------|------------------|----------------------|
| Leonardo | Blue | Twin katana | Calm and determined |
| Raphael | Red | Twin sai | Furious glare |
| Donatello | Purple | Bo staff | Wise and composed |
| Michelangelo | Orange | Nunchaku | Wide grin |

For the mask, ring the sides of the head with bricks with holes or curved pieces rather than sticking a tile flat on the face — it reads far more three-dimensionally. Build the shell by stacking dome bricks into a raised hump, then add two green slope bricks for the back pattern. Flip the figure over and the lighter-colored plastron is still there, which instantly lifts the level of detail.

## Turtle Van: Modular Builds Are Easier

The van is the best value project in a Ninja Turtles build. Work through it as four modules in order:

1. **Chassis** — run technic cross axles through the frame to reinforce it and carry the weight.
2. **Front end** — use slope bricks to shape a rounded, shell-like nose, with transparent pieces as headlights on both sides.
3. **Body sides** — leave openings for doors that swing open so figures can get in and out.
4. **Roof** — make the shell lid one piece that lifts off as a whole; when you want to pose figures, you just pop the top.

The vehicle is mainly green with orange-yellow accents, and a dark gray chassis underneath. That classic palette is recognizable at a glance.

## Sewer Lair: Draw the Layout Before You Build

In a Ninja Turtles build, the diorama is usually what decides the overall result. A complete sewer lair works best as a modular layout so you can widen and extend it later:

| Zone | Main parts | Layout notes |
|------|------------|--------------|
| Entrance ladder | Grille bricks, gray plates | Manhole cover opens and closes |
| Pizza kitchen | Red round plates | Transparent pieces for steam |
| Training room | Cylinder bricks, technic axles | Wooden training dummy in the middle |
| Lounge | Transparent plates, sofa pieces | TV screen made from transparent pieces |

Lay the pipe system out of curved pipe pieces and cylinder bricks crossing over each other, then set a few dark odd-shaped pieces into the pipe walls to suggest water stains. The cool, damp underground feeling comes through immediately.

## Villains and Expansion Ideas

Once the heroes are done, don't forget the villains. Shredder reads as genuinely imposing with silver armor pieces and an iron-face helmet, while Bebop and Rocksteady get their comic look from exaggerated head pieces. If you want a bigger challenge, combine the van, the lair and all four brothers into one full display scene — on a shelf it has real presence.
