# LEGO Instructions Lost? How to Find, Replace, and Back Them Up

Most builders have been there: the box is still on the shelf, every last part is accounted for, but the **LEGO instructions are gone** and the build stalls halfway through step 4. Before you tear the house apart looking for a booklet, know that both the official channels and the wider community have already solved this problem for you. Here is the whole process in priority order — how to replace a missing manual, where to hunt one down, and how to store instructions so it never happens again.

## Official channels: the most reliable way to replace instructions

When your **LEGO instructions go missing**, the official source should be your first stop. LEGO's website offers free PDF downloads of building instructions for every set currently on sale, plus the large majority of retired ones. Just type the set number into the "Instructions" search field on the official site and the right file should come up.

This route is the most authoritative and the clearest, and the version you get is the official one — which is why it belongs at the top of the list. One thing to keep in mind: a handful of very old, long-retired sets may never have made it into the database. If yours is one of those, move on to the options below.

## Rebrickable: one database for every set

If the official site is a single point of access, Rebrickable is the aggregate of the entire LEGO world. The platform catalogues parts lists and instruction information for essentially every official set ever released, and it pulls together resources from both official and community sources in one place.

Search a set number on Rebrickable and you can usually find a download link for the instructions, a full parts inventory, and even a way to work out what you could build from a set using the loose bricks you already own. For MOC builders this site is close to essential equipment — you will use it to check instructions, verify parts, and hunt down substitutes.

## Community sourcing: the power of crowd knowledge

If neither the official site nor Rebrickable has what you need, don't give up — someone in the community is probably sitting on it. Domestic brick groups, Baidu Tieba forums and message boards, and the big international building communities all have members who share scanned or digital copies of manuals. Older sets are the classic case: plenty of helpful builders scan and upload instructions from their own collections.

When you search, use keywords in the form "set number + instructions" (or the equivalent terms for manual or building guide) — that phrasing gives you the highest hit rate. Be aware that community copies vary in resolution and edition, so flip through the whole file before you start building. Get a feel for the quality first, then pick up the bricks.

## How to store instructions so you never lose them again

Rather than hunting down a replacement every time one goes missing, fix the problem at the source. Here is the habit that works for me: the moment a new set arrives, photograph the manual or download the electronic version and save a copy, put the scan in cloud storage, and file the original paper booklet somewhere separate.

Keep at least two copies of the digital version — one in cloud storage, one locally on your phone or computer. That double backup is basically bulletproof. Even if the paper manual gets torn up by a kid or carried off by the cat, you can pull up the high-resolution digital copy and rebuild whenever you like.

## Keep an extra copy for MOC instructions

One last reminder: instructions deserve even more care when you're building MOCs. Many MOC instructions are the original work of their designers and don't have the kind of complete archive that official sets enjoy — if you lose them, they may genuinely be gone for good. So the moment you get your hands on a set of MOC instructions, save a digital copy. That is a habit I've stuck with for a long time.

When you're looking for new instructions to build, have a look at https://brickplan.cn — there's a solid collection of high-resolution brick MOC instructions there. Download what you like and save a backup copy while you're at it, and you'll never have to worry about losing instructions again. Keep the instructions safe, and the fun stays with you.
