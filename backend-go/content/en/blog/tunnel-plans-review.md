# Tunnel Plans Reviewed: Building a Brick Underground Hub

## Introduction

When builders plan a brick city, the buildings and streets above ground usually get all the attention. But a city only feels complete when it has a working underground transit system. **Tunnel plans** are the underground engineering layer of that city, and they add both depth and realism. Whether it is a rail tunnel cutting through a mountain or a road tunnel running beneath downtown, a well-designed tunnel plan finishes the layout. This review looks at several popular tunnel plans and explains how to pick and build the one that fits your city.

## Why Tunnels Matter in a Brick City

### Adding Vertical Depth

A flat brick city can be beautifully detailed and still feel like a diorama. A **tunnel plan** pushes the layout from flat to three-dimensional: a train emerging from a mountainside, a car disappearing into an underpass. Those moments make the city feel believable and give you far more to play with.

### Solving Traffic Layout Problems

On a limited baseplate, tunnels are the cleanest way to untangle crossing routes. With underground passages you can achieve:

- Grade-separated rail and road crossings
- An underground road system beneath the city center
- Hidden rail lines that run out of sight
- Connections between metro stations and commercial districts

## Tunnel Plan Reviews

### 1. Mountain Rail Tunnel

This **tunnel plan** is built around a mountainside bore and is the first choice for rail fans.

**Plan features:**
- Tunnel length: fits 8-12 standard train cars passing through
- Mountain shell: stacked layers of bricks that form a natural-looking hillside
- Portal: arched design with masonry texture detailing
- Included scenery: signal lights, track, tunnel portal nameplate

**Difficulty:** ★★★☆☆ (medium)
**Part count:** roughly 1,200 pieces (train and track not included)

**Design highlights:**
- Trees can be planted on the mountain surface for a natural blend
- Interior space is reserved for lighting
- Modular design lets you extend the tunnel length

### 2. City Road Tunnel

A road tunnel plan aimed at city builders, with more emphasis on connecting to buildings.

**Core design:**
- Dual carriageway, four lanes
- Tunnel portal aligns flush with city roads
- Tiled tunnel walls that mimic real concrete lining
- Ventilation openings and lighting system

**Best used for:**
- Downtown underpasses
- Tunnels passing beneath large buildings
- Transition sections between elevated viaducts and street level

### 3. Metro Station and Tunnel Complex

The most capable option, combining a metro station with its connecting tunnels.

**Combined functions:**
- Underground platform level (waiting area plus track area)
- Concourse level (ticket sales, entry and exit gates)
- Connecting passages linking different lines
- Surface entrances with metro signage

**Space requirement:** a large baseplate footprint (at least 48x48 studs recommended)

### 4. Simple Mountain Bore

A beginner-friendly tunnel plan: simple structure, surprisingly good results.

**Easy to start with:**
- Around 300-500 parts
- Build time of roughly 1-2 hours
- Matches standard track height
- Clean mountain shape that is hard to get wrong

## Buying Guide for Tunnel Plans

### Choose by City Scale

| City scale | Recommended tunnel type | Suggested quantity |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| Small city (32x32 baseplate) | Simple mountain bore | 1-2 |
| Medium city (48x48 baseplate) | Road tunnel plus rail tunnel | 2-3 |
| Large city (multiple baseplates joined) | Integrated tunnel system | 3-5 or more |

### Match Your Train Type

Different trains need different tunnel dimensions:

- **Standard trains:** tunnel at least 8 bricks high and 12 studs wide
- **Narrow gauge trains:** 6 bricks of clearance is enough
- **High-speed trains:** wider tunnel spacing required

### Choose by Function

- **Traffic first:** go with a road tunnel or metro tunnel
- **Scenery first:** go with a mountain tunnel and focus on the exterior shape
- **Both:** go with a combined plan such as the metro station complex

## Build Tips and Things to Watch

### Structural Design

1. **Arched load bearing:** an arched tunnel roof carries weight far better than a flat one
2. **Modular building:** build the portal, middle section and exit as separate modules
3. **Plan your seams:** leave alignment interfaces between modules so they line up
4. **Reinforce:** add support columns in the middle of long tunnels to prevent sagging

### Interior Detailing

A good **tunnel plan** should include interior detail:

- Tunnel wall lights (transparent parts work well)
- Cable runs (hose or thin rod elements)
- Emergency exit signage
- Striped wall banding along the lining

### Blending With the Cityscape

- Plant climbing greenery at the portal for a natural transition
- Add trees and rock formations on top of mountain tunnels
- Place street signs and lamps at city tunnel entrances
- Build a park or a building above the tunnel

## Scene Ideas to Extend Your Tunnel

### Rail Tunnel Scenes

- Twin parallel bores to represent a double-track railway
- A signal box beside the portal
- A lookout tower on top of the mountain

### Road Tunnel Scenes

- A toll plaza at the tunnel entrance
- A staged "accident" scene inside the tunnel
- An overpass built above the tunnel

### Integrated Transit Hub

- Rail and road tunnels crossing at different levels
- A tunnel connecting to an underground parking garage
- A metro station linked underground to a shopping street

## Designing Tunnel Plans Digitally

If none of the existing plans suit you, you can design your own tunnel:

### Recommended Tools

- **Studio 2.0**: the mainstream MOC design software, supports simulating tunnel structures
- **Bricklink Part Designer**: for custom part colors
- **brickplan.cn**: an online plan editing and sharing platform

### Design Notes

- Fix the dimensions of your train or vehicle first
- Test critical structures with real bricks
- Leave working space inside the tunnel so you can place minifigures and vehicles
- Reserve room for lighting and decoration
