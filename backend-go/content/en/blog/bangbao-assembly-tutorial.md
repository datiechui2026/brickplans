# BanBao Building Tutorial: From Unboxing to Finished Model

BanBao has become a favourite among builders looking for an affordable entry point into the hobby, thanks to wallet-friendly prices and a wide spread of themed series. But if this is your first BanBao set, a table covered in loose parts can be intimidating. This tutorial walks you through the whole process, from opening the box to placing the last piece.

## 1. Getting Set Up Before You Open the Box

A little preparation up front saves a lot of time later. Find a flat surface with good lighting and lay down a clean mat or cloth so small pieces can't roll away and disappear. Have a **brick separator** and a pair of small scissors within reach — the separator earns its keep the moment you need to undo a mistake.

Once the box is open, resist the urge to tip every bag out at once. BanBao sets are usually bagged by build stage, so **open one bag at a time** and finish it before starting the next. That keeps your workspace tidy and stops parts from different stages getting mixed together.

## 2. Sorting and Organizing the Pieces

BanBao doesn't pre-sort parts as finely as some brands, but a rough sort by colour and shape makes a noticeable difference to your building speed. Set out a few small boxes or trays and separate **baseplates, standard bricks and special elements**. Small parts in particular — technic pins, gears, small clips — deserve their own container so you aren't hunting for them mid-build.

## 3. Reading the Instructions and Following the Build Order

Every BanBao set ships with a clear **instruction booklet**. Newly added parts are normally shown in a distinct colour, while grey or pale pieces indicate what has already been assembled. Follow the **numbered step order exactly**: build the base and the main frame first, then work your way upward.

One useful habit is to check the arrows before you press anything down. Each step marks the direction of insertion with red or bold arrows, and confirming the orientation first prevents the classic frustration of seating a piece backwards and having to pull it apart again. **Don't skip steps** — with internal structures especially, one wrong placement early on can block everything that follows.

## 4. Pressing Technique and Assembly Tips

When you seat a BanBao piece, press **straight down with even force**. Pushing too hard, or at an angle, is how parts get damaged. If a connection feels tight, line up the studs and slots with your fingers first, then ease the piece down gradually until it's fully seated. If something won't go on, stop and check whether you've picked the wrong part or flipped it — never force it.

On larger sets, **build in sub-assemblies and combine them at the end**. Assembling the chassis, the body and the roof separately and joining them afterwards is easier on your hands and cuts down on errors. After each stage, run through the instructions once more and confirm nothing has been missed.

## 5. Common Pitfalls to Watch For

Make sure **small parts are pressed fully home** — a pin or clip that's only half seated will leave the finished model loose and wobbly. Avoid long building sessions in dim light, and give your eyes regular breaks. If you reach the end of a stage and a piece seems to be missing, search the other bags in the same set first; if it's genuinely absent, contact the seller about a replacement part.

## 6. Making the Build More Fun

Finishing your first BanBao set is a genuinely satisfying moment. Once you're comfortable with the process, try using the leftover pieces for free building, or work from **MOC instructions** to design something that's entirely your own.
