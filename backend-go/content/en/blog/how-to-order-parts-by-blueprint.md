# How to Order Parts From a Blueprint: A 4-Step MOC Buying Guide

The short version: sourcing parts from a blueprint is a four-step loop — export the parts list, sort it into three piles (already own / must buy / substitutable), order through the right channel, then check every line on arrival. The cheapest split is domestic compatible parts for basic bricks and original-brand parts for the key pieces.

Finding the instructions is one thing; rounding up the parts is the step that stops most builds. There is a standard workflow for ordering against a blueprint, and once you learn it you stop wasting evenings hunting through drawers and shipping returns back and forth. Below, the whole process is broken into four steps, with a channel comparison table at the end. Follow it in order and you can source a full model in one pass.

## Step 1: Export the parts list (BOM)

Everything starts with the parts list, better known as the BOM (Bill of Materials). Here is how to get it:

1. Open the parts list page that belongs to your blueprint (most BrickPlan blueprints ship with a complete list);
2. Export it as CSV or XML so it is easy to organise and easy to send to a seller;
3. Check the totals first — total piece count and missing-piece count — so you know what you are dealing with before you spend anything.

Every row in the list carries a part number, a color, a quantity and a name. All four columns have to be present, otherwise the seller cannot pull the order accurately.

## Step 2: Split the list into three piles

Resist the urge to order straight away. Sort the list by what you already have:

- **Parts you already own**: cross them off the list so you do not buy them twice;
- **Parts you must buy new**: purchase these by exact part number, especially rare parts and rare colors;
- **Parts you can substitute with domestic compatible parts**: basic bricks, plates and internal structure parts — swapping in compatible parts saves a substantial amount.

This step is where the budget is won or lost. Most people blow their budget on a parts order because they paid original-brand prices for basic bricks that could have been substituted.

## Step 3: Order through the right channels

Different parts belong in different channels:

- **Basic bricks and plates**: buy domestic compatible parts by weight — the lowest unit price;
- **Special parts and rare colors**: buy by part number from loose-part stores;
- **Missing parts**: first check whether the brand's official free replacement service covers you;
- **Bulk stocking**: when the budget is tight, buy secondhand parts sold by weight — common parts in quantity;

Before you order, always **verify the part counts and merge your orders**. Shipping is the single easiest place for a loose-part store to push you over budget, so never split an order that could have been combined.

## Step 4: Inspect the delivery

Once the parcel arrives, check it line by line against the list: is the quantity right, is the color right, are there obvious color mismatches or flash and burrs on the parts? If something is missing or wrong, contact the seller for a replacement straight away — do not wait until you are halfway through the build to discover it. Storing parts in boxes sorted by color and type will also make the build itself much faster.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I order parts from a blueprint without missing anything?**
A: Export the full parts list (with part number, color and quantity), sort it into the three piles — already own, must buy, substitutable — tick each line off, then check the delivery against the list item by item. Get through all four steps and you will rarely miss a part.

**Q: Where do I export the parts list?**
A: From the parts list page attached to the blueprint, as a CSV or XML file (most BrickPlan blueprints include a complete list). After exporting, verify the total piece count and the missing-piece count, and make sure the list contains all four columns: part number, color, quantity and name.

**Q: Roughly how much does it cost to source a whole MOC?**
A: The spread can be tenfold, depending on what the model is made of. Static display models that are mostly basic bricks are cheap; models with a high share of Technic special parts and motors are noticeably more expensive. Substituting domestic compatible parts for basic bricks while buying original-brand parts for key pieces is the lever that controls the budget.

**Q: What is the cheapest way to source an order?**
A: Three things: substitute domestic compatible parts for basic and internal structure parts while keeping original-brand parts for visible surfaces and special colors; stock common bricks by the kilogram and buy rare parts individually; verify counts and merge orders before checkout to spread the shipping cost.
