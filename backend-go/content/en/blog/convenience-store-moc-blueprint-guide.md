# LEGO Convenience Store MOC Guide: Shelves, Counter & Lights

A cafe MOC has charm, but a **convenience store MOC** has something better: ordinary life. It never closes, its neon sign buzzes over the door, and a clerk is somewhere between the aisles restocking. The footprint is small, yet you still get a storefront, shelving, a drinks fridge and a checkout — which makes it one of the best practice builds you can take on. Build it as a standalone shop or drop it into a street layout as the late-night anchor.

## 1. The Storefront: Make It Readable at a Glance

A convenience store lives or dies by its sign. Use a high-saturation base colour such as **bright blue or fluorescent green**, then add large white printed letters or brick-built lettering so the shop is identifiable from across the room. The entrance should be a **large clear glass door**, flanked by full-height display windows that spill interior light onto the pavement. Finish the frontage with a vending machine, a promo A-frame board and a couple of parked bicycles, and the street-corner feeling arrives immediately.

Don't be timid with the palette. A blue sign against a yellow door frame and white walls, outlined with black window mullions, stays legible without turning messy. Then run a ring of small light bricks along the bottom edge of the sign — that strip of glow is the single detail that sells the late-night convenience store mood.

## 2. Shelving: Details Create the Urge to Browse

Shelves are the heart of the shop. Build the frames from **grey or white plates**, then space the shelf levels so small packaged pieces can stand in as stock: bread, instant noodles, drinks, snacks. Break up the colours deliberately — red crisps, green drinks, yellow bread in a row — because that visual noise is exactly what makes a shopper want to walk in. Leave a walkway between shelving units wide enough for **two minifigures standing side by side**.

Three display rules do most of the work:

- **Layer by height.** Bulky drinks go on the lower shelves, small snacks on the upper ones.
- **Face the aisle.** Every package front points outward, no exceptions.
- **Leave a restocking gap.** Put a box of unopened stock in one corner, add a small hand cart, and pose a clerk minifigure crouching to fill a shelf. The scene instantly has a story.

For the chilled section, use transparent door pieces to build the drinks fridge and pack the interior with white and blue small elements — the cold-air read is immediate.

## 3. Checkout and Bar Seating: The End of the Path

The checkout counter usually sits directly opposite the door, closing the customer route. Build the top from **dark plates** and dress it with a cash register, a barcode scanner and a small coffee machine. Behind it, fit a row of shelving for cigarettes and magazines, and add a hot-food oden cabinet at the side with orange elements standing in for simmering broth. Lay a small floor mat in front of the counter and place queuing minifigures with products in hand; the circulation path now feels complete.

Along the window, add a **window bar counter** with tall stools and a heating station so customers can eat oden or drink hot coffee standing up. Hang a glowing menu light box above it — under warm yellow light, a minifigure who has just finished a late shift sits down and the whole shop gains warmth. Don't forget a microwave in the corner and two hand-wash sinks; the more the everyday logic holds together, the longer people will look at the build.

## 4. Night Lighting and Finishing Touches

The soul of a **convenience store MOC** is the late hour. Fit light bricks into the exterior walls and sign, set the interior pendants to warm yellow, and let the fridge emit cool white. Contrast the two colour temperatures and the warm reflection in the glass windows becomes the best part of the model. A street lamp outside the door, a cat curled up on the pavement, a single drink can beside the bin — these scattered small pieces tell the two-in-the-morning story better than anything larger.

Build in this order: base and floor, storefront, shelving, checkout counter, and lighting last so you can balance every source at once. Stock up on transparent window and door pieces, high-saturation small packaging elements, light bricks and warm-yellow light elements, and sort them into boxes by colour before you start.
