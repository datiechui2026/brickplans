# Brick Parts Classification: A Beginner's Identification Guide

If you have just started building MOCs, a pile of colorful parts can be intimidating. What is this piece called? What category does it belong to? How do I find the one I need without digging through the whole bin? This guide walks through the brick parts classification system so you can go from not knowing the names of pieces to recognizing them on sight.

## The Basic Classification System for Brick Parts

There are thousands of individual parts, but by function and shape they fall into a handful of families.

### 1. Basic Bricks

Basic bricks are the foundation of the brick world and the most common type by far. They are regular rectangular blocks, sized like this:

- **1x1 brick**: the smallest square brick, usually used to fill gaps and add detail
- **1x2, 1x3, 1x4 bricks**: long, narrow basics that form the main body of a structure
- **2x2, 2x4 bricks**: the most-used basics, with strong load-bearing ability
- **2x6, 2x8 bricks**: large basics for big structures

Basic bricks come in standard height, which is three times the height of a plate, and in thin plate form. The two can be combined to fine-tune height.

### 2. Plates

Plates are flat parts one third the height of a standard brick, and they are the key to precise building. Common plates include:

- **1x1 plate**: the smallest flat part, used for micro-adjustments
- **1x2, 1x4, 1x6 plates**: long, thin plates
- **2x2, 2x4, 2x6 plates**: larger plates for bases and topping off a build
- **4x4, 6x6 plates**: big plates, often used as a base

### 3. Slope Bricks

Slopes are the parts that turn a blocky model into a streamlined one. At least one face is angled:

- **Slope**: one angled face and one vertical face, used for roofs, car noses and similar shapes
- **Inverted slope**: a slope with the angled face pointing down, common on vehicle undersides and wings
- **Curved slope**: a part with a curved surface, used for rounded shapes

### 4. Special Parts

Beyond the basics above, there are parts with specialized functions:

- **Connectors**: join parts at different angles and directions
- **Technic parts**: bricks and beams with holes, used for mechanical structures
- **Tiles**: smooth plates without studs, used for floors and tabletops
- **Transparent parts**: clear bricks for windows and windshields
- **Minifig accessories**: hand-held items, helmets, hair pieces and so on

## How to Identify Brick Parts Quickly

### Check the color

Color coding is an important clue. Brands name colors differently, but the main colors (red, yellow, blue, green, white, black, gray) are essentially consistent across brands.

### Check the size

Brick part sizes are given in studs. A "2x4 brick", for example, is two studs wide and four studs long. Once you know this convention, a classification chart lets you locate a part fast.

### Use the parts list in the instructions

Most instructions downloaded on [brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn) include a detailed parts list noting the name, color and quantity of each part. Sorting your parts against that list is the fastest way to learn.

## Why Parts Classification Matters

Knowing the classification system helps your MOC building in four ways:

1. **Faster building**: when you know the categories, finding a part is no longer a search for a needle in a haystack
2. **Better understanding of design**: you can see why the original builder chose a particular part, which improves your own design skills
3. **Smarter parts buying**: you know which types you need and avoid wrong or duplicate purchases
4. **Easier sorting**: storing parts by category makes inventory management clear at a glance

## Building Tips: Using Parts Classification to Improve Your MOCs

### Tip 1: Use plates for layering

When building architectural MOCs, plates create height differences that make a facade richer. Using 1x2 plates to recess or project windows, for instance, gives more depth than using bricks directly.

### Tip 2: Slopes solve shaping problems

On vehicle MOCs, the streamlined nose and tail are the hard part. Using slopes and curved slopes properly makes the vehicle look far more realistic.

### Tip 3: Technic parts for moving mechanisms

If you want a model to actually move, you need Technic parts such as beams, axles and gears to build the mechanism. Searching the brickplan.cn "Technic" tag will turn up many instructions that include working mechanisms.

## FAQ

**Q: What are the major categories of brick parts?**
A: By function and shape there are four: basic bricks (the most numerous, forming the base of a build), plates (one third the height of a brick, used for fine layering), slopes (which turn blocky models into streamlined ones), and special parts (connectors, Technic parts and so on).

**Q: How do I quickly identify a brick part?**
A: Three clues. Color: red, yellow, blue, green, white, black and gray are basically consistent across brands. Size: measured in studs, so a 2x4 brick is two studs wide and four long. And the parts list in the instructions, which gives name, color and quantity.

**Q: What is the difference between a plate and a brick?**
A: A plate is one third the height of a standard brick, so one brick equals three plates. The two can be combined for precise height adjustment. Plates are the key to structural detail and layering, and they appear very frequently in instructions.

**Q: What are the benefits of learning parts classification?**
A: Three main ones. Finding parts while building stops being a hunt, so efficiency goes up noticeably. You can understand why a builder used a given part, which improves your own design. And you can buy parts more efficiently, knowing what to stock up on and what to buy only as needed.
