# Dump Truck MOC Review: Linear Actuator Lift and Bed Build

Bottom line first: this dump truck plan is worth picking up if you are past the beginner stage. It uses two parallel linear actuators, lifts the bed to roughly 55 degrees, runs about 1,200 parts, and sits at the upper end of medium difficulty — the main body takes most builders 6–8 hours.

This review covers a brick dump truck MOC modeled on a heavy mining dump truck. The designer works at roughly 1:12 scale and captures the silhouette of a classic mining hauler, and the overall level of completion is high enough that it stands out among recent engineering-vehicle designs.

## Fidelity to the Real Truck

The cab is built from multiple slope bricks to create the forward-leaning operator cabin, backed up by chunky wheel arches and a high-mounted air intake — the character of the original comes through clearly. The bed uses a three-segment broken-line profile that stays close to the proportions of the real machine, and a yellow-and-black color scheme is recommended for a hard, industrial look. On details, the cab doors open, and both the mirrors and the boarding ladder are present. Within its price bracket, the accuracy is above average.

## The Hydraulic Lifting Mechanism

This is the highlight of the build. The designer drives the bed with two Technic linear actuators working in parallel rather than the more common worm gear plus rack setup. The gear train takes power from the middle of the frame and sends it through universal joints to the actuators on each side, and the two sides move in good sync. Measured lift angle is about 55 degrees, close to the maximum tilt of the real truck. The gear ratio is tuned sensibly, with no tooth skipping or binding through the full travel, and it still raises steadily with two battery boxes loaded on board — durability looks solid.

## Bed and Chassis

The bed connects to the frame through hinge pins at the rear axle, tilts smoothly, and returns under gravity against a limit stop — simple and reliable, with no extra power parts needed. The chassis uses the widened frame typical of engineering vehicles, with differentials in both front and rear axles; combined with large-diameter off-road tires, traction and ground clearance are excellent. Front-wheel steering is linked to the steering wheel and reaches about 40 degrees of travel. There is no suspension, but the structure is rigid enough that heavy pushing and pulling does not loosen it.

## Parts List and Build Difficulty

The plan comes with a complete parts list of roughly 1,200 pieces, bagged by step, and it flags easily confused parts — such as light gray versus dark gray Technic pins — so you can tell them apart. The part quantities check out accurately, with nothing missing or wrong. Difficulty is rated upper-end medium: the main structure takes about 6–8 hours, and the hard parts are meshing the gear train and installing the actuators. The instructions provide step-by-step close-up photos for these, so even a newcomer can follow them through, though the odds of having to disassemble and redo a section are a little higher than usual.

## Verdict

This brick dump truck MOC delivers strong results on both the hydraulic lift and the bed-tipping structure. Lift angle, drivetrain stability and parts-list quality all reach an excellent standard, and the difficulty is well judged for intermediate builders looking for a challenge.
