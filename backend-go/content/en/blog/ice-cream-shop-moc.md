# Brick Ice Cream Shop MOC: Facade, Counter and Display Case

The verdict up front: what makes a brick ice cream shop MOC actually look good comes down to three things — facade color, counter flow, and how densely you pack the dessert display. The full build runs about 2,800 pieces and roughly 3 hours of building time, which makes it the best-looking sign-off storefront in a street scene series. What follows walks you through the step-by-step instructions shared by brickplan, taking you from an empty baseplate to a small ice cream shop that drops straight onto a 32x32 baseplate.

**Step 1: Baseplate and shop frame.** Start with a light-colored 32x32 stud baseplate, and leave a 2x4 stud gap at the doorway for the entrance step. Build the walls in alternating off-white and cream pink, keep every window frame the same pale yellow, and run a ring of 1x1 curved bricks along the cornice as a wave trim. That combination is what makes the building read as a dessert shop at a glance. Build the walls in even horizontal courses rather than running each one to full height in a single pass — it keeps the window openings square and the pink and off-white bands lined up across all four sides.

**Step 2: Dessert display case and checkout counter.** The display case is made from transparent 2x2 round bricks stacked two layers high to form the glass cover. Inside, place colorful ice cream scoops built from 1x1 round plates, 5 scoops per layer, 3 layers in total. On the counter, set a 2x2 tile as the cash register, then build a cup holder and a straw tube from 1x1 bricks beside it. Keeping the case close to the register is what gives the interior a real serving flow instead of a display that just sits in a corner.

**Step 3: Details and lighting.** Build the sign from 6 red letter tiles spelling "ICE", and put a 2-brick-high palm tree at the entrance. If your budget stretches to 200 yuan, add one warm-white LED light set to wash the counter — the whole atmosphere jumps up a level immediately.

The finished shop covers roughly 25x25 cm. Working from brickplan.cn's step-by-step renders and parts list, even a first-time builder can get it right in one pass and slot it straight into a modular main street.
