# Best Chinese Brick Brands 2026: Top 10 Ranked

**If you only read one line: QiJi and Gobricks (Gao Zhuan) are the strongest choices for parts-driven MOC building, CaDA and Double Eagle own the Technic category, Pantasy leads on original design, and Wange is where to go when the budget is tight. Expect to pay roughly one third of the LEGO equivalent for the same part count — sometimes a fifth.** The ranking below weights part precision at 30%, clutch feel at 25%, original design at 25% and value at 20%, using build feedback from BrickPlan builders rather than marketing copy.

One caveat up front, so you can calibrate: these are relative scores within the category, not an absolute scale against LEGO. Chinese brands have closed most of the gap on display quality. They have not closed it on twenty-year play durability.

## How the scores work

| Criterion | Weight | What we actually measure |
|---|---|---|
| Part precision | 30% | Stud clutch consistency, colour matching between batches, moulding-mark cleanup |
| Clutch feel | 25% | Instruction clarity, step logic, how smoothly the build progresses |
| Original design | 25% | Own IP, engineering originality, how MOC-friendly the part library is |
| Value | 20% | Price per part against the quality delivered |

Each criterion is scored out of 10 and weighted. A brand with superb parts but almost no sets of its own cannot win the overall ranking — which is why Gobricks, arguably the best parts maker in the category, sits fourth rather than first.

## The top 10

### 1. Pantasy — 9.5/10
The design benchmark. Its licensed lines (*My Own Swordsman*, *Ali the Fox*) are the most distinctive sets in the category, and the styling holds up next to official premium ranges.

- Part precision **9.8** — smooth clutch, close to official LEGO
- Original design **9.9** — fully original geometry, not a recoloured copy
- Value **7.5** — the most expensive brand on this list, and you are paying for design work
- Watch for: limited part libraries for MOC builders, because the catalogue is set-driven

**Best for:** collectors and display builders.

### 2. CaDA / Double Eagle — 9.3/10
The Technic specialists. Gears, liftarms, differentials and linear actuators are consistently accurate, which is why Technic MOC builders default to them. Powered models, cranes and functional vehicles are where this pair wins.

- Part precision **9.5** — the best mechanical parts outside LEGO
- Original design **9.0** — strong engineering-vehicle lines
- Value **9.0** — around one third of LEGO for a comparable part count
- Watch for: quality variance between ranges; the flagship Technic lines are noticeably better than the entry sets

**Best for:** mechanical builds and functional vehicles.

### 3. QiJi — 9.2/10
The MOC builder's default. A wide catalogue with stable quality, and many published plans are written around QiJi part availability.

- Part precision **9.3** — medium clutch, neither loose nor tight
- Original design **8.5** — solid rather than remarkable
- Value **9.5** — the most competitive per-part pricing at the top of the table
- Watch for: colour ranges change; verify availability before designing around a rare colour

**Best for:** parts buyers and anyone following a parts list.

### 4. Gobricks (Gao Zhuan) — 9.1/10
Gobricks sells parts more than sets, and its moulding quality is the closest thing to LEGO in the category. Designers recommend it specifically for complex plans where one sloppy mould ruins a tight sub-assembly.

- Part precision **9.7** — genuinely comparable with official parts
- Original design **7.0** — very few sets of its own
- Value **8.5** — higher per part, justified by consistency
- Watch for: buying large quantities piece by piece takes planning; start from a proper parts list

**Best for:** builders who value precision above price.

### 5. Sembo — 9.0/10
The military and aerospace specialist: tanks, armoured vehicles and warships with genuinely detailed surface work, plus unusual colour ranges that LEGO does not produce.

- Part precision **8.8** — good, occasionally softer on large plates
- Original design **9.0** — strong theming and consistent scale
- Value **9.3** — priced for volume buying
- Watch for: theme concentration. If you do not build military subjects, the catalogue is narrow

### 6. Panlos — 8.8/10
Strong on modular architecture and street scenes; its city ranges fit the modular MOC format closely, with floor plates and wall panels that interoperate cleanly with third-party part libraries.

### 7. Yuxing (Mould King) — 8.7/10
Long experience with powered and programmable building. If you want motors, battery boxes, receivers and remote control inside one system, this is the most complete non-LEGO ecosystem.

### 8. Wange — 8.5/10
The value option. A wide catalogue at low prices; precision trails QiJi and Gobricks, and it is entirely good enough for practice builds, scenery and bulk filler.

### 9. Lepin — 8.3/10
Best known for replicating retired LEGO sets. Worth stating plainly: copies of sets still in production remain legally contested, and quality varies sharply between batches, so treat it as a category of its own rather than a general recommendation.

### 10. Sluban — 8.0/10
A good entry brand for children and first-time builders, with simple creative ranges and generous part counts at low prices.

## Price benchmarks: what the difference actually looks like

Typical 2026 street prices in USD for comparable part counts. Figures are ranges because licensed themes and flagship engineering sets break the pattern upward.

| Build size | Part count | LEGO equivalent | Chinese brand equivalent | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small display model | 500 | $50 – 70 | $18 – 26 | ~1:3 |
| Mid-size vehicle | 1,200 | $120 – 170 | $40 – 60 | ~1:3 |
| Technic supercar | 2,500 | $350 – 450 | $110 – 160 | ~1:3 |
| Modular building | 2,000 | $180 – 250 | $60 – 90 | ~1:3 |
| Bulk parts (per 1,000 pieces) | 1,000 | $90 – 140 | $30 – 55 | ~1:3 to 1:5 |

Two conclusions follow. The ratio is remarkably stable at about one third, and the extreme "one fifth" claims you see online come from bulk parts buying rather than from boxed sets.

## Compatibility, clutch and colour: the trade-offs

Chinese brands that build to the standard stud geometry click together with LEGO, and mixing brands inside one model is normal. Three real caveats:

1. **Clutch variance.** Clutch force differs between makers by a small but perceptible margin. A mixed-brand wall can feel inconsistent — some joints tight, some loose — even though every part measures correctly. Keep each structural sub-assembly to one brand and the problem disappears.
2. **Colour matching.** Colour between brands is close but not identical, and the same brand can shift between production batches. This is invisible in scenery and very visible on a flat front face. Buy one batch for any colour-critical area.
3. **Long-term durability.** For display builds the gap to LEGO is small. For models rebuilt dozens of times, official parts hold their clutch noticeably longer.

## Three things the scores do not capture

**Batch variance.** A brand's score is an average. Two copies of the same set, bought a month apart, can differ in clutch and colour, because moulds wear and colour master batches change. If a specific colour matters to your design, buy all of it in one order.

**Instruction quality varies more than part quality.** Most brands' parts are better than their booklets. A large set with a poorly sequenced manual will feel worse to build than a smaller set with a clear one, even though the part scores say the opposite.

**After-sales support is uneven.** Some brands ship missing parts free with a photo of the inventory page; marketplace resellers of the same brand often do not. Check the seller's policy before ordering anything large, and keep the inventory page until the build is finished.

## A buyer's checklist before you commit

1. **Check the part count, not the box size.** Photos are shot to flatter. Price per part is the number that matters.
2. **Check whether the set is an original design or a copy.** Originals are safe to buy and support the category; copies of in-production sets carry legal and quality risk.
3. **Check the parts list for rare colours.** A set that depends on a discontinued colour is hard to complete if a bag arrives short.
4. **Check the spare-parts and missing-parts policy.** Reputable brands ship replacements; marketplace resellers often do not.
5. **Check the instruction quality.** Search for a review that mentions the instruction booklet. A great part inventory with a bad booklet is a frustrating build.

## FAQ

**Q:** Are Chinese brick brands compatible with LEGO?
**A:** Yes, if the brand uses standard stud geometry, which most do. Parts click together and mixing brands in one build is normal, though clutch force and colour may differ slightly between makers.

**Q:** Which Chinese brand is closest to LEGO quality?
**A:** Gobricks for parts precision and Pantasy for finished-set quality. Neither matches LEGO's long-term play durability exactly, but for display builds the gap is small.

**Q:** Is a Chinese set really one third of the price?
**A:** For comparable part counts, one third is typical and large sets reach one fifth. Licensed IP and premium design lines narrow that gap significantly.
