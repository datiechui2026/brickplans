# Huile City Series Review: Street Scene Sets Tested

Verdict first: Huile's city line is solidly made, leans heavily on printed parts, and delivers real value at the hundred-yuan price point. It suits beginners starting out and anyone who wants to grow a street layout on a budget.

This review covers Huile's city and street scene series. Huile sits in the mass market of Chinese brick brands, and its theme coverage is broad — the city line especially, running from convenience stores and coffee shops to police stations and fire stations, with prices that stay in the affordable range around 100 yuan. For this test I picked a mid-size street scene set with a signboard and worked through it step by step, from box to finished model, to see whether it earns a place on your shelf.

## Packaging and Bag Numbering

Start with the box. The colour box for Huile street scene sets is cleanly printed, with the finished model photo and set numbering complete on the front, in a plain and practical style. Inside, parts are split into several numbered bags by build step, each clearly labelled and matching the instructions one to one. That matters a lot for street scene sets, which routinely run past a thousand pieces — you are not hunting across the whole table for a part.

The instructions are full colour with large, clear step diagrams. Where a step is easy to get wrong, an enlarged detail view is added, so even a first-time builder can follow along without trouble.

## Part Precision and Feel

Parts are the core of any review. The first impression after opening the bags is even colour: same-colour parts show almost no shade variation, so walls built from colour blocks look clean on the finished street. Sprue marks are mostly tucked onto interior connection faces, and visible surface parts are finished neatly.

Clutch power sits on the tight side. Parts click into place with a clear, positive seating feel, and the structure ends up stable. A few small parts are tight enough that your fingers ache a little during pressing, but the payoff is a model that does not loosen up. Transparent window parts have good light transmission and come out clearer than those of quite a few brands at the same price.

## Instructions and Build Experience

Instruction quality is upper-middle for a Chinese brand: sensible step breakdown, and for symmetrical structures the part orientation is called out. This street scene took me about two and a half hours to finish, and the process ran smoothly with almost no time lost hunting for parts.

The one thing to watch: plates of the same colour but different sizes are easy to mix up. Sort the parts inside each numbered bag before you start and you will save yourself a fair amount of effort.

## Finished Model Accuracy and Value

The finished model beat my expectations. The street scene set carries its signage, shop windows, eaves and balconies, and a high proportion of the parts are printed. Signs are mostly printed rather than stickered, which noticeably improves how the model holds up over time.

Against Chinese bricks at the same price, Huile's city line offers generous part counts and complete design, making value for money genuinely strong — especially for builders who want a full street block without paying a brand premium.

My conclusion after this test: the city line is well built with a wide spread of themes, and it works for beginners, for parents building alongside kids, and for MOC builders who want to expand a street layout cheaply.
