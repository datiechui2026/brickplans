# MOC Sharing Guide: Platforms, Communities and Feedback

Finishing a MOC (My Own Creation) is one thing; the part everyone looks forward to next is putting it in front of other builders. Sharing a brick MOC is both the full stop on a build and the starting point for the next round of ideas. This review looks at the three dimensions that matter — platforms, community and technique — and how to show a build well and get a worthwhile conversation out of it.

## 1. A side-by-side review of the main sharing platforms

MOC sharing channels currently fall into four broad types, each with its own trade-offs:

- **All-in-one platforms**: sites like Rebrickable and BrickLink, where instructions and parts lists live together. The data is well structured and ideal for builders who want a complete, reproducible copy of a model, but the interface is entirely in English and the entry barrier is a little high for newcomers.
- **Content platforms**: brick creators on Bilibili, Xiaohongshu and Douyin show the building process directly through video and photo posts. The interaction is lively and the format is well suited to attracting new viewers and casual onlookers.
- **Domestic communities and chat groups**: Tieba, QQ groups and forums offer friendly, informal exchange and plenty of Chinese-language material, but instruction quality varies wildly and you have to screen it yourself.
- **Dedicated instruction sites**: these focus purely on publishing and managing instructions, with step-by-step notes and parts lists attached. The experience is clean and search efficiency is the highest of the four.

Scored on **ease of use, breadth of audience and depth of content**, dedicated instruction sites win on findability while content platforms win on reach. New builders are best off combining the two rather than picking one.

## 2. What community atmosphere and works exchange actually feel like

After reviewing a number of communities, the thing that decides whether exchange feels good is not user numbers — it is **the granularity of the feedback**. Good communities encourage a habit of "leave a comment when you have built it, ask when something is unclear," and comment sections often have the original builder replying with construction details. That kind of back-and-forth depth does far more for your growth than a pile of likes.

When choosing a community, check three things: whether it supports subscribing to individual authors, whether it has favourites and collection features, and whether the comment sections are active. A community with all three rarely leaves a shared build sitting in silence.

## 3. Four practical points for sharing a build

If you want your brick MOC sharing to land better, these four areas are worth polishing:

- **Photograph the finished model from multiple angles** — close-ups and a scene shot if possible.
- **Make the instructions complete** — step-by-step diagrams plus a parts list (BOM); neither is optional.
- **Explain the inspiration in your intro** — one or two sentences on the design thinking behind the build.
- **Keep maintaining the post after publishing** — reply to comments promptly and fix small errors in the instructions.
