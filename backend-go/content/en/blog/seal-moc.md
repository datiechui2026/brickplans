# Brick Seal MOC Review: Round Body, Movable Flippers, 356 Parts

The short version: this brick seal MOC takes the idea of "rounded" about as far as it can go. The body curvature lands at roughly 92% fidelity to the real animal, the flippers use hinge pieces so they actually move, and the whole thing is 356 parts and about two hours of building. The cuteness is turned all the way up, and it earns a recommendation.

## A Body That Actually Looks Round

The biggest risk with a brick seal is ending up with a cylinder. This design avoids that by offsetting curved slopes against wedge bricks, stacking them slightly out of line so the arched back and the curved belly both read clearly. From the side the silhouette flows in a smooth S-curve with no obvious hard corners, and the finished model feels rounded and smooth in the hand rather than blocky.

## How the Flippers Are Handled

The front flippers connect through hinge pieces and swing up and down through about 30 degrees, which is enough to hold two distinct poses: a "clapping" position and a flat, sprawled-out rest. The rear flippers finish with a double layer of plates to mimic the trailing shape a seal's hind flippers make. The detail is well executed and is the highlight of the whole design.

## Selling the Cute Expression

A large printed eye piece paired with a mouth that turns up slightly at the corners gives the model an innocent face straight-on. The head-to-body ratio is deliberately set at 2:3, enlarging the head to reinforce the pup-like look. A dark gray round plate caps the nose, contrasting with the white body and pushing the cute factor further.

## Build Experience

The set is split across 4 numbered bags, the instructions are clearly labeled, and parts are easy to find as you go. The build takes about two hours to finish, and the completed model measures roughly 15cm, which sits nicely on a desk or bookshelf as a small mood-lifting piece.

## FAQ

**Q: How do you build this brick seal MOC?**
The instructions are available on brickplan.cn. Work through the 4 numbered bags in order, and build the main body first before attaching the front flippers and the head. Line the hinge pieces up with their slots and the whole thing takes about two hours.

**Q: Is this brick seal MOC worth buying?**
Yes. At 356 parts and roughly two hours of building, the rounded body and movable flippers make the finished model stand out. It costs around 130 yuan, and the cute shape works well either as a gift or as a keeper.

**Q: How is this different from an ordinary brick seal?**
Most seals on the market are simple blocky shapes. This MOC uses curved slopes to reproduce the body curve, adds movable front flippers and a cute expression, and reaches a noticeably higher level of finish. It suits intermediate builders and collectors.

**Q: Can it hold different poses?**
Yes. The front flipper hinges allow about 30 degrees of movement, and the head can rotate, so you can set it sprawled out, looking up, or flipper-slapping. That gives it more play value than a fixed-pose model.

**Q: What do the parts cost?**
Loose parts come to roughly 100-130 yuan. The build is mostly white curved slopes and uses common pieces throughout, with no rare parts, so replacements are easy to source. If the budget is tight, you can buy it in two batches.
