# Stilt House MOC Tutorial: Build a Xiangxi Village Home

The short version first: about 1,300 parts and a 350 yuan budget are enough to recreate a Xiangxi stilt house roughly 25 cm tall, and the three-layer structure — elevated pillars, timber walkway, flying-eave roof — is explained in one pass below. A beginner can finish it in around 5 hours. The tutorial runs through four steps: pillars, walkway, railings, roof.

## Step 1: Build the Elevated Pillars and Foundation

The soul of a stilt house is its "legs" — the pillars that lift the building off the ground. Start with a 4x4 baseplate as the foundation, then set 6 pillars along the front and 4 along the back. Every pillar is stacked from 2x2 round bricks: the front row goes 12 bricks high, the back row 8, which produces the front-high, back-low stance of a building set on a slope.

At the foot of each pillar, put down a 2x2 plate plus four 1x1 round plates to form a column base. It looks better and it spreads the load. Once the pillars are up, run a load test: press the top face gently with your palm, and the row only passes if nothing wobbles. This is the one step in the whole build you cannot skip.

## Step 2: Lay the Timber Walkway and Floor Plates

The walkway is the part of a stilt house that photographs best. Across the tops of the pillars, lay 2x6 brown plates to form the floor, with the front projecting out by 2 studs so the walkway cantilevers — this mimics the "half suspended" structure of Xiangxi houses. Lock the plates to each other with 1x2 connectors so the walls you add later cannot collapse them.

Leave one 2x2 opening at the corner of the walkway; that is where the stairs will go. The whole floor layer uses roughly 150 brown parts, the largest share of any single colour in the model. Dark brown reads as aged timber better than light brown does.

## Step 3: Install the Walkway Railings

Keep the railing a consistent 4 bricks tall. The posts are 1x1 brown bricks, one every 2 studs, joined along the top with a 1x2 bar. Add a 1x1 round corner plate at each turn of the railing: it stops minifigures from "falling off" and keeps the corners from scraping your hand while you handle the model.

If you want more of a Xiangxi feel, slot a 1x1 white piece in every third post to suggest white cloth hung out to dry on the wooden railing — it looks especially good in photos. After the railing is complete, press along it section by section and reinforce any loose bar with an extra connector.

## Step 4: Build the Flying-Eave Roof in Layers

The roof comes in two tiers: the main sloped roof first, then a smaller upper flying eave. Lay 2x4 slope bricks as tiles running from the ridge down to both sides, offsetting each row by half a stud so the tiles overlap the way real ones do. Close the ridge with 1x2 curved slopes, with both ends tilted slightly upward to create the flying-eave silhouette.

The lower roof takes 6 rows of tiles and the upper roof 4 rows, roughly 300 dark grey tile parts in total. Finish by dotting 1x1 gold pieces at both ends of the ridge, then add a 2x2 red plaque piece above the main door — the Chinese look lands immediately.

## FAQ

**Q: Can the pillars really carry the weight?**

A: Yes. A pillar stacked from 2x2 round bricks holds roughly the weight of 20 standard bricks, so 10 pillars for the whole building is more than enough. The key is the column base: it must have the four 1x1 round plates, or the pillar will lean under load.

**Q: How long does 1,300 parts take to build?**

A: A beginner usually needs 4.5 to 6 hours. The roof tiles eat the most time — laying them alone takes over an hour. Sort the parts into 4 bags matching the 4 steps, and you can work about 30% faster.

**Q: Is a 350 yuan budget enough for the whole building?**

A: Yes. BrickPlan loose parts average around 0.27 yuan per piece, so 1,300 parts comes to about 350 yuan, of which the dark grey tiles account for around 80 yuan. To save money, drop the upper flying eave — that removes roughly 100 parts and 25 yuan.

**Q: Can I adapt it into another ethnic style of stilt house?**

A: Yes. Swap the tiles for blue-green and the railings for red parts and you have a Miao village look; remove the flying eaves and raise the pillars and it becomes a Dai bamboo house. Colour scheme and pillar height are the main variables — the structural steps carry over unchanged.
