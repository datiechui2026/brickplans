# Brick Aquarium MOC Guide: Blueprints, Parts and Build Order

The short version: build a brick aquarium MOC in four passes — cabinet, tank, interior scenery, lid. Assemble the tank walls from 1x2 and 1x4 transparent plates, add 2x2 corner plates at all four corners to stop the walls from collapsing, and install support columns anywhere the bottom of the tank spans more than 16 studs unsupported.

An aquarium is one of the most photogenic subjects in the ocean-themed brick world. Most builders get hooked the first time they see someone's finished tank: that clear body, the layered scenery behind it. This guide walks the whole path — blueprint, parts, assembly, staging — for newcomers to ocean MOCs and for more advanced builders who are still tuning their tank walls.

## Choosing the Right Blueprint

When you pick an aquarium MOC blueprint, check three things first: the proportions of the tank body, how heavily the design depends on transparent parts, and how rare the required parts are. A good blueprint splits the aquarium into three layers — base cabinet, tank body, scenery — so you can build it module by module. Favor designs that ship with a parts list and a step-by-step breakdown; otherwise you only find out you're missing a critical piece after the order arrives. On https://brickplan.cn you can filter by ocean theme and read other builders' notes on the structure, especially feedback about transparent wall panel thickness and stability — that directly determines how the finished model looks.

## Parts List and Using Transparent Elements

Transparent parts are the soul of an aquarium. For the tank walls, use 1x2 and 1x4 transparent plates or transparent window panels; they transmit light better than standard glass-style parts and are much easier to reorder. Build the water effect by layering transparent blue and transparent light blue: dark blue at the bottom to suggest deep water, light blue plus white above for the foam and wave layers. Corals and anemones come from curved slopes combined with plant leaf elements, while fish schools are dotted in with small moulded pieces. The base cabinet eats a lot of bricks, so use large plates as the foundation wherever you can — it noticeably cuts both the part count and the cost.

## Build Order and Structural Tips

The recommended order is cabinet, tank, interior scenery, lid. Reinforce the cabinet generously: the taller the tank, the greater the sideways pressure, so stack reinforced columns into all four corners. For the tank body, enclose first and fill later — build the three layers of transparent wall, then fill the interior level by level. Work the scenery from back to front: tall coral skeletons at the rear, anemones and caves in the midground, an open swimming area left clear at the front. When you close the top, run a ring of lid plates around the rim; it locks the tank in place and leaves an interface for future modifications.

## Color Scheme and Scene Styling

Follow one rule on color: cool water, warm accents. Keep the water in the blue family, then let corals and anemones jump out in orange-red, pink and purple to create a focal point. For staging, add a small diver minifigure, a shipwreck hull or a treasure chest as a story thread, so the aquarium is more than a static display. Lighting is the biggest bonus point: hide an LED strip above or behind the tank and use warm white to imitate natural light — the transparency of the clear parts immediately reads far stronger.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I choose a brick aquarium MOC blueprint?**
A: Check three things first: tank proportions, dependence on transparent parts, and part rarity. A good blueprint splits the aquarium into cabinet, tank and scenery layers for modular building, and includes a parts list with a step breakdown.

**Q: What do I do when the tank walls collapse?**
A: It's usually missing connectors at the corners. Add 2x2 corner plates to all four corners, offset the wall plates layer by layer, and add a reinforcement ring in the middle as the tank gets taller; any unsupported span over 16 studs at the bottom needs a support column.

**Q: The light looks uneven and the tank seems foggy — why?**
A: It's often caused by mixing transparent parts with ordinary bricks. Keep each wall face in a single material and stack it from 1x2 and 1x4 transparent plates layer by layer; light the tank from above or behind and the clarity improves noticeably.

**Q: Which layer of the aquarium scenery should I build first?**
A: Work from back to front: tall coral skeletons at the rear, anemones and caves in the midground, and an open area left clear at the front for fish to swim through. Express the water with layered transparent blue and light blue, then add a shipwreck or treasure chest as an accent.
