# Brick Teahouse MOC Review: 3,200-Piece Chinese Street Scene

The short version: it is worth buying. This teahouse MOC rebuilds a two-storey Qing dynasty tea house from roughly 3,200 pieces for 268 yuan, and the interior is the star of the show — close to a must-have for anyone collecting Chinese-style street scenes. The weak spot is the roof: tiling is tedious and the whole build takes a long sitting.

## 1. Parts Accuracy: 9/10

The design is unusually careful about what a Chinese tea house actually looks like. The upturned flying eaves, the carved window lattice and the plaque over the entrance each get dedicated elements rather than being faked with generic slopes, and the palette leans on vermilion red and natural wood tones, so the period atmosphere lands immediately. On the second-floor balcony, 1x1 round bricks stand in for timber posts and read convincingly as wooden columns at minifigure scale.

About 92% of the parts list is standard bricks, with trans-clear elements used for the window panes. That ratio matters: it keeps the model looking like a brick build rather than a pile of specialty parts, and it means most of the inventory is already in a typical builder's drawers. Among comparable Chinese street scene instructions, this sits near the top for fidelity.

## 2. Build Experience: 7.5/10

Plan for roughly 8 hours of building across 6 numbered step bags. The ground floor moves fast and is genuinely enjoyable — walls, counter and seating go up quickly and the scene starts to read early. Then you hit the roof, where about 480 tiles have to be stacked one at a time, and the process does start to drag.

The instructions include top-down views to help you place things, but tile angles still need small adjustments by eye, so budget some patience rather than assuming the manual solves everything. On the plus side, there were no clutch problems anywhere in the build, and parts separate cleanly when you need to undo a step.

## 3. Interior Details and Value: 9/10

The ground-floor seating area comes with a teapot, teacups, a dim sum steamer and a counter; upstairs there is a private room with a small tea table. For the price bracket, the density of interior detail is among the best you will find in a street scene MOC — this is a model built to be looked into, not just looked at.

At 3,200 pieces for 268 yuan, the maths works out to about 0.084 yuan per brick, which is solid value. The finished model measures 32 cm wide and 26 cm tall, a comfortable size to display side by side with other Chinese-style street scenes without crowding the shelf.

## FAQ

**What is the best way to build the teahouse MOC without mistakes?**

Work through the numbered step bags in order and pay particular attention to the roof tile angles. Lay down light-coloured pieces first to establish the line, then stack row by row. Each step in the manual stays under 25 parts, and if a view is ambiguous you can check it against the top-down overview — in practice you should not need to redo much.

**Can the teahouse MOC take minifigures?**

Yes. The design reserves 3 standard minifigure positions: one behind the ground-floor counter, one in the upstairs private room and one at the doorway, all compatible with LEGO minifigures. It also includes 8 small accessories such as the teapot and teacups, which are there for posing figures and adding story to the street scene.

**Is this teahouse MOC worth the money?**

Yes. You get 3,200 pieces, a 268 yuan price and about 8 hours of building, with the interior as the main selling point — a good fit for builders who like Chinese street scenes and staged photography. If interiors do not interest you and you only care about the exterior, it is reasonable to wait for a discount.

**How is this different from a regular street scene set?**

Most regular street scene sets are Western-style buildings with simplified interiors. This MOC is built around a Chinese tea house and detailed interiors — tea ware, a steamer, a private room, the lot. The trade-off is that you source the bricks yourself from a parts list, and the roof tiling is time-consuming, so newcomers should honestly assess their patience first.

**How much does the teahouse MOC cost in total?**

The instructions plus a parts pack come to about 268 yuan; the PDF instructions alone are around 25 yuan. If you build from loose parts you already own, you only need to buy roughly 100 yuan of scarce elements, keeping the total under 150 yuan — the exact figure depends on what is already in your brick stock.
