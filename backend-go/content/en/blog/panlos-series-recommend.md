# Panlos Series Guide: Which Set Should Beginners Buy?

Panlos has pushed its way into the value end of the brick market over the past couple of years, and the growing number of series makes a first purchase harder than it should be. If you are asking which Panlos series is the best one to start with, the answer depends on what you want from the build. The five main lines—dinosaur, street scene, military, mecha and architecture—sit in different places on part quality, value and build experience. Here is how they compare.

## Dinosaur Series: Impressive but Display-Oriented

The dinosaur line is Panlos's signature range. The T-Rex and Triceratops are large models with movable joints, and finished builds have real presence. The parts are mostly dark, textured pieces, and the build uses many similar pieces, which gives the models a strong mechanical feel. The downside is that most dinosaurs do not come with rich scenery, so they lean toward display. If you want a shelf centerpiece, the dinosaur series is the first place to look. If you like taking sets apart and modifying them often, it is not the best choice.

## Street Scene Series: The Value King

Street scene is Panlos's most consistent line in terms of reputation. A single set costing a few hundred RMB can produce a European-style building with an interior, three stories high and with a full open-and-close structure. The design is close to the LEGO street scene approach, and part precision and clutch power are in the top tier of domestic brands. Once built, several sets can be lined up to form a street. For beginners asking which Panlos series is good, street scene is close to a safe bet if the budget allows.

## Military Series: A Detail Lover's Favorite

The military series focuses on vehicles such as tanks and fighter jets. Camouflage and track parts are highly faithful, and some models include movable turrets and hatches. The hardcore part is strict plate alignment, which makes the build more difficult and better suited to experienced builders. If you like military subjects and enjoy a high-difficulty challenge, you can buy with confidence. Complete beginners should practice on something else first.

## Mecha and Architecture Series: Choose by Theme

The mecha series has good human proportions and strong articulation, making it a fit for robot fans, but some kits use a lot of stickers and are slightly less precise than street scene. The architecture series focuses on landmarks and ancient buildings, with rich Chinese-style details, and the finished models are made for a display cabinet. These two lines have clear audiences: if the subject is right for you, they are worth it.

## So How Should You Choose?

To sum up: across part quality, value and room for error, street scene is the best first choice for beginners. Go for dinosaur if you want visual impact, military if you want a hardcore build, and mecha or architecture if the theme matches your interests.
