# Your First MOC: Think It Through Before You Snap a Single Brick

Plenty of new brick builders hit the same wall: they see wildly creative MOCs online, feel inspired, open the parts bin — and freeze. There's no obvious place to start.

Good news: the first step of any new MOC is simpler than it looks. **Decide on a theme, plan the approach, and only then start building.** Get the direction right and everything after it falls into place.

## Step 1: Pick a Small, Specific Theme

The most common beginner mistake is aiming straight for a huge building or a complex mech. You run out of parts, the structure gets wobbly, and the project dies halfway through. Instead, choose something **small, concrete and genuinely appealing to you**:

- A little car, a corner house, a small animal, a tree, a street lamp;
- A single prop from a film, game or anime you like;
- Or a straight rebuild of your favourite small module from an official set.

The smaller the theme, the clearer the goal — and the easier it is to feel a sense of accomplishment within a few build sessions. A "retro coffee-brown delivery van" is far easier to get moving on than "a future city".

## Step 2: Collect References Instead of Guessing

The laziest-and-most-effective move in this first step is to **gather reference images first**. Browse BrickPlans at brickplan.cn, where you'll find a large library of free instructions, build write-ups and finished models — ideal hunting ground for beginners looking for ideas. Study how other builders handle the wheels, how they shape the front end, how they combine colours. Note down those small tricks and your own design will start pointing somewhere.

Using references isn't copying homework — it's learning to observe. How does the cargo bed attach to the cab? How does the roofline transition into the wall? The more you look, the more your understanding of what parts can do levels up.

## Step 3: Take Stock of the Parts You Already Own

Theme chosen, references gathered — now inventory your parts. One principle covers this whole step: **use what you have first, then fill the gaps.**

1. Roughly sort your parts by colour and by type.
2. Estimate how many basic bricks, plates and slope bricks you'll need.
3. Write down what's missing, but don't rush to buy — try substituting other parts instead. That kind of flexible swap is exactly where the fun of MOC lives.

If you genuinely need to restock, check the parts lists and buying guides on brickplan.cn and order everything in one go, so you don't end up disassembling and rebuilding over and over.

## Step 4: Start With the Chassis and Skeleton

Everything's ready — time to build. For the hands-on phase, remember one line: **skeleton first, decoration second.**

Take a small car as the example. Build the chassis and axles first and confirm the wheels roll smoothly. Then raise the main body frame and make sure it's structurally solid. Only after that do you add the roof, headlights and wing mirrors. If you decide to change the dimensions partway through, the cost of that change stays low. Ten extra minutes on the skeleton saves an hour later.

## Step 5: Photograph It and Share It

When the model is finished, don't forget to shoot it. Good light, a clean background and a decent angle are all your first MOC needs to look its best. Post it to the BrickPlans community at brickplan.cn and swap notes with other builders. What you get back isn't just corrections and suggestions — it's the momentum to keep building.

## Wrapping Up

The first step of a new MOC really comes down to four things: **pick a small theme, find references, sort your parts, build the skeleton.** Nail that step and your second and third models will go smoother every time. If you're about to start your own first MOC, open brickplan.cn now, look for inspiration, and take that first step.
