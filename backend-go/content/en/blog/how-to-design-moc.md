# How to Design Your Own LEGO MOC: A Complete Beginner Guide

## Introduction

Every brick builder has the same dream: making something that is entirely their own. That is the appeal of a MOC, short for My Own Creation, and it sits at the heart of brick culture. Yet plenty of newcomers who want to learn **how to design their own brick MOC** end up staring at a pile of parts with no idea where to begin. The truth is that MOC design is far less mysterious than it looks. Learn the right method and anyone can become a MOC designer.

## 1. The Core Ideas Behind MOC Design

### What Makes a Good MOC?

A strong MOC is more than bricks pushed together. It needs:

- **Structural stability** — the model survives handling and transport
- **Visual appeal** — balanced proportions and sensible color choices
- **Originality** — its own design language and a clear focal point
- **Reproducibility** — other builders can rebuild it from your instructions

On brickplan.cn you will find a large library of high-quality MOC instructions to study. Analyzing how those designs are put together is the fastest route to learning MOC design.

### Common Beginner Mistakes

New builders tackling **beginner MOC building** tend to fall into the same traps: starting with an oversized model, ignoring color harmony, and underestimating structural strength. Practicing at a small scale is far smarter than opening with a 1:8 supercar.

## 2. Finding Inspiration: Where to Start

### Observe the Real World

The classic source of MOC inspiration is the world around you. A car, a building, an aircraft — any of them can become your subject. Look closely at the silhouette, the proportions and the characteristic details, then express them abstractly in the language of bricks.

### Study Great Builds

Browse the MOC instructions on brickplan.cn and study how experienced builders handle corners, curved surfaces and small details. Borrowing is not copying: you learn a technique, then fold it into your own idea.

### Start With a Theme

Pick a theme you care about — military, sci-fi, modular street scenes or machinery. Tanks and fighter jets, the staples of **military brick building tutorials**, make excellent beginner subjects because their shapes are usually fairly regular and therefore easy to reproduce in bricks.

## 3. Planning Your Parts: Building the Foundation

### Take Stock of Your Collection

Before you design anything, know what you own. **Sorting brick parts** is the basic skill behind MOC design: store pieces by type — bricks, plates, slopes, specialized elements — and by color, so you can find what you need quickly while designing.

### Use Digital Design Tools

Bricklink Studio and LDD, short for LEGO Digital Designer, are the recommended tools for virtual building. They let you:

- Try ideas unlimited times without running out of parts
- Generate a parts list automatically
- Produce professional step-by-step building instructions

### Choosing Your Core Parts

- **Basic bricks**: 2x4 bricks and 2x2 bricks carry most of the structure
- **SNOT techniques**: mounting parts on the side of bricks opens up far more shaping options
- **Hinges**: essential for angled surfaces, curved forms and moving structures
- **Technic elements**: indispensable when you need mechanical function

## 4. Building Techniques: From Idea to Physical Model

### Set the Scale and Dimensions

Decide the final size of the model first, then plan the structure around it. For a 1:35 tank, for example, calculate how many studs correspond to the length, width and height of the hull before you build.

### Skeleton First, Appearance Later

Good MOC work is solid inside and beautiful outside. Build the internal frame from basic bricks first so the structure is stable, then add the exterior detail. It works like constructing a house: the frame comes before the walls.

### Color Principles

- Use no more than three main colors
- Build the base from neutral colors — black, white and gray
- Use bright colors as accents and detail
- Reference the color scheme of the real object

### Iterate Repeatedly

Very few MOCs succeed on the first attempt. Build, review, revise, rebuild — the loop may repeat many times. Publish your model on brickplan.cn and let other builders offer suggestions; it is one of the fastest ways to improve.

## 5. From Model to Instructions

### How to Make Building Instructions

Once your physical MOC is finished, you can turn it into instructions and share it with a wider audience. The Step by Step feature in Studio generates the building steps automatically. Then upload them to brickplan.cn so more brick fans can see your work.

### Sourcing Rare Parts

If your design calls for unusual elements, you can find them through:

- The loose-parts market of Chinese brick brands such as Gobricks and Double Eagle
- Online brick part stores
- Donor sets you already own

## 6. Paths for Leveling Up

### Specialized Skills

- **Architecture MOCs**: learn SNOT techniques and how to create wall texture
- **Vehicle MOCs**: master streamlined shaping and movable structures
- **Minifigure-scale MOCs**: dig into proportion and joint design

### Join the Community

The MOC community is very active. Join forums, WeChat groups or QQ groups and swap experience with other builders. Follow the MOC designers you admire on brickplan.cn and study how they approach a build.

## FAQ

### Do I need a digital design tool to build a MOC?

No. Studio and LDD simply make it cheaper and faster to test ideas, generate parts lists and produce instructions. Plenty of builders design entirely by hand with the parts they own.

### How many parts does a first MOC need?

There is no fixed number. Start with a subject small enough that you can finish it in a few sessions — a small vehicle, a section of a wall, a single piece of furniture — and grow from there.

### How do I make my MOC stable enough to handle?

Build the internal frame from basic bricks and plates first, tie the structure together with overlapping seams, and only then add the exterior details.
