# Coffee Shop Brick Sets: Beginner Buying and Building Guide

The short answer: a coffee shop is the ideal first subject for a street-scene build. Choose a set in the 600–1200 piece range, build it in four passes — storefront, counter, tables and chairs, then sidewalk seating — and you will be finished in 2–4 hours. Most of these sets cost ¥100–250, which makes them excellent value, and the finished building sits on a bookshelf without looking out of place.

## Step 1: Buy a Set or Source Your Own Instructions

### How to choose a ready-made set

Coffee shop sets usually run 600–1200 pieces and ship with minifigures and printed parts, so they suit beginners who want a finished model quickly. Check three things before you buy: whether the storefront sign is a printed element (sticker-based signs tend to curl at the edges over time), whether the counter is built as a separate module, and whether the set includes tables, chairs and a street lamp — these outdoor pieces are what give the shop its atmosphere.

### Sourcing parts yourself is cheaper

If you want to save money or you enjoy MOC building, download coffee shop instructions from brickplan and order the parts from the list yourself. Cost comes in around 30% below a finished set, and you are free to change the colour scheme or add a second-floor terrace, ending up with a one-off corner coffee shop that no one else owns.

## Step 2: Build the Storefront and Counter

### Signage and awning

Start with the ground-floor frontage. Build the awning from dark brown plates with white pieces as a trim edge, then fix the sign above the door lintel using printed elements. Put a clear window on each side of the door and place a dessert display stand behind the glass — passing minifigures will stop to look in. Assembling the awning and sign as one unit before attaching it to the façade keeps the plate lines straight and makes later adjustments much easier.

### The bar area

Run the counter in a straight line against the back wall: light-coloured tiles for the worktop, white plates on the front face. On top, add a coffee machine (dark body with silver nozzle pieces), a grinder, a sugar jar and a cream bottle — the more small accessories, the better it reads. Leave a 2x4 space in front of the counter for the barista minifigure; a printed apron is the detail that sells the scene.

## Step 3: Tables, Sidewalk Seating and Atmosphere

### Tables and chairs

Set a two-seat round table with backrest chairs outside the door, and put a "coffee plus cake" accessory set on top to bring the scene to life immediately. Add another table-and-chair pair plus a railing on a small second-floor terrace — the extra level gives the street scene depth and photographs much better. Keep the sidewalk furniture slightly forward of the door so the entrance stays readable from the front.

### Atmosphere details

Place a few potted plants on the window ledge, a pillar street lamp at the entrance, and hang a printed menu on the wall. Fit warm yellow light elements and the shop becomes genuinely inviting at night — the busiest corner on the whole street. It works just as well standing alone as it does slotted into a larger street scene, so you can build it now and expand around it later.

## FAQ

**How much does a coffee shop brick set cost?**
Most 600–1200 piece coffee shop sets sit at ¥100–250. Versions with light elements and several minifigures go above ¥200; if you only buy instructions and source the parts yourself, you can stay under ¥150.

**What is the difference between a coffee shop and a cafe brick set?**
They are the same street-scene subject under different names. Judge by piece count and interior detail: pick a smaller set around 600 pieces if you want a fast build, or 1000+ pieces if you want a richly detailed interior.

**Is a coffee shop brick set suitable for beginners?**
Very much so. The structure is simple and the interior detail is concentrated in one area, so it builds in 2–4 hours. It is the classic first street-scene set, and a good choice for a first shared build with children.

**Can a coffee shop set sit next to a dessert shop set?**
Yes. The two use similar colour palettes and are close in size, so together they form a complete retail street. Match baseplate dimensions and floor heights so the modules line up exactly and the rooflines stay level.
