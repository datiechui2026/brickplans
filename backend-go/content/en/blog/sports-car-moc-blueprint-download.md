# Sports Car MOC Blueprint Review: Sián, F40, 911 GT3 RS

Every brick builder has a racing dream somewhere in the back of their head. Whether it is a classic Lamborghini or a razor-edged Bugatti, there is a particular satisfaction in building your own supercar out of bricks that nothing else quite matches. Below is a deep look at three of the most popular **brick sports car blueprints** available right now, so you can pick the MOC that actually fits your bench, your budget and your skill level.

## Product Overview

This review focuses on three representative sports car MOC blueprints: the Lamborghini Sián FKP 37 in Technic style, the Ferrari F40 retro supercar, and the Porsche 911 GT3 RS. All three are extremely popular in the BrickPlans community, and together they span the range from entry level to advanced. Part counts run from around 800 pieces up to more than 2,500, so there is a match for different budgets and experience levels.

## Build Difficulty: ★★★☆☆

Sports car MOCs sit at a medium overall difficulty. The **Lamborghini Sián** blueprint is the most complex of the three, packed with gear trains and suspension geometry, and it demands careful step-by-step checking. The **Ferrari F40** is built mainly around exterior accuracy, with comparatively simple mechanical internals. The **Porsche 911 GT3 RS** lands somewhere between the two. Worth noting: a good **sports car blueprint** always ships with a detailed step breakdown, so even a newcomer can finish a sharp-looking car in 3–5 hours.

## Part Quality

For parts, we recommend high-precision bricks from **Double Eagle**, **Mould King** and **Gobricks**. Double Eagle's Technic components stand out for gear meshing and cross-axle tolerances, which makes them a good fit for a drivetrain-heavy chassis. Mould King's shock springs and tire assemblies feel solid in hand and reproduce the original shapes very closely. Gobricks' general bricks come with clean sprue marks and even colour, which makes them ideal for bodywork. All three are fully compatible with LEGO parts, and mixing them causes no problems at all.

## The Build Experience

The most surprising part of the actual build is how these blueprints recreate a car's **flowing body lines**. A well-made MOC blueprint uses wedge bricks, curved bricks and slope bricks to shape the aerodynamic silhouette instead of simply stacking plates. The Sián's rear wing and scissor doors are especially clever, using a handful of key joint pieces to make the functions movable. The F40's round taillights are built from transparent red bricks combined with tiles, and the result looks superb. On [BrickPlans](https://brickplan.cn), every blueprint comes with a full parts list and build notes, so you can check the tricky steps before you start.

## Finished Model

A completed car usually measures between 30 and 50 cm, which gives plenty of visual impact whether it sits on a desk or in a display cabinet. The Sián's green bodywork with gold wheels is remarkably faithful to the real car; the F40's classic red livery is pure nostalgia. Working features include steering wheel linkage to the front wheels, piston movement inside the V-shaped engine, and openable doors, so there is a lot to actually play with.
