# MEGA Bloks Review: Halo, Pokémon and What to Know

Short answer first: MEGA's clutch is tighter than LEGO's, the builds feel sturdier, the licensed themes are strong and the prices are friendly. It suits builders on a budget who care most about the IP.

## Who MEGA is: a veteran of the North American market

MEGA is a Canadian brand. It started out in preschool building blocks and later carved out its own space through heavy licensing. Where LEGO leans on homegrown IP, MEGA puts its weight behind film, TV and game rights, which gives it a distinct market position. For builders outside North America, licensing is the keyword you cannot avoid in any MEGA review — it almost never invents its own storylines, yet it keeps putting the themes players love most into brick form.

## The flagship licenses: Halo, Pokémon and Call of Duty

Mention MEGA to long-time builders and the first thing many think of is Halo. Those sharply angular Spartans, vehicles and dioramas capture the game's hard-edged military look precisely, and part compatibility is high — a favourite among builders who want military models made from bricks. The more recent Pokémon line is just as striking: large-scale Pikachu and Charizard builds are highly faithful, recognizable as that specific Pokémon without disassembling anything. Call of Duty covers modern warfare, and its density of weapon parts and scene detail ranks at the top of the brick world.

## Part quality and feel: how it compares to LEGO

In any MEGA review, the question builders care about most is how it feels to build. Fairly stated, MEGA's clutch power runs tighter than LEGO's. On a first build your fingers work a little harder, but that also means a more rigid structure that does not loosen up easily. Injection-moulded flow marks and parting lines are more visible than on LEGO, and tolerance control occasionally fluctuates. That said, recent products show clear improvement in finish — the Pokémon line in particular now feels comparable to LEGO.

## The special figure system: Constrx

MEGA's most recognizable feature is its highly articulated Constrx figure. It is taller than an ordinary minifigure, with a wide range of joint movement, so it can hold far more varied combat poses and displays beautifully. It is not interchangeable with LEGO minifigures, but this super-posable body has become MEGA's signature and draws plenty of display-focused builders.

## Where to buy and what it costs

Physical retail for MEGA is thin in China; the main routes are cross-border e-commerce or third-party shops. On price, MEGA's official MSRP is usually friendlier than LEGO's, and during promotions the value is excellent — especially for builders with a limited budget who want licensed themes. Obscure sets circulate poorly, though, so resale and ordering spare parts are more hassle than with LEGO. Check reviews and unboxing videos before you commit.

## Verdict: who MEGA is for

If you like Halo, Pokémon or Call of Duty, or you prefer highly poseable figures and a tighter clutch, MEGA is well worth trying. The conclusion of this MEGA review is simple: it may not be the most refined major brand, but on licensed themes it is the one most willing to take chances and put in the work. Builders on a budget who care about subject matter can dive in without worry.
