# Canoe Brick MOC Tutorial: Curved Hull and Paddle Build

The short answer: this canoe is a beginner-friendly water scene that looks good without demanding an advanced parts collection. The hull is about 26 cm long, the curved gunwale gives it a smooth outline, and the model pairs with two homemade paddles on a blue baseplate with white wave pieces. You need roughly 350 parts, a budget of about 100 yuan, and 1.5 hours. That makes it a strong first water MOC.

## Step 1: Build the keel

Start with the bottom keel. Run long bricks along the centerline to create a keel about 26 cm long, then turn both ends upward by about 2 cm. That upward sweep gives the canoe its classic raised bow and stern. The keel carries the structure of the whole boat, so press every joint fully home as you go. A flat, well-seated keel makes the next layers easier to align and keeps the finished hull from feeling loose when you pick it up.

## Step 2: Add the hull and curved edging

Build outward from the keel one layer at a time. Let each layer overhang the one below by roughly half a brick, working toward the gunwale. At the top edge, finish with curved bricks so the hull reads as a U-shaped cross-section. This curved edging is the most recognizable part of a canoe, and it should sit at about a 45-degree angle. The goal is a rounded, comfortable edge rather than a sharp one, both in appearance and when you handle the model. Keep the overhang consistent on both sides so the canoe looks symmetrical from the bow.

## Step 3: Make the paddles

Use 1x8 bricks as paddle shafts and fit a blade plate to each paddle. Make two paddles about 12 cm long. The blade should be about 4 cm, and a cross axle connects it to the shaft so you can rotate the blade angle. That adjustability matters: a paddle can lie naturally inside the hull when the canoe is displayed, or sit in a minifigure's hand as if it is being used. Check both paddles against the hull before you finish the scene so the length looks right in proportion to the 26 cm canoe.

## Step 4: Build the water scene

Use a 32x32 blue baseplate as the water surface. Add about 15 white wave pieces to suggest ripples, then place the canoe diagonally in the center of the baseplate. Put a short run of white splash pieces at the stern to suggest a wake and a direction of travel. The diagonal placement is important: it keeps the scene from looking static and gives the canoe a clear path across the water. Keep the wave pieces grouped rather than spread evenly, so the surface reads as moving water instead of a pattern.

## Step 5: Finish the details

Place one or two brown seated minifigures inside the canoe. Add a fishing rod or a small flag at the bow, and the water scene immediately feels more alive. The full build uses about 350 parts, costs around 100 yuan, and takes about 1.5 hours. As a first water MOC, it teaches useful basics: a centered keel, controlled overhang, curved edge finishing, and simple scene composition. Those skills carry directly into larger boat and harbor builds.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I get the curved canoe edge to look right?**

A: Work curved bricks from bow to stern along the gunwale, offsetting each piece by half a stud. Keep the curve at about 45 degrees, use 1x1 round corner bricks at the transition, and check that the hull cross-section forms a natural U shape.

**Q: Which baseplate and wave pieces should I use for the water?**

A: A 32x32 blue baseplate works well with white wave pieces. About 15 wave pieces are enough to suggest ripples. Add transparent round plates on top to suggest spray; this looks much more lively than a plain blue baseplate.

**Q: Is this canoe worth building for a beginner?**

A: Yes. It uses about 350 parts, costs around 100 yuan, and takes about 1.5 hours. The curved edging and paddle construction are basic techniques that transfer directly to larger boat scenes.

**Q: What paddle length looks best with this canoe?**

A: With a canoe about 26 cm long, paddles around 12 cm long look most balanced. Use a blade about 4 cm and a 1x8 brick for the shaft. A cross axle connection lets the blade rotate to the angle you want.
