# Where to Buy Brick Parts in 2026: A Practical Guide for MOC Builders

Every MOC stalls at the same wall: you have the instructions, you have the time, and you are missing 37 parts in a colour that apparently no longer exists. Where you buy those parts decides whether the build costs 30 dollars or 300.

Here is the short version: **buy common parts in bulk, buy rare parts individually, and never order a big lot without checking the part number first.**

## The four sources, ranked by cost per part

**1. Official Pick-a-Brick (highest quality, highest price).** New parts, exact colours, perfect clutch. Worth it for structural elements you cannot afford to have fail — Technic pins, axles, anything load-bearing.

**2. BrickLink (best for rare parts).** Thousands of independent sellers, so the long-tail parts that no retailer stocks are usually here. Filter by country first: shipping is often the larger half of the bill. Always check a seller's feedback count, not only the rating.

**3. Bulk lots from marketplace sellers (best cost per part).** A 500-gram mixed lot is the cheapest way to fill a parts bin. The trade-off is variance: you get what the seller had left over. Buy from listings that show the actual pile, and expect 5–10% of pieces to be off-brand, discoloured, or chewed.

**4. Compatible third-party brands (best for large builds).** For a 3,000-part castle, a compatible brand can cut the cost by more than half. The clutch is usually 5–10% looser than official parts, which matters for precisely stacked walls and almost never matters for scenery.

## Check the part number before you buy

This is the step that separates a good deal from a wasted order. Every part has a design ID — the number printed on BrickLink and in your instructions. Search that number, not the name: "1x4 brick" returns dozens of results with incompatible moulds, while `3001` returns exactly one part.

Two traps worth knowing:

- **Colour names differ between sources.** What one store calls "dark red" another calls "new dark red". Compare the RGB swatch, not the label.
- **Mould variants are not always interchangeable.** A 1x2 plate with a groove (`3794b`) and without (`3794`) behave differently in thin assemblies.

## How much should a part cost?

As a rule of thumb for common parts in standard colours, aim below these per-piece ceilings:

| Part | Fair price (USD/pc) |
|---|---|
| 1x2 brick (3004) | 0.05 – 0.09 |
| 2x4 brick (3001) | 0.10 – 0.18 |
| 1x4 plate (3710) | 0.03 – 0.06 |
| Technic pin (2780) | 0.02 – 0.05 |

If a listing is far above these, it is either a rare colour, a rare mould, or a seller pricing for convenience.

## Building a parts inventory you will actually reuse

Sort by *function*, not by colour: pins, plates, bricks, slopes, tiles. A MOC builder who can find a 2x2 tile in ten seconds finishes builds; one who sorts by colour spends the evening digging. Store in shallow drawers and keep the count on the label.

## FAQ

**Are compatible bricks as good as LEGO?** For display models, yes — the difference shows mainly in clutch strength and colour consistency. For play models that are rebuilt often, official parts last longer.

**How do I know a part number?** Open the model on BrickPlan, find the step, and read the number in the parts list. Every blueprint page lists the parts used.

**Is buying second-hand bricks worth it?** Yes for bulk plates and bricks, no for anything visible on the finished model's front face — scratches show up under light.
