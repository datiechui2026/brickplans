# Zelda MOC Instructions: Build Hyrule's Iconic Scenes in Brick

Since the first game appeared in 1986, The Legend of Zelda has spanned more than three decades, from an 8-bit adventure to the vast open worlds of Breath of the Wild and Tears of the Kingdom. For brick builders, turning those landmarks into real models — Zelda MOC instructions, meaning fan-made build plans you design and share yourself — has become an increasingly popular direction.

This guide covers the Zelda MOC landscape: which scenes are worth building, where to find instructions, how to choose parts, and the techniques that separate a wobbly first attempt from a display piece.

## Why The Legend of Zelda and brick MOC are a natural fit

The series has a highly recognizable visual identity. Breath of the Wild and Tears of the Kingdom are known for their watercolor-like rendering and wide natural landscapes, while Ocarina of Time and Twilight Princess lean toward a more realistic fantasy style. That range of visual language gives MOC builders endless material.

A good Zelda MOC instruction set needs to do three things: reproduce the signature elements precisely, such as the spires of Hyrule Castle or the elegant curve of the Master Sword; carry the atmosphere of the Zelda world through the texture of the bricks; and balance playability with display value, so the finished model looks good on a shelf while keeping some working mechanisms.

On [brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn) you can find high-quality MOC instructions shared by Zelda fan communities worldwide, covering everything from micro-scale scenes to full-size weapons.

## Five classic Zelda MOC themes

### 1. Hyrule Castle — the ultimate MOC challenge

Hyrule Castle is the most iconic building in the series and appears in nearly every game. The Breath of the Wild version, with its soaring spires, staggered towers and surrounding moat, is a dream build for countless MOC makers.

Hyrule Castle instructions are usually organized in layers: a base with the outer walls and moat, a main body with the great hall, towers and connecting bridges, and a top section with the throne room and the signature spire. Part counts typically land between 2,500 and 5,000 pieces, placing it in the intermediate-to-advanced range — best attempted by builders with some experience.

For color, light gray and dark gray bricks form the castle walls, blue and gold pieces handle the decorative details, and green plant parts recreate the lush landscape around the castle.

### 2. The Master Sword — the most sought-after weapon replica

The Master Sword is the most representative weapon in the series and the first Zelda build for many MOC makers. Its shape is elegant and simple — a slender blade, a distinctive crossguard and a purple hilt — which suits brick reproduction very well.

Good Master Sword instructions usually rely on SNOT (Studs Not On Top, meaning sideways building) to capture the smooth curve of the blade, and use transparent blue parts to suggest the glow effect. On scale: a full-size version around 80 to 100 cm long works as a display piece, while a micro version can serve as a minifigure accessory.

In the [brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn) instruction library you can find versions ranging from beginner to master level, and some sets also include a scabbard and display base.

### 3. Sheikah Towers and ancient ruins

The Sheikah Towers and ancient ruins scattered across Hyrule in Breath of the Wild and Tears of the Kingdom make excellent MOC subjects. These structures have strong, simple geometry, use dark blue and orange as their main colors, and contain glowing mechanisms and rising platforms.

The key to a Sheikah Tower is capturing the boxy geometric outline and the glowing orb at the top. Transparent orange light bricks reproduce the in-game energy effect perfectly. These builds usually run 800 to 1,500 pieces and suit intermediate builders.

### 4. The four Divine Beasts and ancient machinery

Vah Ruta, Vah Rudania, Vah Medoh and Vah Naboris are the most visually striking giant machines in Breath of the Wild. Each Beast has a unique silhouette and color scheme, plus its own moving mechanical parts.

Instructions for the Divine Beasts call for a large number of specialized parts to reproduce the rounded mechanical forms, and also require carefully designed gear mechanisms to drive the movement. These are advanced projects, recommended for builders with Technic experience.

### 5. Korok Forest and the Koroks

Koroks — the little forest sprites of the Zelda series — are among its most beloved characters. Their simple shapes and bright colors make them ideal beginner practice builds. A single Korok takes only 50 to 100 pieces, while a full Korok Forest scene can combine over a thousand pieces into a storybook-style diorama.

## Essential techniques for building Zelda MOCs

### Building techniques

1. **SNOT**: curved and angled surfaces are common in Zelda architecture and weapons, and solid SNOT skills make your model flow much more naturally.
2. **Layered construction**: for large architectural builds such as Hyrule Castle, designing in layers lowers the difficulty and makes it easier to disassemble and show the interior later.
3. **Lighting**: LED light bricks can simulate the Master Sword's glow, the blue energy of Sheikah technology, and the mysterious atmosphere of the shrines.
4. **Terrain building**: the Zelda world has varied terrain, so learn to use slopes and curved bricks in different colors to represent grass, rock and water.

### Choosing parts

When building a Zelda-themed MOC, pay particular attention to these categories:

- **Unusual colors**: the Zelda world uses many distinctive palettes — the gray-blue of Hyrule Castle, the deep blue of Sheikah technology, the purple of the Master Sword — so confirm availability before you start.
- **Transparent and light-up parts**: for energy effects and magical highlights.
- **Plant parts**: for the rich natural vegetation of Hyrule.
- **Printed pieces and stickers**: for Zelda's signature crests and symbols.
