# How to Clean LEGO Bricks: Wash, Dry and Store Without Damage

Every builder hits the same wall sooner or later: a favourite set is covered in dust, greasy from handling, tipped into a bowl of snacks by a kid, or has spent years in a display cabinet quietly turning yellow. Good **brick cleaning and maintenance** keeps parts usable for another decade. Sloppy cleaning strips prints, loosens clutch power and makes ABS brittle — and none of that is reversible. This guide walks through four stages in order: preparation, cleaning methods, drying and storage, and day-to-day protection.

## 1. Preparation: Sorting Matters More Than Scrubbing

Before anything gets wet, do three things.

**Sort by colour and type.** Wash dark parts and light parts separately so dye transfer doesn't ruin a batch. Put printed parts in their own pile — less rubbing means less chance of wearing the pattern off.

**Break assemblies down.** Pull apart combined sub-builds and anything held together with axles and technic pins. Dust trapped in the gaps only comes out once the parts are separated.

**Check the special cases.** Sticker-covered parts should never go underwater. Motors, light units and battery boxes get a dry wipe only. Rubber tyres are best hand-washed on their own.

Sorting is also a good moment to count what you have — a free inventory pass for your MOC spare-parts bin.

## 2. Choosing Between Three Cleaning Methods

**Method one: warm water hand wash.** Use water at 30–40°C with a small amount of mild dish soap, and scrub piece by piece with a soft-bristled brush or a sponge. This is the right choice for small part counts, printed elements and valuable older sets, because you have the most control.

**Method two: machine wash.** Bag the parts in a laundry bag or a knotted pillowcase, then run the machine on a gentle cycle with cold water and no fabric softener. This suits large volumes of loose bricks. Pull out gears, transparent parts and very small pieces beforehand to avoid abrasion and losses.

**Method three: ultrasonic cleaning.** This is the most effective option for stubborn dirt in crevices, and transparent parts come out looking new. The equipment cost is high, so it makes sense for serious collectors.

Whichever route you take, never use hot water, alcohol or strong solvents. ABS plastic deforms and whitens when it meets high heat or organic solvents.

## 3. Drying and Storage: Where Things Go Wrong

Clean bricks are most at risk when they are left sealed up and damp.

**Don't dry them in direct sun.** UV speeds up ageing and yellowing. **Don't use a tumble dryer** — the heat will warp parts outright.

The correct method: lay the parts flat on an absorbent towel or a drying rack in a shaded, well-ventilated spot. Flip transparent parts and thin plates once during drying. Around 24 hours is normally enough to dry through.

For storage, box parts by colour or by instruction booklet, and drop one or two desiccant packs into each box to keep humidity down. Sets you won't build for a long time are best stored sealed and away from light; adding a UV-blocking film to the cabinet is the safer option. Yellowed parts are essentially irreversible, which is why prevention beats any rescue attempt.

## 4. Everyday Protection and Common Mistakes

To reduce how often you need to do a full **brick cleaning** session, habits matter more than tools. Keep display areas away from direct window light. Dust cabinets regularly with a feather duster or soft brush. Wash your hands before building if they're sweaty or oily.

A few mistakes worth avoiding:

- Scrubbing with toothpaste, which dulls and scratches the surface.
- Running parts through a dishwasher — the high-temperature rinse warps them.
- Putting damp parts straight into storage, which leads to mould spots.
- Letting electronic components anywhere near water, which destroys them outright.

Avoid these and your parts bin will keep that fresh-out-of-the-box feel.

## Final Thoughts

Good parts are what give an MOC its confidence. While you're dismantling and washing an old set, you may find missing pieces or start thinking about a new use for what you've got.
