# Temple of Heaven MOC: 4,500-Piece Hall of Prayer Build

Bottom line first: this Temple of Heaven MOC is based on the Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests, runs about 4,500 parts, and finishes at roughly 25 cm in diameter and 35 cm tall. Budget around ¥780, and plan on about 18 hours if you build 2–3 hours a day. Three things make or break the model: the triple eaves, the pyramidal roof, and the blue glazed tiles. Each eave steps inward by 2 studs, the roof closes into a point using dark blue tile pieces, and gold accent pieces trim the eave edges — which is what pushes the resemblance so high. The instructions come from the brickplan platform, and the parts list is grouped by base, three eaves and finial, so even a first-time builder can follow along.

**Step 1: Build the round base and three-tier platform**

The hall sits on a three-tier circular platform. Start the circular outline on a 24x24 baseplate; each tier is 2 plates high and steps inward 1 stud per tier, with a ring of small white railing pieces around the edge. This stage eats about 1,000 parts. The curvature of the round platform sets how every eave above it reads, so keep the studs aligned carefully.

**Step 2: Build the ground-floor hall and columns**

The first storey is about 20 studs across, with an outer ring of 12 red columns, each 8 studs tall, and carved panels between them. The door faces due south, framed in dark red, and you can build the door leaves so they actually open and close. Leave the interior hollow — there is room for a small altar later. This stage uses about 1,200 parts.

**Step 3: Build the triple eaves**

The second storey steps inward 2 studs from the first, with a dense ring of blue tile pieces along the eave and inverted slope pieces flicking up at each corner. The third storey steps in another 2 studs, giving the tapering pagoda silhouette. Add a ring of small bracket-set pieces (dougong) under each eave in brown to create the sense of structural support. The triple eaves are the most time-consuming part of the build, around 1,500 parts.

**Step 4: Close the pyramidal roof and add the finial**

There is no flat top: blue tile pieces converge from all four sides toward the centre, and a single gold finial piece clips into the middle to create the hall's signature pyramidal roof. At the end, check that all three eaves sit level, then verify the parts against the floor-by-floor lists on the brickplan instruction page and reorder anything missing by part number. For a night display, tuck a warm-white LED inside the hall — the light spilling out through the doors and windows looks fantastic.

## FAQ

**Q: Which parts are used for the blue glazed roof tiles?**

A: Use dark blue slope tiles combined with inverted slope pieces, then edge the eave ring with smooth tiles in the same colour. If you run short on blue, mixing in a few light blue pieces adds depth from a distance, and the gold trim is done with 1x1 gold pieces.

**Q: How do I keep each of the three eaves symmetrical?**

A: Build every eave out from the same central axis — the second steps in 2 studs from the first, the third another 2 studs. Check the roundness of each tier before starting the next one, and work through the layered parts lists in the brickplan instructions one tier at a time rather than mixing tiers.

**Q: What does this build cost and how long does it take?**

A: About 4,500 parts, roughly ¥780, and around 18 hours at 2–3 hours a day. Blue tiles and red columns account for most of the cost; if you already have those in stock you can save two or three hundred yuan.

**Q: How big is the finished model — will it fit a display case?**

A: It measures about 25 cm across and 35 cm tall, so it fits comfortably on a middle shelf of a standard bookcase. A dark round baseplate underneath and a ring of light pieces set it off nicely, and the silhouette is especially striking at night.

**Q: Can a beginner build this Temple of Heaven MOC?**

A: Yes, but build one or two modular street scenes first. The triple eaves and pyramidal roof demand precise alignment, so follow the step-by-step instructions on brickplan.cn and allow roughly 5 extra hours to finish.
