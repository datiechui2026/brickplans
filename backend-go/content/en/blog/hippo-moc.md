# Brick Hippo MOC Review: Big Mouth, Bulky Body, Build Tips

The short version: a hippo is recognisable almost entirely by its mouth. Curved pieces and slopes combine into a squarish-round snout, the upper lip tips slightly upward to show white teeth, and everything sits on a heavy barrel body with short, thick legs.

## Brick Hippo MOC Review: The Mouth Is the Core

A hippo lives or dies by its mouth. The first thing to judge in any brick hippo MOC is how well the snout is reproduced. A real hippo's mouth has that unmistakable squarish-round shape, with wide upper and lower jaws, a slightly upturned upper lip and a few big teeth showing. Good instructions build the outline from curved pieces and slopes rather than forcing square bricks to do a round job, use white elements for the teeth, and blend the mouth cleanly into the head. When reviewing, pay special attention to whether the jaw is fixed or hinged: a closed mouth is simple, while an opening one needs hinge pieces to support it. The hinged version is harder, but the finished model has far more presence. Shrink the mouth and the whole hippo loses its character.

## Bulky Body and Leg Structure

Hippos are heavy and round, with short, thick limbs. These MOCs usually stack large curved pieces or round bricks into a thick barrel-shaped torso. Three things to check. First, proportion: a hippo is big-headed and broad, so the body has to be wide enough — too narrow and you end up with a skinny hippo. Second, legs: four short, thick legs built in dark gray, with knees that are essentially invisible, spaced apart so they actually support the body. Third, the belly: a hippo's underside is rounded and sagging, so add curved pieces along the bottom of the body to create that arc. Keep the center of gravity low so the model stands firmly.

## Head Details: Ears, Eyes and Nostrils

The head has its own peculiarities. The design needs small round ears standing upright, eyes set on either side of the top of the head, and raised nostrils above the snout. Check ear and eye placement: on a real hippo both sit high on the skull, a trait of its semi-aquatic life, and the model only reads as a hippo when that is captured. Build the nostrils from two small round pieces or a printed element on the bridge of the snout. A ring of darker pieces around the mouth makes a useful outline and adds a layer of detail. The head-to-body connection must be solid — hippo heads are large, and undersized connection points let it droop.

## Color Scheme and Texture

The palette is fairly simple: a gray-purple or gray-brown main body, lighter tones on the belly and mouth, dark gray legs. The difficult part is choosing among grays — there are many shades, and a poor light-dark mix looks muddy. Check whether the instructions give clear color references; good ones list specific parts for the main color, secondary color and accents. To add texture, scatter a few dark spots across the body or use textured pieces to suggest skin folds. Hippo skin is smooth and thick, so keep the surface pieces as flat as possible and avoid too many bumpy elements.

## Instructions Quality and Difficulty

Overall difficulty is medium, usually 200 to 300 pieces, a step up from small animals like hedgehogs and rabbits. When evaluating the instructions, focus on whether the steps for the mouth and the body curves are clear — these are the two places builders most often get wrong and have to redo. Beginners should start with a closed-mouth version and move to the open-jaw build once comfortable. Display the finished model with a water scene: a blue baseplate plus a few water-ripple pieces, with the hippo half-submerged, matches its real habits and lifts the presentation considerably. In short, a mid-level animal MOC worth trying.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I judge whether brick hippo MOC instructions are any good?**
A: Start with the mouth. A hippo's mouth is that signature squarish-round shape, with wide jaws, a slightly upturned upper lip and a few big teeth showing. Good instructions combine curved pieces and slopes to build the outline instead of forcing it out of square bricks.

**Q: How do I build the heavy body?**
A: Lay a base of large basic bricks and wrap the outline with curved pieces, flaring the belly outward. The four legs should be short, thick and vertical so they carry the weight — no floating body — which gives the model that solid hippo mass.

**Q: What head details matter?**
A: Small round pieces for the ears standing on either side of the top of the head, 1x1 round plates for the eyes kept symmetrical, and two small black dot pieces for the nostrils. Get those three right and the expression appears.

**Q: How hard are these instructions?**
A: Low to medium. The mouth curves and the roundness of the body are the main challenges; the structure is mostly basic parts with no complex gearing, which makes it a good animal MOC warm-up for builders with some experience.
