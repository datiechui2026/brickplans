# How to Build a Brick Water Scene MOC with Transparent Plates

The short version: a convincing water scene is not about piling up blue parts. It comes from three stacked layers — a transparent plate base, light and dark blue for depth, and white for foam. A beginner scene about 30 cm square runs roughly 600-900 pieces, costs CNY 200-400, and takes a first-time builder 4-6 hours.

## 1. Gather the Parts: Transparent Plates Are the Canvas

Transparent blue plates are the heart of the build. Make 1x2 and 2x4 transparent light blue plates your main material — they should account for more than half of the total part count. Add 20-30 dark blue plates for the deep water zone and 10-15 white plates for foam. On a tight budget you can substitute regular light blue plates for the transparent ones, but you lose the see-through quality: transparent plates let the terrain underneath and the light show through, and that is exactly what makes the water look alive.

Beginner water scene parts list (about 600 pieces, CNY 200-400 total):

- Transparent light blue plates: 350-400 pieces, roughly CNY 120-180
- Dark blue plates: 80-100 pieces, roughly CNY 30-50
- White plates and 1x1 round plates: 60-80 pieces, roughly CNY 20-30
- Transparent slopes and curved pieces: 40-60 pieces, roughly CNY 40-60

## 2. Building the Water Surface: Layers Create Depth

**Step one: lay the base.** Cover the deep water zone with dark blue plates, then build up 2-3 layers near the shoreline so the bed slopes from shallow at the edge to deep in the middle. The sense of depth appears immediately.

**Step two: add the middle layer.** Stagger transparent light blue plates across the dark blue base. The two colours blend through the transparent layer and the surface reads as a natural gradient. Offset each layer by 1-2 studs to imitate ripples.

**Step three: make waves.** Scatter white plates or transparent curved pieces along the crests, then run a dotted line of 1x1 white round plates along the wave line to create foam. Never lay the wave line out straight — S-curves and gentle arcs look far more natural.

## 3. Finishing Touches: Dock, Waterfall and Details

**Dock:** build a platform that juts out over the water from brown plates, supported underneath by 2-3 pillars. Keep the pillars 4-6 bricks tall so the dock hangs above the surface and casts a stronger reflection.

**Waterfall:** stack transparent slope pieces downward from a high point, offsetting each layer by half a stud so the falling water has a real drop to it. Pile a ring of white round plates at the bottom to suggest splash. A 15 cm waterfall needs about 50-70 transparent slope pieces.

Finally, dress the shoreline with minifigures, a small boat and trees. Shoot from a 45-degree high angle, where light passing through the transparent plates reads most clearly. You can look up more detailed parts lists in the catalog on brickplan.cn.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I keep a water MOC from looking like a flat blue puddle?**

A: Layers are the answer. Dark blue underneath, transparent light blue in the middle, white for foam — all three are needed. Then build up the slope near the shore so the light-to-dark contrast appears and the surface gains real volume instead of being one flat sheet of blue.

**Q: What is the difference between transparent blue bricks and ordinary blue bricks?**

A: Transparent plates let light through and show the colours beneath them, so they catch the light and read as water. Regular blue plates are opaque and suit deep water zones or structural support. Use transparent plates for the main surface layer and ordinary dark blue plates for the deep zone — that pairing works best, and the price difference is about 30%.

**Q: Which parts should I use for a waterfall, and can transparent slope plates be used generally?**

A: Transparent slope plates are the workhorse for waterfalls. Choose 1x2 or 2x2 sizes and stack each layer offset by half a stud. Remember to pile white round plates at the base of the flow to suggest splash. If you have no transparent slopes, stand transparent plates on edge instead, though the sense of drop will be weaker.

**Q: Roughly how much does a water MOC cost, and how many parts does it need?**

A: A beginner scene 30 cm square uses about 600-900 pieces and costs CNY 200-400 in total, with transparent plates taking the largest share at roughly CNY 120-180. If you only build a partial water surface, 300 pieces and about CNY 100 can still produce a good result — it scales to your budget.

**Q: Can a water scene be combined with a street scene MOC?**

A: Absolutely. Water works best as the foreground or base of a street scene — put a river channel and dock along the front edge and use transparent plates to blend into the ground, which noticeably lifts the overall sense of depth. When combining, make sure the baseplate heights of the two builds line up so the transition looks continuous.
