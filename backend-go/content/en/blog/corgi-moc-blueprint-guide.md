# Brick Corgi MOC Blueprint: Proportions and Build Steps

Want to build a smiling little low-rider with stubby legs? The corgi is one of the most popular subjects in pet MOCs for good reason: the round rear, the legs that sit almost flat on the ground and the oversized head are natural material for brick building. This breakdown of a brick corgi MOC blueprint covers proportions, color blocking and leg construction, then walks through the build order so you can start with the parts you already own.

## Proportions Come First: Big Head, Short Legs

The thing that grabs people about a corgi is the big head and short legs. Don't rush into the body — lock the overall proportions first. A common approach: the body length should be about 3 times the width of the head, and the leg height should be only 1/3 of the body height, or even shorter. You can lay a ring of 1x2 plates on the baseplate as a leg-height reference line, then stack upward from there. Get the proportions right and even ordinary parts will read as a corgi at a glance; get them wrong and you end up with a generic dog, or something that isn't quite a dog at all.

## Color: Spreading the Tan, White and Black

The classic corgi is tan back, white belly. Use tan or orange for the back, white for the belly, chest and all four feet, and black as an accent on the ears and around the eyes. Build the colors bottom-up: white for the belly base first, then the tan back, then the black details last. Alternating 1x1 plates and 1x2 plates gives you a softer boundary between color blocks, so the model has depth without a single sticker. Most brick corgi MOC blueprints open with a color reference image — follow it and you can't go far wrong.

## Head Structure and Ears

Build the corgi face in three parts: muzzle, cheeks and the top of the head. Use light-colored slope bricks for the muzzle (a 2x3 slope brick works well) to create the forward-jutting profile, use 1x2 curved slopes on both sides for the cheeks, and cap the skull with plates. The ears are the soul of the corgi: two large upright ears can be fixed to the sides of the head with hinge pieces (1x1 hinges or clip-and-joint parts), with the tips angled slightly outward — that instantly gives you the alert little corgi look.

## Joining the Short Legs to the Body

Weak legs are where a corgi build falls apart. Two approaches work well. First, let the body base sit on the ground and tuck the legs in from the side using 1x2 plates or 1x1 bricks, so the dog looks planted. Second, connect legs to body with ball joint pieces so you can adjust the stance freely — much easier when you want to pose it for photos. At the joints, add a few interlocking layers (staggered overlapping between adjacent layers) for strength, otherwise the model will come apart the moment a kid picks it up. At this stage a brick corgi MOC blueprint usually gives you a separate parts breakdown for the legs — follow the order and you save yourself a lot of trouble.

## Tail and Expression Details

The corgi tail can be a little pom-pom: stack curved pieces or round plates, mount it high on the rear of the body and tip it up slightly for extra charm. For the face, a black 1x1 round tile works as an eye on each side of the front face, a black slope brick makes the nose, and a white curved piece around the muzzle gives the outline of a smiling mouth. Those few details together more than double the cuteness.

## Build Order at a Glance

A quick recap: set the proportions, lay in the colors, then work through the head, ears, body, legs and tail in that order. A small parts collection is no obstacle — many brick corgi MOC blueprints are designed with part commonality in mind, sticking to standard 1x1 and 1x2 plates and basic slopes, so you can usually cobble one together from what's in the bin.
