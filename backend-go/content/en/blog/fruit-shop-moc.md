# Brick Fruit Shop MOC: Sign, Displays and Awning in 3 Steps

Short answer first: a brick fruit shop MOC on a 32×16 stud baseplate needs about 1,500 pieces, costs roughly 140 yuan, and takes an experienced builder around 4 hours. Three features carry the whole look — the shop sign, the fruit display and the awning — and the awning, built from movable hinge parts so it actually opens and closes, lifts the finished result by roughly 50% on its own. The build breaks into three steps.

## Step 1: Build the Shop Body and Sign

Start with a 32×16 stud baseplate as the footprint, then raise four walls and leave a 10-stud-wide opening in the front face for the doorway. That opening sets the scale for everything else: a 10-stud entrance is wide enough to read as a shopfront without eating the wall space you need for the sign above it.

The sign itself is four 1×8 tiles laid side by side to spell out the shop name — the two characters for fresh fruit. Tiles are the right choice here because they have no studs on top, so the sign face stays flat and the lettering reads cleanly from street level. Frame the outer edge with a ring of 2×2 curved slope bricks to soften the corners, then fit two 1×1 transparent light bricks as night lighting. Mount the whole sign 12 layers off the ground: that is the height a pedestrian's eye lands on, so the name is readable from the pavement rather than only from above.

## Step 2: Set Up the Fruit Display

Flank the entrance with a sloped-top shelf on each side. Lay every shelf level flat with 1×2 tiles, which gives you a smooth surface so the fruit sits evenly instead of perching on studs. On top, build round fruit bricks: red 2×2 round bricks for apples, orange for oranges, yellow for lemons, 15 of each. That is 45 fruit pieces in total, and the point is not the individual pieces but the grouping — keep each colour together as its own block so the shelves read as colour-block arrays from a distance.

In the corner, place a 4×4 watermelon slice brick as the visual centrepiece, then add two crates of 1×1 round plates beside it to suggest loose, unbunched fruit. The contrast between ordered colour blocks and a slightly messier corner is what creates depth; without it the display looks like a parts catalogue rather than a shop.

## Step 3: Fit the Awning and Finish

Above the door, connect a 10×6 slope plate to two hinge sets to form the awning. Alternate red and white stripe plates across the surface in 5 strips: that reproduces the look of fabric and, because the whole panel is on hinges, lets you fold it up or angle it down by hand whenever you want a different display pose.

Finish with a 2×6 anti-slip floor tile at the entrance and one green plant as an accent. Then shoot two photos — one straight on from the front and one from overhead — and the street scene is complete. The overhead shot matters because it is the only angle that shows the colour-block layout of the shelves properly.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I make a brick fruit shop MOC look real?**
A: Colour-blocked displays and sign height do most of the work. Group fruit by colour family, lay every shelf level flat with tiles, mount the sign 12 layers off the ground, and add transparent light bricks for the night look. Realism improves noticeably with those three moves; the blueprint breakdown at brickplan.cn covers the details.

**Q: Can I connect the fruit shop to other street scenes?**
A: Yes. Standardise on a 32×16 stud baseplate and the shop will combine with other street modules. Neighbouring shops that share a side wall save around 20% of the brick count, and you should leave a 1-stud-wide gap between buildings as a drainage channel so the join looks natural.

**Q: Is the hinged awning worth the extra parts?**
A: Yes. A single hinge costs about 0.3 yuan, and two sets give you the open-and-close action for under 1 yuan extra. That buys playability and a second display angle — the best value detail in the entire build.

**Q: What does the whole parts list cost?**
A: At roughly 0.09 yuan per compatible brick, 1,500 pieces come to about 135 yuan, and hinges, transparent light bricks and the baseplate put the ceiling around 150 yuan. Original-brand parts would run to about 450 yuan, so compatible bricks are the sensible choice for a first version.

**Q: Lettering the sign is fiddly — is there an alternative?**
A: Yes. Stickers or printed parts can carry the shop name and skip the lettering step entirely, which suits beginners working to a deadline. If you want the built-up brick texture, stay with the 1×8 tiles. The difference between the two approaches comes down to feel and detail, so pick according to budget.
