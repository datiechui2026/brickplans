# Brick MOC Street Light Review: Cost, Wiring and Layout

The short version first: a brick-built MOC street light is one of the best-value decorative builds you can add to a city layout. A single post runs about 60 pieces and around 30 minutes of building, and once a light brick goes inside, the night-time effect improves far more than that piece count suggests. Difficulty is low, the parts are ordinary, and building them in batches is the entire point — one post is a detail, six posts are a street. It is well worth picking up.

## 1. Lamp Head Design

The lamp head is where most street light MOCs succeed or fail. Three shapes come up again and again: a round dome, a square box lantern, and a retro curved-arm head that hangs the light out over the pavement. The set reviewed here uses 1x1 trans parts combined with 1x2 curved slopes to form the shade, with a 1x1 round axle piece underneath acting as the lamp base.

The nice part is that the head tilts up by roughly 30 degrees, so you can aim it rather than just parking it flat on the column. That single joint does a lot of work: angle the head slightly upward and the post reads as a real street lamp; leave it level and it looks like a toy standing in a road. The design sits between retro and practical, which is exactly what you want in a street scene where the light has to look period-appropriate but still be usable.

## 2. Light Bricks and Cable Routing

There are two ways to light a MOC street light. The simple one is to drop in a light brick that carries its own light source. The tidier one is to run a hidden cable down the post to a battery box, which keeps the lamp glowing for long sessions.

The reviewed version takes the first route: two 1x1 light bricks stacked inside the shade. Brightness is enough to cover roughly a 4x4 stud area of pavement, which is plenty for a street light — you want a pool of light under each post, not a floodlit road. The cable, when you do use one, runs inside the hollow axle of the column, and the connection point at ground level is hidden inside the 2x2 base. The result is a very clean exterior: no wires climbing the outside of the post, no battery box sitting in the middle of the street.

If you plan to display the layout for hours at a time, the cabled version is the one to build. If you just want a lit street for photos, two stacked light bricks per post do the job.

## 3. A Row of Street Lights: Difficulty and Cost

The builder here arranged six posts in a line at 5-stud intervals, with buildings on both sides. That spacing is what creates the sense of depth — the street reads as a corridor rather than a wall of facades, and the repeated lamps pull the eye down the row.

Cost is the headline. A single post comes in at roughly 8 to 15 CNY, and a full set of six with light bricks lands around 120 CNY, which puts street lights among the cheapest categories in all of street-scene MOC building. Structure is simple and the parts are common, so beginners can finish one without trouble and then repeat the build five more times without getting bored.

If you want a full set of street scene plans, it is worth browsing the light-themed builds on brickplan.cn. Building the same post six times also teaches you something useful: by the third one you will be changing the shade slightly, swapping a dome for a curved arm, so the row does not look stamped out.

## FAQ

**Q: What should I use as the light source in a brick MOC street light?**
A: A light brick with its own built-in source is the first choice — stack a 1x1 or 1x2 light brick into the shade and you are done. If you want the lamp to stay lit for long periods, run a hidden cable to a battery box instead. Keep the light source inside the hollow column so the outside stays clean and the brightness stays consistent.

**Q: The lamp head on my street light sits loose. How do I fix it?**
A: Add a 1x1 round axle piece between the head and the column as the connection point, then clamp the inside of the shade with two thin plates. That holds the head firmly enough to tilt up about 30 degrees without coming loose. Press each connection fully home while building — most wobble is a piece that was never pushed down.

**Q: How much does a row of brick MOC street lights cost?**
A: A single post costs about 8 to 15 CNY. Six posts in a line plus light bricks comes to roughly 120 CNY for the full set. If you already have the common parts in your own stock, you only need to buy the light bricks, which keeps the total under 60 CNY.
