# How to Read a MOC Blueprint: Parts List, Colors, Steps

The short answer: reading a brick MOC blueprint comes down to three moves — scan the parts list first, then decode the color and quantity markings, then build in step order. Once that rhythm becomes automatic, you can read through a 5,000-piece plan in about 10 minutes, and you do not need any English to do it.

**Step 1: Start with the parts list.** The first thing to read in any MOC blueprint is the parts list on pages one and two. Every entry carries a part number, a color and a quantity. Set aside ten minutes to sort your parts by color and lay them out, then count each line against the list. If anything is missing, contact the seller for a replacement right away — far better than discovering a critical part is absent halfway through a build. On a typical 2,000-piece MOC from BrickPlan, sorting by color takes around 15 minutes and cuts the time you spend hunting for parts mid-build by roughly 80%.

**Step 2: Learn the color codes and quantity counts.** Most blueprints label colors with letter abbreviations or printed swatches — LBG for light bluish gray, DBG for dark bluish gray, and so on. Each step also states how many new parts that step adds, usually printed right beside the step number, so you can pull those pieces before you start connecting anything. Watch the difference between symmetric parts and directional parts: arrows in the diagram show which way a piece faces, and misreading them is the single biggest source of beginner rework, accounting for roughly 60% of do-overs.

**Step 3: Build in step-number order.** Numbering starts at 1, and each step typically contains 5 to 20 parts. Cross each step off with a pen as you finish it so nothing gets skipped. When a subassembly repeats, the blueprint marks it "x2" or "x4" — that means build the same structure two or four times, not just once. Stay in sequence and a 3,000-piece set averages 5 to 6 hours from first brick to last.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I read a MOC blueprint without making mistakes?**
A: Sort your parts by color and count them against the list first, then build strictly in step-number order and cross off each step as you finish it. Pay particular attention to arrow directions and to "x2" / "x4" repeat markers — those two habits prevent most build errors.

**Q: What if I can't read the English on the blueprint?**
A: The English on a blueprint is mostly part names and color abbreviations. Parts already carry numbers, and colors are shown as swatches, so you can simply match them up. Memorize the four common abbreviations — LBG for light bluish gray, DBG for dark bluish gray, BL for black and WH for white — and you can follow about 90% of what any blueprint shows you.

**Q: Do I need to check the part quantities before I start?**
A: Yes. Spend 10 to 15 minutes counting against the parts list before you build, and treat it as essential for any set over 2,000 pieces. If something is missing, contact the seller for a replacement early — it is far cheaper than stopping mid-build to wait for a part.

**Q: Can I build a 5,000-piece blueprint in one sitting?**
A: You can, but it is better to split it up. A 5,000-piece MOC usually comes as 8 to 10 step bags. Build one or two bags a day in roughly 2-hour sessions; it protects your neck and back and keeps fatigue from creeping in. Total build time runs about 12 to 15 hours.

**Q: Are digital or paper blueprints better?**
A: Digital blueprints let you zoom, rotate the model and switch to night mode, which suits fine detail work. Paper blueprints need no charging and are more intuitive to flip through. Use paper for small MOCs and a digital version on a tablet for anything over 2,000 pieces.
