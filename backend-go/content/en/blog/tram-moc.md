# Brick Tram MOC Guide: Track Gauge and Pantograph

First conclusion: a tram MOC succeeds or fails at the wheels and pantograph. The wheels need to match a six-stud-wide track gauge and keep half a stud of clearance. The pantograph should be a diamond frame made from Technic beams and pins. The articulation point should sit near the center of the bogie.

Brick-built trams are among the most characterful city rail vehicles: long, narrow bodies, a bell, and a roof-mounted pantograph. To build a proportional tram MOC that can run through curves, you need to plan the chassis gauge, drivetrain and body structure together. A tram is harder than a standard train model because the body is narrower and the curves are tighter, so every part position affects running. This guide shares a tested build approach from track fit to finishing details.

## Track Gauge Fit

Establish the baseline before you build. Most commercial brick track is six studs wide, so the wheels should be train wheelsets or flanged wheels that match that gauge. If you use a standard four-stud wheel gauge, the inner wheel can rub the rail in curves. Leave half a stud of clearance on each side of the bogie. For power, prefer a Technic gearbox with a direct-drive M motor; avoid a large battery box that eats up car space. Add a turntable plate between the bogie and the body to improve passage through small-radius curves.

## Pantograph Structure

The pantograph is the signature detail of a brick tram MOC. Build a diamond frame from Technic beams and pins, fix its base to a hinge part on the roof, and make the top from two curved pieces to form the bow. This looks right and can mimic the raise-and-lower motion of a real pantograph. For more play value, add a rubber band between the frame and the roof so a light press springs back with a damped action. Use a black hose piece for the contact strip so it reads like a real collector pole.

## Articulated Cars

Modern trams are often multi-section articulated vehicles. In bricks, split the body into two or three cars and join them with a cross axle plus hinge parts. Keep the articulation point near the center of the bogie; otherwise the cars will collide in curves. Cover the side walls with tiles, use trans-clear parts for the window band, and use sideways SNOT building so the windows sit flush with the roof. Stack small Technic parts and rounded plates on the roof for air-conditioning units, creating a stepped, layered look.

## Cab and Interior

Build the cab as a removable module so the interior can be shown. Install the windshield as a large trans-clear part at a slant, and use curved pieces on both sides to draw a streamlined nose. Inside, add a steering wheel, a dashboard and two rows of passenger seats; 1x2 plates plus armrest parts make quick work of the seats. Put trans-yellow parts in the bumper for headlights and trans-red parts for taillights. These are small details, but they lift the whole model.

## FAQ

**Q: What should I do if the wheels slip on the track?**
A: First check that the wheelset is fully engaged with the track; a mismatched gauge will make the inner wheel rub the rail. If needed, add rubber rings to the drive axle for more friction, and make sure there is half a stud of clearance on each side of the bogie.

**Q: How do I stop the pantograph from falling over?**
A: If the frame is too heavy, add Technic support beams at the base and fix the base to the roof hinge part. Form the top with two curved pieces to make the bow, and lower the overall center of gravity so it will not tip.

**Q: Why do the cars collide in curves?**
A: The articulation point must sit near the center of the bogie. Split the body into two or three cars and join them with a cross axle plus hinge parts. For long cars, give each car its own bogie so they do not collide or break in curves.

**Q: What track gauge does a brick tram need?**
A: Most commercial brick track is six studs wide, so use matching train wheelsets or flanged wheels. With a four-stud wheel gauge, the inner wheel tends to rub in curves. For power, use a gearbox and rubber rings to increase friction.

Three common fixes: wheel slip, a falling pantograph and a broken articulation joint. Check that the wheelset is fully engaged with the track, and add rubber rings to the drive axle if needed. If the pantograph frame is too heavy, add Technic support beams at the base. Hinge parts have limited load capacity, so long cars should each have their own bogie. Finally, keep the color scheme consistent; white, red and gray come closest to real tram liveries.
