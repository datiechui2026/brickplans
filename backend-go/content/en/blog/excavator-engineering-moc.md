# Caterpillar Excavator MOC: 1,560-Piece 1:20 Technic Build

**Difficulty**: ★★★★☆ (intermediate)
**Piece count**: 1,560
**Recommended brands**: Double Eagle, Mould King
**Finished scale**: 1:20 | **Estimated build time**: 8–10 hours

Engineering machinery is the theme that shows off mechanical beauty best in Technic MOCs, and the excavator is the most three-dimensional of the lot — digging arm, slewing platform and tracks, all three systems required. This Caterpillar excavator blueprint takes a 1:20 scale and a 1,560-piece budget and turns all three systems into working, movable structures, which makes it a solid challenge for an intermediate builder stepping further into Technic.

## 1. Accuracy: mechanical detail at 1:20

The classic Caterpillar livery is a bright yellow upper body over a black running gear. The blueprint puts yellow on the large blocks — counterweight, engine hood, cab — and concentrates black in the lower chassis, so the silhouette reads close to the real machine from a distance. 1:20 is a golden ratio here: roomy enough for detail without a parts-count explosion. The cab gets glass elements and an interior, the hood carries cooling grilles, and the rear counterweight is colour-separated. Overall length stays around 45cm, so it sits comfortably on a shelf or a desk.

## 2. Structure and movement: a three-joint digging arm

The digging arm is the heart of the design: boom, stick and bucket are all articulated, the slewing platform turns a full 360°, and the tracks drive independently. Multi-joint linkage matters for poseability — you can set it up digging, lifting, or slewed round to dump, instead of being locked into one fixed stance.

Structurally, the blueprint leans heavily on Double Eagle Technic parts to model hydraulic rams, using beams, axles and multi-stage gears to carry drive from the body out to the arm tip. That is the fun of this design: as you build, you can see exactly how force travels through the assembly, which gives it far more mechanical character than a static display model.

## 3. Parts and colour: choosing your Technic

Technic elements make up a clearly higher share of the 1,560 pieces than basic bricks do — normal for an engineering MOC. Worth knowing: Technic parts are more tolerance-sensitive, and how tight the cross axles and pins sit will decide whether the digging arm holds its pose.

## 4. Build notes

1. **Build the running gear first.** The tracks and chassis carry the most load. Get the chassis flat and square before the slewing platform goes on, or the whole machine will lean once the upper body's weight lands on it.
2. **Leave travel in the hydraulic rams.** Keep working clearance between the two beams that model each ram; pack them too tightly and they will bind solid.
3. **Don't over-mesh the gears.** Nine out of ten stiff drivetrains come from gears meshed too tight or an axle not pushed fully home. Turn the train by hand once before you close the covers.
4. **Centre the slewing platform.** Any offset in the platform's axis gets multiplied out at the arm tip. Once it is fitted, swing it a full turn each way and check the travel is even.
5. **Tension the tracks correctly.** They should hug the road wheels without being stretched tight — too loose and the chain jumps, too tight and it locks up.
