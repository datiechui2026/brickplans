# Brick Clock Tower MOC Review: Gothic Detail and Solid Build

Clock towers never seem to go out of fashion in the MOC world. This time we are looking at one of the more popular brick clock tower designs, breaking it down piece by piece: how faithful the exterior is, how well the frame holds up, and what it is actually like to build. It has picked up a lot of positive feedback from builders on brickplan.cn, and it is easy to see why.

## Exterior Design: Gothic Character, Detail Everywhere

The model takes a classic European Gothic clock tower as its starting point. The main colour scheme is warm yellow paired with off-white, accented with dark brown timber beams, which together recreate the feel of an old town square. The standout feature sits at the top: four clock faces, one on each side. The designer used printed elements instead of stickers, so the numerals and markers stay crisp, and the hands turn smoothly. It is the detail that defines the whole build.

The tower tapers as it rises, stepping inward level by level, and the smaller details are all present: flying buttresses, arched windows and projecting cornices. There is also a removable roof section, which lets you show off the interior floor plates and the bell mechanism. That combination of display value and hands-on access is a nice touch.

## Structural Design: Balance Between Rigidity and Clever Engineering

For any architectural MOC, a stable frame is the baseline requirement. This clock tower uses a skeleton of cross-interlocking construction reinforced with internal brick pillars, and the corners are handled solidly, so the model can be moved without falling apart. The spire transfers its load through four diagonal braces, which avoids the usual top-heavy problem that plagues tall towers.

Worth calling out: the designer left a connection point at the base for a mini street scene, so the tower can be combined with other builds into a full row of streets. That significantly expands the options for later modification, and several reviewers have singled it out as a highlight.

## The Building Experience: From Loose Parts to Finished Model

The full model runs to roughly 2,600 pieces, with a build time of about 6 to 8 hours. The steps are bagged in clear stages, and the instructions can be downloaded and viewed for free on brickplan.cn. Colour grouping between bags is well organised, so finding parts is efficient. The tower's symmetrical structure means a moderate amount of repetition: you get the relaxing, meditative stretches of wall building, but also the more demanding challenge of the spire at the top. The pacing is well judged.

## Playability and Modification Potential

Beyond static display, this clock tower leaves room for light bricks and mechanism linkages. Add a motor and you can produce an hourly chime or a flashing light effect. For more advanced builders, the designer has also released renders of the internal structure, making it straightforward to add height or convert the model into an astronomical clock tower. It is a rare design that offers meaningful scope beyond the finished model.
