# LEGO Compatibility Guide: Mixing Brands, Parts and Colors

Almost every new builder asks the same thing early on: **is LEGO compatible with other bricks?** The question really covers two cases — whether LEGO can be mixed with third-party, mostly Chinese-made bricks, and whether LEGO's own product lines can be combined. The answer to both is "yes, with conditions." Here is how compatibility actually works, which brands deliver it, and what to watch for in clutch and color.

## 1. The Short Answer: Compatible, But Know the Standard

Direct answer: **nearly all mainstream brick brands are compatible.** LEGO defined the global stud system — stud spacing, the brick-to-plate height ratio and the underlying unit system are all published standards, and the vast majority of Chinese-made bricks are produced to that same standard, so they clip together. But "compatible" has two levels. **Generic parts** — standard bricks, plates, slopes — are essentially 100% compatible. **Specialised parts** — purpose-built gears, unusual hinges, printed pieces — depend on the exact specification and are not guaranteed to interchange.

## 2. Under the Hood: The Stud System and Unit Conversion

To judge compatibility you first need the unit system. Bricks are measured in **multiples of one stud width**: a 1x1 brick is roughly one stud wide, and in height **one brick equals three plates**. That ratio is the foundation of every mix. When building a MOC, use **plates for fine thickness adjustment and bricks for the main structure** — the "one brick = three plates" rule is what lets the two line up, and it is the most common trick when combining Chinese bricks with LEGO. Once you have the conversion down, judging whether LEGO is compatible with LEGO, or with anything else, becomes straightforward.

## 3. The Compatible Brands: GaoZhuan, Double Eagle, SEMBO, StarBricks

Plenty of Chinese brands make compatible parts, each with a strength. **GaoZhuan** is known for precision; its standard bricks come closest to LEGO clutch and suit MOCs that need structural strength. **Double Eagle/CaDA** is strongest in technic: gears, links and differentials interchange with LEGO Technic. **SEMBO** has a rich parts range for dioramas and military subjects. **StarBricks** and **Qiji** are also tidy on everyday parts. Pick brands by subject: trust GaoZhuan for structure and Double Eagle/CaDA for mechanical work, and you save a lot of rework.

## 4. Clutch and Tolerances: Test Small Before You Build Big

Tolerances vary between brands, and this is where the compatibility question shows up most clearly: genuine LEGO has the tightest tolerance and the most even grip, while some third-party parts run tight or loose. Before mixing, **test-fit the handful of key parts you plan to use together** — especially hinges, technic pins and cross axles, which carry load. On structurally critical sections, stay with one brand and one tolerance so uneven grip does not loosen the build. Cross axles and gears are the toughest test of tolerance, so buy loose parts from brands with a good reputation.

## 5. Color Variation and Restocking Parts

Colors last. **The same color name can differ between brands and between production batches** — light gray and sand, for example, are visibly different between Chinese parts and LEGO. For symmetrical or color-critical visible areas, take parts **from the same brand and the same batch**; when restocking, don't go by color name alone — check a physical color card or a photo of the finished model. The rule for mixing: **structural parts by specification, not brand; cosmetic parts by color and batch.** That keeps the budget down without putting an obvious color mismatch on the finished model.

One-line summary: is LEGO compatible with LEGO and with other brands — generic parts yes, specialised parts depend on spec, clutch is settled by test-fitting, color by matching batches. ## FAQ

**Q: Are LEGO and Chinese-made bricks compatible?**
A: Yes. LEGO defined the global stud system — stud spacing, the brick-to-plate height ratio and the unit system are published standards, and most Chinese bricks follow them, so they can be mixed directly. Clutch, however, needs to be tested in practice.

**Q: Can different LEGO product lines be mixed?**
A: Yes. Standard LEGO System sets are fully interchangeable with each other; DUPLO large-format bricks use a different size and need adapter pieces; Technic is built on hole spacing and also needs adapters when mixed with System.

**Q: What should I watch when mixing parts from different brands?**
A: Test-fit the key parts against each other first, especially hinges, technic pins and cross axles, which carry load. On structurally critical sections, use parts from a single brand with consistent tolerance so mixed grip does not loosen the build.

**Q: Are color differences between brands large?**
A: Yes, they exist. The same color name can vary noticeably between brands and batches, and mid-tones such as light gray and sand show it most. For color-critical areas, take parts from the same brand and batch — and when restocking, don't rely on the color name alone.
