# How to Build a Chinese New Year MOC: Zodiac, Couplets, Dinner

Start with the short answer: a Spring Festival MOC only needs to nail one thing, the festive feel. Make the zodiac your theme, the couplets and door gods your facade, the reunion dinner your scene's core, and then light it up with red lanterns and warm lamps.

Chinese New Year is one of the richest subjects you can pick for a Chinese-themed brick MOC. The red-and-gold palette is celebratory on its own, and elements like zodiac animals, couplets, the reunion dinner and red lanterns are both instantly recognizable and easy to render in brick. A strong Spring Festival MOC does not need complicated structure. Get the festive feel right and the build stands out on its own. Below is a buildable plan covering scene ideas, detail work and lighting.

## Zodiac Theme: From Animal to Auspicious Beast

The zodiac is the most central subject for a Spring Festival MOC. When you build a zodiac animal, start with curved bricks and slope bricks to shape the body outline, then use 1x1 tiles to pick out the eyes and pattern details. If your part supply is limited, go for a simplified chibi build: oversized head, small body, rounded lines. It is often more recognizable than a realistic attempt. Builders who want to push further can try an auspicious beast composition, pairing the zodiac animal with a base of auspicious clouds, or using SNOT techniques to suggest the creature riding the clouds in motion. You can also work the current year's sexagenary cycle element into the same model, for example by spelling the year in brick on the side of the base, which gives the piece extra commemorative value.

## Couplets and Door Gods: Detail on the Chinese Facade

The Chinese facade is the face of your Spring Festival MOC. Couplets can be built from red tiles with thin white strips, and you can hang four small red lanterns from the lintel. If you do not have printed parts for door gods or the fu character, a red baseplate with gold 1x1 studs arranged into the outline of the character works well. Use dark red bricks for the pillars on both sides of the doorway and add gold accents, and the entrance immediately has the presence of a grand household.

## Reunion Dinner Scene: Festive Flavor on the Table

The reunion dinner is the most story-driven scene in a Spring Festival MOC. Build a round table on a turntable piece and fill it with dishes: stack 1x1 round tiles for dumplings, use hemisphere pieces for lion's head meatballs, and transparent pieces for soup bowls. Seat minifigures around it in red tang suits or scarves, place a hot pot build under the table, and show the steam with white transparent pieces. The more specific the details, the more lived-in the scene feels. You can assign the seats around the round table to family members, with the elders in the seat of honor and the children on low stools; minifigure expressions and hair pieces can also suggest different characters. If the scene has enough space, build an open kitchen alongside it, with a stove, steamer and cutting board in a row, so the everyday warmth of the meal carries from the kitchen all the way to the table.

## Red Lanterns and Lighting: Bring the Scene to Life

Red lanterns are the finishing touch of a Spring Festival scene. Build the lantern body from red curved slope bricks, add gold round plates at the top and bottom, and make the tassel from a string piece or flexible hose. If you can, add lighting components: hide a warm yellow light plate inside the lantern, or run a light strip under the eaves to simulate warm nighttime light. Once the lights come on, the festive feel of the whole MOC doubles immediately.

## Color Scheme and Parts List Suggestions

Use Chinese red and gold as the dominant colors for a Spring Festival MOC, supported by dark brown and off-white. Common parts include curved bricks, slope bricks, 1x1 round tiles, transparent pieces and printed pieces. Write a parts list before you start and check whether you have enough red bricks: a Spring Festival theme consumes far more red than an ordinary street scene, and stocking up in advance saves you from running short halfway through the build.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I get the festive feel into a Spring Festival MOC?**
A: Focus on four things: use a zodiac animal or auspicious beast as the theme, couplets and door gods as the facade, the reunion dinner as the scene's core, and red lanterns with warm light for atmosphere. The structure does not have to be complex; if the festive feel lands, the build works.

**Q: How do I make the couplets and door gods?**
A: For couplets, attach thin red plates vertically on both sides of the door frame and pair them with a gold horizontal scroll. For door gods, use printed pieces or minifigures with red backgrounds on the door panels. Keep them symmetrical left to right and at matching heights, and the Chinese facade comes together immediately.

**Q: How should I arrange the reunion dinner scene?**
A: Put the round table in the center and spread the dishes around it. Use dark brown pieces for the tabletop, add small builds like fish, dumplings and a soup pot, and seat a ring of minifigures around it. Keep the tabletop elements to five to eight pieces so it looks plentiful without becoming cluttered.

**Q: How do I use red lanterns and lighting?**
A: Build lanterns from red curved or round pieces and put a warm-colored light piece inside. A color temperature of 2700 to 3200K works best, lighting the entrance and the dining table as the focal points. That gives the strongest festive atmosphere; avoid cool white light.
