# Milk Tea Shop MOC Guide: Counter, Cups and Signage

A milk tea shop is the youngest-looking storefront on any street scene: a queue of minifigures, towers of stacked cups, a neon sign buzzing over the door. It is the kind of build people photograph and share. If you want your block to feel busier and more modern, a solid **milk tea shop MOC blueprint** is the place to start. This guide walks through blueprint selection, the counter, the cups themselves and the signage, so you end up with a shop that makes passers-by want to order.

## 1. Choosing the Blueprint: From Single Bay to Corner Flagship

### Footprint and Scale
Milk tea shops are usually built at 1:48 street scene scale. A 16x16 single bay is enough to fit a takeaway window plus a small interior counter. If you want the "internet-famous" look, choose a corner L-shaped blueprint and add a large floor-to-ceiling window; using curved transparent pieces at the corner makes the transition much more refined.

### Checking Parts and Colors
Milk tea shops lean heavily on transparent and printed elements: transparent panels for cup bodies, straws, and printed sealing-film pieces all need to be in stock. For the main color scheme, cream white, matcha green and caramel brown are recommended — soft, and they photograph well. Before you order, confirm you have enough transparent cup pieces so you do not run short halfway through the build.

## 2. The Counter: The Traffic Hub of the Whole Shop

### Countertop and Work Area
Build the counter four bricks high and six studs deep, with a glossy light-colored plate for the top surface. On the work area, place a sealing machine (a cylinder piece combined with a round plate), a boba tub (a dark round bucket with a brown round plate on top) and syrup bottles (small round bottle pieces). The sense of order appears immediately.

### The Takeaway Window
Open a takeaway window facing the street, using a small openable door plus an extended countertop plate. Put a cat-shaped or duck-shaped "queue mascot" minifigure under the window — the detail instantly brings the build to life and gives the street scene a story to walk into.

## 3. Milk Tea Cups: Stacking a National Drink Out of Parts

### Cup Body and Seal
The cup is the soul of the build. For the transparent cup, stack two layers of 1x1 transparent round plates to make the body, then cap it with a printed round plate as the sealing film. Use small dark round studs for the "boba" — two or three sunk at the bottom is plenty. A full row of cups on the rack can hold six to eight cups, which maxes out the display effect.

### Cup Rack and Pickup Spot
Set a cup rack on the inner side of the counter, using 2x2 round plates plus post pieces to make tiered trays. Do not forget the size difference between a "medium" and a "large": give the large cup one extra layer of round plate and the display immediately gains depth, while the minifigure's pickup path reads more realistically.

## 4. Signage and Overall Atmosphere

### Three-Dimensional Sign
A milk tea shop sign works best as a cup-shaped 3D sign: build the cup body from cylindrical pieces and add a domed cap on top, suspended above the facade. Paired with a light strip, it glows beautifully at night and becomes the highlight of the whole street after dark.

### Neon and Color Scheme
You can add neon tube pieces to the walls (transparent colored plates stacked sideways), and use a contrasting color on the door frame. For the ground, combine light gray and pink-gray tiles — very photogenic — and once the warm yellow light strip comes on, the atmosphere lands instantly.

## 5. Build Order and Practical Tips

### Suggested Sequence
Walls and baseplate first, then the counter and cup racks, and finally the sign, the light strip and the minifigures.

### Common Problems
Transparent cup pieces scratch easily, so wear gloves or use tweezers during assembly. Reinforce any cantilevered sign parts so they do not bend under load. And match the takeaway window height to your minifigures so nobody ends up taller than the window they are ordering at.
