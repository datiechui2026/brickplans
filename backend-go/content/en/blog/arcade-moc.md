# Brick Arcade MOC Guide: Cabinets, Neon and Bar Details

Short version first: an arcade scene lives or dies on three things — the cabinets, the neon, and the bar. Line the cabinets up to create depth, build the neon from transparent fluorescent parts backed by a light strip, finish with the bar, then let the lighting do the rest.

An arcade is one of the most atmospheric subjects you can build in a street-scene layout. Flickering neon, rows of cabinets, a dim counter in the back — the cyberpunk mood comes almost for free. You don't need a huge footprint for a brick arcade MOC. Nail the cabinets, neon and bar, add lighting, and you'll have a corner arcade that makes people want to walk in and drop a coin. Here's the breakdown, from the star of the show to the atmosphere around it.

## Arcade Cabinets: The Star of the Show

The cabinets are the absolute lead in a brick arcade MOC. The standard approach: build an upright cabinet body from dark bricks, set the screen as a black tile with a frame around it, make the joystick from a 1x1 cylinder with a round plate on top, and use colored 1x1 round plates for the buttons. Place two cabinets side by side facing the same direction, leaving standing room between them. For extra detail, add a printed sticker to the screen or use transparent parts to suggest screen glare. On the floor you can lay down a few arrow-direction stickers to mimic the traffic flow of a real arcade, and tuck a claw machine into the corner — a transparent canopy piece over a few small figures instantly adds playfulness.

## Neon: The Key to the Cyberpunk Mood

Neon is what defines the night-time character of an arcade MOC. The simplest method is to spell out the shop name and decorative lines with transparent fluorescent parts, lit from behind by a light plate underneath; a more advanced method runs a flexible light strip along the outline of the sign. Favor highly saturated colors — pink, purple, teal — so they contrast hard against dark walls. You don't need much neon: two or three accent points are enough, and too many just looks messy.

## Bar and Cashier Area: Extending the Scene

The bar is one of the easiest places to score points in an arcade MOC. Build the counter from dark bricks, put a cash register on top (a tile plus small pieces), drink cups (transparent cup pieces with straws), and shelving behind holding rows of "drinks." Above the bar you can hang a row of small pendant lights, or a little sign reading welcome, to make the scene feel complete. Use a different floor color between the bar and the cabinet area so the spatial layering reads clearly. Behind the bar, hang a price board too: white tiles with colored studs for the drinks and their prices. It's a small detail, but it does a lot for realism.

## Color Palette and Lighting Plan

Keep the base of an arcade MOC in dark blue, dark purple and black, with neon pink, teal and yellow as accents. Lighting is the soul: warm light simulates interior fixtures, cool light strips simulate neon, and alternating the two instantly produces depth. Transparent and fluorescent parts are essential, so stock up in advance — the worst outcome is running short on fluorescent pieces halfway through the sign.

## Pitfalls to Avoid and Ideas for Going Further

Common beginner mistakes: too many neon colors, which turns the scene into visual noise, and cabinets built out of scale so minifigures can't fit. Decide your module dimensions first and keep cabinet height around minifigure waist level. More advanced builders can give the cabinets working joysticks, build a hinged back wall that opens, or set the arcade MOC on a corner plot and link it with a bubble tea shop and an internet cafe into a single nightlife block — the storytelling gets much stronger.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I give a brick arcade MOC atmosphere?**
A: Focus on the three essentials: rows of cabinets for depth, neon for mood, and the bar to extend the scene. Offset the cabinets rather than lining them up perfectly — the layout then feels crowded and lively without looking stiff.

**Q: How do I get a real cyberpunk look from the neon?**
A: Build the tubes from transparent fluorescent orange and fluorescent green parts, and hide an LED strip behind them. Use no more than two neon colors and keep everything else dimmed down with dark gray and black building colors — that's what makes the neon pop.

**Q: How do I build an arcade cabinet that actually reads as one?**
A: Use an upright body with a slanted control panel: large single-color pieces for the side panels, a transparent dark or printed piece where the screen goes, and two small round pieces angled on the control panel as buttons. Get the proportions right and it's instantly recognizable.

**Q: How should colors and lighting work together?**
A: Dark base, neon accents. Build the structure in dark gray and pure black to lower the overall brightness, and let the neon and light strips handle the highlights. A cooler color temperature (above 5000K) better matches how an electronic screen looks.
