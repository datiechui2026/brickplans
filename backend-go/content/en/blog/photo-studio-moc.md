# Photo Studio MOC Review: 5,600-Piece Retro Studio With LED Lights

## The Verdict First

This photo studio MOC uses 5,600 bricks and measures 38 cm wide, 24 cm tall and 22 cm deep. Plan on roughly 28 hours to build it, at a reference price of about 420 yuan. The short version: it is the most interactive mid-size MOC in the street scene category right now, with built-in LED fill lights, a rotating backdrop and a tripod camera. Value for money is strong. If you like diorama-style builds and want something that photographs well once finished, this is a safe pick, and the instructions are available on the brickplan.cn platform.

## 1. Scene and Detail Accuracy

The whole build recreates a retro photo studio. The shop sign is a flip-over neon "photo studio" lettering panel, and three vintage cameras of different eras sit in the display window. Indoors, the layout follows a real shooting studio: four swappable backdrop patterns (solid color, starry sky, red wall, forest), and two LED fill lights that switch on and off independently, throwing a warm yellow light that gives the scene plenty of atmosphere. The floor is laid with 600 tan 1x2 bricks standing in for wood planks, and the counter, seating and prop crates are all present. Every corner holds up to close inspection.

## 2. Play Features and Moving Parts

Playability is where this MOC shines. The camera is a tripod plus gimbal assembly: the lens extends forward and back, and the shutter button actually presses. The backdrop rotates through four positions on a gear axle at the top, so each click of the gear swaps in a different background. The LED wiring is routed through the walls and is completely invisible from the outside, and two button cell batteries keep the lights on for about 40 hours. Eight minifigures come as standard, including a photographer, customers, a photo editor and a couple posing for wedding photos, with well-judged poses and expressions.

## 3. Building Experience and Value

Difficulty sits at medium-high. The parts are split across 6 numbered bags with roughly 400 pages of instructions, about 450 bricks per bag. The only genuinely tricky steps are the printed parts on the rooftop sign and the light strip routing; work through them slowly in instruction order, and expect each of those two steps to take an extra 30 minutes. Per-brick cost comes out around 0.075 yuan, roughly 25% cheaper than an official street scene of the same size, and you get lighting play on top. The downsides: it occupies about 0.5 square meters, and the LED battery compartment is at the rear, so changing batteries means lifting the roof panel. Overall it justifies the price and is worth picking up for street scene builders.
