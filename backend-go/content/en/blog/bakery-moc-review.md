# Bakery MOC Review: 1800-Piece Street Corner Shop Test Build

Food themes are everywhere in modular street layouts, but a bakery hits differently: it carries its own early-morning warmth. For this **bakery MOC review**, we built a corner bakery with an awning exactly as the instructions specify, then worked through the display cases, the oven and the bread rack one by one. If you want to add a sunrise to your street, here is the straight answer.

## 1. Instructions and Parts: A Warm First Impression

The instructions use **1:48 street scene scale**. The whole shop runs about 1,800 pieces, which is light for a modular, and it is split into 6 numbered bags. Steps are clear, and the sections with lots of curved surfaces get extra notes on stud engagement direction, so even first-time builders are unlikely to attach anything backwards. The palette leans on **wheat yellow, cream white, warm brown and coffee**, so the box already smells like fresh bread when you open it. The best part is the printed elements: the round shop sign, the menu chalkboard and the paper bags are all printed rather than stickered, so they hold up to play and will not peel at the edges. Missing parts were rare. Instructions and parts score **9/10**.

## 2. Structure and Facade: The Awning Is the Best-Looking Eave on the Street

The shop footprint is 16x16 studs. The ground floor is storefront plus back kitchen; the upper floor is a living space with windows. The facade is packed with highlights:

- **Awning frontage**: red-and-white striped slope bricks form a wavy awning that overhangs the facade, making it the most recognizable eave on the block;
- **Glass display cases**: there is a **bread display case** on each side of the door, built from transparent panels and thin frames, with room for three shelves of "bread" inside, all clearly visible through the case doors;
- **Lattice windows**: the upper floor uses white frames plus transparent panels for a clean, tidy grid window that lets light through.

Structurally, the walls are reinforced with inner plating, and the second floor carries weight solidly with no bulging. The only caution is the cantilevered awning, which needs careful handling when you press down on it. Structure scores **8.5/10**.

## 3. Playability Core: An Oven That "Bakes" and a Bread Rack

As a **modular street building**, what won me over is the **back-kitchen oven interaction**. The arched brick oven uses a hemispherical piece as the chamber, with orange transparent panels at the top to suggest the fire, and the baker minifigure holds a tray to slide loaves in. Next to it, a metallic upright oven has a door that opens and closes and will take a whole baguette. Even better is the **bread rack detail**: a three-tier wooden rack beside the counter, each tier holding 4x4 "bread", with wheat-yellow round plates plus small dark round pieces for the flour dusting, and long bars with scored cuts for the baguettes. The oven-to-rack-to-counter flow is complete. The rack does not rotate and the oven does not actually heat, but the "just out of the oven" feeling beats most modular shops. Playability scores **9/10**.

## 4. Lighting and Accuracy

On accuracy, nothing is missing: awning, display cases, oven and bread rack are all there, and it reads instantly as a standard European bakery with very high recognizability. Add a warm yellow light strip and the "bread" in the window glows, paired with the sign light, pushing the early-morning street atmosphere to the max. Lighting scores **8.5/10**.

## 5. Overall Score and Buying Advice

| Category | Score |
| --- | --- |
| Instructions and parts | 9/10 |
| Structure and facade | 8.5/10 |

| Overall recommendation | ★★★★★ |

**Verdict: worth buying.** This **bakery MOC** sits at medium difficulty (roughly 4 to 5 hours), and a beginner can finish it with guidance from an experienced builder. It suits players who want to add morning bustle to a street layout, players who love display cases and back-kitchen detail, and players who prefer printed elements over stickers. The weak points are the slightly delicate cantilevered awning pieces and the fixed bread rack, but the flaws are minor. It remains one of the most polished **modular street** builds we have handled recently.
