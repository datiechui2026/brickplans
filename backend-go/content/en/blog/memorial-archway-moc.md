# Brick Archway MOC: Build a Three-Bay Chinese Paifang

Here is the short version: this paifang MOC uses about 3,800 pieces, measures roughly 48 cm wide and 32 cm tall, takes around 10 hours to build at 2-3 hours a day, and comes in at a budget of about 600 yuan. The design is a three-bay, four-column archway — the central opening is wide enough for a small car and a minifigure to pass through, while the two side bays are slightly narrower — and it includes stone columns, architrave beams, dougong bracket sets and a full roof. The instructions come from the brickplan platform, and the parts list is split into three groups (columns, beams, roof), which makes this a good entry point for anyone new to Chinese architecture.

**Step 1: Columns and foundation**

Start with a long baseplate as the foundation. The four main columns are stacked from dark grey tall column pieces, and each one sits on beige pieces that read as a stone plinth, adding visual weight and stability at the base. The columns stand in two rows of two — front pair and back pair — with roughly 12 cm between the rows, and that spacing sets the depth of the archway. Leave cross-axle sockets at the top of each column so the architrave can be dropped on later. This stage uses about 1,100 pieces. Check the columns for plumb as you go and square them up with right-angle pieces.

**Step 2: Architrave and dougong**

The architrave spans all four columns: two layers of long plates form the cross beams, and the central bay carries a printed plaque piece. Underneath the beams, build the dougong bracket sets — small bricks stepped outward one layer at a time, each layer half a stud wider than the one below, so the corbelled stack carries the eaves. Spread the brackets across the three bays, denser in the main bay and sparser in the side bays; that rhythm is what makes the model read as Chinese architecture rather than a generic gate. About 900 pieces.

**Step 3: Roof and decoration**

The roof is built in three sections — a wide roof over the main bay and narrower roofs over the side bays — with dark grey tile pieces laid at a gentle slope and inverted slope pieces trimming the eaves. Small ridge beast pieces sit at each end of the main ridge, and the eave corners turn up slightly. Outside the columns you can add a pair of small stone lion pieces, or red lantern pieces for a festive look. This stage uses about 1,200 pieces and is the part of the build that delivers the most visual payoff.

**Step 4: Assembly and placement**

Stack the three modules — roof, architrave and column base — and check that the seams line up. The centre of gravity is high, so stick anti-slip pads under the baseplate or clamp the column bottoms with base clamp pieces. It looks right at the entrance to a street scene, in front of a temple, or at the end of an old-town road. Work through the decoration parts list on the brickplan instructions page and the archway is done.

## FAQ

**Q: How much does this archway MOC cost?**

A: About 600 yuan for the whole thing. The four main columns and the tile pieces account for roughly 250 yuan, the printed plaque and decorative pieces another 100 yuan, and the rest is ordinary bricks — good value compared with other Chinese architecture builds.

**Q: Won't it fall over, being that tall?**

A: The centre of gravity is high, but if you build the plinths and baseplate solidly as the instructions show, it stands fine. Carry it by supporting both ends of the baseplate rather than grabbing a column; for long-term display, put anti-slip pads underneath or cover it with a display case.

**Q: How do I fix it to a floor or a scene?**

A: The baseplate is a large stud plate, so it can bite directly onto your scene baseplate. On a wooden desk, use double-sided tape or anti-slip pads under the baseplate. Don't glue it down permanently — you will want to take it apart and rebuild later.

**Q: How is this different from a city gate MOC?**

A: A paifang is an open structure — just columns, beams and a roof — and minifigures can walk through the opening. A city gate is a closed wall with an arched passage and a gate tower you can stand figures on. The archway needs fewer parts and builds faster, so it suits a scene entrance; the gate suits the main body of a walled town.

**Q: Can a beginner build this?**

A: Very much so — about 10 hours and you're finished, which makes it one of the most approachable Chinese architecture MOCs out there. The dougong section needs a little patience, but if you follow the brickplan drawings and step outward one layer at a time, it works out.
