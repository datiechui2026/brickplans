# Stickers vs Printed Bricks: An MOC Detail Test

Whether a brick model reads as "right" and stays worth looking at usually comes down to the details. A bare brick and a brick carrying a shop sign are worlds apart in expressiveness. The two mainstream ways to get those details — **brick stickers** and printed parts — each have trade-offs. In this review we tested both for material, expressiveness and durability, so you can make the right call and push your build's details further.

## 1. The Fundamental Difference Between Stickers and Printed Parts

**Brick stickers** means applying an adhesive decal to a brick face to simulate a pattern. They are cheap, can carry very complex artwork, and a single sticker can transform an entire wall. **Printed parts** have the pattern printed directly onto the brick surface, fused with the part — nothing raised to the touch, and edges that will never lift. The comparison is straightforward: printed parts look good longer and last, but cost more per piece and come in limited designs; stickers are cheap and flexible but depend on application skill and have a limited lifespan. Experienced builders mix both — stickers for large signage, liveries and backdrops, printed parts for minifigure faces, badges and key identifiers.

## 2. Sticker Test: Material and Expressiveness

Stickers on the market fall into two broad types: **standard paper stickers** and **waterproof high-tack stickers**. Paper stickers are cheap and reproduce colour well, but they hate water and oil, and a damp touch easily wrinkles them. Waterproof high-tack stickers cost a bit more but hold better on curved and irregular parts. Our testing found that **base colour** matters just as much: on dark bricks, stickers let the seams and colour variation show through, while light bricks give the best result — so use light base bricks wherever a sticker is doing important work. The precision of the die-cut edge also varies enormously. Good stickers have clean, flat edges with no glue squeeze-out; cheap ones fray, which looks bad up close. When buying, look for **brick stickers** described as cleanly cut with an even adhesive layer — that is what protects your detail quality.

## 3. Printed Part Test: Clarity and Durability

The core measures for printed parts are **clarity and adhesion**. Mainstream printed parts we tested had sharp pattern edges and saturated colour, with even tiny text and fine lines legible — something stickers struggle to match. On adhesion, a properly made printed part survives repeated assembly and disassembly without losing paint, but low-quality parts scuff easily when pulled apart, especially when they sit in structural positions. Another printed-part advantage is **light transmission**: print on a transparent brick, light it from behind, and signs and portholes look far better than anything a sticker can do. The drawbacks are equally clear — high unit cost and fixed designs, so you cannot customise your own signage.

## 4. Which Option for Which Situation

Based on our testing, here are practical combinations. For **street-scene signage**, use printed parts or transparent printed parts — light them and they look their best. For **large backdrops and liveries** (wall advertising, spaceship wing markings, base signage), use stickers; the value is unbeatable. For **minifigure details** (faces, chest badges, printed skin), use printed parts only — stickers on minifigures lift at the edges. For **temporary or prototype builds**, start with stickers, confirm the look, then upgrade to printed parts. Remember one rule: **printed parts where a surface is touched often, exposed outdoors or needs to transmit light; stickers where the area is large, low-cost and replaceable.** That balances detail and budget.

## 5. Application Technique and Care

Half of a good sticker result is technique. Clean the brick face of dust before applying. Use tweezers or a squeegee to align it, then press from one end to the other slowly to avoid air bubbles. If you misplace it, peel it up carefully and re-set it — do not yank. On care: **brick stickers** dislike humidity and sunlight, so keep display cabinets out of direct sun, and dust them with a dry soft cloth rather than scrubbing with a damp one. Printed parts are durable but should still not be pulled apart with force over and over. Master these habits and your details will stay crisp for years.

## Final Thoughts

Neither stickers nor printed parts are inherently better — only better suited.
