# Brick Gift Guide: What to Buy by Budget and Recipient

Short version: lock in the budget first, then pick the series. Under 100 yuan buys a small, thoughtful set, 100-300 yuan is the mainstream safe zone, 300-500 yuan gets you a licensed collector-grade model, and from there you match the set to the person and the theme.

## Set the Budget Before You Pick a Series

The easiest mistake to make when giving bricks as a gift is buying the most expensive thing instead of the right thing. Budget always comes first, because brick pricing spreads enormously, from boxed sets costing a few dozen yuan to flagship models running into the thousands. Framing the budget narrows the field fast. My tiers: under 100 yuan suits a small token gift, 100-300 yuan is the mainstream safe zone, 300-500 yuan buys a mid-range set with a licensed IP or technic functions, and above 500 yuan is for hardcore builders or a major occasion. Beyond money, weigh the recipient's age and building experience, since those decide whether the set's difficulty actually fits.

## The Four Budget Tiers

**Under 100 yuan:** favor small car models, mini street scenes or keychain sets. These run 100-300 pieces and take around two hours to finish, which makes them ideal for the everyday small gift between colleagues or classmates.

**100-300 yuan:** the golden zone. Entry-level technic race cars, medium-sized flower bouquets and classic street-scene buildings all sit here, and any of them is presentable for a friend or an older relative.

**300-500 yuan:** you can move up to licensed IP sets (mid-size Star Wars or Harry Potter releases, for example) or a mid-size technic model with power functions. The sense of occasion is noticeably stronger.

**Above 500 yuan:** large street scenes, UCS-class vehicles or flagships with a thousand-plus pieces. These suit birthdays, anniversaries and other important dates, but confirm the recipient has both the time and the space to build them.

## Match the Gift to the Recipient

Different recipients need completely different logic.

**Kids (6-12):** prioritize large-granularity parts or sets marked 4+, which have bigger pieces and fewer steps and avoid the choking risk of small parts. Animal and vehicle themes work well.

**Students:** budgets are usually tight, so pick a mid-to-low priced set with high playability, such as something that transforms or has working mechanisms, and that still looks good on a shelf once finished.

**Display-oriented adults:** builders who care about the finished look tend to prefer high-visual-appeal models. Flower bouquet series, bonsai and jewellery-box sets are the popular picks here, since the finished build goes straight onto the shelf as home decor.

**Hardcore builders:** give them what they actually want. Technic flagships, out-of-production reissues or obscure MOC instructions land far better than a generic set. Do a bit of quiet research beforehand to find out which set they have been eyeing.

## Choosing by Theme When You Are Still Unsure

If budget and recipient both leave you stuck, choosing by theme is the reliable fallback. Street scene themes (cafés, flower shops, bookshops) work across almost every age group, and the finished model carries its own display value. Bouquets and botanical series look great and suit almost anyone, making them the safest card to play. Licensed IP series (Star Wars, Marvel, Harry Potter, Disney) work when you already know the person's taste, because picking the right IP is picking the right sentiment. Technic series suit people who enjoy hands-on assembly and mechanical principles, and go down especially well with male friends or anyone in a STEM field. One thing to avoid: brick blind boxes have become popular, but they are a poor formal gift, since the randomness makes duplicate pieces likely.

## Gift-Giving Checklist

1. Verify the set number. Buy through official brand channels so you do not end up with a counterfeit or knockoff.
2. Confirm the piece count printed on the box, so a missing part does not stall the build halfway through and spoil the fun.
3. If the recipient is a beginner, do not hand them a thousand-piece set. Start at medium difficulty and build the interest first.
4. A sorting and storage box makes a practical companion gift that shows some thought.
5. Warn them about build time. A large set can take eight hours or more, so say up front that there is no need to finish it in one sitting.

Get those five right and the gift is very unlikely to fall flat. Hopefully this guide helps you pick a set that makes the recipient's eyes light up and passes the joy of building along.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I choose bricks as a gift?**

A: Start with the budget. Under 100 yuan suits a small token gift, 100-300 yuan is the mainstream safe zone, and 300-500 yuan buys a licensed collector-grade set. Fix the budget first, then choose a theme, and you are unlikely to go wrong.

**Q: What bricks should I give a child?**

A: Choose by age. From 3-6, go for large-granularity parts or entry-level themed sets; from 7 up, city and technic series become viable. Avoid sets with too many small parts or too high a difficulty, since a child who cannot finish a build quickly loses interest.

**Q: How do I pick bricks for an adult?**

A: Match their interest and the setting. Architecture, floral or landmark models suit someone who wants a display piece, technic suits the mechanically minded, and licensed IP sets suit anyone with an attachment to the source material. Adult builders care more about how the finished model looks and how reusable the parts are.

**Q: What should I watch out for when giving bricks?**

A: Three things: check whether the person actually enjoys building rather than just displaying, verify the piece count and the estimated build time, and avoid sets that need extra parts bought separately. Add a handwritten card and the gift carries a lot more warmth.
