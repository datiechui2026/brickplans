# How to Choose a Brick Display Cabinet: Dust, Light, Shelves

Start with the conclusion: match the cabinet to your collection before you pick a size. Modular street scenes and large vehicles need shelves with real depth — 30 cm or more per shelf is the usual recommendation. Small dioramas and minifigures work better in a shallow cabinet with dividers. Then add dust sealing and layered lighting.

## First, Decide What You're Displaying

Every builder eventually faces the same question: where do the finished models go? A **brick display cabinet** is not a case of bigger is better. What matters first is the shape of your collection — large street scenes, Technic supercars, or a crowd of small scenes and minifigures?

Street scenes and big vehicles need deep shelves. Small scenes suit a shallow cabinet with dividers. Minifigures deserve a dedicated minifigure shelf. A good method: pull out the ten models you most want on display, measure length, width and height for each, and work backwards to the cabinet dimensions. Do it in that order and you avoid both failures — the model that will not fit, and the cabinet that looks empty.

## Dust Sealing: The Real Reason to Buy a Cabinet

Dust protection is the single biggest argument for a **brick display cabinet**. Studs, gaps and tiles trap dust, and once it settles it is genuinely hard to clean out. Sealing matters more than looks.

Check three points when you inspect a cabinet:

- How tightly the door meets the frame
- Whether there is a gasket where glass meets the frame
- Whether the back panel is closed

Doors with magnetic or clip-on gaskets hold dust back far better than plain sliding doors. On a tight budget, you can add self-adhesive weatherstrip to the door gaps of an ordinary cabinet plus a brush strip along the bottom, and the improvement is immediate. If dust is a constant battle, put a small low-power desiccant box inside the cabinet — especially useful in humid southern regions, where it helps prevent parts from going mouldy and stickers from curling.

## Lighting: Making the Models Read

Lighting directly changes how good a finished model looks. There are three common setups: a top LED strip, per-shelf strips, and spotlights. A top strip gives even overall light. Per-shelf strips mounted along the front edge of each shelf highlight each level individually. Spotlights are for singling out one large MOC.

Watch the color temperature. Neutral light around 4000K reproduces brick colors most faithfully; too warm and white parts turn yellow, too cool and everything looks cheap. Hide strips under the front lip of each shelf or inside the front frame so you never look straight into the source — visible LEDs blow out photos. Remote-dimmable strips cost a bit more but are far more convenient day to day.

## Shelf Layout and Load Planning

Lay out shelves by weight. Large street scenes and Technic models are the heaviest, so they belong on the lowest shelf. Mid-size builds go in the middle, light scenes and minifigures up top.

Check the load rating. Standard glass shelves carry limited weight; for heavy builds choose solid wood shelves or tempered glass. For height, leave at least 20% clearance above each model so you can lift it out and add height later. If your collection keeps growing, a cabinet with adjustable shelf heights is far more flexible than replacing the whole unit for one new build.

## DIY Options: Low-Cost Upgrades

If a purpose-built cabinet is out of budget, there are several reliable DIY routes:

- An IKEA glass cabinet plus weatherstrip — the classic option, cheap and good looking
- A bookshelf plus acrylic dust covers for smaller models
- Shelving units with clear PVC strip curtains for large storage-style displays

Acrylic covers are made to measure; for a few dozen yuan a single model gets its own private gallery. The two non-negotiables in any DIY build are sealing and tip-over safety: anchor the cabinet to the wall, always, and especially when it holds heavy models.

## Budget and Buying Advice

**Brick display cabinets** run from a few hundred to a few thousand yuan. Beginners can get by with a small tempered-glass cabinet at 300-600 yuan plus a top LED strip. Intermediate builders should look at 800-1500 yuan for a mid-size cabinet with gaskets, adjustable shelves and per-shelf strips. Heavy collectors can go straight to a large solid-wood-framed unit with custom lighting.

Do not buy on looks alone. Sealing, load capacity and lighting are what determine how the cabinet performs over years. When it arrives, clear one shelf, test it with your heaviest model, and only then set out the rest. A well-chosen cabinet turns your builds into the best decoration in the house.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I choose the size of a brick display cabinet?**
A: Start from your collection. Street scenes and large vehicles need deep shelves — 30 cm or more per shelf. Small scenes and minifigures suit a shallow cabinet with dividers, and a shelf height of about 1.2x the model height looks better long term.

**Q: Does a display cabinet really need dust sealing?**
A: Yes, ideally. Dust sealing is the core value of a display cabinet. Brick surfaces collect dust and are hard to clean, and glass doors with gaskets noticeably reduce how often you have to clean — which matters most for models on permanent display.

**Q: How should I light a display cabinet?**
A: Layered lighting works best: one warm-white LED strip under the top of each shelf, at 3500-4500K to approximate natural light. Avoid a single strong spotlight, because specular highlights off the bricks make a model look cheap.

**Q: Can I build a display cabinet myself on a small budget?**
A: Yes. A common approach is a ready-made shelving unit plus custom acrylic panels, typically about half the cost of a finished cabinet. Add a metal stiffening bar under each shelf so heavy models do not warp it over time.
