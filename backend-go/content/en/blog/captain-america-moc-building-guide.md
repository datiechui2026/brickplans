# Captain America MOC: Shield, Suit & Minifigure Guide

When builders start on Marvel characters, Captain America is usually the first pick. The reason is simple: the blue, white and red color scheme is instantly recognizable, the round shield and helmet are distinctive shapes, and the parts are easy to source. This guide breaks down the build in three stages: shield, suit, then minifigure modification.

## The Round Shield: The Most Recognizable Piece

The shield is the soul of the character, and it is not hard to build. The core approach is working from the outside in: start with a large round dish or plate as the base, with a diameter between 6x6 and 8x8 working best. Ring the outer edge with 1x2 curved slopes to create a smooth rim, which instantly gives the shield visual thickness. For the inner ring, use red and white round plates or 1x1 round tiles to form the color bands, close the center with blue parts, and add the star emblem.

The star is the tricky part, and there are two common solutions. The first is to use a printed piece, which is easy and gives the highest accuracy. The second is to build the five-point star outline from tiles and slopes, which suits builders who want visible stud texture. You can also add two or three hinge pieces to the outer ring as a handle or attachment point, so the shield can be detached and held in a figure's hand.

## Suit Colors: How to Distribute Blue, White and Red

The Captain America suit follows a simple rule: blue on the upper body, white on the chest, red at the belt. Build the torso in layers of dark blue and medium blue, use white tiles on the chest to form the star-and-stripes area, and accent the belt, boots and gloves in red. A rough ratio is 60 percent blue, 30 percent white and 10 percent red. Too much red looks busy, and this is especially important to watch in any Captain America build.

For a more detailed result, use slopes on the shoulders and armor to transition between colors, which adds depth when viewed from the front. The white V shape on the helmet and the small wings on the sides can be built from 1x1 slopes or wedge pieces. They are small, but details like these are exactly what make the character readable at a glance.

## The Helmet: Angular Beats Rounded

Captain America's helmet is defined by clear angles at the forehead and cheeks. Stack 1x1 curved bricks for the main head shape, then use slopes on both sides of the forehead to press in the lines, and close the chin with a 1x2 inverted slope. Keep the color mostly dark blue, with the white V on the forehead and the small white pieces at the ears as focal points. Before building, study the helmet structure of an official minifigure and scale that proportion up; the result will rarely disappoint.

## Minifigure Modification: Turn an Official Figure Into Cap

Minifigure modification is the easiest entry point into Captain America building. If your goal is a more playable figure, this route is more flexible. The most common method is swapping parts on an official minifigure: use the existing head and torso, add a small star shield part yourself, and you have a Captain America in two or three steps. For higher accuracy, build the star on a white torso with 1x1 round tiles, then use stickers for the red and blue details. Trim sticker edges cleanly and press the edges down with a fingernail after applying; they will stay put for a long time.

## Multi-Character Scenes: Bringing Cap to Life

Once the solo figure is done, consider building a scene. Give Captain America a motorcycle or a standing flag, set a classic pose, and the display value changes immediately. If you want an Avengers assemble scene, watch your scale: when mixing minifigure scale with mech scale, control the height difference or the figures will look wrong standing together. This area offers the most freedom and the most room for creative play.
