# Temple of Heaven MOC Review: 2,680-Part Brick Blueprint

## Product Overview

The Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests at the Temple of Heaven is one of the high points of classical Chinese architecture, and its triple-eaved round roof is instantly recognizable. This review covers a **brick building blueprint** for that building: a Temple of Heaven MOC designed by "Gu Jian Xin Sheng," a veteran architectural MOC builder in the BrickPlans community. It is a 1:100 scale miniature, and the blueprint includes a detailed parts list and full step-by-step instructions. Since it went live on [BrickPlans](https://brickplan.cn), the file has passed 5,000 downloads, making it one of the platform's most popular Chinese-architecture MOC blueprints.

## Build Difficulty

**Difficulty rating:** ★★★★★

This sits in the high-difficulty bracket. The main challenges are the curved construction of the round eaves and the load-bearing design behind the three stacked roof tiers. The designer recommends at least six months of MOC experience. Expect 10 to 14 hours of building time, and splitting the work across two or three days is the sensible approach.

## Parts Review

The blueprint calls for roughly **2,680 pieces**, within a 2,200–3,000 pcs range. Recommended brands: **Gobricks** (the tightest tolerances, which keeps the curved structures more stable) or **SEMBO** (its red palette is especially pure, a good match for historical architecture).

Key parts in detail:

- Red round bricks: about 80, used for the column shafts. Gobricks dark red is recommended for minimal color variation between batches.
- Curved slopes: about 200, used to build the round eaves of all three tiers. SEMBO's curved pieces have more rounded edge finishing.
- Gold decorative elements: about 60, used to reproduce the gilded finial and painted ornamentation.
- Trans-clear tiles: for windows and lattice doors, adding a sense of depth and light.
- Large quantities of 1x1 bricks: used to reproduce the dougong bracket sets, which have to be stacked layer by layer with patience.

## Building Experience

The instructions are a full-color PDF running to 245 steps. The build starts at the base: three tiers of white marble terracing are recreated with plenty of tiles and slope bricks, and the detailing is dense. The column structure uses a double-ring layout, an inner circle plus an outer circle, which reproduces the essence of the building's traditional timber-frame system.

The highlight is the three-tier roof. The designer uses wedge plates and curved slopes together with SNOT technique to achieve the upturned eave corners. Every tier has to be built at a precisely calculated angle; a small deviation throws off the overall look. The dougong bracket sets are the second major hurdle. Stacking that many 1x1 bricks demands real patience, but the finished result is striking.

In the comment section of the [BrickPlans](https://brickplan.cn) listing, many builders share shortcuts, such as swapping large curved pieces in for some of the small-brick assemblies. That substantially lowers the difficulty without changing the exterior.

## Finished Model

The completed model measures about 30cm x 30cm x 25cm, an ideal size for desktop display. The three stacked eaves read clearly as separate layers, the gilded finial catches the light, and the blue glazed roof tiles (built from blue tiles) contrast sharply with the red columns, giving the whole thing a strong classical Chinese aesthetic. Displaying it on a dark base with warm lighting improves the effect further.

## Verdict

**Overall: ★★★★★**

This is a Chinese-architecture MOC blueprint with serious collector value. The build is demanding, but the finished model justifies the effort.
