# Coffee Shop MOC Plans: Build a Corner Cafe in Bricks

If you ask me which subject in brick street scenes is the most comforting, I'll always say the coffee shop. A small cafe with warm yellow light, a counter that seems to smell of fresh espresso and relaxed customers at the door is enough to lift your mood just thinking about it. That's why coffee shop blueprints are the first pick for so many builders starting a modular street. Below I've collected a few strong categories of plans, plus notes on how to give your cafe more character.

## Corner Cafes: The Prime Street Position

In a modular street, a corner building has a natural advantage: two facades mean the model reads as genuinely three-dimensional. Many corner cafe designs put a curved glass shopfront right at the turn, paired with a sign and outdoor seating under umbrellas, and the result photographs beautifully the moment it sits on a street base.

When you're choosing a plan like this, pay attention to the footprint. Ideally the base size should line up with the street modules you already own, so the cafe connects cleanly instead of leaving an awkward gap or overhang. Check where the pavement studs fall too, since a corner shop usually needs its entrance and its seating area to sit on different sides of the building.

## Street-Side Shops: Small, Charming, Beginner-Friendly

If you're new to street scenes, don't jump straight into a three-storey tower. A single shopfront coffee house plan is a better fit. A one- or two-storey shop keeps the part count manageable and puts the focus on the facade and the details around the doorway.

These coffee shop blueprints tend to spend their effort where it shows most: brick texture on the exterior walls, striped awnings, and a dessert display in the window. Finish one and you have a small piece you can set on a desk and study up close, which is exactly what you want for a first street build.

## Minifigures and Scenes: Bringing the Cafe to Life

Once the building is done, minifigures and props are what actually make it feel alive. A few combinations I like:

- Put an owner in an apron behind the counter, holding a coffee cup
- Set a terrace table and chairs outside with one or two customers chatting over drinks
- Add a cat or a dog in a corner for instant everyday atmosphere
- Fill the window with small food parts such as croissants and cupcakes

None of these cost many pieces, but they change how the model reads. A cafe with nobody in it is architecture; a cafe with a queue at the counter is a scene.

## Interior Details: Where the Extra Points Come From

The soul of a coffee shop is inside. Details that reliably earn praise include an espresso machine on the counter, a chalkboard menu on the wall, sofa seating by the window and pendant lamps hanging from the ceiling. Individually these small assemblies look unremarkable, but combined they create a strong sense of place.

When you build the interior, leave clearance for doors and windows so the inside stays visible from several angles. That same clearance makes later lighting modifications much easier, whether you're running a strip of LEDs under the ceiling or hiding a battery box in the roof cavity.

## Adding a Sense of Season

I also like giving a cafe a seasonal edition. In winter, add a small tree strung with lights by the door; in summer, swap in a parasol and an iced-drinks cart; in autumn, scatter a layer of leaf pieces across the pavement. The cost is low, but the same coffee shop blueprint feels fresh again every few months.

If your build lives on a display shelf long-term, this wardrobe-change approach is worth trying. You won't get bored of the model, and photos you post get a new talking point each time.

## Build Your Own Corner Cafe

Everyone's mental image of a cafe is different. Some builders lean vintage timber and warm browns, others want clean modern lines with glass and steel. That's the appeal of MOC building: the same coffee shop blueprint grows into completely different things in different hands.
