# Build a Water Park MOC: 9,800 Parts, 3 Slides and a Wave Pool

The short version: this water park MOC uses roughly 9,800 parts, covers 86 x 64 cm once finished, and takes about 30 hours to build at 2-3 hours a day, with a total budget of around CNY 1,600. The design packs in one wave pool, three slides at different heights, a lazy river circling the entire layout, and a two-story changing building. The colour scheme leans on sky blue, lemon yellow and white, so the moment it sits in your living room it reads as summer. The instructions come from the brickplan.cn platform, with the parts list split up by module, which makes it a good project for parents and kids to work through together.

## Step 1: Lay the base and build the wave pool

Start with a 40x32 stud baseplate to mark out the site, leaving a 28x16 stud area in the middle for the wave pool. Lay two layers of dark blue plates across the pool floor to suggest water depth, then run a ring of white curved bricks around the edge as the coping. Coconut palms and parasols go in the four corners. On one side of the pool, install the rotating wave mechanism: turn the handle and the white wave pieces push forward row after row. It is the most crowd-pleasing moving feature in the whole build.

## Step 2: Assemble the three slides

The slides stand 8, 12 and 16 bricks tall, with the pitch increasing each time, mapped to three difficulty levels: children, family and adult. Build them from half-cylinder curved pieces and add a support column every 3 layers to keep the structure rigid. The tallest slide tops out at a water bucket filled with transparent blue studs to simulate water; the bottom of the slide becomes an entry point that ties into the wave pool. All three slides use about 2,600 parts.

## Step 3: Finish the lazy river and landscaping

The lazy river loops around the outer edge of the layout, 4 studs wide, with the water surface laid in transparent light blue plates and dotted with life rings and rubber ducks. Plant a palm every so often along the bank, building the canopy from green curved pieces stacked three layers deep. Lemon yellow and white flowers run through the planting beds to keep the palette fresh. This module uses about 2,200 parts and is the best section for parents and kids to work on together.

## Step 4: Build the changing building and final details

The changing building is two stories: changing rooms and lockers on the ground floor, a viewing platform above, with the railing made from small white bar pieces so two minifigures can stand and look out over the park. Finish with the entrance turnstiles, the ticket booth and signage — the turnstiles in red and white, the signs carrying homemade stickers reading "Entrance", "Slides" and so on. When everything is done, switch on the light bricks under the wave pool floor; the night effect is more striking than daylight.

## FAQ

**Q: What age is this water park MOC suitable for?**

A: 6 and up is a good guide. Parents handle the big structures such as the base and the wave pool, while kids can build the slides, palms and small accessories on their own. Split the work by module and a family can finish in about 30 hours.

**Q: How does the wave mechanism actually move?**

A: A hand crank at the poolside drives an eccentric gear that pushes the wave pieces back and forth. No motor, no batteries — the mechanism is simple enough for a child to work out in a few turns, and it will not pinch fingers.

**Q: Can I swap the transparent water pieces for ordinary blue bricks?**

A: Yes, but the difference in look is obvious. Transparent pieces let the light bricks under the pool floor glow through, while plain blue bricks look flat. On a tight budget, lay plain blue first and upgrade to transparent later — the structure is fully compatible either way.

**Q: What does the whole build cost?**

A: About CNY 1,600 in parts. Transparent water pieces and curved slide pieces are the big-ticket items at roughly CNY 500. The brickplan.cn instruction page breaks the list down by module, so you can buy month by month and spread the cost.

**Q: How is this different from a shop-bought brick play set?**

A: Retail sets usually run 500 to 800 parts with one slide and one pool. This MOC runs 9,800 parts across a wave pool, three slides, a lazy river and a changing building, with a much more complete scene and a moving wave mechanism that gives it far more play value.
