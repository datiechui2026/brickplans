# Gas Station MOC Blueprint Guide: Fuel Island, Store, Canopy

A gas station is one of the few service buildings in a brick city layout that generates its own traffic and stories: pull in, fill up, step inside, buy a drink, drive off. This guide walks through a gas station MOC blueprint piece by piece, from the fuel island and pumps to the convenience store and the canopy overhead, and flags the spots where builders most often run into trouble.

## 1. Choosing a Blueprint and Planning the Layout

### Layout planning

A gas station usually breaks into three zones: the fuelling area (fuel island plus canopy), the convenience store (counter plus shelving), and the parking area. When you pick a gas station MOC blueprint, check first whether the canopy is built as a separate module and whether there are enough pumps for two units. A footprint of 32 x 16 studs works well: put the store on one side and the fuel island in the middle so the traffic flow stays clean.

### Parts preparation

The main build needs roughly 700 white and light grey plates and bricks for the structure, about 120 red-and-white striped pieces for the canopy, around 40 transparent plates for the store windows, and about 60 black tiles for the ground. For the pumps, printed parts or sticker pieces save a lot of time.

## 2. Building the Fuel Island and Pumps

### The island base

Build the fuel island from yellow or white plates into a low 2 x 8 platform, one plate higher than the ground. Run 1 x 4 slope bricks along both sides as crash bumpers. Tile the top in black to suggest asphalt, then mark the lane with white 1 x 1 pieces running from the entrance up to the fuelling position.

### Pump details

Each pump uses 2 x 2 bricks for the body, with a printed fuel-grade tile on the front and a transparent piece on top as the screen. Clip a black hose to the side, bent from 1 x 1 pieces with a hole, and hang a grey 1 x 1 round piece at the end as the nozzle. Between the two pumps, place a 2 x 4 price sign built from black plates with white printed numerals. It immediately reads as a place where fuel costs money.

## 3. Canopy Structure and Column Reinforcement

The canopy is the visual anchor of the whole build. Use red-and-white striped slope bricks to create the wave-shaped roof, cantilevered about 4 studs past the front edge of the fuel island. The two supporting columns must be built from 2 x 2 round bricks with a cross axle running inside them all the way down into the baseplate, and add an extra 2 x 4 plate at the overhang. Without that, the roof will start to sag. Finish the canopy edge with white 1 x 1 tiles for a crisp, tidy line.

## 4. Fitting Out the Convenience Store

### Counter and shelving

Set three shelving units against the store wall: off-white plates for the uprights, transparent plates for the shelves, and each level stocked with drink bottles (coloured 1 x 1 round pieces) and snack bags (printed pieces). The checkout counter is a 2 x 6 plate with a top surface; put a grey register with a printed screen on the front and stand an open-for-business sign beside it.

### Cooler and small details

Place an upright cooler by the door: a transparent door plus a blue light bar, with two rows of drink cans (metallic 1 x 1 round pieces) stacked inside. Open the door and the chilled-drinks feeling comes through. Don't forget a folding chair and a newspaper rack outside the window. The everyday-life atmosphere arrives instantly.

## 5. Ground, Colour Scheme and Surroundings

The ground is where a gas station earns the most visual payoff. Lay black tiles as the base, run two rows of yellow dashed lines using 1 x 1 plates, and drop two orange 1 x 1 cone pieces beside the fuelling position as traffic cones. For the colour scheme, keep the building white, the canopy red-and-white striped, and the ground black and yellow. That three-way contrast is strong enough that people recognise the gas station from a distance. Park a car and a minifigure at the pump and you capture the moment the driver steps out to fill up.

## 6. Common Problems and Reinforcement Tips

The canopy overhang is where collapses happen most often, so always run a cross axle or an extended pin down the inside of each column. Keep the ground between the fuel island and the store level: lay the full baseplate first and then build the raised platforms zone by zone, otherwise you end up with height mismatches.
