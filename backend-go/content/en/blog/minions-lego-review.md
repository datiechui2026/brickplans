# Minions LEGO Sets Reviewed: 75551, 75547, 75549

With their capsule-shaped bodies and hopeless devotion to Gru, the Minions have won over fans worldwide. Put that yellow chaos into brick form and you get one of the most charming licensed themes around. This guide ranks the official Minions sets, then looks at how builders are pushing the parts further with MOC work.

## Official Set Recommendations

### 1. 75551 Kevin and Stuart

This is the flagship of the theme and the one most collectors start with. **75551 Kevin and Stuart** gives you two transformable characters: Kevin builds into a tall "Big Kevin" configuration, while Stuart's back opens up to reveal an interior movie scene. The set runs 876 pieces and includes a Bob minifigure, so there is plenty of building to do before you even start posing them. Kevin's oversized form leans on a large number of **2x4 yellow bricks and curved bricks** to capture that rounded silhouette, and the internal structure holding it together is clever and solid.

If you only buy one Minions set, this is the one. The piece count is high enough to feel like a real build rather than a quick afternoon, and the transformation feature means the model stays interesting after it goes on the shelf.

### 2. 75547 Minion Flight Training

Working to a smaller budget, or just after a relaxed build? **75547 Minion Flight Training** fits the bill. At 136 pieces it recreates the Minions' flight training gag, with a small posable aircraft and two minifigures. The eye graphics on the fuselage are built from 1x1 round plates rather than printed, which gives the plane a great textured, slightly goofy look up close.

It is a fast build, but the finished model is sturdy enough to actually swoosh around, and the round-plate eye technique is worth stealing for your own builds.

### 3. 75549 Minion Motorcycle Chase

**75549 Minion Motorcycle Chase** packs a sidecar motorcycle and a single-wheel bike into one box, along with Stuart and Kevin. The sidecar is shaped like a banana, which is exactly the kind of absurd design decision the theme should be making, and the whole thing makes an excellent base for modification. Plenty of builders in the [BrickPlans community](https://brickplan.cn) have posted their own takes on this set.

## MOC Directions

Official sets are only the starting point. MOC (My Own Creation) building is where the theme really opens up.

**Scene MOCs.** Rebuild Gru's underground lab, the Minion prison, or any of the other classic locations. The [BrickPlans instruction sharing platform](https://brickplan.cn) has a large library of Minion scene MOCs uploaded by builders, so you can see how other people solved the layout problems before starting your own.

**Mech MOCs.** Use technic pieces for the skeleton, cover the outside in yellow panels, then add goggles and a pair of overalls. The result is a cute-combat robot that reads instantly as a Minion while still being a proper mech build. Technic pins and axles give you joints that hold a pose, and the panels hide the mechanics cleanly.

**Group MOCs.** Line up a whole row of Minions and distinguish the characters by hairstyle and eye count rather than by colour. Scale control matters a lot here, so it is worth reviewing our [brick building basics collection](https://brickplan.cn/blog/brick-building-basics), which covers minifigure proportions in detail.

## Final Thoughts

The official sets nail the character likeness, and the MOC side shows what happens when builders take those parts and run with them. Whether you are buying for a child, a display shelf or a parts bin, the Minions theme delivers a lot of usable yellow.
