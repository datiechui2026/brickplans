# Brick Cat MOC Plans Reviewed: Parts, Difficulty and Looks

Cats are one of the most popular animal subjects in brick building, and there is no shortage of cat MOC plans on the market. Quality varies wildly: some builds are startlingly lifelike, others come out looking like an alien lifeform. This review sorts the popular styles into groups and scores them on parts list, build difficulty, instruction quality and how the finished model actually looks, so you can decide which plan is worth buying.

## Common styles: from Ragdoll to black cat

The cat plans that circulate most widely in the community fall into four groups: **Ragdoll** (long fur, blue eyes, cream patches distributed across the body), **ginger tabby** (the classic yellow-and-white striping), **black cat** (all black with highlights in the eyes), and **cat head or cat body ornament** builds. The Ragdoll's challenge is the fur colour gradient; the ginger tabby's is the transition between stripes; the black cat tests the quality of single-colour parts, because a purely black body tends to blur into one mass and needs light and dark grey parts to read as volume.

## Parts list and completeness

A trustworthy plan has to include a **parts list** with part numbers, quantities and suggested alternative colours. Three things matter most. First, the **share of rare parts**: ear slope pieces and printed round eye plates get used heavily, and they push the cost up noticeably. Second, the **total part count**: an adult cat ornament usually runs 600-1200 pieces — too few and detail disappears, too many and the build becomes fatiguing. Third, **colour substitution notes**: a good plan flags where ordinary bricks can stand in, so you can complete the model from loose parts you already own.

## Difficulty tiers and instruction clarity

Cat MOCs split into three tiers: **beginner** (200-400 pieces, a simple seated cat), **intermediate** (500-900 pieces, involving SNOT upside-down construction and curved parts to close the edges), and **expert** (1000+ pieces, focused on fur direction and a dynamic pose). On instructions, a quality plan adds no more than eight new parts per step and includes angle callouts and colour highlighting. Weak plans often **dump twenty or thirty parts into a single step**, which is where beginners go wrong and have to backtrack. Prioritise versions with 3D-rendered step images.

## Build notes: patterns, face and curves

What decides whether a build "looks like a cat" comes down to a few details. For **fur patterns**, lay ginger tabby stripes with 1x2 striped bricks in a staggered arrangement — it reads far more naturally than large tiled patches. **Eyes and whiskers** are the soul of the model: pair printed round eyes with 1x1 trans-clear pieces for the highlight, and make whiskers from thin long plates wedged into the cheek slope pieces. **Rounded curves** come from SNOT technique — turn plates on edge, then wrap the edges with 1x1 curved panels and 4x4 round corner plates to avoid that stiff "box cat" look. For the **base**, add a baseplate with a plant or a ball of yarn and the presentation jumps a grade.

## Accuracy and finished look

Comparing actual builds side by side, the plans with high accuracy almost all meet the same bar: correct silhouette, coherent proportions, and one or two details that carry the model. The black cat is the hardest to pull off, since single-colour parts lack light and dark layers; good plans build a gradient in dark grey across the back. The Ragdoll wins on the blaze pattern on the face and the blue eye highlights. In terms of presence, a lying pose is easier to make cute than a standing one, while a cat head ornament uses fewer parts and sits well on any desk.

## Who it suits, price and where to get it

Cat plans suit a very wide **audience**: beginner versions work for families building with kids and for newcomers, intermediate versions for experienced builders, and expert versions for collectors chasing maximum accuracy. On **price**, a single plan usually runs CNY 20-80, and you can buy the loose parts yourself from the parts list; a complete part pack runs about CNY 200-500, considerably cheaper than a finished retail set. Beyond plan communities such as BrickPlans, Taobao and Xianyu also have proxy builders and loose-part sellers.

## Compared with official sets

**Official retail sets** win on opening the box and building straight away, with all printed parts included, but the model is fixed and play value is limited. **Cat MOC plans** win on freedom: you can change colours, swap expressions and add a base or scene, and the building process is part of the fun — usually at a lower price. If you enjoy the creative process of turning a pile of bricks into a cat, a plan-based MOC is the better buy.
