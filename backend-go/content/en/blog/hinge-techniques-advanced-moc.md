# LEGO Hinge Techniques for Advanced MOCs: Arms, Wings, Folding

Short version first: advanced hinge work comes down to three habits — pick the right parts, lock the angle, and leave room to move. Get comfortable with the 3937/3938 pair and the 60470 plate, and you can build robot arms, poseable wings, folding structures and animal jaws, turning a static display MOC into a model that actually moves.

## Why Advanced Builds Need Different Parts

The single-point hinges you use as a beginner are really only good for a 90° swing. That is nowhere near enough for a robot arm or a wing. At this stage, keep two groups of parts on hand:

- **3937 + 3938**: a turntable-style hinge that rotates a full 360° with ratchet detents. Ideal for shoulder joints on mechanical arms and for turning an animal's head. The 3938 is the matching base; combined, they index every 22.5° and feel solid in the hand.
- **60470**: a 2x2 hinge plate with a connecting pin. It folds through 180° and accepts side pins for extra bracing, which makes it the go-to choice for wing flapping and folding structures.

Beyond those, 30565 and 30566 (1x4 hinge plates with a folding leaf) are ideal for small jaw openings. Pair them with a 2x2 plate and you can mimic an upper and lower jaw biting down.

## Build 1: Robot Arm Joints (Step 1 to Step 3)

**Step 1**: Build horizontal rotation at the shoulder with a 3937 turntable, meshing the 3938 base in the opposite direction, with a 2x2 round plate sandwiched between them to cut friction. **Step 2**: Switch the elbow to a 60470 hinge plate and limit it to a 90° bend only — going past the ratchet travel can crack the part. **Step 3**: Add a 1x1 hinge with handle (44509) at the wrist for fine pitch adjustment. The whole arm now has three degrees of freedom: rotate, bend, pitch.

## Build 2: Poseable Wings

Use 60470 as the wing root, fixed to the 2x4 plates on either side of the torso. The key detail is clearance: leave at least 1 LEGO unit (about 8mm) of gap around the hinge, or friction builds up and the wing seizes. For a true flapping motion, run two 60470 plates in parallel and link them with an axle #3 so both wings swing up and down in sync.

## Build 3: Folding Structures and Animal Jaws

For folding structures, use 30565 hinge plates for doors or hatches, with a 1x2 plate as the latch. For an animal jaw, mount two 30566 plates to the upper and lower jaw, then join them in the middle with a 60470 flipped back-to-back — the mouth opens up to 120°. Add an axle #2 inside as a linked "tongue" and it lifts naturally as the jaw closes, which reads very convincingly.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the difference between 3937 and 3938, and can they be mixed?**
A: The 3937 is the ratchet turntable body and the 3938 is its matching base. They only hold a detent position when used as a pair. Swapping in a different turntable loses the 22.5° indexing feel, so buy them as a set.

**Q: My hinge arm keeps sagging and won't hold weight. What now?**
A: Three fixes: first, swap in a 3937 ratchet turntable for more damping; second, add 1x2 side plates on both sides of the joint to lock it; third, keep the arm under 8 LEGO units long so the center of gravity stays close to the joint.

**Q: How far does a 60470 hinge plate open, and can it be used for wings?**
A: It opens about 180° at most, which makes it excellent for wings and folding structures. For wings, run two plates in parallel with an axle linkage — a single plate has limited load capacity and will deform under fast flapping.

**Q: Is a dedicated hinge worth it for a moving animal jaw, or will ordinary plates do?**
A: It is worth it. 30565/30566 hinge plates have built-in detents, so the opening angle stays stable. Ordinary plates rely on friction alone and go loose after a few cycles, and the difference in the finished model is obvious.
