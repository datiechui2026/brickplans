# LEGO Statue of Liberty MOC: 2,200-Part Build Tutorial

The short version first: the Statue of Liberty is one of those landmark subjects that looks spectacular but doesn't actually require complicated building techniques. Around 2,200 parts will get you a 58 cm model, the oxidized copper patina is simulated with dark green plus light green bricks, and the full build runs about 7 hours. It suits intermediate builders who want to take on a large-scale landmark.

## Step 1: The Pedestal and Base

The statue stands on a star-shaped foundation, so start with a 32x32 baseplate. Build the star outline around the edges with 1x4 bricks, then pull the layers inward step by step to form the pedestal. The pedestal is about 10 cm tall and hollow inside to cut weight, which also leaves room for a display light. Use tan bricks for the pedestal walls to suggest granite, and add a plaque tile above the doorway — small details like this make it read as a real landmark. Once the base is done, the model weighs about 2.3 kg.

## Step 2: The Robe and Body Curves

The hardest part of the whole build is getting the robe to look like it drapes. Working upward from the pedestal, alternate curved bricks and slope bricks to lay out the folds of the skirt. On the front, 2x2 corner bricks create the rise and fall of the pleats, while the back tapers gradually inward. Build the waist with cylinder pieces and pre-install connection points at the shoulders for the arms. Dark green dominates the robe with light green as the secondary tone; roughly 70% of the green bricks go into this section, which gives the closest match to real oxidized copper.

## Step 3: The Crown and Head

Start the head with a neck cylinder on top of the torso, then build the face: two 1x1 round plates for the eyes, a 1x1 slope for the bridge of the nose, and a 1x1 tile for the mouth. The crown uses 7 thin rods topped with spike elements to imitate the statue's seven-ray crown, each spike about 2 cm tall. Brown slope bricks sweep the hair back from the face into the classic bun silhouette.

## Step 4: The Torch and Right Arm

The raised right arm with the torch is the focal point of the entire model. Connect the arm with long rod pieces and ball joints, then cap the torch with trans-orange flame pieces — a single LED is hidden inside so it can actually light up. The left arm cradles a book made from a 2x4 tile plus a gold sticker. Finish by attaching the nameplate to the front of the pedestal. The finished statue stands about 58 cm tall with a 22 cm wide base, which gives it real landmark presence in a living room or study.

## FAQ

**Q: Where can I download the Statue of Liberty MOC instructions?**
A: Search for "Statue of Liberty" on brickplan.cn to find the plans. The PDF costs about 30 yuan and includes a parts list and step-by-step instructions, so you can source the bricks yourself or buy the parts as a bundle from the list.

**Q: Is the Statue of Liberty MOC hard to build?**
A: Medium-to-hard. The robe curves and the small crown pieces are a bit of a challenge for beginners, and 2,200 parts take about 7 hours. It's better to try this after you have some castle or minifigure-scale MOC experience.

**Q: How many parts does the Statue of Liberty MOC need?**
A: About 2,200. Green bricks make up roughly 70%, tan pedestal bricks about 15%, and there are a small number of trans-orange flame pieces. Rare parts are limited, so sourcing the bricks stays affordable.

**Q: How tall is the statue MOC in centimeters?**
A: The standard version is about 58 cm tall with a 22 cm wide base, or about 62 cm total height once the base lighting bay is added. There is also a mini version around 25 cm tall that works well on a desk.

**Q: How do you mix the copper-patina green for the Statue of Liberty MOC?**
A: Use dark green as the main color, light green for highlights, and dark gray for shadows at roughly a 5:3:2 ratio — that layering comes closest to real copper patina. Don't build it all in one shade of green, or the surface will look flat.
