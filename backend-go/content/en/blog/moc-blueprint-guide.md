# MOC Blueprints: Where to Find Them and How to Use Them

Anyone who builds with bricks has run into the word "MOC." It stands for **My Own Creation** — a design that is yours rather than something that came out of an official set. The moment you stop following the box art step by step and start imagining your own aircraft, castle or mech, you have entered MOC territory. The bridge between that idea and a finished model is a **MOC blueprint**.

## What a MOC blueprint actually is

Put simply, a MOC blueprint is the construction plan for an original build. It works like an instruction booklet: it records the parts you need, the order in which they go together, and the structural logic behind the design.

A complete blueprint usually has three parts. First comes the **parts list** (often called a BrickList), which gives the element number, colour and quantity for every piece required. Second is the **step-by-step build sequence**, showing which parts are combined at each stage. Third is a **rendered overview image** so you can see the finished model before you start sorting. More advanced blueprints add the designer's notes on structure and approach, which is especially helpful when you are new.

## Where to get MOC blueprints

There are several main channels:

- **Blueprint-sharing communities**: international platforms such as Bricklink and Rebrickable host a huge volume of high-quality original designs. Domestic brick communities are growing quickly too, and many designers share their work for free.
- **Official and designer pages**: plenty of MOC builders post downloadable instructions on their own websites or social profiles. Some are free; others ask for paid support.
- **Dedicated blueprint sites**: communities focused specifically on brick plans, such as BrickPlans ([https://brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn)), collect, categorise and check large numbers of blueprints so you can filter by theme, difficulty or parts count and find something suitable quickly.

For a first attempt, start with something small and structurally simple — a little car or a small house — then work up to bigger builds once you have some experience.

## How to read a MOC blueprint

Once you have the file, resist the urge to start pulling parts immediately. Spending a few minutes going through the whole blueprint first makes the build far smoother.

1. **Read the parts list first**: check your stock against it and confirm you have every type and quantity. Mark anything missing; you can often substitute a part in the same colour or with a similar function.
2. **Skim the full sequence**: get a sense of the build order and the final shape so you have the overall picture in your head.
3. **Build layer by layer**: begin with the base structure and work up one step at a time. In each step, the parts shown in colour are the new additions; the grey parts are what you have already assembled.
4. **Compare and check often**: after every few steps, verify position and orientation against the drawing. Symmetrical sections are the easiest place to flip something by mistake.

The core skill in reading a blueprint is patience. Rushing tends to create errors that cost more time than they save.

## Building tips

Building a MOC feels different from building an official set. A few reminders:

- **Sorting matters**: before you start, separate parts by colour and type into small trays so you are not hunting through a pile.
- **Keep the structure solid**: MOCs are usually designed to look good, but do not neglect the strength of the internal frame. Reinforce load-bearing areas with sturdier elements or cross-bracing so the model does not fall apart.
- **Watch part orientation**: check which way each element faces. A slight rotation error early on can throw off everything that follows.
- **Use substitutions freely**: if a part is missing, do not get stuck. A functionally similar element will often give the same result.
- **Keep your files safe**: photograph the finished MOC and back up the blueprint so you can rebuild or modify it later.

## Final thoughts

MOC building is where bricks show their real appeal — turning an ordinary pile of parts into something that exists only because you made it. A well-made **MOC blueprint** is the key that opens that door. Hopefully this guide helps you take the first step into exploring and creating your own brick world.
