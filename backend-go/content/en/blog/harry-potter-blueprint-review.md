# Harry Potter MOC Blueprints: 7 Builds Worth Your Bricks

The Harry Potter films are childhood memory for a whole generation, and rebuilding their locations in brick has long been one of the most popular themes in the global MOC community. Whether it is the vast Hogwarts Castle, the secretive curve of Diagon Alley or the lived-in clutter of Hogsmeade, a good blueprint lets you turn the loose parts in your bins back into those scenes. The picks below are the most consistently well-regarded Harry Potter blueprints around, arranged from starter builds to serious undertakings, so you can find the one that fits your shelf and your spare time.

## 1. Hogwarts Castle: The Epic Build

### 1. Micro-Scale Hogwarts Castle (~3,000 pieces)

This blueprint compresses the entire castle into a desktop display piece. The design is modular, split into four sections: the main building, the towers, the astronomy tower and the courtyard. Each section can be built on its own, and assembled the model stands more than 50 cm tall. The designer has been careful with the film's signature details: the domed Great Hall ceiling, the round windows of Gryffindor Tower, the tree layout of the Forbidden Forest, and even a clever representation of the moving staircases.

**Why it works:** it is compact and displays beautifully on a desk or in a cabinet. The instructions are clearly sequenced and come with a part-number cross-reference table, so a builder with moderate experience can finish it over a weekend.

### 2. Full-Scale Hogwarts Great Hall (~8,000 pieces)

If maximum detail is the goal, this Great Hall blueprint is the one to consider. It reproduces the film's enchanted ceiling in full, using transparent bricks and layered construction to create the impression of a starry sky overhead. Banners for all four houses, the long tables, the candles, the teachers' seats and even the Sorting Hat and the house hourglasses are all included as decorative detail.

**Why it works:** the detail density is extremely high, which suits experienced builders who want an immersive build rather than a quick one. The finished model is a centrepiece for a room and an obvious grail project for a Harry Potter collector.

## 2. Diagon Alley: Magic on the High Street

Diagon Alley blueprints have always been a popular category in Harry Potter MOC design. These two take very different approaches.

### 1. Classic Diagon Alley Street (~5,000 pieces)

Built around three landmark shops — Ollivanders wand shop, Weasley's Wizard Wheezes and Gringotts Wizarding Bank — this blueprint forms a complete stretch of magical retail street. Each building lifts off separately for play, and the interiors are dense: the stacked wand boxes in Ollivanders, the joke props in the Wheasley shop, all carefully modelled. The facades use dark bricks with bright, colourful signage, capturing the old and slightly secretive mood of the films.

**Why it works:** a street-scene format sits naturally alongside other city building series. The modular layout lets you rearrange the buildings or extend the street to whatever length you like.

### 2. Knockturn Alley, Dark Version (~3,500 pieces)

Where Diagon Alley is bright and busy, Knockturn Alley is secretive and dangerous. This blueprint centres on Borgin and Burkes, flanked by dark buildings and a narrow lane that together create an eerie atmosphere. It is packed with dark-magic detailing: skull ornaments, cobwebs and dark-coloured lanterns. A strong choice for builders who like gothic styling.

**Why it works:** the theme is unusual and the styling is distinctive, giving a sharp contrast to the bright street scenes most people display. If you want a Harry Potter model that does not look like everyone else's, this is worth a try.

## 3. Magical Transport and Locations

### 1. Hogwarts Express (~2,500 pieces)

A dark red steam locomotive with its signature chimney and tender, plus a single passenger carriage, make up this classic Hogwarts Express blueprint. The locomotive can be pushed along, and the carriage interior reproduces the compartment layout. The nicest surprise is a mini Platform Nine and Three-Quarters scene included in the same blueprint, which can be displayed together with the train.

**Why it works:** play value is high — the train actually rolls — which suits builders who like models that move. The difficulty is moderate, making it a good entry point into Harry Potter MOC building.

### 2. Hagrid's Hut (~1,800 pieces)

Set at the edge of the Forbidden Forest, Hagrid's Hut is one of the warmest scenes in the series. This blueprint captures the rough texture of the stone hut, the oversized pumpkin patch and Hagrid's motorbike. Interior details include the fireplace, the shotgun, the teapot and sausages hanging on the wall, all of which sum up Hagrid's hospitable, rough-around-the-edges character.

**Why it works:** the piece count is moderate and the cost is friendly, which makes it suitable for beginners or builders on a budget. It is a very complete little model and pairs well with the other scene blueprints here.

## 4. How to Choose the Right Harry Potter Blueprint

1. **By experience level:** newcomers should start in the 1,800–3,000 piece range, such as Hagrid's Hut or the Hogwarts Express; experienced builders can take on the 8,000+ piece projects.
2. **By display space:** with limited desk space, choose the micro-scale castle or a single street building; with a dedicated cabinet, go straight for the full-scale Great Hall or the complete Diagon Alley.
3. **By budget:** parts for a Harry Potter blueprint can run from a few dozen yuan to over a thousand yuan, so check the parts list before buying and confirm which pieces you already have in stock.

## 5. Closing Notes

The appeal of Harry Potter blueprints is that the wizarding world stops being something on a screen and becomes something you can touch and assemble yourself. Whether it is the great sweep of Hogwarts Castle or the crowded shopfronts of Diagon Alley, each blueprint carries the designer's own reading of the source material. Hopefully one of the seven above is the one that ends up on your building table.
