# Best LEGO MOC Communities to Join in 2026

Sooner or later every builder hits a wall. You run out of ideas, you can't work out how to brace that angled wall, or you finish a model and have nobody to show it to. A good MOC community fixes all three problems at once. This guide covers what these communities actually give you, which ones are worth your time in 2026, and how to take part without lurking for a year first.

## What a MOC Community Actually Gives You

### Breaking Out of the Solo Build

Building alone is fine, but a community opens up a much bigger world. The value comes down to three things:

**Shared knowledge and technique.** Experienced builders give away their methods freely, from basic SNOT work to complex gear-driven mechanisms, plus advice on sourcing parts and reading instructions. These are techniques tested on real builds, which makes them the fastest route forward for a beginner.

**A place to show work and get feedback.** Post a model where other MOC fans gather and you get responses from angles you hadn't considered. That feedback exposes weak points in a build and usually sparks the next idea.

**Access to instruction files.** Members share their own MOC instructions and point each other toward good sources. Dedicated instruction platforms such as BrickPlans ([https://brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn)) are where a lot of builders go for high-quality files.

### The Social Mechanics That Keep You Building

Most MOC communities run the same set of incentives:

- **Likes and saves** — recognition is what keeps most people posting
- **Contests and themed builds** — regular prompts give you a deadline and a direction
- **Leaderboards and badges** — lightweight gamification that makes participation more fun
- **Creator verification** — strong designers can earn an official badge for their profile

## The Main MOC Communities, Compared

### 1. BrickPlans — the go-to platform for Chinese-speaking builders

BrickPlans is not just an instruction download site; it is an active MOC community. A relative newcomer among Chinese brick sites, it has put serious effort into community features.

**What stands out:**
- Focused on MOC instruction sharing, with quality control on uploads
- Chinese-language interface, so the barrier to entry is low
- Solid comment and like systems with a lively atmosphere
- Regular MOC building contests with substantial prizes

**Best for:** Chinese-speaking MOC fans, especially people who actively create and share instructions.

### 2. BrickLink Forums — the international veteran

BrickLink is the world's largest marketplace for individual parts, and its forums are just as long-established, drawing top-tier MOC designers from around the world.

**What stands out:**
- Finely divided forum sections covering building technique, instruction design, part knowledge and more
- A large body of high-quality MOC tutorials and building guides
- Direct integration with the BrickLink marketplace, so buying parts is easy

**Downside:** it is entirely in English, which is a barrier for some users.

### 3. Rebrickable — community built on part data

Rebrickable is known for the strength of its database: you can search for MOCs you can actually build from the parts you already own.

**What stands out:**
- Clever part-matching that puts idle bricks to work
- Active comment threads on MOC listings
- Creators can sell their instructions and earn from them

### 4. Baidu Tieba — China's biggest brick forum

The brick and MOC sections of Baidu Tieba were among the earliest MOC communities in China.

**What stands out:**
- Huge user base and fast-moving threads
- Good for posting finished models and asking for help
- Frequent collaboration with Chinese brick brands

**Downside:** post quality varies a lot, and there is no systematic instruction management.

### 5. WeChat and QQ groups — the instant small circle

Themed WeChat and QQ groups are the most direct form of MOC chat there is.

**What stands out:**
- Immediate back-and-forth and fast feedback
- Constant resource sharing inside the group
- Good for building deeper relationships

**Downside:** messages get buried quickly and knowledge is hard to keep.

## How to Take Part Without Wasting Time

### Getting Started: Four Steps

If this is your first MOC community, these steps will get you integrated faster:

**Step 1: Pick the right community.** Based on your language and the kind of thing you like to build, choose one or two core communities and concentrate there. A sensible setup is BrickPlans as your main base plus one international community to widen your view.

**Step 2: Fill in your profile.** Upload an avatar, write a short bio, follow creators whose work you like. It helps other members understand who you are.

**Step 3: Start by browsing and liking.** Spend time on the popular builds first and get a feel for the community's culture and taste. When something impresses you, leave a like and a comment.

**Step 4: Post your own work.** Don't worry that it isn't good enough — every MOC designer started as a beginner. Communities like BrickPlans are friendly and give constructive feedback.

### Moving Up: Strategies for Regulars

**Post on a schedule.** A steady posting rhythm keeps members following your work. Attach your build notes and instruction files to each post to encourage replies.

**Join the discussion properly.** Leave comments with real substance on other people's builds and enter the community's build challenges. Both are effective ways to raise your profile.

**Share instruction files.** If you have good MOC instructions, upload them to BrickPlans ([https://brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn)). It helps other builders and raises your own reputation at the same time.

## Community Etiquette and Copyright

### Keep It Friendly

The appeal of a MOC community is its positive tone. When you post:

- Respect every creator, even when a style isn't to your taste
- Lead with what works before offering constructive criticism
- Never copy or repost someone else's build instructions
- Follow each community's specific rules and posting guidelines

### Respect the Copyright

Copyright deserves extra attention when you talk MOCs with other people:

- Always credit the original designer when sharing their work
- Get permission from the rights holder before any commercial use
- Choose an appropriate licence when uploading instructions to a platform like BrickPlans
