# Brick Flower Shop MOC: Shelves, Windows and Facade

A flower shop is one of the most photogenic subjects in street-scene building. Walls of blossoms and greenery bring their own bright colour, so the model stands out the moment it goes on a shelf or into a display case. This guide works through the three focal points of a brick flower shop MOC — the shelving, the shop window and the facade — from the parts list down to the structure, so you can build a small shop that stays interesting up close. Whether it sits out as a standalone piece or joins a street-scene collection, the same approach applies.

## 1. Set the Layout First: A Three-Zone Structure

Before you place a single brick, decide how the shop's space is divided. A useful approach is to split the storefront into three horizontal bands: the facade and signboard on top, doing the job of catching the eye; the shop window and flower shelving in the middle, doing the job of showing colour; the work counter and checkout area at the bottom, adding a sense of daily life. With this top-middle-bottom arrangement, the shop reads clearly from any angle you photograph it.

For the baseplate, if you are building to a standard street-scene module, aim for a depth of 16 studs and a width of 12 to 16 studs. A standalone display piece can be smaller. When planning, reserve the large window opening first — the whole point of a flower shop is putting the flowers where passers-by can see them, so the glazed area needs to be genuinely large and genuinely open.

## 2. Building the Flowers: Colour Is the Shop's First Language

Roughly seventy percent of a flower shop MOC's success comes down to the flower colour scheme. For the structure, use a "fixed base" method: lay out the planting surface on the shelving first with bricks that have holes through them or with studded baseplates, then push the stems (thin bar elements or long bar pieces) into that base and cap each one with petal pieces. The advantage is that flowers can be moved and recoloured at any time, which makes fine-tuning easy later on.

Petal shapes can be mixed freely. A single large bloom can be a ring of curved petal pieces; small flowers can be stacked 1x1 round plates; trailing vines can hang from chain links or flexible hose pieces. For colour, work to "one dominant colour per bay, rhythm across the whole" — for example, give each shelf level its own main tone, rotating pink, yellow and purple, so the wall doesn't turn into a colour fight. Slipping green leaf pieces in between keeps the flowers looking fresh.

## 3. Shelving and Counters: A Staggered Display Structure

The shelving is what carries the shop's colour. A stepped arrangement works best: put the low planter tables at the front, and build two or three tiers of trapezoid shelving against the wall, low at the front and high at the back, offset left and right, so every level of flowers stays visible. Fix the shelf boards horizontally with plate pieces and leave at least one stud of clearance between levels so taller potted plants will still fit.

Above the counter you can add a canopy or awning built from slope pieces. It adds design interest and visually closes off the shop's silhouette. On the bottom work counter, place a loose bouquet, a roll of wrapping paper built from cylindrical pieces, and a pair of scissors made from shaped elements — these small signs of life are what suddenly make a brick flower shop MOC feel inhabited.

## 4. The Shop Window: Put the Colour on Show

The window is the face of the face. Inside it, build a miniature display platform: raise the flower pots on columns of different heights to create level differences, then add one or two hanging plants, and the window instantly gains depth. Glaze it with transparent pieces, and if you can, leave a lighting position at the top of the window — warm light falling on the flowers at night looks excellent.

Don't leave the outside windowsill empty either. Line up a row of small potted plants and a little "open" sign board so the inside and outside answer each other, and the whole window reads as one complete vignette.

## 5. Facade and Sign: Make the Shop Memorable at a Glance

The key word for the facade is recognisability. Spell the shop name with letter pieces and hang a large bouquet or wreath above the entrance as a mark. A wreath can be a ring of curved pieces dotted with small flowers — cheap to build and very eye-catching. Small hanging lamps or flower baskets on either side of the sign, placed symmetrically, make it look more refined.

Don't forget the parts of a facade that are easiest to overlook: the entrance steps and the canopy. Build the steps as two levels of plates, project the canopy out with slope pieces, and set two "welcome" flower pots by the door. From a distance the shop already has a complete outline.

## 6. Parts List and Beginner Tips

Here is a basic parts list (adjust quantities to your shop's size):

- Baseplate and wall panels: 1 to 2 large baseplates, assorted 1x4 and 1x6 plates
- Flowers and leaves: a batch of petal pieces, curved pieces, round plates, stem pieces and leaf pieces
- Shelving structure: bricks with holes, studded baseplates, assorted 1x8 plates
- Doors and windows: transparent glass pieces, door frames, window frames
- Signage: letter bricks, transparent baseplates, column pieces
- Decoration: flower baskets, small animal pieces, hanging hose pieces

Three tips for beginners. First, build flowers in batches — make a whole run of one flower type before it goes on the shelf, so the colour stays consistent. Second, work in the order "shelving first, flowers second": get the empty shelving structure solid, then fill and adjust. Third, don't overreach: finish one shelf level, photograph the result, and expand only once you're happy with it. That produces a far more refined model than filling every shelf at once.

Build your flower shop MOC and let a roomful of colour say "welcome in" for you. Once it's finished, line it up beside your other street-scene shops — you'll often find that the life in the whole street starts spreading out from this one flower shop.
