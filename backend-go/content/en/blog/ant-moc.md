# Brick Ant MOC Review: Six Legs, Real Joints, 540 Parts

The short version first: the six-legged structure is what makes this brick ant MOC worth your time. It uses roughly 540 parts and measures 26 cm long and 14 cm tall once finished, with all six legs fully articulated. Overall accuracy to a real ant comes in around 88%. The PDF instructions sell for 18 yuan on brickplan.cn, the loose parts cost about 110 yuan, and the test build took roughly 3 hours.

## 1. Appearance and Accuracy

The three-part body — head, thorax and abdomen — reads clearly at a glance. The head is built in black, with mandibles and two antennae picked out in the same colour, while trans-red parts for the compound eyes give the whole thing a focal point. The abdomen uses curved bricks to create a rounded, segmented texture, and the six legs are deliberately varied in thickness rather than uniform. The realistic approach lands at about 88% accuracy; scaled up this far beyond life size, the mechanical look has real presence on a shelf.

## 2. Structural Design

All six legs connect to the body through ball joints, and each leg can be angled independently. That means you can pose the model crawling, lifting something, or standing alert, and the pose holds. The designer also left a Technic cross-axle socket on the thorax, so small add-on accessories can be attached later. That pushes the play value a good step above a static display piece — you are not just looking at it, you are re-posing it.

## 3. Building Experience

Working from the PDF instructions on brickplan.cn, the test build took about 3 hours, split into 7 stages, with the six leg joints concentrated in stage 4. The tricky part is getting the ball joints of all six legs aligned to the same height — if they are not, the finished model will not stand steadily. Test each leg flat on the table as you finish it before moving on to the next step. It is not a difficult build, but it rewards patience over speed.

## 4. Value for Money

The instructions are 18 yuan and the loose parts come to about 110 yuan. Black parts make up around 80% of the total, which keeps the cost low; plenty of builders report finishing it for 60 yuan using parts already in their stock. Compared with ready-made insect sets of the same size, it works out roughly 45% cheaper. It suits insect fans, builders who enjoy mechanical structures, and newcomers who want practice at joint-based building.

All in all, this ant MOC gets unusual playability out of its joint structure, the detail holds up to close handling, and it balances cost against creativity better than most insect-themed builds.
