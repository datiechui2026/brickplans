# Qiji Brick Sets for Beginners: Where to Start

The short version first: Qiji is one of the friendliest brands in the Chinese brick market for new builders. A small street-scene set costs entry-level money and takes two or three hours to finish.

If you are new to the hobby and do not want to open with a several-thousand-piece monster, Qiji sets are worth a serious look. The brand concentrates on street scenes, architecture, vehicles and dioramas, with clear instructions and accessible prices — which is why so many builders treat it as a go-to first set. This guide weighs structure, parts and value for money to help you choose.

## Where Qiji sits in the market

Qiji's positioning is straightforward: make building easy and make the finished model feel like an achievement. Sets are sensibly bagged, with each bag covering a handful of steps in the manual, so sorting as you go never becomes a chore. The instructions are clearly labelled, and there is almost no abstract sub-assembly you have to guess at.

On subject matter, the range leans on popular themes — street scenes, buildings, vehicles — rather than complicated mechanical structures. The effort goes into how the finished model looks and how smoothly it goes together, which is exactly what a beginner needs.

## Sets worth buying

The most popular corner of the Qiji catalogue is the street-scene building series. Single small houses and shops are available at entry-level prices, with a moderate part count and a two-to-three-hour build. Facade detailing and interiors are both handled properly, and the model is ready to go straight into a display cabinet.

The vehicle series suits builders who prefer cars and boats: small sports cars and construction vehicles use simple structures and are easy to handle and play with. If you want a slightly bigger project, the mid-range combination street scenes can be joined across multiple sets, letting you assemble your own small block district over time.

## Parts quality and build experience

Qiji's parts hold up well for the price. Standard bricks have moderate clutch power — they push together smoothly, neither loose nor tight — while special elements have stable moulding precision and printed pieces show crisp patterns. The manual is organised by bag with detailed step callouts, so the margin for error is generous; if you misplace a part, pulling it apart and starting again is no big deal.

Qiji parts are fully compatible with the mainstream brick system, so mixing in elements from other brands for modifications later on is no obstacle at all.

## Value for money and who it suits

The core strength here is value. Entry-level prices buy sets with a high degree of completion, which makes Qiji a good fit as a child's first brick set, for parent-and-child building, or for an adult teaching themselves the basics — the cost of a failed experiment is very low.

Once you have built a few Qiji sets and want to push further into free building and your own MOCs, the [BrickPlan blog](https://brickplan.cn/en/blog) has beginner-oriented instructions and tutorials to take you from copying to creating.
