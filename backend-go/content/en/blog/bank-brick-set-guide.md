# How to Build a Bank Brick Model: Street Corner Guide

Bottom line first: the hard parts of a bank build are the columned portico and the vault interior. Nail the four stages — steps, columns, entrance, vault — and a roughly 2,000-piece plan takes about 5–7 hours and 250–400 RMB to turn into an imposing corner bank.

## Step 1: Pick a Plan and Plan the Layout

### 1. Plan specifications

Aim for a plan of 1,800–2,400 pieces on a 16×32 stud footprint, in the long-and-shallow format: a wide frontage with shallow depth is naturally suited to a street corner position. Search "bank" on BrickPlan to find several downloadable plans, but check the parts list first to confirm it includes the gold parts and printed parts — those two categories decide how premium the finished model looks.

### 2. Colors and parts

Banks read as off-white and light gray with gold accents. Budget roughly 800 off-white bricks, 300 light gray plates, 60 gold parts (door frame, signage, vault door) and 50 transparent parts for the shop windows and chandeliers. Gold parts are pricey; yellow parts are an acceptable stand-in that you can upgrade gradually later.

## Step 2: Build the Steps, Columns and Main Door

### 1. Steps and foundation

Lay the baseplate first and build three steps across the front, each one plate high and two studs deep, with railing posts on both sides. Reinforce the four corners of the foundation with 2×4 bricks, and add one layer of plates as a horizontal beam every four brick courses so the whole building stays stable and does not lean.

### 2. Columns and the main door

Stand one column on each side of the entrance: stack 2×2 round bricks, binding every third course with a ring of 1×1 bricks for grip, and cap each column with a 2×4 plate. The door uses a dark door frame with a printed gold handle, and a semicircular arched window sits above it. Add the printed "BANK" sign and the portico's presence arrives immediately.

## Step 3: Vault, Counter and Interior

### 1. Vault details

The soul of a bank build is the second-floor vault: gray 2×2 bricks for the walls, a gold dish part plus a wheel part for the vault door, and rivet parts around the frame. Stack a few gold 1×1 plates inside as bullion so it is visible through the window — this is the most story-rich corner of the whole building.

### 2. Counter and office area

On the ground floor, run a long counter: light gray plates for the top surface, off-white plates for the front, with a desk lamp, a printed abacus tile and document boxes on top. Behind it, place an office chair and filing cabinets, and give the teller minifigure a tie and a ledger — the scene turns professional at once. The second floor becomes the manager's office with a large desk and a safe, which uses the space more completely.

### 3. Lighting and finishing touches

Mount two street lamps above the portico (transparent shades plus light bars) and add a crystal chandelier in the hall. Check that the columns are vertical and the steps are flush, then park a small armored cash truck at the door — and the whole street starts telling a story.

## FAQ

**Q: How much does a bank brick set cost?**

A: Chinese-brand bank sets of 1,800–2,400 pieces mostly run 200–400 RMB; versions that include gold parts and printed parts cost more. Buying only the plan and sourcing the parts yourself can be kept to around 250 RMB.

**Q: How do I make the round columns sturdy?**

A: Stack 2×2 round bricks, bind every third course with a ring of 1×1 bricks, cap the top with a 2×4 plate, and lock the base to the baseplate with 2×2 parts — the column will not wobble.

**Q: Is a bank build good to do with kids?**

A: Yes. Sorting parts, finding parts and building step by step is a great family activity. Let the parent handle load-bearing sections such as the columns, and let the child handle the gold bars and small counter items. It can be finished in 5–7 hours with a strong sense of achievement at the end.

**Q: How different is a bank from an ordinary street-scene building?**

A: Mostly in the parts: a bank needs more gold parts, round bricks and printed parts, and structurally it adds steps and columns, making it more formal overall. When placed alongside other street shops, it works best as the corner anchor building.
