# Brick Destroyer MOC Blueprint: Hull, Guns and Deck Details

The short answer first: a destroyer is hard because of the hull's flowing lines and the packed weapons up top. The bow rake has to be planned at the keel stage, and you should expect around a thousand pieces at small scale and three to five thousand at large scale.

Destroyers are among the most satisfying military MOCs to look at: clean hull lines, forests of radar masts, a forward main gun and rows of vertical launch cells. Park one on a display shelf and the whole "sea zone" presence arrives with it. The difficulty concentrates in two places — the streamlined hull shape and the dense weapon fit on the superstructure. This guide walks through blueprint sourcing, keel, hull and weapon details in build order.

## Finding Blueprints and Choosing a Scale

Common places to find brick destroyer MOC blueprints:

- Overseas communities such as Rebrickable and Bricklink, strong on warship subjects but usually English-instructed.
- Domestic brick forums and builder share threads, where the annotations are friendly Chinese labels.
- Digital blueprint bundles on e-commerce platforms, often packing land, sea and air military vehicles together.
- Dedicated blueprint sites such as BrickPlan, where you can search "destroyer" and "warship" directly for Chinese-annotated instructions and parts lists.

Destroyer scales run from 1:700 desktop miniatures up to 1:100 large hulls. A small scale sits around a thousand pieces: quick to build, easy to display, no space problem. A large scale easily hits three to five thousand pieces, and the bridge, VLS and helicopter deck can all be done properly — but confirm your loose-parts stock and your display space before you commit.

## The Hull Keel: From Waterline to Deck

The hull is the skeleton of the ship, and the build order should run keel, waterline, deck, superstructure. Start by laying the central keel with technic beams, then plate the bottom section by section, using slope bricks and wedge pieces to pull the bow into a sharp line. A destroyer bow normally has a pronounced forward rake, and reproducing it means fixing the angle from the keel stage rather than trying to bend it later. For the waterline, use dark grey pieces to separate it from the hull bottom and echo the layered real-life paint scheme. Lay the deck flat with large tiles, and leave the mounting positions for the main gun, the VLS and the helicopter deck open in advance.

While building, watch the symmetry of the two sides and how firmly the deck pieces clutch to the hull — a top-heavy superstructure pressing down on a weak deck is the classic failure point.

## Weapon Systems: Main Gun, VLS and Missiles

A destroyer's fighting character comes entirely from its weapon detail. For the bow main gun, use cylindrical pieces for the barrel and round plates for the turret base, so the mount can actually rotate. The vertical launch system (VLS) amidships is the signature of a modern destroyer: build the cell array from rows of small dark round plates or square pieces, and regular alignment alone is enough to sell the effect. For the close-in weapon mounts and anti-ship missile boxes along the sides, mount slim elongated pieces at an angle to mimic launch elevation.

Keep the proportions in check. The barrel should not be thicker than the hull is wide, and the VLS cell count and spacing should stay uniform — cramming too much in is what kills the sense of realism.

## Superstructure and Detail Work

The superstructure is where a destroyer gains its layering: bridge, radar mast, funnel and helicopter hangar should step at different heights. Build the bridge from plates that narrow level by level to create a stepped profile; stand the radar mast up from slim technic pieces and cap it with a ball or antenna piece to suggest a phased-array radar; rake the funnel outward so it reads as a different height from the hangar. Lay the helicopter deck in dark plates at the stern and add the landing markings, printed or as stickers. Finish with the small mechanisms: the pennant number from a printed part or sticker, railing pieces along the deck edge, and warning lights dotted on the mast. Once those details land, the destroyer is done.

## FAQ

**Q: What scale should I choose for a destroyer MOC?**

A: Scales commonly run from a 1:700 desktop miniature to a 1:100 large ship. Small scale is around a thousand pieces — fast to build, easy to store. Large scale runs three to five thousand pieces, letting you detail the bridge, VLS and helicopter deck, but confirm your loose parts and display space first.

**Q: How do I get that sharp, raked bow line?**

A: Plan the angle from the keel stage. Lay the central keel with technic beams, plate the bottom section by section, and close the bow rake with slope bricks and wedge pieces. Keep the waterline separated from the hull bottom with dark grey pieces, and tile the deck flat while leaving the weapon mounting points open.

**Q: What if the superstructure crushes the deck?**

A: Add a plate sandwich in the midship section to route the load into the keel. At the same time keep weapon proportions under control — a barrel no thicker than the hull is wide, uniform VLS cell count and spacing, and no over-cramming that costs realism.

**Q: How do I build the VLS and the radar mast?**

A: For the VLS, form the cell array from rows of dark small round plates or square pieces; neat alignment is all it takes. Stand the radar mast from slim technic pieces and top it with a ball or antenna piece to suggest a phased-array radar. Step the bridge inward plate by plate so it reads as a staircase.
