# How to Build MOC Trees: Trunk, Canopy and SNOT Guide

Short answer first: you do not need exotic parts to build a MOC tree. A trunk core made from brown basic bricks and technic pieces, plus leaf parts stacked into a canopy, gets you there. A 25 cm tree runs roughly 300-500 parts and a budget of 30-60 yuan, and once you have the hang of it, 1-2 hours of building. Pines, deciduous trees and autumn trees each follow their own pattern.

## 1. The Trunk: Build the Core, Then Wrap It

The biggest risk with a trunk is the straight-pipe look. Stack 2x2 bricks in a staggered pattern and work technic pins into the stack so the trunk can take a bend. Offset each course by half a stud every 3-5 layers and the trunk picks up natural thickness variation on its own. To suggest old bark, apply brown tiles and 1x1 slopes on the outside in an irregular scatter — deliberately do not line them up. Spread plates outward at the base to form root feet, then connect the canopy with a 2x2 turntable part so you can rotate or remove it and swap seasons later.

## 2. The Canopy: Choosing Leaf Parts

There are three main leaf-part families: plant leaves, flower/calyx pieces, and studded shrub pieces. For conifers (pines), layer dark green plant leaves with each layer rotated 45 degrees from the one below, shrinking each ring as you climb, to produce a pagoda silhouette. For broadleaf trees, insert 1x1 leaves with stems into SNOT bricks — the more you add, the denser the result, and a large canopy usually takes 80-120 leaves. For an autumn version, swap only the top layers to orange and yellow at roughly a 3:2 ratio and the sense of depth appears immediately.

## 3. SNOT: Making the Canopy Three-Dimensional

SNOT (Studs Not On Top) is the key to a canopy with real volume. Using headlight bricks or 1x1 bricks with side studs, you move leaves from lying flat to plugging in sideways, and the canopy opens out 360 degrees. The method: build a hemisphere base from plates, leave side studs exposed evenly across the surface at roughly 2-stud spacing, then insert leaves layer by layer until the canopy is full with no bare patches. Finish the crown with a 1x1 cone so the tree does not end up bald on top.

## 4. Three Fast Recipes

**Pine:** dark green leaves plus a brown trunk, stacked in a pagoda shape — a small bonsai is done in 15 minutes. **Deciduous tree:** broad leaves with a curved, flowing trunk line, good enough to anchor a main scene, about 1.5 hours. **Autumn tree:** take the deciduous build, switch to orange and yellow leaves, then add 2-3 red fruit parts as accents for the strongest visual punch. ## FAQ

**Q: Which parts are cheapest for a MOC tree?**
A: Brown basic bricks plus plant leaves are the cheapest route. Bulk loose parts average 0.1-0.2 yuan per gram, and a 25 cm tree comes to about 30-50 yuan in loose parts. Avoid rare printed parts and chrome parts — ordinary parts already deliver the effect, and your budget drops by another 30 percent.

**Q: What if the canopy looks too sparse?**
A: Sparse canopies usually mean too few leaf layers or spacing that is too wide. Push the leaf layers up to 4-6, compress the gap between layers to 1-2 studs, raise the leaf count above 100, and use SNOT side insertion to fill the gaps on the flanks — density improves immediately.

**Q: Can pine and broadleaf leaves be mixed?**
A: Yes, but not within the same layer. Plant leaves and stemmed leaves differ a lot in shape, and mixing them at one level looks messy. Use them by layer instead: plant leaves as the base, stemmed leaves for detail above, and the transition reads naturally with richer layering.

**Q: How long does one tree take?**
A: A small bonsai takes 15-30 minutes, a standard 25 cm scene tree 1-2 hours, and a large 2-meter Christmas tree has to be built in modules, roughly 6-8 hours in total. Beginners should start with a tree under 30 cm and move up to the big builds once the technique is familiar.

**Q: Will a heavy canopy tip the tree over?**
A: It can. When the canopy exceeds 300 grams or the center of gravity sits high, add technic beams inside the trunk as a load-bearing spine, and use a baseplate of 8x8 or larger at the bottom for extra weight. A turntable part between trunk and canopy also lets you split the tree for transport and avoids crushing the structure.
