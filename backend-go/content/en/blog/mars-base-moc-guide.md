# Mars Base MOC Guide: Modular Habitats, Domes and Lighting

The short version: a convincing Mars base MOC comes down to modular habitats plus transparent accents. Build each habitat as a standard module you can plug in and pull out, then let trans domes and lighting carry the sci-fi mood. Roughly 3,000-4,000 parts and 12-16 hours of building gets you a base on a 48x48 stud footprint that holds 4-5 minifigures. Keep transparent parts at 15%-20% of the total and the texture reads as genuinely sci-fi.

## 1. Set the Layout First: Modularity Is the Core Logic

Never build a Mars base as one big box. Split it into five functional modules: living quarters, laboratory, greenhouse, airlock and power station. Build each one independently on a 12x12 baseplate, then join them with 2x4 connector plates into a U shape or a straight line, leaving a walkway down the middle. Every habitat can then be lifted out on its own for play, and it photographs much more easily. This is the smartest trick in sci-fi MOC building, and far more flexible than assembling everything as one shell.

## 2. Build the Habitat Shells: White First, Accents for Contrast

Step one: use mostly white bricks for the habitat walls, 6-8 bricks high, with 1x2 dark gray pieces at the corners as vertical columns to suggest a metal frame. Step two: cut a round skylight into each roof, cover it with a 4x4 trans round plate, and place a 1x1 trans light brick inside so it lets daylight through and glows at night. Step three: attach a 2x4 gray corridor piece to the outside of each habitat to link the modules, and run black 1x2 plates along the seams as seals so the joints look like real docking structures.

## 3. Solar Arrays and Antennas: The Power System

Build the solar panels from 2x6 trans blue plates mounted on dark gray supports, with a diagonal brace that adjusts to 45 degrees so the panels can track the light like real arrays. Give each base 4-6 panels; one 2x6 trans plate plus a 1x2 dark gray support per panel uses very few parts but delivers a lot of visual payoff. For the antenna, cap a 6-stud-tall gray mast with a 1x1 gold round tile and add a 2x2 satellite unit alongside it, and the base's tech dashboard is complete.

## 4. Domes and Greenhouse: Where Trans Parts Earn Their Keep

The greenhouse is the highlight of the whole base. Use a 6x6 trans hemisphere as the dome, with green plant pieces and 1x2 light blue hydroponic troughs inside; foliage viewed through the trans dome looks remarkably alive. I recommend stocking transparent parts at 15%-20% of the total, so for a 3,000-piece plan that means 450-600 trans pieces, including 30 trans round plates, 2-4 trans hemispheres and a batch of trans square plates. Trans parts cost a little more per piece, so budget about 150-250 CNY for this portion, but it pays you back.

## 5. Add Lighting and Minifigures: Night Mode Is the Finished Build

More than half of a Mars base's finished look comes from lights. Put a 1x1 trans light brick under every roof skylight and under the greenhouse dome, and use 1x8 light yellow plates in the corridors to fake strip lighting. Kill the room lights and warm glow spills out through the trans parts while orange minifigures move between modules, with a 6x4 Mars rover (about 60 parts) parked outside, and the base looks like it is really running on the red planet. For photos, use a 2-3 second long exposure to get the most dramatic lighting.

The Mars base MOC is a classic case of easy structure, hard atmosphere. Modular habitats keep it from collapsing; trans parts and lighting make it worth photographing. Search "Mars base" on brickplan.cn and you will find plans ranging from 1,500-piece small builds to 6,000-piece flagships; beginners are best off starting at the 3,000-piece level.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I build a brick Mars base MOC so it actually looks like sci-fi architecture?**
A: Break the base into modular habitats such as living quarters, greenhouse and airlock, keep the walls mostly white with dark gray framing, cut trans skylights into the roofs and light them, then add solar panels and antennas. That is where the sci-fi feel comes from.

**Q: How many parts and how much budget does a Mars base MOC need?**
A: A mid-size base runs about 3,000-4,000 parts, costs 1,000-1,800 CNY and takes 12-16 hours. Transparent parts should be 15%-20% of the total, with an extra budget of roughly 150-250 CNY, because the texture depends on them.

**Q: How should transparent parts be used in a Mars base MOC?**
A: Trans hemispheres for the greenhouse dome, trans round plates for roof skylights, and trans blue plates for solar panels. Put light bricks behind the trans parts so they pass daylight by day and glow at night, which is the core source of the sci-fi look.

**Q: How many minifigures and accessories fit on a Mars base MOC?**
A: A mid-size base on a 48x48 baseplate holds 4-5 minifigures. Add a 6x4 Mars rover, a 2x2 satellite unit and 4-6 solar panels and the scene is complete.

**Q: What separates a Mars base MOC from an ordinary house MOC?**
A: Modular habitats, transparent part ratio and lighting. A Mars base uses 15%-20% transparent parts versus under 5% for a house, and the habitats are plug-in, pull-out modules, so the structure leans much harder on docking corridor design.
