# Brick Drum Kit MOC Review: 1,250-Part Jazz Drum Display

The short version first: this brick drum MOC is one of the most popular instrument blueprints on brickplan.cn. It uses roughly 1,250 parts and the finished model is 38 cm wide, covering a full five-piece kit — bass drum, snare, tom, hi-hat and crash cymbal. The shapes read convincingly at a glance, which is why music-themed builders keep coming back to it.

## 1. Structural Accuracy

The designer builds the drum shells from cylinder pieces combined with smooth tiles, and represents the drum heads with dark grey round plates. The cymbals are stacked gold curved pieces, while the stand is put together from technic elements that allow the angles to be adjusted. Overall accuracy sits at roughly 85% — you obviously cannot play it, but the static model has a lot of depth and layering. Drumsticks and a seat are included as well.

## 2. Parts and Color Scheme

Of the roughly 1,250 parts, the palette is mostly red and black with gold cymbals as accents, which gives the whole thing a stage-ready look. The printed drum head pieces have crisp graphics, and small silver elements edge the metal hoops on the shells, so the fine detail holds up. The part mix spans technic pieces, standard bricks and plates, and compatibility is good — mixing it with parts from common brands is no problem.

## 3. Building Experience

The instructions can be previewed for free on brickplan.cn, and the full version is split into 6 stages. Our test build came in at about 5.5 hours, averaging 220 parts per hour. The stand has to be built symmetrically, so it is worth alternating left and right as you go to avoid mistakes. Cymbal mounting angles can be fine-tuned at the end, which is the finishing touch that really makes the model.

## 4. Value and Who It's For

Sourcing the individual parts costs roughly CNY 220–300, about 25% cheaper than a retail set of the same size. It suits rock fans, drummers and MOC builders who like building scenes — the finished kit looks great in a gaming room or a music room.

## FAQ

**Q: Can the brick drum MOC actually make sound?**

A: No. The drum heads are smooth round plates and the shells are closed structures, so tapping only produces the sound of plastic knocking together — nothing like a real drum's tone. This MOC is designed as a static display piece and does not aim for sound.

**Q: How is it different from a real drum kit?**

A: The difference is mainly functional. A real kit can be played and tuned, while the brick version only reproduces the appearance. The finished model is about 38 cm wide, which suits desktop display. The upside is that you can recolour or modify it whenever you like, so the play options are more open.
