# MOC Blueprint Review: Supercar, 4x4 and Urban GT Builds

## Introduction

Vehicle designs are the most popular category in the brick MOC world, and every vehicle MOC blueprint carries a designer's full attention to chassis layout, bodywork and mechanical detail. This review looks at three vehicle MOC blueprints side by side, from an Italian-styled supercar to a hardcore off-roader, to show how different vehicles change the way you build.

## Product Overview

This review covers three vehicle MOC blueprints: the **Fury-S Supercar**, the **Titan 4x4 Off-Roader** and the **Urban GT Road Coupe**. All three come from well-known MOC designers, and all rely mainly on standard Technic and brick parts, so they stay compatible with mainstream brick brands.

- **Fury-S Supercar**: roughly 1,800 pieces, a streamlined mid-engine supercar with opening scissor doors and a working V8 engine with moving pistons.
- **Titan 4x4 Off-Roader**: roughly 2,200 pieces, a rugged off-road build with independent suspension, four-wheel drive and linked steering.
- **Urban GT Road Coupe**: roughly 1,500 pieces, a compact front-engine sports car built around an elegant shape and a tight structure.

## Build Difficulty

The three blueprints form a clear difficulty ladder, so players at different stages have somewhere to start:

| Blueprint | Difficulty | Estimated build time | Best for |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| Fury-S Supercar | ★★★★☆ | 6-8 hours | Players with intermediate experience or above |
| Titan 4x4 Off-Roader | ★★★★☆ | 7-9 hours | Players who enjoy mechanical structures |
| Urban GT Road Coupe | ★★★☆☆ | 4-5 hours | Newer players with the basics down |

The Fury-S scissor doors and the Titan suspension system are the hard parts of each build and need careful cross-checking against the blueprint. The Urban GT is far more forgiving and works well as a first step into more advanced vehicle MOC building.

## Part Evaluation

**Fury-S Supercar**: a red and black colour scheme, with generous use of curved panels and slope bricks to shape the body lines. The rarer parts include red scissor-door hinge pieces and custom wheels, so it asks a fair amount of your parts collection — around 70% of the pieces are common types.

**Titan 4x4 Off-Roader**: dark green and dark grey, built largely from Technic parts — gears, differentials, suspension arms and shock absorber springs. The drivetrain is fully modelled, which makes it a good pick for anyone who likes understanding how the mechanics work. Around 85% of the pieces are common Technic parts.

**Urban GT Road Coupe**: a pure white body with blue stripes and a clean, simple look. The part selection is fairly basic, which suits builders working from a limited collection. Around 90% of the pieces come from common brick sets.

## Build Experience

The Fury-S build is full of surprises. The chassis is modular: you build the central transmission tunnel first, then extend outward to both sides. Installing the scissor doors is the real test of patience — the angles need repeated trial and error — but the finished result is worth it. The V8 piston linkage under the engine cover runs smoothly and feels genuinely mechanical.

Building the Titan 4x4 feels closer to assembling a real off-roader. You start with the chassis rails, then work through the front and rear axles, the driveshaft and the steering links, and only then add the body panels. The instructions are very detailed, with piece counts and installation angles clearly marked at each step. The independent suspension has a long travel range, so the model sits and moves in a convincing way.

The Urban GT is the most relaxed build of the three — the chassis is done in about 90 minutes, and the rest is bodywork and detailing. There is no complex mechanical linkage, but the body lines are reproduced very faithfully, and the front grille and tail lights are cleverly designed. It is a good project for a spare afternoon, and the sense of achievement arrives quickly.

## Finished Models

Placed side by side, the three finished models could hardly look more different:

- **Fury-S**: low and aggressive, with sharp lines and a bold red finish. With the scissor doors open it commands the whole shelf and becomes a centrepiece for a display cabinet.
- **Titan 4x4**: bulky and imposing, with large off-road tyres on a high chassis for real visual impact. Rolling it forward shows smooth suspension travel and linked steering, so it is highly playable.
- **Urban GT**: compact and refined, with a clean white body that pairs well with a city street scene. The proportions are well balanced and it makes an excellent desk display piece.

## Verdict

Each of these vehicle MOC blueprints has its own focus. The Fury-S is for builders chasing maximum exterior shape and mechanical detail. The Titan 4x4 is for anyone who loves mechanical structures and off-road style. The Urban GT is the obvious entry point for newer builders. Whether you are a supercar fan or an off-road enthusiast, one of the three will give you a build you enjoy.

If you are hunting for high-quality vehicle MOC blueprints, these three are a solid place to start — and a good way to see how much variety the brick building hobby can offer.
