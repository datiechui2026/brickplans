# Brick Goose MOC Review: A 450-Part Long-Necked White Goose

The short version first: this goose MOC uses roughly 450 parts and builds into a model about 13 cm tall and 18 cm long. Curved white slopes capture the bird's round body and long neck, the bill is orange, and the overall likeness lands around 90%. It is a standing display piece with a lot of personality, aimed at builders aged 10 and up, and sourcing the loose parts costs about 100 to 140 CNY.

## Appearance and Accuracy

Everything about a white goose comes down to that smug, faintly ridiculous expression. The designer stacks curved white slopes into a plump, rounded body, then runs the neck up and forward using white lengths 2 to 3 studs wide so the curve reads correctly from any angle. The orange bill and the rounded knob at its base are built from dedicated parts, and the eyes are placed so the bird looks like it is side-eyeing you — tip the head and the comedy writes itself. Overall accuracy is around 90%, and among poultry MOCs this is the one that gets the attitude right.

## Structure and Design

Inside the body, frame bricks do the load-bearing work, and the curved slopes step inward layer by layer from the bottom up to give the goose a full, rounded chest and belly. The neck connects to the body through a ball joint, which means you can pose it in three ways: head lowered, head raised, or head tilted to one side. The wings are built from plates and angled slightly outward, and the tail feathers flick upward. The stance is stable and does not lean backward, so it sits safely on a windowsill or a bookshelf without wanting to topple.

## Build Experience

The instructions come from brickplan.cn. Our editor finished the model in about 1.5 hours across three stages. The hard part is closing the curved body: roughly 40 parts have to be layered at specific angles to keep the shell even. The neck also needs its flexible hose pre-bent and shaped before you fix it in place. The instructions include close-up views for those steps, so mistakes are rare, and there are no stickers anywhere in the build.

## Value and Who It Suits

At about 100 to 140 CNY in loose parts, this sits at the entry level for animal MOCs. It suits younger builders who like meme-worthy desk pieces, poultry and animal fans, and families looking for a parent-and-child build. Parked on a office desk, this confident, social goose does a surprisingly good job of taking the edge off a workday.

## FAQ

**Q: Can the goose MOC's neck move?**

A: Yes. The neck attaches to the body through a ball joint, so you can pose it by hand with the head lowered, raised, or tilted. Paired with the outward-angled wings, that gives you plenty of silly poses — good material for photos.

**Q: How much does the brick goose MOC cost?**

A: Loose parts run about 100 to 140 CNY, and part of the instructions can be previewed free on brickplan.cn. White is a very common colour, so if you already have white in your stock the cost can drop below 80 CNY, which makes it an easy entry build.

**Q: What age is the goose MOC suitable for?**

A: Ages 10 and up is the recommendation. At roughly 450 parts the difficulty is moderate, and the body and neck are built in three stages; with a parent helping, it takes about 1.5 hours. Younger children can build just the body while an adult handles the neck and wings.

**Q: How is this different from a brick duck MOC?**

A: The goose is bigger, its neck is longer and usually curved, and its bill and the knob at the bill's base are thicker and more orange. A duck is smaller with a short, flat bill. This MOC follows the long-necked goose, so putting it next to a yellow-billed duck model makes the difference obvious.

**Q: Is the brick goose MOC worth buying?**

A: Yes. With 450 parts, around 90% accuracy and an entry-level price of 100 to 140 CNY, it is strong value for an animal display model — and once it is built it sits on your desk as a genuinely funny centrepiece.
