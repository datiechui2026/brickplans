# How to Choose a Super Mario Brick Set: Interactive Play Review

After several years of reviewing brick sets, **Super Mario brick sets** are one of the lines I get asked about most. They pair classic NES-era characters with interactive bricks, and the design logic is nothing like a standard street scene or vehicle. Rather than listing specs, this review covers what actually makes them fun, how the popular sets differ, and whether they are worth your money.

## What a Super Mario brick set really is: the core mechanic first

The heart of a Super Mario brick set is an interactive electronic play system: a small electronic Mario figure with a screen, a speaker and sensors, plus brick-built level scenery. Place the figure on the start gate and it plays the theme music and shows the time; send it along the track bricks and it runs forward, "bumps" enemy bricks it passes, picks up points for clearing obstacles, and reaches the flagpole to finish the level. The route itself is whatever you build — where the enemies go and how the jumps are designed is entirely up to your imagination.

## Interactive play: fun, and not one-note

Unlike a purely static display piece, the appeal of a Super Mario brick set is that you build it and then keep playing with it. Levels can be rebuilt again and again: a flat sprint today, a two-tier jump course tomorrow, dressed up with coin, pipe and mushroom element bricks for something new each time. The electronic figure also responds to sound, so when the music kicks in, kids and adults alike start bouncing along. Be clear about one thing, though: this is building first and interactivity second. Don't expect handheld-console responsiveness — treat it as a brick racetrack that makes noise and you'll get the most out of it.

## Choosing between the popular sets

Budget and level size are the two things that matter most. If money is tight and you want to test the water, a starter set is the lowest entry point: one complete track plus the electronic figure, which is plenty for a long time. If you want richer scenery, look at mid-size sets with more enemies and mechanisms. Long-time builders or anyone planning a cabinet display should go for the large scene sets — more parts and higher accuracy, and they hold their own as a display piece. There is also an official "level pack" category: few parts but interesting accessories, ideal for adding to a track you already own.

## Collectibility and play value: who should buy

As a collectible, the **Super Mario brick set** holds up just as well: iconic character designs, a soft rounded colour palette and well-executed printed pieces look great grouped together. Its value also lies in a double identity — a moving toy that gets kids hands-on, and a nostalgia collectible that lets you revisit childhood. For families where everyone builds, the value is genuinely high: one set covers two generations of fun.

## Buying pitfalls and final advice

Three things to keep in mind. First, the electronic figure is not waterproof and doesn't like dust — store it separately. Second, the battery and speaker components should be protected from drops and knocks. Third, the price gap between grey-market and official-channel stock is small, so buy from a channel that offers after-sales support. Overall, the **Super Mario brick set** suits anyone curious about interactive play, and it is especially good for families with kids.
