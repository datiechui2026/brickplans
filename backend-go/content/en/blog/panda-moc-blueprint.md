# How to Pick a Panda MOC Blueprint and Build It Right

A panda is one of the most popular subjects in animal MOCs, and it is easy to see why. That round body and unmistakable black-and-white pattern are made for bricks. The problem is that panda MOC blueprints online vary wildly: some are beautifully accurate, others fall apart halfway through the build. This guide covers how to pick a blueprint and how to handle the techniques that make a panda actually stand up and look right.

## 1. How to Choose a Panda MOC Blueprint

Do not judge a blueprint by its render alone. Check three things. First, does it include a clear parts list, ideally with part numbers and colors, so you can gather bricks without guessing? Second, are the build steps photographed stage by stage, especially the internal structure where the head meets the body? That joint is where most builds go wrong. Third, look at the finished size. A panda 20 to 30 cm tall works best as a desk display: any smaller and the facial details get hard to express, any larger and you burn through bricks and budget.

Beginners should start with a simple structure where the body is a stack of core bricks and the limbs attach on the outside. More experienced builders can take on fully rounded, no-right-angle designs that push accuracy further.

## 2. Black-and-White Zones: Shape First, Color Second

The key to a convincing panda is a clean separation between black and white. Before you place a single brick, mark the color boundaries on the blueprint: black belongs on the ears, around the eyes, across the shoulder band and on the limbs, while white fills the torso and face. A practical approach is to build the white torso frame first, then apply the black bricks as a skin on the surface, rather than mixing black and white randomly as you stack.

For the eye patches, combine 1x1 round plates with curved slopes to build an oval outline, then set dark gray printed eye bricks inside. The face instantly gains expression. For the ears, use 2x2 round dome bricks or mount 1x1 curved plates sideways for a rounded, three-dimensional look. Keep one rule in mind: the more restrained the black areas, the cuter the panda reads.

## 3. The Secret to a Rounded Body: Wedge and Arch Bricks

Color is not the hard part of a panda. Roundness is. A panda stacked from square-cornered bricks looks like a box. The fix is to use wedge (slope) bricks and arch bricks generously. On the back, taper 1x2 and 2x3 wedge bricks layer by layer to create a gentle hump. On the belly, use inverted wedge bricks as a transition so the stomach curves outward and looks full. At the shoulders and hips, arch bricks such as 1x3 arches create the turning points.

Do not build the limbs as straight tubes either. Connect them with ball joint pieces, finish the ends with wedge bricks to suggest claws, and add black claw pad tiles at the tips. That is what produces the charming look of a panda resting on its paws.

## 4. Sitting or Standing? Decide Your Display Needs First

Sitting and standing poses each have trade-offs, so decide where the model will live before you commit. A sitting panda has a low center of gravity and a stable structure, which makes it ideal for a bookshelf or desktop. It is also easier to build, and the limbs can be simple attachments, so a beginner can finish one in an evening. A standing panda has far more presence, but the higher center of gravity means you must bury long axles and reinforcement columns inside the core, and the leg joints need high-friction ball parts or the model will topple at the slightest touch.

My advice: build a sitting panda first to practice the black-and-white zoning and the rounded shaping, then take on a high-accuracy standing version once you have the feel for it.

## 5. Parts List and Budget

Always check your stock against the blueprint's parts list before you start. The core parts for a panda MOC are white and black basic bricks, wedge bricks, arch bricks, curved slopes and ball joint pieces. Specialized parts such as printed eye bricks and claw pad pieces are worth buying in advance.

For a sitting panda about 25 cm tall, expect roughly 800 to 1,200 bricks and a cost of about 150 to 250 CNY. If you already have a healthy loose-brick supply, many of the white basic bricks can be reused directly, which brings the cost down further.
