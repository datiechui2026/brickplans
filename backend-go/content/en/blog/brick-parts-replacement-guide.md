# How to Replace Missing Brick Parts: LEGO and Chinese Brands

![Finished model built with replacement brick parts](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/brick-parts-replacement-guide/brick-parts-replacement-guide-1.jpg)

Few things deflate a new set faster than pouring out the bags and finding a couple of pieces short, or reaching bag three and discovering a key part is the wrong size. Brick part replacement is far less painful than most builders assume: find the right channel, get the part number right, and the overwhelming majority of missing-piece cases get resolved. This guide walks through it brand by brand, from filing a claim to the workarounds that keep the build moving.

## 1. First: Is It Really Missing, or in the Wrong Bag?

![Steps for replacing brick parts](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/brick-parts-replacement-guide/brick-parts-replacement-guide-2.jpg)

Before you file anything, confirm the part is genuinely absent. Empty every bag and sort the contents by colour and size — a surprising number of "missing" pieces are sitting quietly in a different bag, or got swept into a box by a child. Step two: work through the Parts List at the back of the manual and tick off each entry line by line. Once you are certain something is gone, photograph the gap. A clear photo of the missing part is mandatory evidence for most claims. Chinese brands usually also ask for the batch code printed on the packaging, often on the manual cover or the side of the box — do not skip that step, because a claim without it tends to bounce back.

## 2. LEGO's Official Missing-Piece Service

LEGO handles replacements in a very organised way. Log into the LEGO website, open the missing parts service page, enter the set number (printed on the manual cover, for example 21322) and the quantity you need, and the system generates a request. Each set can have parts replaced free of charge within a set period after purchase; once that window closes, it becomes a paid order. Attach a photo of the missing piece where the form allows it — it speeds up review. One thing to plan around: LEGO ships these out internationally, so 2–4 weeks is completely normal for many countries. If you need the part this week, jump to the alternatives in section 5.

## 3. Replacement Channels for Chinese Brick Brands

![Close-up of individual brick elements](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/brick-parts-replacement-guide/brick-parts-replacement-guide-3.jpg)

Most Chinese manufacturers — SEMBO, Panlos, Double Eagle, Sluban, Qiji and others — run a replacement form through their WeChat official account or their customer service desk. The flow is nearly identical everywhere: find "after-sales service" or "replacement request" in the account menu, enter the order number, the set model and the missing parts, attach photos and submit. The advantage here is speed and easy communication; most brands ship within 3–7 days. Can't locate the form? Message the customer service of the shop you bought from, say the parts are missing and quote the batch code — the vast majority of sellers will send replacements out. Keeping your proof of purchase is the prerequisite for a smooth claim, so file the receipt somewhere you can find it later.

## 4. How to Find a Part Number

Whichever channel you use, an accurate part number roughly doubles your efficiency. On LEGO elements the number is moulded into a stud or a recess on the inside of the piece, so shoot it with your phone's macro mode and read it off the screen. For lookup, Bricklink and BrickSet are the tools to reach for: type the number into Bricklink's catalogue page and you get the official part name, the full colour list and any known alternate parts. Chinese manufacturers generally follow the LEGO numbering system, but some special pieces carry their own numbers — when that happens, ask in the brand's official parts library or in a builders' community group.

## 5. Alternatives and Money-Saving Tricks

If replacement is too slow, or you are only short one or two obscure pieces, swapping in something from your own stock is the faster fix. 1×1 and 1×2 plates and bricks of the same size are effectively universal across brands, and a slight colour mismatch will not affect the structure at all. A few practical substitutions: transparent parts can be replaced with another colour in the same spec and covered with a sticker; a broken hinge can be rebuilt from two connectors; gears and other drivetrain parts are best kept to the original number, and load-bearing pieces should not be swapped casually. On cost, join a local builders' group to trade parts with other people, or buy bulk lots by weight on secondhand marketplaces — plenty of replacement needs are solved by one handful of generic pieces.

Whether you are building LEGO or a Chinese brand, missing parts are no reason to panic: verify first, go through the official channel next, and let substitutions cover the obscure pieces. Brick part replacement rarely fails when you work in that order.
