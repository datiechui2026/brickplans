# Farm MOC Review: Building Country Life Out of Bricks

Farm-themed brick builds have grown steadily more popular over the past two years. Whether it is a cottage, a barn, a vegetable plot, or a spread of small animals and farm machinery, a farm MOC always delivers a quiet, comforting atmosphere. As a long-time BrickPlans builder, I have been putting together and testing a number of farm MOCs, and today I want to walk through the highlights and the points worth watching out for.

## Structure and Build Experience

The core of a farm build usually revolves around a barn, livestock pens and a main house. In the farm MOC I tested, the roof mixes slope bricks with tile bricks, and the pitch is handled so naturally that it reads as a real roofline. A ring of decorative brackets runs under the eaves, which gives the whole thing a strong sense of layering. Inside, attic space has been left open so you can store hay bales or crates, adding a good amount of play potential beyond the exterior. The instruction manual divides the steps clearly, and at no point did parts fight each other during assembly. Built in stages, it took roughly four to five hours, which puts it in the medium-to-large range.

## Parts Usage and Color Palette

The part selection here is genuinely surprising. Beyond the usual bricks and plates, the model leans heavily on plant elements, fence pieces and wheeled chassis parts. The cornfield is built by stacking groups of small leaf elements over and over, which looks tidy while still giving that real furrowed-field feel. The palette is built on off-white, wood brown and grass green, accented with a small amount of red roof tile and yellow wheat ears. Saturation is kept well under control, so the model does not look busy or cluttered sitting on a desk. Worth calling out: there were almost no leftover parts after the build. Every part category is used to its full purpose, which is not something you see often in farm MOCs.

## Scene Atmosphere and Playability

What really won me over is the everyday life the farm MOC creates. The tractor's driver seat rotates and the cargo bed tips up; the chicken coop door opens, and the hen-and-egg linkage mechanism is very neatly done. Add a few cows, a sheepdog and a row of sunflowers, and the whole scene comes alive at once. Beyond play, you can find matching accessories and alternate build options for the same series on the BrickPlans site, so you can add to the farm to your own taste.

## Buying Advice and Verdict

If you like display pieces with a soothing feel and still want something you can actually play with, this farm MOC is worth a try. Its strengths are a solid structure, a palette that holds up to long viewing, and light, clever mechanisms. The weak points are a fairly large footprint, which means you need to set aside enough display space, and the fact that some of the small parts will be a bit of a challenge for younger builders. Overall, I give it 8.5/10. For more farm MOC instructions and building plans, head over to brickplan.cn. May your desk have its own little patch of countryside too.
