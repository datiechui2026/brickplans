# Brick Hot Spring MOC Tutorial: Pool, Rocks and Zen Garden

Bottom line first: a brick hot spring is one of the most photogenic subjects in the street-scene category. Expect roughly 3,000 pieces, a budget of about 250 RMB and 10–12 hours of building time, for a finished model around 32×24×18 cm. Transparent pieces make the water surface, curved pieces pile up into the rocks, and three soaking minifigures finish the scene — drop it in a display cabinet and the effect is immediate. Beginners can build it by following three stages: base, pool, garden. Instructions, including the parts list, are available by searching "hot spring MOC" on brickplan.cn.

## Step 1: Base and Pool (Water From Transparent Pieces)

Lay a 32×24 dark-colored plate as the foundation, then stand a ring of lighter-colored bricks around the perimeter, reserving three zones: the pool, the garden and the walkway.

For the pool, wall the inside with light blue plates to form a rectangle of about 12×10, and line the bottom with dark blue plates to suggest the pool floor. The water surface itself is flat-laid trans-light-blue plates, left with a half-stud drop at the edge; scatter a few transparent round tiles on top as pebbles and the water immediately gains depth. Finish the rim with brown curved pieces to imitate the look of a wooden tub — this is the single step that sells the whole model.

## Step 2: Rocks and the Japanese Garden

Build the rocks from dark grey and light grey curved slopes, stacked from the bottom up. Use large pieces at the base for stability and small pieces at the top, staggered to a point, then tuck green leaf elements into the gaps as moss.

For the garden, create a dry landscape group: pave a sand area from small off-white round plates and drag curved pieces through it to cut ripple lines. In the corner, place a stone lantern (stacked dark grey pieces with a transparent piece on top as the light) and a bamboo fence (brown bar pieces set side by side). Plant two pine trees — brown trunks topped with dark green leaf clusters — staggered in height on either side of the rocks.

## Step 3: Soaking Minifigures and Atmosphere Details

The bathers are the soul of the build: remove the legs from each minifigure and set only the torso into the pool, tilt a white plate over the head as a towel, and place a tray, teapot and small sake pieces on the poolside. The scene comes alive instantly.

Add a few clusters of white round plates above the water as steam, stack transparent pieces over warm-colored studs at the stone lantern and the doorway curtain for lighting, and hang a string of red lanterns under the eaves to push the Japanese atmosphere all the way. Finally, pile small white pieces around the pool rim as "snow" — the finished model photographs extremely well.

## FAQ

**Q: How many pieces and how much money does a brick hot spring MOC need?**
A: About 2,600–3,400 pieces and a budget of 220–300 RMB. Sourcing loose parts from the list yourself comes to roughly 250 RMB, including the transparent water pieces and curved rock pieces — around 30% cheaper than buying finished sets.

**Q: How do you build the water surface effect in the pool?**
A: Flat-lay trans-light-blue plates for the surface, back them with dark blue plates underneath, then pile white pieces at the edge for foam. Three layers of drop give it a steamy look, with no special printed pieces required.

**Q: Can a beginner build a hot spring MOC?**
A: Yes — the difficulty is medium. Work through base, pool and garden in that order and you can finish in 10–12 hours. Rock stacking and pool construction both follow clear rules, and although there are many transparent pieces, the tolerance for error is high.

**Q: How do you pose the soaking minifigures naturally?**
A: Remove the minifigure legs and put the torso straight into the pool, tilt a white towel piece over the head, set a tray and teapot at the poolside, and add one or two clusters of steam pieces. The scene reads as alive right away.
