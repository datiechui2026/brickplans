# LEGO Clothing Store MOC Tutorial: Window Display & Racks

The short version first: this brick clothing store MOC uses roughly 2,900 parts on a 32x32 baseplate to create a two-storey fashion shop about 25 cm tall. Budget around 250 CNY and about 8 hours of building time. The window mannequin and the clothing racks are what decide how polished the whole shop looks.

A clothing store is the shop type that shows off "refinement" best in a street scene: a mannequin in the window, racks full of garments, a fitting room hiding a small surprise, and a clean, tidy colour scheme. This tutorial covers the build in four steps — window and mannequin, racks and display area, fitting room and counter, then awning and overall atmosphere. Instructions and the parts list are available on brickplan.cn.

## Step 1: Window and Mannequin

The shop window is the face of the store. Leave a front opening 12 cm wide and 6 cm high, and use large clear glass elements as the full-height window. Inside, lay a light pink baseplate as the display platform. The mannequin is built from a white minifigure body modified with a stand, placed in the centre of the window wearing a printed shirt, with a handbag and a pair of shoes beside it to form a complete outfit display. This step takes about 700 parts. Remember to frame the glass elements with 1x1 bricks around the edges.

## Step 2: Racks and Display Area

On the ground floor, build two rack groups along the wall. The wall-side rail uses 1x2 bricks with holes plus a technic axle as the crossbar, holding 6-8 garments. The free-standing shelf in the middle has three tiers for folded T-shirts and pants. Mix printed elements with solid-colour parts, and arrange them by a "light on the left, dark on the right" colour rule — it reads as neat and upscale at the same time. The rack area is about 12 cm tall, matching minifigure height so you can pose a figure reaching for a garment.

## Step 3: Fitting Room and Counter

Put the fitting room at the back of the shop. Enclose a cubicle roughly 4x4 cm with 1x2 bricks, hang a curtain at the entrance (a soft rubber element or a printed piece), and place a mirror inside — a silver reflective element with a frame. The counter sits beside the doorway: a white countertop with a cash register, and a rail in front holding a "new arrivals" sign. The fitting room and counter use about 200 parts; they are small, but they make the shop function as a shop and give it a sense of story.

## Step 4: Signage and Overall Atmosphere

Build the awning from pink 1x2 slope bricks, spell out "FASHION" on the sign with letter bricks, and add a ring of warm yellow transparent elements under the awning to suggest lighting. Place two small spotlight elements inside the window as well. At the entrance, set down a potted plant and a welcome mat, and hang two "garments" over the second-floor windowsill — the lived-in feel appears immediately. The whole build is about 2,900 parts and 8 hours. Put it next to ordinary street models and this shop will be the brightest frontage on the block.
