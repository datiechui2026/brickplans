# MOC Beginner Toolkit: Parts, Tools and a Realistic Budget

Here is the short version: you need four categories of parts, plus a brick separator, a sorting box and tweezers. CNY 300-500 is enough to start. Do not hoard bricks.

Most people who stall at the beginning of their MOC journey are not blocked by "I can't build" — they are blocked by "what do I buy, how much, and where from." This guide is a kit list plus a mistake-avoidance sheet: stock the parts and tools below, pick the right first plan, sidestep the traps beginners fall into, and get moving for as little money as possible.

## Core Part Inventory: Start With Four Categories

You do not need a warehouse. Cover these four categories by frequency of use and you are ready. **Basic bricks**: common sizes such as 2x4, 2x2 and 2x6, a few dozen of each — this is the skeleton of any model. **Plates**: thin 1x4, 1x6 and 2x8 plates for walls and vehicle bodies; this is the highest-volume category by far. **Slopes and curved slopes**: what gives roofs and car fronts their shape. Non-negotiable. **Baseplates and foundation plates**: at least one 32x32 or 48x48 baseplate, the ground your model stands on. Roughly 1,500 pieces across these four groups will cover more than ninety percent of beginner plans.

## Essential Tools: Separator and Sorting Box

The tool list is short, but three items are mandatory. A **brick separator** is the MOC builder's second hand — every wrong connection gets undone with it, so buy one on day one. A **sorting box** lets you organize parts by size and color; when you are halfway through a build and hunting for one specific brick, clear sorting saves you a huge amount of time. **Small tweezers** handle tiny parts and are especially useful for micro-scale scenes. If your budget allows, add a soft cloth mat — loose parts are less likely to get scratched and cleanup is easier. Together these cost only a few dozen yuan, but they are the dividing line in the beginner experience: with the right tools, building actually feels good.

## Choosing Your First Plan

Pick the plan well and you are most of the way to success. Three criteria matter. **Check the difficulty label**: choose plans marked beginner or entry level, ideally between 200 and 500 pieces. **Check the parts list**: confirm the parts and element numbers are either already in your stock or easy to reorder, so you are not importing single elements for one build. **Check the completeness**: favor plans with step-by-step instructions and a clear color scheme. Rebuild two or three simple plans first to get a feel for how parts connect and how easily they come apart, then work up to harder models.

## Four Mistakes Beginners Make

First, **buying the wrong parts** — ordering by shape alone without checking the element number, then finding the size does not match. Always buy against the numbers printed in the plan. Second, **inconsistent scale** — doors, windows, minifigures and walls at mismatched sizes make a model look fake. Set a scale reference before you build and keep the whole structure on it. Third, **starting with a plan that is too complex** — jumping straight into a thousand-piece build, abandoning it halfway, and losing confidence. Start simple; a finished model is the fuel that keeps you building. Fourth, **hoarding bricks** — buying several kilograms of assorted bulk because it looks cheap, and ending up with a pile of parts you never use. Buy to a list and top up as you go; that is how you save money.

## Budget: A CNY 300-800 Starter Plan

Getting started does not cost much. The **CNY 300-500 basic tier** is around 1,500 loose basic bricks (about CNY 200-300) plus a separator, sorting box, baseplate and other tools (about CNY 50-100) — enough to finish your first few small models. The **CNY 500-800 step-up tier** adds a batch of special elements, transparent elements and foundation plates on top, a spare baseplate, and a simple work lamp so evening building is comfortable. If money is tight, put it into common bricks and good tools rather than into sheer part count. Once you know which direction you actually love — buildings, vehicles or dioramas — expand your inventory in a targeted way.

A good first move is to find a simple plan on brickplan and build it; the site labels plans by difficulty and lists the parts each one needs, which makes it easy to pick a beginner-friendly starting point. Kit ready, plan chosen, traps avoided — your first MOC is already most of the way there. The only real failure mode in this hobby is never starting.

## FAQ

**Q: Which basic parts does a new MOC builder need?**
A: Four categories: basic bricks (a few dozen each of 2x4 and similar), plates (1x4, 1x6, 2x8 — the highest volume), slopes and curved slopes, and at least one 32x32 baseplate. Around 1,500 pieces across these covers ninety percent of beginner plans.

**Q: What tools are essential for MOC building?**
A: Three: a brick separator (for undoing mistakes), a sorting box (organize by size and color to save search time) and small tweezers (for tiny parts). Add a soft cloth mat if you can. A few dozen yuan total, but it changes the whole experience.

**Q: How do I choose my first plan?**
A: Use three checks: a beginner or entry-level difficulty label with 200-500 pieces; a parts list you already own or can easily reorder; and step-by-step instructions with a clear color scheme. Rebuild two or three before moving up.

**Q: How should a beginner split the budget?**
A: CNY 300-500 covers about 1,500 loose basic bricks (CNY 200-300) plus separator, sorting box and baseplate (CNY 50-100) — enough for your first small models. Prioritize common bricks and good tools over part count.
