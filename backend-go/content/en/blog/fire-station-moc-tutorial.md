# Fire Station MOC Tutorial: Build Your Own Brick Rescue HQ

A fire station is one of the most recognizable buildings in any brick city. The bold red garage doors, the engine waiting on standby, and the lookout tower on top make it instantly readable from across the layout. This fire station MOC tutorial walks you through building your own rescue headquarters one stage at a time, whether you are putting together your first modular-scale building or you have a shelf full of MOCs already. You can find matching instructions and parts lists at [brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn).

## 1. Setup and Parts List

Before you snap a single brick, sort out what you need. The main body works best in four colors: **red, white, dark gray, and black**. Red handles the garage doors and trim, white carries the walls, and dark gray plus black build the roof and frame. Expect to use roughly 2,500 to 4,000 pieces, with baseplates, corner bricks, and 1x2 plates doing most of the structural work.

A brick separator and a few sorting trays will save you real time here. Sort by color first, then by size, so the 1x2 plates and corner bricks are always within reach when a wall run needs closing.

## 2. Baseplate and Main Structure

Start on a 32x32 or 48x32 baseplate and map out the footprint of the station before you build upward. The main building is usually two stories: the ground floor holds the garage bays and a duty room, while the upper floor holds a rest area and office space.

Begin by raising load-bearing columns at all four corners, then fill in the exterior walls with wall bricks, leaving openings for windows and the large garage door. Put the staircase on the side of the building — it saves interior floor space and still gives you a clean route to the second floor. Stability is everything at this stage: pack thicker plates under the floor layer so the finished building does not flex or wobble when you move it.

## 3. Fire Engine Bay Design

The garage is the heart of the whole build. The common layout is a **double bay**, with each door about 8 to 10 studs wide and 6 to 7 studs tall so a fire engine can roll in and out cleanly. For the doors themselves you have two good options: sliding door parts with tracks, or a roller-shutter look built from curved red and white bricks.

Leave plenty of depth inside the bays. Lay the floor in dark gray plates — the darker surface makes the fire engine pop and hides smudges from handling. Do not skip the signage either: print or sticker a clear station identifier above the bay openings so the building reads as a fire station from a distance.

## 4. Red Color Scheme and Details

Red is the soul of a fire station. Use it on window and door frames, along the roof line, and on the chimney, letting it contrast hard against the white walls. The small details are what sell the realism:

- Build an **alarm** on the roof from a trans-red dome element stacked on a small cylinder.
- Hang a **fire hose** on the exterior wall using flexible hose or chain elements coiled against the brickwork.
- Add a **ladder** that extends out past a window, built so it can be raised and lowered.
- Place a pair of minifigures on the second-floor balcony to suggest firefighters on standby.

## 5. Scene Styling and Finished Display

Finish the build by dressing the scene around it. Park a red and white fire engine at the front, stand a hydrant beside it, plant a few small trees, and run a short stretch of sidewalk along the street edge. If you want more presence, add a clock tower or lookout tower above the bays — it gives the silhouette a strong vertical anchor.

Once it is done, photograph the station from several angles. The richer the detail work, the more the finished model rewards a close look. For more scene ideas and instructions, head to [brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn).

A convincing fire station comes down to more than accurate part counts — it is the deliberate color choices and the small details that make it feel lived in. Start sorting, lay down that baseplate, and give your brick city a little more safety and street life.
