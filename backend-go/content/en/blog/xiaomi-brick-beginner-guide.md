# Xiaomi Brick Beginner Guide: How to Pick Your First Set

The hardest question for anyone new to Xiaomi bricks is simple: what should my first set be? The brick kits from Xiaomi's ecosystem partners have always punched above their price in the Chinese market, but the lineup is wide and the positioning is messy. Without someone pointing the way, it is easy to buy wrong. This guide skips the fluff and covers how to choose a starter set by budget and interest, plus the traps to dodge, so your first purchase is money well spent.

## Start with piece count and product positioning

The first rule of buying Xiaomi bricks as a beginner: look at piece count and which line the set belongs to before you talk about value. Xiaomi's kits roughly split into three lines — a small-brick technic line built around mechanical structures and gear transmission, a large-brick preschool line aimed at parent-and-child building, and a themed line leaning toward display models. New builders should start with a mid-small set in the 300–800 piece range. Too many pieces and your first build may wear you down before you finish; too few and you never get to enjoy mechanical structures. Around 100 RMB is enough to buy a good entry-level kit, so there is no need to jump straight to a thousand-piece monster.

## Part quality: are Xiaomi bricks actually reliable?

Many newcomers worry that Chinese bricks feel loose and sloppy. Xiaomi's parts are actually solid. This is the key point for beginners: the ABS parts clutch firmly, sprue marks are cleaned up well, and the colors are reasonably accurate — anyone who has built LEGO will transition without friction. One thing to know is that Xiaomi parts generally run tight. Press them down firmly and fully when building, and do not use brute force when taking things apart; a brick separator makes life much easier. Wheels and gears are smoother than most rivals at the same price, so the linked mechanisms in the technic sets will not disappoint.

## Instruction experience: easy to follow the manual

Xiaomi's instructions are among the friendliest to beginners at this price point. Steps are broken down in detail, each step lists the color and quantity of the parts involved, and the way parts stack from one step to the next is drawn clearly — you basically never wonder where a piece is supposed to go. A useful beginner trick: pre-sort parts following the step numbers in the manual, for example one bag per five steps. It is far faster than dumping the whole box out. Occasionally the manual includes parts whose color callouts are not obvious; when you are unsure, compare the outline against the physical piece instead of guessing from the color alone.

## Choose by interest: three types of builder, three ways to buy

Rather than agonizing over which kit has the best rating, match the set to what you actually enjoy. If you like mechanisms and vehicles, pick an engineering truck or race car from the technic line — the transmission and steering structures are genuinely satisfying to complete. If you like display pieces and anime themes, pick a showcase model from the themed line; it looks great on a desk. If you are buying for a child at home, pick the large-brick preschool line — bigger parts are safer and the build is easier to pick up. On a tight budget, stay with the small and mid-size sets around 100 RMB, get a feel for building first, then think about upgrading to something bigger.

## Beginner pitfall checklist

A few traps that real beginners have fallen into. First, do not chase piece count — a first set that is too big is easy to abandon halfway. Second, do not get swept up by licensed tie-ins; check whether the piece count actually fits an entry-level build. Third, make sure your desk is well lit before you start — dark parts are genuinely hard to find in dim light. Fourth, do not panic when parts scatter; sort them into rough color piles and you will find what you need fast. Follow these and your first build should go smoothly.

## How to progress after the first build

When the first set is finished, do not rush to put it back in the box. Take it apart by working backwards through the manual steps to practice your disassembly and reassembly feel, then try small changes on top of the original design — swap the color scheme or add decorations. That is the first step from building what you are told to building your own MOC modifications. Once you have built up a stock of spare parts, you will find the room for modification is bigger than you imagined.
