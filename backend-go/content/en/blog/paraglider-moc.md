# Paraglider MOC Review: 290 Pieces, 18cm Curved Canopy

**Verdict first:** the paraglider MOC from brickplan is a very complete little build. The curved canopy is the standout, the suspended minifigure is genuinely fun to handle, and at roughly 290 pieces with an 18cm wingspan it photographs beautifully on a desk. Overall score: 8.8/10, recommended for anyone who likes flight-themed models.

## 1. Design and Shape

The canopy is what sells this model. Instead of a flat plate build, the designer stacked plates in two layers offset sideways and then fine-tuned the shape with hinge pieces, producing a natural concave curve — like a sail filled by wind. The proportions work: an 18cm wingspan against about 14cm front to back, in a red-and-white colour scheme that reads instantly from across a room. Paired with the transparent stand, the whole thing looks like it is floating.

## 2. Detail and Accuracy

The harnessed minifigure is the second highlight. It connects to the lines through two short bar pieces and hangs in a seated pose, mimicking the suspended position of a real paraglider pilot. Helmet and goggles are both present, and the figure's hands can grip the brake handles. The lines use three different lengths of hose pieces, staggered so the load path looks more believable — far more convincing than the usual straight-up, straight-down approach.

## 3. Build Experience

The whole model is about 290 pieces, split across four numbered step bags: canopy, lines, harnessed pilot and stand. You can finish it in roughly 45 minutes. The only part that needs attention is the canopy shaping stage — the hinges have to be adjusted by hand for tension, so it pays to compare against the reference render as you go. There are no stickers and no rare parts, which keeps replacement-part stress low.

## 4. Value for Money

Measured by price per piece, this MOC lands in the upper-middle range. You get 290 pieces in exchange for a self-contained display piece, and the instructions on the brickplan site are included free, so the entry cost stays low. The deduction is mostly for the stand, which is a little thin — if you want a dynamic pose you will need to balance the centre of gravity yourself. That is a small flaw in an otherwise strong package.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the paraglider MOC worth buying?**

A: If you like flight subjects, or you want a photogenic piece for your desk, it is well worth it. About 290 pieces, 45 minutes of building, a high level of finish and strong display value — for a small scene MOC of this type, the value is above average.

**Q: How is the curved canopy actually made?**

A: The core technique is stacking plates in two offset layers, then adjusting hinge pieces section by section to set the angles. The brickplan instructions label the deflection angle for every hinge segment, so if you follow them you can reproduce the same natural canopy curve.

**Q: Is it suitable for beginners?**

A: Yes. The steps are split into four bags and the instructions are clear. The only tricky part is shaping the canopy, where you need to fine-tune the hinge tension by hand. With a bit of patience, a beginner can finish it in under 50 minutes.

**Q: Do I need to buy extra parts?**

A: No. All roughly 290 pieces come in the kit, with no stickers and no rare parts. Spare hose pieces for the lines are included too, so a normal build will not leave you short — you can open the box and start straight away.

**Q: Can it fly once it is built?**

A: No, it is a static display model. That said, the transparent stand lets you set up several flight poses, and combined with the hanging design of the harnessed minifigure, the visual effect already reads as flight well enough.
