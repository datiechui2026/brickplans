# Brick Animal MOC Guide: Pets, Forest Animals, Sea Life

Animals are the subject where brick building delivers the most for the least: a few dozen pieces can produce a bright-eyed little cat, and a few hundred can rebuild a lion with real presence. This guide to **brick animal plans** works through three families — pets, forest animals and marine life — and covers how to choose a plan, how to control proportion and how to handle the detail work.

## 1. Pets: Choosing a Cat or Dog Plan

Cats and dogs are the most popular animal subjects, and the number of available plans is genuinely overwhelming. Start by checking whether the **overall silhouette reads correctly**. A cat needs an arched back, a long tail and upright ears; a dog lives or dies on its head-to-body ratio and its pose (sitting, lying down, mid-run).

For beginners, go for a **150–300 piece chibi (Q-version) cat or dog**. Chibi proportions are forgiving, the parts are mostly basic bricks, and you can finish in under two hours — ideal practice. If you want a realistic look, step up to a 500+ piece advanced plan. Those designs use far more slope bricks and SNOT (studs-not-on-top) construction to build muscle lines and layered fur.

## 2. Forest Animals: Structure and Pose Come First

Forest animals — lions, tigers, deer, bears — have real mass, and the **internal frame decides whether the model succeeds**. When picking a plan, look at three things:

- **Legs and body:** are they built as one integrated unit? Separate, bolt-on limbs tend to fall apart.
- **Interaction:** can the head turn left and right? Can the jaw open and close?
- **Colour accuracy:** a tiger's stripes and a deer's spots depend on slope bricks and specialised parts, so check the palette matches the real animal.

During the build, **assemble the body frame first, then wrap the shell around it**. Connect the limbs with hinges or ball joints — that keeps posing flexible and makes later modifications much easier.

## 3. Marine Life: Curves and Transparent Parts

The hard part with marine animals (whales, dolphins, sharks, jellyfish) is the **streamlined body**. Whales and dolphins use curved plates plus wedge bricks to stack up a rounded form, with hinge pieces at the tail to suggest the swing of a swimming motion. Sharks lean on the dorsal fin, tail fin and sharp teeth to project aggression.

Transparent parts are the soul of the marine theme. A transparent light blue plate as the base simulates seawater, white wave pieces dot the surface as foam, and a light colour gradient sells the effect of an animal **suspended in the water**. When choosing a plan, check the ratio of **transparent and curved parts** in the parts list — these pieces are relatively expensive, so weigh the budget in advance.

## 4. Proportion and Colour: Making the Animal Feel Alive

Whether it is a house cat or a big cat, **proportion is the lifeline**. Use the rough head-to-body ratios of the real animal as a reference (a cat is about 1:2.5, a dog about 1:2 to 1:3), though chibi designs can exaggerate freely.

Colour is the other half. Animal fur is complex, and a single flat colour looks stiff. The technique: use the main colour for the body, then drop one shade lighter for the belly, the inside of the ears and the tip of the tail, and use small dark pieces to set the eyes. Make good use of **1×1 printed eye elements** — they give an animal its expression instantly. Expression details are numerous and small, and the personality of the finished model usually lives or dies on exactly those small spots.

## 5. A Beginner Starter List and Pitfalls to Avoid

New builders can start with any of these: chibi cats and dogs (100–300 pieces), red panda and koala (300–500 pieces), dolphin (200–400 pieces). All are simple in structure and use common parts.

Pitfalls to watch for:

1. **Avoid plans that lean heavily on retired or rare parts** — costs will spiral out of control.
2. **Check the stated finished dimensions.** Some plans build far smaller than you expect.
3. **Look at real photos or other builders' shots first** to confirm the accuracy before you download.

Once you have the plan, sort and bag the parts by colour against the parts list — the build itself will run much more smoothly.
