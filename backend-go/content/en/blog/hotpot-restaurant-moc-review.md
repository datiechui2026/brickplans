# Brick Hotpot Restaurant MOC Review: Sichuan Street Corner

If a sushi shop is the street's late-night canteen, a hotpot restaurant is the loudest, warmest corner on the block. For this review we broke down a Sichuan-style corner hotpot restaurant MOC, working from the red oil broth up to the nine-grid divider, and scored each area so you can decide whether it earns a plot in your street layout.

## 1. Unboxing and parts: the red palette lands first

As with the rest of the range, this build uses 1:48 street-scene scale: roughly 2,000 pieces split across 7 bags. The palette is Chinese red, dark brown, warm grey and gold — a red-walled, gold-trimmed Sichuan shopfront that stands out hard against a grey-toned street. Bag sorting is sensible, and the printed parts (the main sign board and a menu stand) are cleanly printed. The only gripe is light scratching on some of the gold elements; they all end up on the interior, so it never shows. Unboxing: **8.5/10**.

## 2. The facade: instantly readable as a Sichuan hotpot shop

The shopfront sits on a 16×20 stud footprint, two storeys with an overhanging eave. The frontage is unmistakable:

- **Signage**: a large gold printed sign above the lintel, flanked by red lantern elements — the festive Chinese look arrives immediately.
- **Eaves and tile ends**: dark grey slope bricks laid course over course, with the corners turned up slightly by propping them on 1×1 slope pieces, which produces the flying-eave curve.
- **Entrance**: big red double doors with gold rings; swing them open and you find a reception counter and small waiting stools, which sells the everyday life of the place.

Structurally the walls are braced with backing plates and the second-floor mezzanine carries weight reliably, but the red brick banding around the doorway is densely spaced and needs patience to keep aligned. Facade: **8.5/10**.

## 3. Core details: the boiling pot is the centrepiece

On a food-themed street build, everything hinges on the pot.

- **Nine-grid pot**: a 3×3 divider insert creates the nine compartments. Red and orange 1×1 round pieces inside simulate rolling red oil broth, with a few trans-red studs scattered on top as oil bubbles — the pot reads as boiling at a glance.
- **Ingredient platters**: tripe on grey ribbed plates, shrimp paste as pink-and-white round pieces, mushrooms as small brown-and-white elements, each plated in its own compartment on 2×4 plate trays.
- **Steam**: a translucent white band above the pot, built from 1×1 clear-white pieces stacked out of alignment, gives an instant rising-steam effect — the single most effective detail in the whole build.

Minifigures sit around the table, some with chopsticks raised, some cooking meat, so the scene tells a complete story. The weak point is that the steam band is only one layer deep; if you want proper haze you will have to build it up yourself. Details: **9/10**.

## 4. Lighting and overall atmosphere

Switch on warm orange lighting and the red walls, gold plaque and the glow rising off the broth make the shop feel like it is genuinely simmering — it is especially effective in a night street layout. Add a red lantern light over the door and a warm light plate behind a second-floor window and the mood steps up again. Compatibility with lighting kits is good. **8.5/10**.

**Verdict: strongly recommended.** Difficulty is moderate — around 5 to 6 hours — and the festive palette plus the constant sense of steam and noise make it the rare street model that reads as *busy*. It suits:

- builders who want a marquee Sichuan restaurant in a Chinese street scene;
- anyone who likes lively scenes and shooting night-time layouts with lights on;
- builders buying their first food-themed street model (it is easier to get into than the sushi shop).

The downsides are minor: quality control on the gold pieces is a little loose, the steam detail is thin, and the red brick seams around the doorway are hard on the eyes while you align them. On atmosphere and how completely it sells its theme, though, it sits in the top tier of food-themed street builds.
