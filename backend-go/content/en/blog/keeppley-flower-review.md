# Keeppley Bouquet Series Review: Brick Flowers on a Budget

## Introduction

Recent years have seen Chinese brick brands spring up one after another, and Keeppley has managed to stand out from the crowd thanks to its licensed IP tie-ins and original designs. Today we're reviewing the Keeppley Bouquet series, a lineup built around realistic-looking brick flower arrangements that plenty of builders have already crowned the best-looking brick bouquets to come out of China.

We previously reviewed the [Keeppley succulent series](/en/blog/keeppley-succulent-review), which wins on being small and delicate. The bouquet series takes a different route, aiming for a "you'd never know it's plastic" effect. Each has its own strengths.

## Unboxing and Part Quality

The Keeppley Bouquet series ships in a rigid full-color box, with the finished model on the front and combination shots showing how the set pairs with other bouquets on the back. Inside, parts come bagged separately, and each bag corresponds to one flower type's building steps. That sorting is genuinely thoughtful and saves a lot of digging around for pieces.

In the hand, Keeppley's ABS parts are solid overall. Clutch power is moderate: nothing falls apart, nothing needs forcing, and the building feel is roughly on par with LEGO. Worth noting is how heavily the bouquet series leans on **specialty molded parts** such as petals, flower centers and leaves. Mold precision on these is high, with smooth edges, no flash, and reasonably clean sprue marks.

## The Build Experience

The series covers more than ten different flower types, including roses, tulips, sunflowers, hydrangeas and daisies. Every flower has its own building logic, so this is never just a recolored repeat of the same model.

Take the sunflower. Its central disc uses a layered, nested structure: yellow petals on the outside (each installed individually), brown round parts in the middle standing in for the seed head, and a calyx and stem underneath. The whole flower takes about 80 parts and roughly 15 minutes to assemble, with a satisfying rhythm to it.

The tulip takes a different approach: three curved parts wrap around to form the petals with an open interior, which saves parts while keeping the flower looking light. That "less is more" thinking is exactly what sits at the heart of good MOC work.

If the design principles behind brick bouquets interest you, we strongly recommend our earlier [brick bouquet MOC design guide](/en/blog/moc-flower-design-guide), which walks through petal angles, color schemes and stem-and-leaf structures in detail.

## The Finished Model and Display Shots

Once everything is built, you slot the various stems into the included vase base, which is also brick-built and designed to read as simple ceramic. The overall visual effect is striking.

From a distance, the silhouettes and color combinations come remarkably close to a real bouquet: yellow sunflowers anchor the composition, pink tulips and red roses create a layered transition, and white daisies and blue hydrangeas fill in around them. Up close, the seams and stud texture of the bricks become a kind of pixel-art aesthetic of their own, a quality that even the LEGO Botanical Collection can't escape.

## Compared With LEGO Bouquets

For reference, the LEGO 10280 bouquet set (Botanical Collection) is priced at around 500 CNY, while the Keeppley Bouquet series runs just 100-150 CNY, roughly one third to one quarter of the price. In part count and flower variety, Keeppley is arguably the richer offering.

LEGO still holds an edge in mold precision on petal parts and in color consistency, but at less than a third of the price, that gap is easy to accept. For builders on a budget who still want something to dress up a room, the Keeppley bouquets are outstanding value.

## Play Value and Expansion Ideas

The biggest draw of the bouquet series is **customizability**. You can freely mix flower types and quantities, or buy multiple sets and combine them into a larger arrangement. We suggest browsing the BrickPlans community ([brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn)) for bouquet MOCs shared by other builders. Many creators have designed distinctive vase shapes, wreath decorations and even preserved-flower boxes, all of which extend the play value of the series considerably.

brickplan.cn also hosts plenty of tutorials on building brick scenes, such as pairing bouquets with modular street models or using them as decorative elements in minifigure dioramas. The results can be surprisingly good.

## Verdict

### Pros

- Excellent value: a complete bouquet for around 100 CNY
- Strong part quality, with good clutch power and mold precision
- Wide variety of flowers, each with its own distinct design
- Display-ready finished model, great for home decor or gifting

### Cons

- Slight color variation on some petal parts
- Instruction diagrams are unclear in a few steps
- Fixed stem lengths, so adjusting height requires DIY work

**Overall score: 8.5/10**

All told, the Keeppley Bouquet series is a sincere effort from a Chinese brick maker. It delivers a building experience and finished look close to LEGO bouquets at a friendly price, and it's worth picking up whether as a new experiment for a brick fan or as a creative gift for a friend.
