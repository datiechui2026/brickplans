# MOC Curved Structures: Hinges, Wedges & SNOT Techniques

Build MOCs for a while and a pattern shows up: straight walls and right angles almost assemble themselves, but the moment you want a round tower, a curved roof or a streamlined car body, the bricks seem to fight back. In practice, curved structures follow a few reliable rules. Once you know which parts do the work and in what order to use them, a smooth surface is not much harder than a flat one. This guide covers the core techniques.

## Why Curved Structures Are Hard: The Core Problem

Bricks are a right-angle world by design. Every brick and plate is square, so a curve can only come from one of two ideas: **replace right-angle connections with angled parts**, or **stack many small pieces to build up a stepped, gradually changing outline**. Understand those two routes and you stop freezing in front of a circle.

## Hinges: Angles on Demand

The hinge is the entry-level tool for curved work. It introduces a fixed angle between standard bricks, and the common versions are 1x1 hinge bricks, 1x2 hinge plates and hinge pieces with a 45-degree detent.

- **1x1 hinge brick**: good for polygonal approximations of a circle, such as a hexagonal tower
- **Detent hinges**: hold fixed angles of 0, 22.5 or 45 degrees, which makes rings and curved walls much easier
- **Tip**: build the curve from several hinge segments; the smaller the angle per segment and the more segments you use, the smoother the result

For example, a round tower can be made from eight 45-degree hinge sections closed into a ring, then levelled on the outside with thin plates. That is a classic first exercise for curved building.

## 1x1 Wedge Bricks and Slopes: Refining the Curve

Hinges alone get you a polygon. To move from polygon to genuinely round, bring in wedge bricks and slopes.

| Part | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| 1x1 wedge brick | Fills the stepped gaps on the outside of the curve so the outline reads smoother |
| 2x2 round corner brick | Creates a smooth right-angle transition quickly |
| Curved plate | A plate with a built-in arc that can be laid straight onto a curved frame |
| Slope | Pairs with curved layers to create gentle gradients and a streamlined feel |

**Tip**: build the curved skeleton with hinges first, fill and level the gaps with wedge bricks, then cover the surface with curved plates. Three steps and you have a rounded surface that feels right in the hand.

## SNOT and Sideways Building: The Secret Weapon for Arcs

SNOT (Studs Not On Top) earns its place in curved structures too. Turning bricks sideways lets you use side studs to build a curve in the **horizontal plane**, rather than simply stacking vertically.

Take a streamlined car nose: lay a turntable at the base as a rotation reference, then extend outward through side studs layer by layer, narrowing gradually into an arc profile. The same approach works for rounded hulls, arches and statue bases.

## Tire Rings: A Ready-Made Circle

Many builders overlook a treasure here: **tires**. Different tire and wheel combinations are naturally perfect rings. Whether you need a mechanical bearing ring, a band around a planet or an energy ring for a sci-fi scene, you can drop one in directly. Combined with a round base, you can assemble a tidy circular structure in minutes.

## Round Base Techniques: Where Every Curve Starts

To get a clean circular structure, start by getting the base right. Common approaches:

1. **Centre-axis method**: use a turntable as the centre point and lay bricks in rings around it so the build stays symmetrical
2. **Pythagorean positioning**: use corner bricks and a symmetrical layout to build an accurate regular polygon base
3. **Fill-and-sand method**: lay a large block first, then trim the edges into a curve with small pieces

If the base is true, the curved structure above it will stand up and rotate properly.

## A Practical Progression: From Small Circles to Large Curves

- **Step 1**: build an eight-sided tower from hinges to understand how multiple segments approximate a circle
- **Step 2**: add wedge bricks and curved plates to smooth the tower into a rounded column
- **Step 3**: try SNOT sideways building for horizontal arcs on a car nose or a hull
- **Step 4**: combine tire rings with round bases to complete a sci-fi ring structure

Curved building is a skill that improves with feel. Take a model apart and rebuild it a few times and you will develop your own rules of thumb.
