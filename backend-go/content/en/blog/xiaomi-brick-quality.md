# Xiaomi Brick Quality Review: Is It Worth Buying?

Since launching, Xiaomi Brick has carved out a solid place in the building market on the back of the Xiaomi ecosystem name and friendly pricing. But the question plenty of builders keep asking is simple: **how good is Xiaomi brick, really?** Below is a full assessment across part precision, clutch power, design quality and value for money.

## 1. Part Precision and Molding Standards

Xiaomi Brick sets are produced by companies inside the Xiaomi ecosystem, and part precision sits in the upper-middle tier of Chinese-made bricks.

**Strengths:** Surfaces are smooth, gate marks are cleanly trimmed and flash is rare. The contract manufacturer, Gobricks Precision, keeps tolerances very close to LEGO's — most visible on basic bricks and plates, where performance is especially strong.

**Weaknesses:** Some specialised parts (gears, hinges, ball joints) are occasionally inconsistent in how tight they sit, and a few transparent parts are slightly less clear than LEGO's.

Overall, part precision earns **8.5/10** — first tier among Chinese bricks at a comparable price.

## 2. Clutch Power and Building Feel

Clutch power is the heart of the building experience. Too loose and the model falls apart; too tight and your fingers pay for it.

Xiaomi Brick sits on the tight side overall. Fresh out of the box, some bricks take noticeably more pressure to seat, but a few cycles of assembly and disassembly smooths them out.

**How it compares:** LEGO is moderately tight and glides together. Xiaomi runs roughly 10%–15% tighter, on par with major Chinese manufacturers such as SEMBO and Qiji, and better than most smaller brands. The click on seating is crisp and parts land with a clear, definite feel. **Clutch power score: 8/10.**

## 3. Design Quality and Creativity

Xiaomi Brick leans toward a techy, mechanical, practical style.

**Notable series:**
- **Engineering machinery:** excavators, bulldozers and similar machines that make heavy use of gear transmission, with some sets supporting motor upgrades — high play value.
- **Mitu Robot:** a programmable brick robot that pairs with Scratch and Python coding education, and the most distinctive product line Xiaomi offers.
- **Licensed and themed sets:** subjects such as the Hall of Supreme Harmony and China Aerospace, balancing accurate exterior detail with structural stability.

A real design highlight is the instructions: clear, sensibly broken down into steps, and very rarely skipping ahead or misleading you.

**Weaknesses:** The creative modularity is not as deep as LEGO's, and parts-library compatibility for MOC builders still has room to improve. Some sets end up as static display pieces once finished, so the play value after the build is not fully realised.

## 4. Price and Value for Money

Price is Xiaomi Brick's biggest advantage. At a comparable part count and complexity, sets typically cost one third to one fifth of LEGO — sometimes less. A 1000+ piece technic set priced at RMB 800–1200 from LEGO comes in at just RMB 150–300 from Xiaomi at the same spec. For builders on a budget who still want a quality experience, the value is close to the ceiling for Chinese-made bricks.

Why so cheap: no steep brand premium, no IP licensing fees, and heavy use of standard parts instead of newly tooled specialised pieces.

## 5. Verdict: Is Xiaomi Brick Worth Buying?

Back to the opening question: **how good is Xiaomi brick?** The answer: **among Chinese brick brands, Xiaomi Brick sits in the first tier and is worth recommending.**

| Category | Score | Notes |
|------|------|------|
| Part precision | 8.5/10 | Gobricks-made, excellent tolerance control |
| Clutch power | 8/10 | On the tight side but consistent |
| Design quality | 8/10 | Strong tech flavour, excellent instructions |
| Value for money | 9.5/10 | One third to one fifth of LEGO, best in class locally |

**Who it suits:** builders on a limited budget who want a high-quality brick experience; fans of mechanical structures and tech subjects; parents buying a coding-education brick set for their kids; anyone curious about trying Chinese-made bricks.

**Who it does not suit:** die-hard LEGO fans chasing the absolute best building feel; MOC builders who frequently need specialised parts and rare colours; collectors who need licensed characters.

Overall, Xiaomi Brick has done a very good job on the "good enough quality, low enough price" formula. It is not the best brick out there, but it is consistently one of the best buys at its price point. Starting with an engineering machinery set or a Mitu Robot kit is a safe bet — it is unlikely to disappoint.
