# Compatible Brick Parts Guide: Types, Tolerances, Buying Tips

Anyone who builds MOCs runs into the loose-parts problem sooner or later, and compatible brick parts are usually the best value answer. "Universal" or compatible parts means bricks that clutch with mainstream systems such as LEGO and can be mixed into the same build. This guide covers how they are classified, how to judge quality before you spend, and how to use and store them so a build goes together smoothly.

## 1. Classifying universal parts

By function, compatible parts fall into four groups:

- **Structural parts** — bricks, plates and columns. These form the base layer and lock the overall shape.
- **Connectors** — pins, axles and hinges. These decide how strong the structure actually is.
- **Decorative parts** — printed bricks, windows and doors. These carry most of the finished model's look.
- **Special parts** — gears, tires and light units. These add play functions.

Once you have that map in your head, buying and restocking stops being guesswork: you know whether a gap in your stock is a structural shortage or just a cosmetic one.

## 2. Compatibility and real-world tolerances

The deciding factor between good and bad compatible parts is tolerance. In practice the clutch on Chinese loose parts varies widely between brands. Premium lines from the leading brands now sit close to LEGO: tight without feeling gritty, loose without wobbling. Cheap unbranded parts more often show oversized axle holes, scratched brick faces and uneven colour.

Before buying a large batch, look at buyer photos from people who actually measured the parts, or order a small sample first. The test standard is simple: a cross axle should slide in smoothly, two bricks pressed together should hold firmly, and a few repeated plug-and-pull cycles should not take the colour off the edges.

## 3. What to check before you buy

1. **Confirm the brand before you argue about price.** With loose parts, paying a little more beats buying junk. A cheap bag of bricks that will not clutch costs more in the end than the money you saved.
2. **Buy by weight for common parts, by piece for rare ones.** Basic bricks you use constantly are cheaper per unit by weight; rare and one-off parts are better bought by the piece so you do not end up with a pile of things you will never use.
3. **Watch for batch differences.** The same brand can show slight colour variation between production batches. If the model is going on display, buy all the parts of one colour in a single order.
4. **Buy special parts from the official source or a top-tier brand.** Gears and springs are exactly where poor tolerances hurt most — a gear that binds or a spring that is too weak ruins the building experience far more than a slightly loose brick.

## 4. Using and maintaining your parts stock

Sort compatible parts as soon as they arrive. Separate by colour first, then by function, so that when you are filling a parts order for a new build you can see what you have instead of digging through a mixed bin. After you disassemble a finished MOC, sort the parts back into the system straight away rather than leaving them in a heap. Common parts get topped up by the kilogram; rare parts get reordered individually as they run out.

Build your own standard for what counts as an acceptable compatible part — which brands you trust for structural work, which you allow for decoration, how much colour drift you will tolerate — and your loose-parts spending drops noticeably while the build quality stays on par with original parts.

## Final thoughts

Compatible parts are the value play in MOC building: the same model, the same techniques, a much lower parts bill. ## FAQ

**Q: What are compatible brick parts?**

A: They are bricks that clutch with mainstream systems such as LEGO and can be mixed into the same build. By function they split into structural parts (bricks, plates, columns), connectors (pins, axles, hinges), decorative parts (printed bricks, windows and doors) and special parts (gears, wheels).

**Q: How good are the tolerances on compatible parts?**

A: It varies a lot. Premium lines from the leading brands now come close to LEGO — tight without binding, loose without wobble. Cheap unbranded parts more often have oversized axle holes, scratched faces and uneven colour, so always check buyer photos before ordering.

**Q: Should I buy by weight or by piece?**

A: Match the method to the part. Common basic bricks are cheaper per unit by weight; rare and special parts are better bought by the piece so you avoid ending up with a mixed bag of things you do not need. For special parts, stick to brands with a solid reputation, and never cut corners on load-bearing pieces.

**Q: Is it a problem to mix compatible parts from different brands?**

A: Structurally it is generally fine, but watch for batch colour variation — even within one brand, different batches can drift slightly in colour, so buy all parts of one colour in a single order for display models. For key load-bearing parts, stay with one brand so the tolerances match.
