# Crane Truck MOC Review: 1,260 Pieces, a Boom That Works

The verdict up front: the big draw of this crane truck MOC is a telescoping boom driven by a real cable linkage. It comes in at 1,260 pieces and roughly five hours of building, reaches about 90% fidelity to the machine it copies, and stands out as unusually good value for money in the engineering-vehicle category. It is worth downloading.

### 1. Instruction Structure and Readability

The instructions run to 72 steps, and every single step lists the part number and the quantity required. That sounds like a small thing until you build it: you can pre-sort bags against the step list instead of hunting through piles mid-build. The three main modules the model is built around, the boom, the chassis and the cab, are printed in separate colors, so the pages remain readable even if you print them in black and white. The PDF is about 35 MB and ships alongside a parts-list spreadsheet, which lets you sort your purchase by color rather than working through one long alphabetical list. You can find it by searching "crane brick MOC instructions" on brickplan, and the file opens on both phone and desktop.

### 2. Parts List and Cost

The parts list totals 1,260 pieces. Around 96 of those are transmission parts: gears, pulleys and cable. That is a small slice of the total count but the functional core of the whole model, and it is where part quality matters most, since a slipping gear or a fraying cable shows up immediately in how the boom behaves. At an average of 0.12 yuan per domestic part, sourcing the pieces yourself comes to roughly 150 yuan; the instructions themselves are about 8.8 yuan on brickplan. That puts a complete build at around 160 yuan, less than half the price of an official crane set at the same scale. The spreadsheet matters here, because 1,260 pieces bought by color in one pass is far cheaper than ordering in dribs and drabs as you discover shortages.

### 3. Build Experience and Fidelity

Allow about five hours. The slowest stretch by a wide margin is threading the boom cable, which takes roughly 1.5 hours on its own; tweezers make a real difference there. Finished, the model stands 38 cm tall, and with the boom fully extended it reaches 45 cm. The boom slews a full 360 degrees, and it genuinely lifts small objects of around 200 g rather than just posing. Fidelity to the real machine is about 90%. The chassis also has working steering: push the model along and the front wheels follow the turn, which makes it far more engaging to handle than a static shelf piece. The combination of slewing, telescoping, lifting and steering is what separates this from a display model of the same size.

### 4. Value for Money

For under 170 yuan you get an engineering vehicle that both moves and works, and the design reserves a bay for motorization, so the upgrade path stays open. It suits builders who like construction machinery and who want playability rather than a static ornament.

## FAQ

**Q: How is this crane truck MOC different from an official crane set?**
A: Official sets mostly use a fixed boom shape. This MOC uses a gear-and-cable linkage, so the boom telescopes and slews and can lift real loads, which makes it more playable, and its parts cost about 40% less than an official set.

**Q: Can I motorize this crane?**
A: Yes. The design reserves a motor bay and is compatible with common L motors and M motors. With a motor fitted, the boom raises and lowers under power. You will need to buy the motor and battery box separately, which adds about 60 yuan to the total.
