# How to Make a Brick Photo Frame for 6-Inch Photos

The short version: a brick photo frame is a display piece and a memory keeper in one, and it takes about 100 bricks and 40 minutes to build. A 6-inch (4R) print sits perfectly on a 32x32 baseplate — leave a 16x24-stud window in the middle for the photo, run a four-stud-wide colored border around it, then add an L-shaped stand and it will stand up on a desk. Cost: 20-50 yuan.

The real charm is that you can change it whenever you like. Tired of the border color? Swap the bricks. New photo? Slide it out and put another one in. You can also build a name, a date or a little heart into the frame and turn an ordinary snapshot into a one-off piece of family decor. Build it with a child and you get a quiet lesson in measuring and proportion thrown in for free.

## Step 1: Work Out the Dimensions

A 6-inch print measures 10.2 x 15.2 cm, which works out to roughly 16 x 24 studs in brick terms. That is why a 32x32 baseplate is the recommendation: it leaves 8 studs of margin on every side for the border, with a 16x24-stud window in the middle. Going for 5-inch prints instead? Use a 24x24 baseplate. Want several photos side by side? Use a 48x48 baseplate to build a three-panel frame. Measure your photo before you pick the baseplate — it saves you from pulling the whole thing apart later.

## Step 2: Build the Border

Run two courses of 2x4 bricks and 1x4 bricks around the edge of the baseplate, two layers high, so the border stands proud of the surface. At each of the four corners, interlock the 2x4 bricks horizontally and vertically so the frame locks together, and leave the central window empty. Then add an inner lip of 1x2 bricks around the inside of the border — this is what pinches the edge of the photo so it stays put when you slide it in from the front.

## Step 3: Make the Stand

On the back of the baseplate, about 6 studs up from the bottom edge, build a fold-out L-shaped stand from 2x4 bricks and hinge pieces: the leg should be 10 studs long and open to roughly a 75-degree angle against the baseplate. No hinges in your bin? Two offset runs of brick work just as well as a support. Put a photo in and test it — make sure the frame stands firm on the desk without tipping backward.

## Step 4: Hanging and Decoration

If you want it on the wall, clip a brick with a hole into the top of the back of the baseplate and hang it on a nail-free hook or picture hook. You can also leave a 2-stud gap at the top and build a small handle instead. Then add the finishing touches: a mini sun in the top-left corner, the initials of whoever is in the photo in the bottom-right, or a gradient border — any of these instantly doubles how good it looks.

For a photo wall, three to five brick frames hung slightly out of alignment works best, and mixing border colors gives the arrangement a nice rhythm. When you are done, share your frame and your photo wall layout at https://brickplan.cn to give other builders ideas.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I make a brick photo frame, and what photo sizes fit?**
A: A 6-inch print (10.2 x 15.2 cm) goes on a 32x32 baseplate with a 16x24-stud window in the middle; a 5-inch print goes on a 24x24 baseplate. Build the border two layers high and slide the photo in from the front — the inner lip holds it in place.

**Q: How do I get a brick photo frame to stand up?**
A: Build a fold-out L-shaped stand about 6 studs up from the bottom of the back of the baseplate, with a 10-stud leg opening to around 75 degrees. If you do not have hinge pieces, two offset runs of brick will also stand stably.

**Q: How many bricks does a brick photo frame need, and what does it cost?**
A: About 100 basic bricks. With domestic compatible bricks it costs 20-30 yuan; with original LEGO bricks, around 50 yuan. The main parts are 2x4, 1x4 and 1x2 bricks plus one 32x32 baseplate, and it takes 40 minutes to finish.

**Q: Is a brick photo frame a good project to do with kids?**
A: Yes — ages 5 and up is a good starting point. Let the adult handle measuring and structure while the child builds the border and decorations, then arrange the photo wall together. It makes a great parent-and-child craft project.
