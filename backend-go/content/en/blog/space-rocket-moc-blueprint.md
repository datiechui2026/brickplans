# Space Rocket MOC Blueprint Guide: Long March 5 to Falcon Heavy

The short answer: start with a Long March 5 MOC at roughly 2,800 parts and a budget of about 500 RMB. If you want a real challenge, move up to the Falcon Heavy at close to 4,200 parts, with a finished model that can reach 65 cm tall. When you pick a blueprint, weigh parts availability, how clear the step-by-step instructions are, and the final display size — not just the price.

## Why Space Rocket MOCs Are Worth Building

The Long March 5 and Long March 7, along with the Falcon 9 and Falcon Heavy, are among the most popular space MOC subjects of recent years. Compared with typical architecture builds, rocket MOCs have clean lines and symmetrical structure, which makes them friendlier for newcomers. A finished rocket is instantly recognizable on a shelf, which makes it a strong value pick for a space-themed collection.

## Step 1: Pick Your Target Model and Budget

Decide which rocket you want to build first. The Long March 5 family has the deepest pool of available blueprints, and most of them are free to download. The parts are mostly domestic bricks, with a total cost of roughly 480 to 620 RMB. Falcon series designs put more emphasis on the detail of the payload fairing and side boosters, and they use a higher proportion of imported bricks, so prices usually start at 800 RMB and go up. If your budget is tight, begin with a blueprint built around domestic parts.

## Step 2: Check the Parts List

A good blueprint always ships with a parts list broken down by color and type. Take one Long March 5 MOC as an example: it uses 2,789 parts in total, of which white bricks make up 62 percent and the gray booster bricks come to about 340 pieces. When your blueprint arrives, go through your stock against the list first. If more than 5 percent of the parts are missing, order them before you start so you do not stall halfway through the build.

## Step 3: Build Along the Instructions

MOC blueprints are usually split into 15 to 20 step packs, with each step covering 30 to 60 parts. Build the core first stage and the four boosters first, then assemble the second stage and the payload fairing. If something ends up misaligned, do not force it apart — check the part numbers and back up one step, which saves you a lot of rework. The full build takes about 18 to 22 hours, so you can spread it across several evenings.

## Finished Size and Display Tips

The Long March 5 MOC stands about 45 cm tall with a diameter of about 8 cm, while the Falcon Heavy exceeds 65 cm. Measure your shelf height before you commit. A clear acrylic display case is a good pairing: it keeps dust off and highlights the detail on the fairing and boosters, and the display effect improves noticeably.

## FAQ

**Q: Where can I find space rocket MOC blueprints?**
A: You can find them in MOC blueprint communities at home and abroad and on the BrickPlan site. Most Long March 5 blueprints are free to download, while Falcon series blueprints cost around 20 to 40 RMB. Check the part count and size notes before you download.

**Q: Can a first-time builder go straight to the Falcon Heavy?**
A: It is not recommended. The Falcon Heavy runs close to 4,200 parts and the side booster connections are complex, so it is easy to make mistakes around the payload fairing. Finish a Long March 5 in the 2,800-part range first, then step up.

**Q: Is there a big difference between domestic and imported bricks?**
A: Domestic bricks clutch a little more loosely but offer better value, which suits large display models. Imported bricks hold tighter tolerances, which suits small structures. Mixing the two according to your budget works fine and does not hurt the overall accuracy of the model.

**Q: Can a finished rocket MOC be converted into a model that actually launches?**
A: No. MOC blueprints are designed for static display and leave no room inside for motors or launch mechanisms. Do not add power functions just for a conversion, as it can compromise structural stability and the look of the finished model.
