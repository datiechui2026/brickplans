# Detective Conan Brick MOC Review: Eyewear, Scenes, Instructions

Bottom line first: on a Conan MOC, the glasses and the fringe decide how recognizable the character is. Minifigures are the easy entry point, while the Mori Detective Agency and the street scene are the builds actually worth collecting.

Detective Conan has grown up alongside several generations of viewers, and "there is only one truth" is a line almost everyone knows. Recreating the grade-school detective in brick has long been a goal for anime builders, so this review looks at Conan-themed MOCs from three angles: character shaping, the classic locations, and the quality of the instructions.

## Character Accuracy: The Kid Detective's Signature Look

The challenge with a Detective Conan brick MOC is capturing the character's signature features in brick form: the blue suit, the red bow tie, the oversized glasses and that unmistakable fringe. In the builds we tested, the glasses were the dividing line between a good and a mediocre result — a design that combines transparent elements with a black frame looks far better than a simple printed piece. The fringe angle needs repeated adjustment; tilt it slightly wrong and the face goes stiff and lifeless. Conan's voice-changing bow tie is tiny, but it is the detail that makes the whole character, and a good instruction set will not leave it out.

## Classic Scenes: Crime Scenes and the Mori Detective Agency

Scene design is where Conan-themed MOCs get fun. Crime-scene builds usually come with a body outline, a magnifying glass, a briefcase and other prop elements, and the mystery atmosphere is turned all the way up. The Mori Detective Agency is the most popular subject of all: a two-storey structure with the café on the ground floor and the office above, paired with Conan, Ran and Kogoro Mouri minifigures, gives the build real narrative pull. The biggest surprise in this review was a Beika-cho street scene design, where street lamps, a phone booth and sign elements recreate the neighbourhood look from the anime.

## Instruction Quality and Build Difficulty

On build experience, Detective Conan brick MOCs sit at a medium difficulty overall. Minifigure builds are simple with a low part count, which makes them a good starting point for newcomers. Street scenes and detective agency builds ask for some interior-building experience, since there are plenty of door and window structures and furniture elements, and how clear the step diagrams are directly determines how smoothly the build goes. When choosing instructions, check the completeness of the parts list first and how easy the special elements — glasses, printed pieces — are to obtain, so you don't get halfway through and find something missing.

## Advice for Conan Fans

If this is your first Conan-themed MOC, start with a small scene like the Mori Detective Agency, which gives you both characters and setting in one build. Builders who care about display presence can take on the Beika-cho street scene instead. Either way, Detective Conan brick MOCs combine mystery nostalgia with the pleasure of building, and they are worth adding to an anime brick collection.

## FAQ

**Q: Which Conan brick MOC should a beginner build first?**

A: Start with a small scene like the Mori Detective Agency. The two-storey layout with the Conan, Ran and Kogoro minifigures has a low part count and can be finished in two or three days.

**Q: Which detail makes the biggest difference to Conan's likeness?**

A: The glasses are the watershed — transparent elements combined with a black frame clearly beat a simple printed piece. The fringe angle also needs repeated fine-tuning, since a slight tilt makes the face look stiff, and don't skip the voice-changing bow tie.

**Q: What should I check when picking Conan-themed MOC instructions?**

A: Prioritise the completeness of the parts list and how hard the special elements are to source. Confirm the glasses, printed pieces, magnifying glass and other rare elements up front so you don't discover gaps mid-build.

**Q: How do crime-scene and Beika-cho street MOCs differ?**

A: Crime-scene builds rely on props like the body outline, magnifying glass and briefcase to build a mystery atmosphere. The Beika-cho street scene uses street lamps, a phone booth and sign elements to recreate the neighbourhood, so it has stronger display appeal.
