# Brick Pizza Shop MOC Review: A Corner Street Scene That Cooks

Food shops are always the easiest buildings to make shine in a street scene, and a pizzeria doubles up on interaction: an oven to feed and a delivery run to chase. For this brick pizza shop MOC review we built a two-storey corner restaurant from the instructions, recording everything from opening the box to placing it on the shelf, so anyone hoping to drop a restaurant into their block gets a straight answer.

## 1. Instructions and Parts: First Impressions Out of the Box

The model is drawn at **1:48 street scene scale** and runs to roughly **2,100 pieces**, split across 7 numbered bags. The manual is crisp, highlighting the part added at each step, and the pacing from page to page feels comfortable.

The palette leans on **brick red, cream white, dark brown and olive green** — warm tones that read as "tasty" at a glance. The best touch is the printed elements: the "PIZZA" shop sign, the menu board and the takeaway box lid are all printed rather than stickered, so they hold up to play without curling at the edges. Missing parts were close to zero, and the bag sorting was clean and obvious. Instructions and parts: **9/10**.

## 2. Structure and Facade: The Oven Is the Soul of the Shop

The building sits on a **16×16 stud footprint** over two floors: dining area and back kitchen below, a residence with balcony above. The facade is dense with highlights:

- **Brick-built walls**: brick red and dark brown plates alternate in stacked courses to mimic mortar joints, with studded bricks slipped in between so the wall gains depth immediately.
- **Awning**: red and white striped slopes form a wavy canopy — the most photogenic roofline on the block.
- **Grid windows**: white frames plus transparent panels build the square-paned windows on the upper floor, letting light through cleanly.

Structurally, the walls are reinforced with inner plating, and the second floor carries its load solidly with no outward bulge. Only the balcony railing feels a little thin. Build: **8.5/10**.

## 3. Playability: An Oven That Actually Serves and a Delivery Loop

As a food-themed street build, the part that won me over is the **oven interaction**. In the back kitchen an arched brick oven uses a dome piece for the chamber, with orange transparent plates tucked at the top to suggest glowing coals; the chef minifigure holds a pizza peel and reaches in to load a pie. Even better is the delivery route: a small scooter parked at the door carries an insulated pizza box whose lid flips open and swallows a whole 4×4 pizza — the pizza itself is an off-white round plate with small dark round pieces as toppings, a small detail that lands well.

The compromises: there are only two dine-in seats, and the oven does not rotate. Still, the sense of an actual working kitchen outclasses most street scene shops. Playability: **9/10**.

## 4. Lighting and Authenticity

On accuracy, the red and white awning, the brick walls and the delivery scooter are the standard-issue signals of a pizzeria, and the building is instantly readable. Add a warm yellow light strip and the orange-red glow from the oven chamber paired with the lit sign pushes the late-night street atmosphere all the way up. Lighting: **8.5/10**.

## 5. Overall Score and Buying Advice

**Verdict: worth buying.** This brick pizza shop MOC sits at medium difficulty (roughly 5–6 hours), and a newcomer can finish it with a more experienced builder looking over their shoulder. It suits players who want a late-night food stop for their street layout, players who go for interactive details, and picky builders who prefer printed elements and hate stickers. The shortcomings are mostly the small number of dine-in seats and a fixed oven, but they do not outweigh the strengths — this is the most complete food-themed street build to come along recently.
