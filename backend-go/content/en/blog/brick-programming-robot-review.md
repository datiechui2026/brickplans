# Coding Robot Kits Compared: LEGO, Jimu, Makeblock

**Bottom line first:** A brick coding robot is only a good buy when it matches your budget and the child's age. For a complete beginner aged 6-8, start with an entry UBTECH Jimu kit (about RMB 600-900, pure drag-and-drop graphical programming). If the child is 8 or older and genuinely interested in coding, go straight to LEGO Spike Prime (about RMB 2,800-3,500, more sensors and a complete curriculum). With a budget around RMB 1,000, choose Makeblock mBot (graphical and Scratch modes, strong expandability). Prices run from a few hundred RMB to two or three thousand, and the gap is mostly in sensor count, motor count and official course ecosystem. Do not chase the most expensive kit blindly.

## 1. Price and Positioning: How the Three Tiers Break Down

Tier one is the sub-RMB 1,000 domestic entry group, represented by the UBTECH Jimu entry series. It costs about RMB 600-900, focuses on app-based graphical programming, has 1-2 motors and suits younger children who are just meeting coding.

Tier two is the around-RMB 1,000 general-purpose group, such as Makeblock mBot (about RMB 800-1,200) and LEGO Boost (about RMB 1,200-1,500). These support graphical programming and can move up to Scratch or Python, and they have plenty of expansion parts.

Tier three is the RMB 2,000-plus education group, such as LEGO Spike Prime at about RMB 2,800-3,500. It comes with 3-4 motors and multiple sensors, is aimed at schools and competitions, and has the most complete curriculum.

## 2. Age Fit: Choose by Cognitive Stage

At 3-5, a child can only handle button-style pre-programming or a parent-controlled remote. It is too early to expect more. At 6-8, pure drag-and-drop graphical programming fits well; the Jimu entry kit and Makeblock are both suitable, and the child can drag blocks together to make the robot move. At 9-12, a child can try Scratch and even Python text programming; the advanced modes on Spike Prime and mBot match this stage better. The practical test is simple: can the child understand that the order of commands equals the behavior result? If yes, it is time to move up.

## 3. Hardware: Sensor and Motor Count Matter Most

Hardware decides both playability and the ceiling. Entry kits usually have only 1-2 motors plus one ultrasonic or color sensor, so they can do simple line following and obstacle avoidance. Mid-range kits such as mBot have 2 motors and can add several sensors. The high-end Spike Prime provides 3 motors and color, distance and force sensors, four categories in total, and with a tilt sensor it can complete competition-style actions. Before buying, check whether accessories can be bought separately. Expandability is what determines the room to grow.

## 4. Course Ecosystem: What Keeps a Child Learning

At the same price, course content can vary a lot. LEGO offers an official education platform and thousands of lesson plans, which suits a long-term path. UBTECH Jimu courses lean toward early-childhood education settings. Makeblock has a free graphical programming community with many projects and full tutorials. For weekend parent-child play, a strong community is enough. For competition or systematic study, prioritize the LEGO or Makeblock ecosystem.

## 5. Buying Summary

By budget: under RMB 1,000, choose Jimu or a domestic entry kit; RMB 1,000-1,500, choose mBot (the best value); with a larger budget and long-term study in mind, choose Spike Prime. By age: 6-8 should stay with pure graphical programming, while 9 and up can move to a text-programming-capable kit. Whichever you choose, check real player feedback in the review and building-plan community at https://brickplan.cn before buying, so the kit does not end up gathering dust.

## FAQ

**Q: How should I choose a brick coding robot?**
A: Set the budget first, then match the age. For a 6-8 beginner, choose a RMB 600-900 graphical entry kit. For 9 and up, choose an around-RMB 1,000 kit that supports Scratch or Python. If you want a competition-level experience within RMB 3,000, choose LEGO Spike Prime. The core specs to check are motor and sensor count.

**Q: What age is right to start?**
A: Around age 6. At 6-8, pure drag-and-drop graphical programming builds logic concepts; at 9-12, move up to text programming. At 3-5, fine motor skills and abstract thinking are not yet ready, so start with static bricks or a parent-assisted remote-control kit.

**Q: Do you need programming experience?**
A: No. Mainstream brick coding robots include a graphical drag-and-drop interface: you drag command blocks together much like building with bricks, and you can get started in 5 minutes. Only advanced modes involve Scratch or Python, and those are optional.

**Q: Which brick coding robot offers the best value?**
A: The around-RMB 1,000 Makeblock mBot is the best value at about RMB 800-1,200. It has 2 motors, supports several add-on sensors, and offers both graphical and Scratch modes. On a tighter budget, the RMB 600-900 UBTECH Jimu entry kit is enough, and it still comes with a fair amount of course content.
