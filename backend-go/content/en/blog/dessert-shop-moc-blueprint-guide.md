# Brick Dessert Shop MOC: Windows, Display Case and Sign Guide

A dessert shop is the most sweet-toothed building on any street layout: pastel colours plus a clear window, and it becomes the photo spot of the whole block. This guide to building a brick dessert shop MOC concentrates on three elements — the display window, the dessert cabinet and the shop sign — so the finished model looks both precise and inventive.

## 1. Choosing a Plan and Setting the Palette

### Style and plan

Decide the style before anything else. A European macaron look suits pink and white; a Japanese wagashi look suits natural wood tones. Beginners should pick a plan in the 1,600–2,200 piece range, on a 16×16 stud footprint, with two floors (shop on the ground floor, living quarters above) — that is the most practical configuration to build and to display.

### Colour scheme

Run the main body in cream white plus sakura pink, keep door frames and window frames white, and add contrast accents in mint green or light purple. Inside the cabinet, use high-saturation small parts — strawberry red, matcha green, mango yellow — to stand in for the desserts themselves. Open up the window and the sweetness reads instantly.

## 2. The Display Window: Transparent Parts and Lighting

### Window frame

The window is the shop's face. Build four large windows from white 1×2 window frame elements, with transparent plates for the glass. Fit the frame first and seat the glass afterwards — that way you avoid scratches on the transparent surfaces. Inside the window, add a miniature dessert counter: 1×2 tiles for the countertop, then "cakes" (a white round plate topped with small coloured round pieces as frosting) and "macarons" (one row each of 1×1 round pieces in red, pink and blue). Seen through glass, it looks genuinely appetising.

### Lighting

A dessert shop MOC window is the best place in the building for lights. Run a warm-white light strip along the inside of the window's top edge so it shines down onto the counter — late at night the outlines of the desserts stay legible from the street. If you are adding lighting, remember to leave room for the battery box; it usually hides inside the second-floor ceiling.

## 3. The Dessert Display Case: Building in Layers

Place a dessert display case against one interior wall — it is the shop's core prop. Transparent panels form the four sides, and three shelves (transparent plates) divide the inside by product type: birthday cakes on the bottom shelf, cream puffs and donuts in the middle (ring pieces plus brown plates), cup puddings on top (transparent cup elements with cream-white tiles). Make the door openable by building a hinge from a 1×2 plate with hole plus a pin — it opens and closes, which adds a lot of play value.

## 4. Sign Design: Making the Signboard Come Alive

The shop sign is where creativity shows. Beyond the usual printed-tile signboard, try a three-dimensional sign: 1×1 round pieces spelling out "SWEET", standing above the awning. Alternatively, build an ice cream cone mascot from a pink cone element plus a white ball and fix it to the lintel. Add a chalkboard menu stand at the side (black plate plus white printed chalk lettering) reading "Today's Special" — it gives the model the lived-in feeling of a menu that changes daily.

## 5. Interior Layout and Minifigure Interaction

On the ground floor, set two small round tables by the window (1×2 round table elements plus legs) with two white chairs, so minifigures can sit down and eat. Put a pink cash register on the counter, with a dessert price list standing beside it. Upstairs, leave a small balcony and hang a white "towel" (1×4 tile) over the rail — the street scene immediately feels inhabited. If you want more dessert shop MOC plans to reference, browse brickplan.cn and search for "dessert shop" to see finished builds in different styles along with their parts lists.

## 6. Common Problems and Tips

Pink bricks vary noticeably in shade, so check the colour codes against the plan before you order parts, otherwise the joints will show obvious mismatches. Transparent parts are the easiest thing to scratch — wear gloves or work on a soft cloth while assembling. Dessert detail parts are numerous, so sort them by colour into small boxes before you start. When the build is finished, look at it from the front: if the window, the sign and the display case all read clearly as three highlights, your dessert shop is sweet enough.
