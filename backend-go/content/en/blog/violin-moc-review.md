# Brick Violin MOC Review: A 980-Piece Classical Display Piece

The short verdict first: this brick violin is one of the best-regarded instrument designs on brickplan.cn. It uses about 980 pieces and stands 32 cm tall, reproducing the curved silhouette of a classical violin with a very high degree of accuracy. On a shelf it looks genuinely striking, and it is aimed at builders aged 12 and up as well as anyone who plays or simply loves string music.

## 1. Structural Accuracy

The designer builds the body from dark brown smooth tiles, so the surface reads as varnished wood rather than as a field of studs. The side panels use hinge pieces to form the pinched waist that defines a violin's outline — that inward curve is where brick instruments usually fall apart, and here it holds cleanly. The F-holes are picked out precisely with small black elements, and the scroll at the head is made from multiple stacked layers that give it a real spiral instead of a blocky stand-in.

The four strings are flexible cord pulled taut, and the bridge, chinrest and end button are all present. We would put the overall fidelity at roughly 90%: stand it on a bookshelf beside real sheet music and most visitors will look twice before they realise it is brick.

## 2. Parts and Colour Scheme

About 980 pieces in total, dominated by dark brown and natural wood tones with a small amount of black and gold for accents — a restrained, grown-up palette that keeps looking good over time.

There are no stickers anywhere: every patterned element is a printed part, which matters for a model that is meant to sit in a study. The wood grain is suggested by alternating bricks in two shades of brown, so up close the body has visible depth rather than one flat slab of colour. Part tolerances are well controlled as well — elements grip firmly without needing force, which is exactly what you want on a model with this many thin, curved sections.

## 3. Building Experience

Working from the PDF instructions available on brickplan.cn, our editor finished the build in about four hours. The steps are split into five major stages of roughly 200 pieces each, and the sequence is logical — you always know what you are adding and why.

The curved sections of the body are the demanding part: expect to re-adjust angles a few times before the waist profile looks right, but patience is all it takes. The only real annoyance is stringing — the flexible cord tends to spring loose while you are seating it, so keep a pair of tweezers within reach.

## 4. Value and Who It Suits

Buying the individual parts costs roughly CNY 180–260, depending on where you source them. Sourcing parts from a list is clearly better value than a finished display set of the same size, and it also lets you swap colours if you want a different finish.

Who is it for? MOC builders who like music themes, intermediate builders after a project that stretches their technique without being punishing, and anyone who wants a conversation piece for a study, library or music room. It also works well as a gift for exactly those people.
