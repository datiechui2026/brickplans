# Harry Potter MOC Blueprint Review: Castle to Diagon Alley

Harry Potter is one of the most crowded corners of the MOC scene. From Hogwarts Castle to Diagon Alley, from wands to Quidditch pitches, official sets never ship with enough pieces, so more and more builders are turning to custom designs. And the quality of your **Harry Potter MOC blueprint** is what decides whether the finished model looks screen-accurate or like a cautionary tale. I have built my way through castle, Diagon Alley and wand instructions, and here is what I actually found, judged on structure, detail, minifigures and value.

## Hogwarts Castle: a structural stress test

Hogwarts is the centerpiece of any replica collection, and it is also the hardest to engineer. A good castle blueprint splits the build by tower — Gryffindor Tower, the Astronomy Tower and the main building are each assembled separately and then joined, which makes the model far easier to move and display. Watch the spires closely: cones and SNOT techniques give you spires with real depth, a clear step up in quality from simply stacking bricks. The stained-glass windows of the Great Hall and the columns inside it are the other key checkpoints for judging how faithfully a design reproduces the source material.

## Diagon Alley and the magic in the details

Compared with a vast castle, a scene like Diagon Alley lives or dies on small details. The Ollivanders wand shop and Weasley Wizard Wheezes signage usually depend on printed elements and stickers. In one Diagon Alley **Harry Potter MOC blueprint** I reviewed, the designer recreated the shop signs with tiles plus printed parts, and laid the cobblestone street with dark-colored plates — the result was genuinely impressive. The hard part with this type of blueprint is the connection between each building: whether the base clips hold firmly has a direct impact on overall stability.

## Wands and minifigures: small parts, real skill

Do not underestimate small items like wands. A high-fidelity wand needs special transparent elements for the core effect, then slopes to taper the shaft into its curve. Minifigures are the other draw — Harry, Hermione and Ron need their robes and house crests, and a good blueprint offers a printed-element substitution plan instead of just padding the lineup with plain bodies.

## Build quality and overall scores

After reviewing multiple **Harry Potter MOC blueprints**, three things matter most to me: are the step diagrams clear, is the parts list complete, and are special parts tagged with a source. I would give structural design 8.5, detail reproduction 9, and the building experience 8. Value depends on your situation — if you already have a healthy parts stock, building large scenes from domestic compatible parts is very cost-effective.
