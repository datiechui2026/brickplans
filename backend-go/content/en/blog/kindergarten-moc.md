# Brick Kindergarten MOC: Classroom & Playground Layout

The short version: a kindergarten is one of the most lively subjects you can pick for a street-scene MOC. Roughly 2,000 parts on a single 32x32 baseplate gets you a complete build with a classroom block, a playground and a garden, standing about 24 cm tall. The whole thing takes around 7 hours, and the bright colour scheme makes it a very good first street scene for a newer builder.

## Step 1: Plan the Layout on the Baseplate

A kindergarten splits into two parts: the main building and the playground. The main building takes 3/5 of the baseplate and the playground takes 2/5. Start on a 32x32 baseplate, with the main building about 20 cm deep and the playground sitting in front of it on the left side.

Outline the building footprint with plates first, then pave the playground floor. Use small red, yellow and blue bricks to imitate a soft play mat, so the kindergarten feel is there before you have built any walls.

## Step 2: Classrooms and the Main Building

The main building has two floors: an activity room on the ground floor and a rest room above it. On the ground floor, use large glass window elements for floor-to-ceiling windows, then furnish the inside with small tables and chairs, a brick table and an easel, and hang a welcome sign by the door. Upstairs, line up a row of small beds and a toy cabinet.

For the roof, use warm yellow bricks with a red trim line along the eaves. The finished main building is about 24 cm tall. The exterior walls can mix light pink and light blue, with a different colour for each classroom to keep it playful.

## Step 3: Playground Equipment

The playground is the part kids go for first. Build the slide from curved bricks with a yellow chute, make the swing from chain links plus a seat, use a lever element for the seesaw, and park a small tricycle nearby. Ring the equipment with a low fence and pave the ground in soft-mat colours.

The slide is about 8 cm tall and the swing frame about 10 cm tall, so the scale matches minifigures. Add a few figures and the scene comes alive immediately.

## Step 4: Garden and Finishing Details

Leave a small garden strip along the side of the main building: a green baseplate for the lawn, two trees and a small sandpit. You can stick children's drawings onto the perimeter wall and put a small flag by the entrance.

Do a final colour check across the whole build: pale tones for the main building, high-saturation colours for the playground and green for the garden, balanced against each other. Total build time is around 7 hours and the budget comes to roughly 170 yuan, making this the warmest building on the street.

## FAQ

**Q: How many parts does a brick kindergarten MOC need?**
A: About 2,000. That includes roughly 30 glass window elements, around 300 small red, yellow and blue bricks, and about 40 green plant pieces. Most of it is common parts, so sourcing the bricks is easy.

**Q: Is a kindergarten MOC hard to build?**
A: No. The structure is simple and the colours are bright, and 2,000 parts take about 7 hours. The classroom and playground zones are clearly separated, which makes it a great first street scene for a beginner.

**Q: How many minifigures should I use?**
A: 4-6 works well: 2-3 children and 1-2 teachers. Short-leg pieces give the child figures better proportions, and a brightly coloured top suits the teacher.

**Q: How is a kindergarten MOC different from a primary school MOC?**
A: A kindergarten is lower and livelier, with the emphasis on play equipment and vivid colours. A primary school is taller, has more classrooms, and includes a sports field and flagpole. The kindergarten uses fewer parts and more colour, so it is better for practice.

**Q: How do you secure the playground equipment?**
A: Fix the base of the slide and swing to the baseplate with plates plus pins, and slot the fence in with low wall bricks. That keeps everything from shifting when you photograph or move the model, which is sturdier than simply placing the parts down.
