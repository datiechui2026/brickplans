# Brick Lion MOC Blueprint: Mane, Expression & Build Steps

With a tiger MOC, stripes do most of the work. With a lion, everything rides on the mane. A good brick lion blueprint succeeds or fails right there: build it well and you have the king of the savanna, get it wrong and you have a large cat wearing a scarf. This guide takes you from choosing a blueprint to the finished model and covers the details that actually matter.

## Lion vs. Tiger: Where the MOC Differs

A lot of builders assume a lion and a tiger are the same model with a colour swap. The differences are bigger than that. A tiger reads as a tiger through its stripe pattern; a lion reads as a lion through the three-dimensional layering of the mane and the broad, heavy feel of the face. So when you are comparing brick lion blueprints, watch three things: how many different part types the mane uses, the angle of the wedge bricks across the face, and whether the chest has that V-shaped splay.

The more variety in the mane parts — wedge bricks, slopes and curved pieces mixed together — the fuller and more natural the finished mane looks. A blueprint that reaches for a single slope piece and repeats it 60 times will give you a flat collar rather than a mane.

## Three Main Mane-Building Methods

- **Layered outward fan:** start at the inner ring around the head and work outward ring by ring, using progressively longer wedge bricks laid in a radial pattern. This produces the inverted-fan silhouette closest to a real lion's mane, but it demands a blueprint with genuinely clear step-by-step sequencing.
- **Modular assembly:** build the mane as two separate left and right blocks, then attach them to the sides of the head. It is easy to take apart, works well for a mane you want to keep movable, and is the most economical option.
- **Gradient colour blocking:** run the mane from gold into dark brown, using different shades to build up visual layers. This gives the strongest visual punch, and it is also the method that tests your colour selection the hardest.

## Facial Expression: The Lion's Star Feature

The eyes and the muzzle are where a brick lion blueprint shows how well it was designed. For the eyes, stack 1x1 round plates to build a raised eye socket, then set a dark printed piece into it as the pupil. For the muzzle, angle two wedge bricks against each other to form a V-shaped nose bridge, leave one stud open below the jaw for a slightly parted mouth, and finish with two small white pointed teeth. The fierce-but-cute look arrives immediately.

## Body Proportions and Structure

A lion is front-heavy: strong at the chest, lighter at the rear, so the chest has to feel full. Blueprints worth building usually widen the chest with 2x6 basic bricks, then use sideways building (studs-not-on-top) to spread the forelegs apart and settle the lion into a seated pose.

The hind legs are usually built on hinge pieces so they work as movable joints, which lets you pose the model lying down, standing up or anywhere in between. A full build runs about 1,300 pieces, sits at intermediate-to-advanced difficulty, and takes an experienced builder roughly 3 to 4 hours.

## Part List and Budget Reference

The parts in a complete brick lion blueprint break down into four rough groups: about 40% basic structural elements, about 25% mane-shaping pieces, about 20% face and decorative parts, and about 15% special connectors. At current market prices, a full set of parts generally lands between 550 and 800 yuan. If you already hold a decent stock of gold and brown loose parts, you can pull that budget down noticeably.

Worth noting: plenty of long-time builders deliberately stockpile gold and brown curved slopes and wedge bricks precisely for mane-heavy models like this one. If you build animal MOCs regularly, you will often dig more than half the mane out of your own loose-parts bins, and the real cost ends up well below what the blueprint listing suggests.
