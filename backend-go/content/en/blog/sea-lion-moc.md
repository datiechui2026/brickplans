# Sea Lion MOC Review: Standing Pose, Whiskers, 90% Fidelity

The short version: this brick-built sea lion MOC lands at roughly 90% fidelity. It stands without a support, its whiskers are the standout detail, and it comes in at 1,680 pieces, about 4.5 hours of building and a reference price of 380 CNY. Among animal MOCs, it sits firmly in the upper tier — and it is worth picking up.

## Standing Pose: Center of Gravity Is Everything

A sea lion MOC lives or dies by whether it can stay upright. The designer solved this by widening the tail support and hiding weighted bricks inside it, so the model stands steady with no stand, no bracket and no clear acrylic crutch underneath. That single decision changes how the finished piece reads on a shelf: it looks like an animal holding a pose, not a sculpture propped up by hardware.

The front flippers connect to the body through ball joints, which means you can swing them into a clapping position, tilt the head back, or reset the whole thing to a neutral resting stance. Poseability like this puts it well ahead of mass-produced sets, where the silhouette is fixed at the factory and the only thing you can adjust is the angle you view it from. Handling it is genuinely part of the fun here — you can re-pose the model every few weeks and it reads as a different display piece.

## Whisker Detail: The Finishing Touch

Whiskers are where most animal MOCs give up. Here they are built from transparent flexible tube paired with short white rods, cut to staggered lengths and arranged in a total of 24 strands. Each one reads separately rather than blurring into a fuzzy mass, and the effect is remarkably close to the real thing.

Smaller structures get the same care. The nose tip and the ear outline are handled with SNOT (studs-not-on-top) technique, which lets the designer build shapes sideways and at angles instead of stacking straight up. The result is a side profile that matches a real sea lion almost exactly — the jawline, the slope from crown to muzzle, the way the ear sits slightly back. Ask anyone who has seen the model in person what they remember, and it will be the whiskers.

## Fidelity and Color Scheme

The color work is a gray-brown gradient that follows the shading of a real sea lion, with a cream-white belly that transitions naturally into the darker back rather than switching abruptly at a seam. The head-to-body ratio is about 1:3, which matches the proportions of an adult sea lion.

Set against comparable models, this one wins clearly on two counts: the texture of the "skin" — how the gradient and the brick surfaces combine at a distance — and how alive the pose feels. That combination is what gets it to the 90% fidelity figure.

On the building side, the instructions are clearly laid out and split across 4 numbered bags, so even a newcomer can finish in about 6 hours at a relaxed pace. The full instructions are available for download on brickplan.cn, along with a complete parts list, which makes sourcing replacements straightforward.
