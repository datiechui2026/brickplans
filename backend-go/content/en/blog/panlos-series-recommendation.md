# Panlos Bricks: Which Series and Sets Are Worth Buying

Chinese brick brands have come a long way in the past couple of years, and **PANLOS** has positioned itself in the mid-to-high end of that market. Rather than chasing the lowest price, it sells on mold precision, printed parts and large-scale display models. If you have outgrown entry-level brands and want domestic sets that are solidly built and hold up on a shelf, this Panlos guide will save you some wrong turns.

## Brand Positioning: A Mid-to-High-End Player

Panlos comes out of the Chenghai brick manufacturing cluster and is a brand that lets quality do the talking. Injection precision on its bricks is high and the clutch is tight, while its investment in printed elements is clearly a tier above entry-level competitors. Finished models sit flat and look good in a display case. Pricing generally runs 20-30% higher than value-focused brands like Sluban and Gudi, but remains far below LEGO — right in the sweet spot for builders who want quality without paying a brand premium.

## Product Lines: Street Scenes, Architecture, Military, Animals and Licensed IP

- **Street scenes and architecture**: the flagship line, covering European-style street buildings, large-scale landmarks such as the Empire State Building and the Eiffel Tower, plus a good number of traditional Chinese buildings. Detail is rich and each model is instantly recognizable.
- **Vehicles**: cars, trucks and construction machinery, with a realistic slant to the subject matter.
- **Military**: tanks, warships and helicopters, with hard-edged color schemes and structures.
- **Animals**: the dinosaur range is the breakout line — Tyrannosaurus, Spinosaurus, Triceratops and others as realistic large-scale models, made posable through multi-joint construction.
- **Licensed IP**: in the past two years the brand has picked up several anime and film licenses, with good reproduction on printed parts and minifigures.

## Recommended Sets: Set Numbers, Part Counts and Build Experience

**Street scene buildings (6130 series)**: roughly 1,500-1,800 pieces per set, and three sets connect into a single street. Standard bricks make up a high proportion of the parts and the build logic is clear, so even beginners can finish one without trouble. Facades use plenty of printed elements, and the level of detail ranks among the best domestic sets at the price. At CNY 200-300, this is the best entry point.

**Empire State Building (large-scale architecture)**: around 4,000 pieces, with the tower's setbacks and window grid reproduced to true proportions and an impressive finished height. The downside is a lot of repetitive steps and eye-straining part hunting, so it suits patient architecture fans. At roughly CNY 500 it costs an order of magnitude less than LEGO's take on the same subject.

**Traditional Chinese architecture series**: flying eaves, dougong brackets and columns are all faithfully reproduced, the color palette is restrained, and the models are instantly recognizable on a shelf — they also make good gifts for older relatives. Part counts range from just over 1,000 to more than 3,000, priced at CNY 250-450.

**Dinosaur series (Spinosaurus, Tyrannosaurus)**: ball joints form the skeleton, covered by scale plates, so you can bend the model into all sorts of hunting poses. Its articulation has almost no rival among Chinese animal sets. The Spinosaurus stands out for its dorsal sail, the Tyrannosaurus is the face of the whole line, part counts run into the thousands and prices sit at about CNY 300-400.

## How Panlos Compares with LEGO and Other Chinese Brands

- **Against LEGO**: feel and building smoothness still lag behind, but for the same subject the part count and finished size far exceed LEGO sets at a comparable price — and Panlos often costs a third of the price or less. It suits builders who want big, full and good value.
- **Against Sluban**: Sluban is more specialized in military subjects, while Panlos is broader across architecture and scenes. The two emphasize different things.
- **Against Gudi and SEMBO**: Panlos sits higher on price overall, and what you get in return is finer printed parts, sturdier animal skeletons and better shelf presence — the "pay more for quality" option.

## Value for Money and Who It Suits

Taken together, Panlos delivers value by offering large-scale finished models close to the LEGO experience at domestic-brand prices. It fits three kinds of builder best: those who love architecture and street scenes and care about how a model looks on the shelf; fans of dinosaurs and other animal subjects who want a posable model; and advanced builders on a budget who still insist on part quality and refuse to settle. If your only goal is the absolute lowest price, or the smoothest possible building feel, Panlos may not be the best pick.

## Closing: Pick the Series That Fits

Overall, Panlos is strongest in architecture, street scenes and animal subjects — solid construction and good-looking results that are worth coming back to.
