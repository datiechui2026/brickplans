# Mill Windmill MOC Review: Blades, Stability, Instructions

The short version: this mill windmill MOC instruction set is well above average. The moving blade assembly reproduces the real mechanism to roughly 85%, the base is genuinely engineered rather than just decorated, and the 1,287 parts take about 4–6 hours to build. It sits firmly in the mid-size MOC bracket and is worth picking up. Here is the breakdown across three areas.

## 1. The Rotating Blade Assembly: Fidelity Is the Highlight

The four rotating blades are what sell this model. The plan runs a technic cross axle straight through the tower body, and the blades connect through a gear train to a tail vane at the rear, so turning the rotor swings the whole head into the wind the way a real mill does. In testing the blades spun smoothly with no binding at all.

One thing to watch: leave 1–2 units of backlash where the gears mesh. Without that play, long spinning sessions will wear down the axle bushes. Compared with other windmill MOCs on the market, this plan's treatment of the classic Dutch blade-plus-tail-vane arrangement is noticeably more faithful.

## 2. Base Stability: Load-Bearing Design Done Right

The tower stands about 42 cm tall, which means a high centre of gravity — exactly the kind of structure that exposes weak base design. The plan answers it with three layers of interlocking bricks plus four diagonal support columns. Pushed across a flat desk in testing, the finished model showed no obvious wobble.

The one weak point: the mid-tower joint is held by just two cross axles. If there are children in the house who will handle it, plan on reinforcing that section yourself.

## 3. Parts List and Instructions: Details Decide the Experience

- **Sensible parts list**: of the 1,287 parts, roughly 12% are specialised — mainly curved bricks and gears — with everything else standard. Most builders will already own more than 70% of the inventory. The list is broken onto separate pages by colour and type, which makes gathering parts straightforward.
- **Clear building steps**: 96 steps in total, with no more than 8 new parts added per step, each highlighted with a colour block so even beginners are unlikely to misplace anything. A few top-down steps — the gear assembly at step 47, for example — are shown at a fairly small scale and need zooming in. That is the only real blemish.

## 4. Overall Verdict and Buying Advice

Taken together, this mill windmill MOC plan reaches a solid upper-middle standard on structural fidelity, base stability and instruction clarity, and the value for money is good. It suits intermediate builders who enjoy mechanical structures. For more mill MOC reviews and instruction resources, the [brickplan](https://brickplan.cn/blog) blog is worth a browse.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the mill windmill MOC hard to build — can a beginner manage it?**
A: The difficulty is moderate to hard. With 1,287 parts and 96 steps, a beginner will need around 6 hours. The gear linkage needs care; the rest follows the instructions without much risk of error. It is best attempted after a few small and mid-size sets.

**Q: Do the blades turn smoothly, and can you spin them by hand?**
A: Yes. The blades are linked to the gears through a cross axle and turn very smoothly by hand, and you can add a rubber band for a spring-back effect. Testing showed no binding, and with reasonable handling it will hold up to long-term play.

**Q: Is this plan worth buying, and how is it different from an ordinary windmill set?**
A: It is. Most brick windmills are static display pieces, while this one reproduces the blade rotation and tail-vane steering as working mechanisms, so there is far more to play with. At roughly 120–180 yuan, the value is strong.

**Q: Are there many specialised parts, and is it easy to source missing ones?**
A: Specialised parts are about 12% of the total — mainly curved bricks and gears — and they can be bought individually on the usual e-commerce platforms. The brickplan blog review also covers substitution options, so completing the inventory is not difficult.

**Q: How big is the finished windmill, and where should it go?**
A: The model is about 42 cm tall with a base roughly 20 cm on each side, so a bookshelf or the top of a display cabinet works well. The weight distribution is sensible and it stands stably without leaning on a wall — just be careful not to knock the blades when dusting.
