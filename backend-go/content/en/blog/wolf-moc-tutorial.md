# Brick Wolf MOC Tutorial: Skeleton to Tail Step by Step

Among animal MOCs, the wolf has a reputation for being hard but worth it: it combines the smooth, flowing body of a canid with fierce facial detail. Build a good **brick wolf MOC** and your animal scene instantly gains wild tension. This tutorial follows the order skeleton, head, torso, limbs, tail, and gives you a build plan you can actually follow.

## 1. Build the Skeleton First: The Base That Decides the Pose

The biggest risk in animal MOCs is a model that cannot stand up straight. Start the wolf's skeleton with **Technic parts**: use a 1x10 Technic axle as the spine and connect the limbs with ball-joint parts, then set the pose first, either a crouch ready to pounce or a head-raised howl. Once the skeleton is set, half the proportion work is already done.

**Body shape notes**: a wolf is front-heavy and light at the rear, so the chest should sit slightly higher than the hips, the back should slope gently downward, and the waist should be narrow. If your build looks like a dog or a German shepherd, the back is probably too flat and the waist not pinched enough. Step the chest-section bricks inward by one stud per layer to fix it.

## 2. SNOT for the Head: Character Lives in the Details

The character of a wolf head comes from a long muzzle, upright ears and a cold stare. Build it by stacking bricks sideways with the **SNOT (Studs Not On Top)** technique; the face reads best when viewed from the side.

1. **Muzzle**: pair two 1x4 white slope bricks top and bottom, then taper the tip with a 1x1 angled piece to get a sharp bridge of the nose.
2. **Nose**: a 1x1 dark gray round piece on top of the muzzle gives you that signature wolf nose.
3. **Ears**: stand two 1x1 45-degree slope bricks on either side of the top of the head, with the bases angled inward, and the upright ears appear immediately. To show alertness, tilt one ear slightly forward.
4. **Eyes**: a 1x1 transparent yellow stud makes the pupil, with dark bricks wrapped around it, and the stare turns cold at once. This is the highest-impact step in a **brick wolf MOC**, so it is worth spending extra time here.

## 3. Torso and Fur Texture: Sculpting Hair With Bricks

Wolf fur does not need special parts; it comes from **mixing bricks**. Stack dark gray and light gray 1x2 bricks in an alternating pattern along the spine to create the flow of fur. Use off-white plates on the chest and belly for the classic white-belly, gray-back coloration. To get a fluffy look on the sides, push one brick outward every few layers like bristling hair, and the model gains instant depth.

**Color list**: main color dark gray (about 60%), light gray (about 25%), off-white (belly, muzzle and nose area, about 15%). Do not mix in brown, because that makes a wolfdog, not a wolf.

## 4. Limbs and Tail: Stable and Posable

Use ball-joint parts for the shoulders and hips of the limbs, and Technic pins at the knees to keep a natural bend. Build the paws from dark gray 1x2 claw-tip parts or 1x1 slope bricks, making the front paws slightly larger than the rear ones to match the wolf's front-loaded weight bearing. **Stance suggestion**: spread the hind legs slightly and set the front legs one ahead of the other, with the center of gravity over the lower back; that is the most stable arrangement.

The tail is the wolf's most expressive part. String 3 to 4 sections of 1x2 hinge bricks into a swingable tail and taper the tip in dark gray. Let it hang low and relaxed or lift it high and alert, and the same wolf can express different moods.

## 5. Pitfalls and Upgrades

- **Exposed skeleton**: when covering Technic parts with bricks, mind the order. Cover the belly first, then the back, and finish with the flanks so the seams line up.
- **Unstable center of gravity**: do a few dry test poses before finishing. If the model tips forward, put a 2x4 counterweight brick inside the chest cavity.
- **Proportion drift**: the wolf head should be about 1/5 to 1/6 of the whole body. Err on the small side; an oversized head kills the fierce look.

Advanced builders can also give the wolf a snow or rock base, with white plates for snow and dark gray bricks for stone. The moment the wolf stands on it, the scene gains narrative.

## 6. Final Thoughts

Building a **brick wolf MOC** is an excellent exercise in sculpting with bricks. Get comfortable with the three techniques, skeleton, SNOT and hinges, and you have the basics of animal MOCs.
