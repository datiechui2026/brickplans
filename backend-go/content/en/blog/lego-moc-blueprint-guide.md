# LEGO MOC Instructions: Where to Find Them and How to Use Them

A reliable set of LEGO MOC instructions is your starting point, and also the thing that decides your parts budget, your building experience and how the finished model looks. This guide covers where to get instructions, the formats you will run into, how to work from them properly, and the traps to check before you spend anything.

## 1. Where to Find Good Instructions: Platform Comparison

Sources online vary wildly. By quality and usability they fall into roughly three tiers.

**BrickPlans (brickplan.cn)** is a community where brick builders share instruction files. It runs in Chinese with a friendly interface, uploads are screened and organised with a parts list and step-by-step pages attached, and the site supports search, favourites and discussion. For Chinese-speaking builders the communication cost is the lowest, and it is easier to find someone who has already solved your problem.

**Rebrickable** is one of the largest MOC instruction platforms in the world, with an enormous catalogue across almost every theme. It can import your parts inventory and generate a missing-parts list in one click, so you can estimate what filling the gaps will cost. The interface is English-only and some files require a proxy to access.

**Official LEGO sources and individual creators** — LEGO publishes plenty of free instructions, and many well-known MOC designers post their models on blogs and social media, with some paid files sold through storefronts. This route has the most personality, but you have to judge the quality yourself.

Beyond these there are aggregator sites and forum or community shares. One practical rule: look for high download counts, an active comment section and photos of finished builds. When all three line up, your odds of a bad experience drop sharply.

## 2. The Three Common Types of Instructions, and What to Check in Each

A set of MOC instructions is usually made of three parts, each with its own job:

- **Parts list**: every required part number, colour and quantity. This is the most important file, because it determines what you buy. Once you have it, check the total piece count and an estimated cost before committing to the build.
- **Step-by-step pages**: the build broken down in order. Files split by level or module are much smoother to follow; files organised by part type tend to mean constant page-flipping.
- **Renders and finished-model photos**: the end result, used to check colour scheme and details. Always scan the whole set before building, especially the sides and back — otherwise you end up with a model that looks flawless head-on and falls apart in profile.

A proper instruction set normally has all three. Be very wary of anything sold on the strength of a single render.

## 3. Sourcing Parts from the List

Once you have the list, group the parts by colour and size and tally them, then compare prices across platforms. The usual approach is bulk buying plus top-ups: order the main parts in volume to keep costs down, then buy rare pieces individually. If the instructions you found on [BrickPlans](https://brickplan.cn) include a parts list, you can work straight from it and buy the whole lot at once from the secondhand market or a parts seller — far less effort than digging through your own stock.

Two more sourcing notes: limited-run or discontinued colours can be swapped for a close match, but check the render before you commit to the substitution; and when a dimension is unclear, trust the part number rather than guessing from appearance.

## 4. Common Problems and a Pitfall Checklist

1. **Missing pages or skipped steps**: flick through the entire file after downloading. If a step jumps, ask the author or the community rather than forcing your way through.
2. **Part numbers that don't match the real part**: common in re-uploaded or re-edited files. Spot-check a few obscure parts before you build.
3. **Blurry, low-resolution pages**: high-resolution renders are far safer. Fuzzy files lead to errors, and are not worth buying at all.
4. **Outdated versions**: older files may rely on discontinued connector parts. Check whether the author has released a V2 or V3 update.

## 5. Paid vs Free: How to Choose

Free instructions are good for practice, but quality varies and the steps are often incomplete. Paid files are more rigorously structured with more professional explanations, which suits builders aiming at a high-completion model. Choose by how much the project matters to you: free files for practice pieces, paid files for collector-grade builds. Build up experience first, then move gradually into paid premium files — it is the cheapest route and the least likely to put you off the hobby.

Whichever channel you use, the point of MOC instructions is to make the build enjoyable. Save more files, compare more of them, build more — and check [BrickPlans](https://brickplan.cn) regularly for community models and shared instructions. You will soon develop your own criteria for picking files and start turning out builds you are proud of.
