# Gudi Space Series Review: Long March Rockets and Station Sets

**The short version:** Gudi's space line picks the right subject matter and keeps pricing steady — large launch rockets run RMB 200–400 and the space station sets sit around RMB 200, which makes them one of the better-value entries into space-themed building.

**Gudi's space series** is the main focus of this veteran Chinese brick maker in recent years. It runs from Long March launch vehicles to space stations and lunar rovers, and it has put space building within reach of a lot of builders at friendly prices. This **Gudi space series review** looks at how the line actually performs, based on real builds.

## 1. Brand Positioning and Series Lineup

Gudi is a long-established Chinese brick manufacturer that made its name with military sets, and it has poured a lot of effort into space subjects lately. The **space series** breaks down into three parts. **Launch vehicles** are the signature: Long March rockets reproduced to scale, with separable body sections and a complete set of launch pad accessories. **Space station sets** sell themselves on modular cabin sections — the core module and experiment module can be docked and combined for a variety of display and play setups. **Small sets such as lunar rovers and satellites** are compact and neatly detailed, good for beginners or for filling out a full space fleet. The color scheme leans on the standard white, blue and red of real space hardware, so the finished models stand out clearly on a shelf.

## 2. Rocket and Space Station Sets in Practice

Among the sets tested, the **large rocket and launch pad** delivered the biggest surprise on accuracy: the printed markings on the rocket body are crisp and legible, boosters and the escape tower are all represented, and the finished model is genuinely tall — it has real presence in a cabinet. The **space station set**'s modular design is the highlight. The cabin connection points are snug without being loose, and they hold up through repeated assembly and disassembly, while the minifigures let you stage an extravehicular activity scene. Smaller sets like the lunar rover don't have many parts, but the wheels and robotic arm are structurally complete, which makes them excellent value.

## 3. Part Quality and Build Experience

Gudi's parts sit at the **upper-middle level** for domestic production. The main structural elements clutch tightly, the share of printed parts is high, and the national flag and model designations on the rocket body are printed rather than stickered, which noticeably improves how the finished model holds up. Instructions are clearly stepped and the bags are sensibly divided; a large rocket takes roughly four to six hours to finish. A couple of things to note: some small parts are on the tight side, so your fingers will ache a little during assembly, and transparent canopy pieces occasionally show faint scratches — neither affects the overall result. For space subjects at this price band, the level of completion is genuinely competitive.

## 4. Value and Who It Suits

The verdict of this **Gudi space series review**: it is the representative of "right subject, steady price" among Chinese space brick sets. Large rocket sets generally land at RMB 200–400 and space station sets around RMB 200, and against first-tier brands that charge over a thousand for the same subject matter, the value advantage is obvious. It suits **space fans on a limited budget, parents who want to use building as a space-science lesson for their kids, and collectors of Chinese brick brands**. If you are chasing the absolute best part feel, a higher-end brand is the way to go — but for coverage of space subjects and overall value, the Gudi space series is hard to replace.
