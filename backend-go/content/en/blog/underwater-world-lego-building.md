# Underwater World LEGO Build: Reef to Deep-Sea Submarine

Want a patch of deep blue ocean sitting on your desk? An underwater world build is a fun project that sharpens your hands and lets your imagination run — whether you are picking up bricks for the first time or already have a shelf full of MOCs. With a handful of colored pieces you can put together a coral reef, a few sea creatures and a deep-sea submarine, and end up with a lively miniature ocean. This guide walks you through the whole thing from an empty baseplate to a finished scene.

## 1. Start With a Vivid Coral Reef

The reef is the heart of the scene, and it is the part of an underwater build where color does the most work. Start by laying down a blue baseplate to stand in for seawater, then stack bright red, orange, pink and purple pieces into coral clusters at different heights.

Slopes and curved elements are your best friends here — they mimic the branching structure of real coral. Stack several pieces in related colors, offsetting the edges slightly as you go, and you get a natural grown-in-place look. Don't forget a few strands of green or light blue seaweed: long, thin plates bent into sweeping shapes make the rock look like it is moving in the current. Symmetry is not the goal. The more irregular and organic the reef looks, the better it reads.

## 2. Bring the Sea Creatures to Life

Once the reef exists, it is time to bring in the animals. This is the most challenging part of the build and also the most rewarding.

- **Clownfish**: build a rounded body from orange and white pieces, add black stripes and a dorsal fin, then use transparent pieces for the tail fin. Cute and surprisingly convincing.
- **Sea turtle**: use green and brown bricks to suggest the hexagonal pattern of the shell, and pair it with broad flipper-like limbs posed mid-swim.
- **Octopus and starfish**: bendable hose pieces make great octopus tentacles, while five angled pieces give you a starfish with a proper radiating shape.

If your basic brick supply runs thin, start from an existing animal-themed set and modify it upward from there. It lowers the barrier to entry for an underwater world build considerably.

## 3. Build a Deep-Sea Submarine

With the shallow-water coral and fish schools done, push deeper — a submarine instantly gives the scene a story. Build the hull from cylindrical or curved pieces, and work in transparent porthole elements plus searchlight pieces.

Don't skip the two propeller thrusters, the mechanical arm and a few probe lights. A gray, dark blue and yellow color scheme keeps the sub looking technical while separating it visually from the blue water around it. Park it beside the reef as if it is on a deep-sea expedition, and the whole composition snaps into focus.

## 4. Scene Design and Overall Layout

Good layout work is what makes all those separate parts read as one scene. Decide on zones first: a shallow coral zone, a mid-water fish zone and a deep-sea submarine zone. Use baseplates or transparent pieces in different shades of blue to signal the change in depth.

For detail, add a treasure chest, shipwreck debris, bubble pieces and seabed plants to build up layers. When placing things, follow a front-low, back-high rule: put the taller rock formations and the submarine toward the rear, and keep the small starfish and sandy patches in front. That creates real depth. Finally, hang a few "floating" fish schools from clear stands or fishing line so they look like they are actually drifting in the current.

## 5. Tips and Next Steps

If this is your first underwater world build, start with a single small scene. Get comfortable with the coral and fish techniques, then scale up. When you are done, photograph it — and share it with the BrickPlan community to swap ideas with other builders.

More advanced builders can add a power module to the submarine, or build an ocean diorama display case with a lid that opens and closes. There is no single correct answer for an underwater world build; every odd idea you have is worth putting into bricks.

From one coral branch to a full submarine, from the shallows down to the dark, an underwater world build packs an entire ocean into a small box of bricks. Grab your pieces and start making your own stretch of ocean.
