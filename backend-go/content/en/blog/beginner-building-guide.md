# Beginner Brick Building Guide: Your First Model From Scratch

![Finished brick model built by a beginner](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/beginner-building-guide/beginner-building-guide-1.jpg)

Maybe you have never touched a brick, or you have only put together one or two sets. This **beginner brick building guide** is written for exactly that situation. Building does not require talent or prior experience — patience is enough. The six steps below walk you through preparation, sorting, reading instructions, building, finishing, and what to try next, all the way to your first finished model.

## Step 1: Preparation — What Do You Need?

![Beginner brick building step-by-step photo](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/beginner-building-guide/beginner-building-guide-2.jpg)

### 1.1 Choosing the Right Set

Your first move is not to start building, but to pick the right set. A few suggestions:

**Good starter sets:**

- **Small scenes**: flower shops, cafés, small stores — usually 300-600 parts and finishable in 2-3 hours
- **Vehicles**: small cars and motorcycles, compact in structure and great for learning symmetrical building
- **Animals**: small animal models with few parts and a quick sense of achievement

**What to avoid:**

- Do not start with a giant set of a thousand-plus parts — you are likely to abandon it halfway
- Do not chase the cheapest unbranded bricks — loose tolerances make the build feel bad
- Start with well-known brands such as LEGO, Qiji or Double Eagle

### 1.2 Tools to Prepare

You do not need much, but a few items make the process much smoother:

- **Brick separator**: included with almost every brand's sets, used to pry apart bricks you placed wrong
- **Sorting trays**: household storage boxes work fine for separating parts
- **A flat table**: building needs a level surface so parts do not roll away

### 1.3 Get to Know the Basic Parts

Before you build, spend a few minutes learning the parts you will use:

- **Brick**: standard-height block, the most basic element
- **Plate**: about one third of a brick's height, used for fine adjustments
- **Slope**: a brick with an angled face, used for roofs and ramps
- **Special pieces**: wheels, windows, doors, plants and so on, which add detail to a model

## Step 2: Sorting Parts — The Key Step Before You Build

Many beginners tear open the bag and start building immediately, which is the easiest way to make mistakes. The most important habit in this **beginner brick building guide**: **sort first, build second**.

### 2.1 Sorting Methods

- **By color**: keep parts of the same color together
- **By type**: bricks in one group, plates in another, special pieces in a third
- **By size**: large parts together, small parts together

### 2.2 Recommended Sorting Tools

- Small zip bags from the supermarket (5-10 of them)
- The compartment trays from a mooncake or chocolate box
- A dedicated brick storage box (search "brick sorting box" on Taobao)

The 10-15 minutes you spend sorting will save you several times that during the build.

## Step 3: Reading the Instructions — The Bridge From Parts to Model

### 3.1 How Instructions Are Structured

Brick instructions usually contain these parts:

1. **Parts list**: every part type you need and how many of each
2. **Step sequence**: the build broken down into individual steps
3. **Step diagrams**: a detailed drawing of each step, marking where the new parts go

### 3.2 Reading Tips

- **Build in order**: do not skip steps; the part count for each step was worked out during design
- **New parts are marked**: instructions usually highlight the parts added in the current step
- **Direction cues**: watch the arrows and dotted lines, which show how a part is oriented
- **Symmetry checks**: halfway through, check whether the left and right sides match

If you are working from MOC instructions downloaded from [brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn), the same method applies. MOC instructions usually pack more into each step than official sets, so read every step more carefully.

## Step 4: The Build — From First Part to Finished Model

![Close-up of bricks during a beginner build](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/beginner-building-guide/beginner-building-guide-3.jpg)

### 4.1 Building the Base

Take a small house as an example. Each stage has its own points to watch.

**Step 1: Lay the baseplate**
- Place the baseplate flat on the table
- Check that all four corners sit fully against the tabletop
- If the baseplate is warped, bend it gently in the opposite direction a few times

**Step 2: Build the walls**
- Start at a corner and work toward the middle
- Stagger the seams (offset interlocking): do not line up the joints of one layer with the layer below
- After each layer, press down lightly by hand so the bricks are fully seated

**Step 3: Build the roof**
- If you are using slopes, start from the lowest point
- Watch the orientation of the slopes — the angled face should point outward
- Add a few plates where the roof meets the walls to close any gaps

**Step 4: Add details**
- Windows, doors, a chimney, a garden
- This is the most fun stage, and you can improvise freely

### 4.2 Common Problems and Fixes

**The parts will not press together?**
- Check that the part is facing the right way
- Press straight down from directly above
- Rest the part on the table and use the table's resistance to push it home

**You built something wrong — how do you take it apart?**
- Use a brick separator: slot it into the gap under the brick and lever it up
- For small parts you can gently bite them loose (but use this rarely)
- Check removed parts for distortion and do not reuse any that are deformed

**You cannot follow the instructions?**
- Look at the diagram from a different angle — sometimes it is a viewpoint problem
- Compare the current step with the previous one and find what changed
- Ask in the [brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn) community; plenty of helpful builders will answer

## Step 5: Finishing — Photograph and Share

### 5.1 Showing Off Your Model

Once your first model is done, take a few good photos to remember it:

- **Natural light**: daylight by a window is the best light source
- **Plain background**: white paper or light-colored cloth makes the model stand out
- **Multiple angles**: one shot from the front, one from the side, one at 45 degrees
- **Detail close-ups**: photograph a few interesting details

### 5.2 Share With the Community

One of the great joys of brick building is sharing. You can post your photos to:

- The [brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn) community, to swap experience with other builders
- Brick hobby WeChat or QQ groups
- Social platforms such as Xiaohongshu and Douyin

## Step 6: What's Next — Leveling Up

After finishing your first model, you may want a bigger challenge. Here are some directions:

### 6.1 Try Larger Sets

Start with sets of 500-800 parts, then work up to large sets of 1000+ parts. Every set teaches you a new building technique.

### 6.2 Get Into MOC Building

MOC (My Own Creation) is the advanced side of the hobby. You can start by:

- Downloading other builders' MOC instructions from [brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn) and building them
- Modifying official sets to add your own creative touches
- Learning brick design software such as Stud.io and designing your own models

### 6.3 Build a Parts Library

As you gain experience you will start accumulating parts. Some advice:

- Disassemble sets you no longer love and move the parts into your library
- Store by type and color
- Watch the loose-parts market from Chinese brick brands, which offers good value

## Wrapping Up

This **beginner brick building guide** has taken you from preparation through to a finished model. The key to brick building is patience and care — do not rush, and enjoy each step. Remember that every master builder started with a first model.

Stop reading tutorials and open that first box. If you get stuck while building, or want instructions that suit your taste better, [brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn) has a large library of them and a community of builders who are happy to talk.
