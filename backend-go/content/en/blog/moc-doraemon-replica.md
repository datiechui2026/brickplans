# Doraemon MOC Instructions Review: Brick-Built Blue Cat

Doraemon has grown up alongside several generations of viewers, and rebuilding him out of bricks is a bucket-list project for a lot of MOC builders. The good news is that you do not have to design him from scratch. This review compares several **Doraemon replica instruction sets**, from small chibi figures to full scene builds, so you can pick the one that matches your part collection, your budget and your patience.

## Popular Doraemon MOC Instructions

### 1. Chibi Doraemon

- **Instruction name**: Doraemon Chibi Big-Head Figure
- **Piece count**: about 350
- **Difficulty**: Low
- **Rating**: ★★★★★

This **Doraemon replica instruction set** is the friendliest entry point for anyone new to MOC building. The rounded head is put together with curved bricks, and the blue body paired with the white belly gives you the classic color scheme right away. The details are all there too: the pocket, the bell and the little red tail. At roughly 350 pieces, it is a one-evening build, which also makes it a low-risk way to test whether you enjoy the character's rounded shaping before committing to a bigger model.

### 2. Scale Model Doraemon

- **Instruction name**: Doraemon 1:1 Scale Standing Model
- **Piece count**: about 1,200
- **Difficulty**: Medium
- **Rating**: ★★★★☆

The 1:1 scale Doraemon model stands about 50 cm tall, which makes it a genuine display piece rather than a desk ornament. The building difficulty is moderate, but be aware that it calls for a fair number of specialized parts, so check your inventory against the parts list before you start ordering. The payoff is a figure with real presence, and the taller proportions mean the face and body shaping read clearly from across a room.

### 3. Doraemon Scene Set

- **Instruction name**: Doraemon Anywhere Door Scene
- **Piece count**: about 800
- **Difficulty**: Medium
- **Rating**: ★★★★★

This is a scene set rather than a single figure: it includes Doraemon, Nobita and the Anywhere Door. Once it is finished you can recreate classic story moments on the shelf instead of just looking at one standing character. With around 800 pieces and medium difficulty, it sits comfortably between the chibi figure and the 1:1 model in terms of both time and cost.

## Key Building Points

### 1. Head Shape

The round head is Doraemon's single most recognizable feature, and it is also where most builders struggle. Stacking curved bricks and round plates is the reliable way to reproduce that circular silhouette; straight bricks alone will leave you with a boxy head that never quite looks right. Build the head first and check the outline before you move on to the body.

### 2. Facial Expression

The signature smile is best handled with printed parts or stickers. Printed elements hold up better over time and look sharper up close, while stickers give you more freedom if you want to tweak the expression. Whichever route you take, line the eyes and nose up carefully — a millimeter of drift is very visible on a face this simple.

### 3. Body Proportions

Pay attention to the ratio between head and body. The chibi look depends on keeping the head large relative to the torso, while still making sure the figure stands stably without a hidden support. If your model starts tipping forward, the fix is usually in the feet and the lower torso rather than the head.
