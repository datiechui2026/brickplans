# Great Wall MOC Tutorial: Interlocking Walls and Beacon Towers

The Great Wall is one of the most photogenic subjects in Chinese-style brick building, and one of the easiest to get wrong. A wall that runs dead straight has no presence, and the moment you try for a winding, mountain-hugging curve, the corners start falling apart. This tutorial splits the build into four stages — proportions, wall structure, beacon towers and terrain — so the finished run looks like a proper mountain pass and still survives being picked up and played with.

## 1. Set the Scale First: Size Decides Presence

Decide your scale before the first brick goes down. At minifigure scale (roughly 1:42), a wall top 2 studs wide works perfectly — a minifig can walk it. If you are building a micro diorama instead, a 1-stud-wide wall top at 3 to 4 bricks tall is plenty to read as the Great Wall.

Once the scale is chosen, sketch an S-shaped route on paper and mark where the beacon towers will sit. Then test-lay that route with plates in a contrasting colour as a rough draft, and check the curve flows before you commit real bricks. Because every part count downstream is calculated from the scale you picked, locking it in early saves a lot of rework later.

## 2. Wall Structure: Interlocking Is the Soul

The wall is the core of the model. Use a staggered bond: offset each brick course by half a brick against the course below, so the wall bites together as one solid mass instead of a stack that topples the first time it is pushed. Build the outer face in the old-grey family (beige-grey, dark grey) to suggest weathered wall brick, and use a lighter grey on the inner face where you can save parts.

Every 3 brick courses, insert a course of plates as a belt line — it stiffens the wall and gives the brick courses some relief at the same time. One warning worth repeating: never span a corner with a single large plate. Use small bricks that grip in both directions. That hard-pressed corner is the single most common reason a Great Wall MOC comes apart.

## 3. Crenellations and Brick Texture: Detail Sells It

Whether the wall looks right comes down to the crenellations. Along both edges of the wall top, stand a 1x1 brick every other stud as parapet, leaving the space between them for minifigures to walk. Every few studs, swap one for a 1x2 slope to read as an arrow slit.

For a weathered look, randomly replace a few bricks with dark brown and a few plates with slopes to fake damaged sections, then press 1x1 round plates into the wall face as rivets. Remember to build crenellations on both faces: the inner face of the Great Wall is lower and the outer face is taller, and that height difference is exactly what separates it from a generic castle wall.

## 4. Beacon Towers: The Visual Anchors

Beacon towers are the centrepiece. Put one at each end of a wall run, or on the high points. Build them in three tiers:

- **Base:** a solid block of large interlocked bricks.
- **Middle:** arched openings on all four sides, built from wedge plates and slopes, with the opening tall enough for a minifig to pass through.
- **Top:** a low ring of crenellations with a removable firewood stack (brown plates layered up) so you can stage the signal-fire scene.

Make the base 1 stud thicker than the wall and 2 to 3 courses taller, so it holds the visual weight of the whole run.

## 5. Terrain: Make the Wall Look Alive

Half the drama of the Great Wall comes from it following the hills. Build gentle slopes from wedge plates and slope bricks, and where a wall section sits on a slope, step the wall up course by course: add a layer of slope bricks under the footing at each step and raise the top to match.

Use dark green and olive green plates for the ground, dot small clusters of light green as shrubs, and lay tan plates at the foot of the hill as exposed rock. Always leave connection points between the wall and the terrain — technic bricks with pins — and anchor the wall to the hillside. Without them, the sloped section is the first thing to come loose.

## 6. Build Order and Common Failures

A build order that works:

1. Build the terrain base and fix it to the baseplate.
2. Lay the wall route following your paper sketch.
3. Work the wall from one end to the other — outer face first, then inner face.
4. Drop in the beacon towers as you pass their positions.
5. Finish crenellations, arrow slits and vegetation last, in one pass.

The three failures beginners hit most:

- Starting the next wall section before the corner is properly interlocked.
- Forgetting to leave minifig-height clearance in the crenellations and arrow slits.
- Skipping the pin connection between wall and hill, so the whole run collapses the moment you move the model.

Interlock, bite, anchor — keep those three in mind and your Great Wall will stand firm.

Once a section is finished, try adding a gatehouse and an archery tower to round out the scene.
