# How to Clean LEGO Parts: Hand Wash, Soak or Ultrasonic

Short answer first: for used bricks, warm water and a little mild dish soap with hand washing is the default choice. For big batches, switch to the soak method. Keep dark and light colors apart, rinse printed parts instead of soaking them, dry in the shade, and only then put everything away.

You buy a pile of second-hand LEGO, open it up, and find dust, fingerprints and even old sticker glue on the parts. Don't throw it out. Cleaning LEGO parts is less hassle than it looks, and with the right method old bricks can come back to roughly 90 percent of their original feel and go straight back into MOC building. This guide runs through three things: which cleaning method to pick, how to sort parts into batches, and what to do about the difficult cases.

## 1. Choosing Between the Three Main Methods

There are three common ways to clean LEGO parts. **Hand washing in water** is the safest: warm water, a small amount of mild dish soap, and gentle rubbing with your hands or a soft brush. It suits everyday cleaning of small quantities and carries almost no risk. **Soaking** is more efficient: put the parts in a laundry bag or mesh bag, soak them in warm soapy water for ten to fifteen minutes, then rinse in clean water. It works well for large batches, but don't leave them in too long, or water can work its way into printed patterns. **An ultrasonic cleaner** takes the least effort: it suits small parts like axles, pins and gears, and the high-frequency vibration shakes dirt out of tight gaps. Be careful with printed and stickered parts, though, since the vibration can speed up pattern loss. Beginners should start with hand washing and move to batch methods once they're comfortable.

## 2. Sorting: Separate by Color and Material

The worst thing you can do is throw everything in one tub. The first step in cleaning LEGO parts is sorting: wash dark parts and light parts separately so dark parts can't bleed color onto light ones. Wash transparent parts on their own and wipe them with a soft cloth rather than scrubbing hard with a brush, which causes scratches. Printed parts and stickered parts need extra care — rinse them with water only, don't rub and don't soak. Electronic parts and spring parts must never touch water; just wipe them with a dry cloth. When washing is done, lay the parts flat on a large towel and dry them in a shaded, well-ventilated spot. Never leave them in direct sun, because UV light speeds up aging and yellowing of the plastic. Store parts only once they are completely dry, otherwise leftover moisture can encourage mold inside a sealed box.

## 3. Yellowing, Stickers and Mold: Three Difficult Cases

These are the three most common problems with old bricks, and each has an answer when you clean LEGO parts. **Yellowing:** white parts that have oxidized under light are very hard to fully restore. You can try the hydrogen peroxide plus UV light method, but results vary and it only works on solid-colored parts. **Sticker residue:** soak the sticker area in warm water, then gently rub away the glue with an eraser or a little alcohol. Never scrape with a sharp tool, which leaves permanent scratches. **Mold:** first wipe off the surface mold with a dry cloth, then wipe with diluted white vinegar, rinse in clean water and dry thoroughly. Keep treated parts separate from normal ones and check them over the next few days. If a part is already brittle or badly deformed, don't fight it — replacing it with a new part is less trouble.

## 4. Storing and Maintaining Parts After Cleaning

Once the parts are clean, build good habits right away. Sort them into boxes by color or by type, keep frequently used parts within easy reach, and store transparent and rare parts separately. Choose storage boxes with sealing strips to keep out dust and moisture. For models in a display cabinet, dust them regularly with a soft brush — that's much kinder to the parts than frequent washing. With these habits in place you'll need to wash far less often, and your parts will last much longer.

Cleaning LEGO parts is something every brick builder runs into, and with the right method even second-hand parts come out looking fresh. Wash, dry and store in one go, and your old bricks become an ammunition depot for your next MOC. ## FAQ

**Q: How do I wash second-hand LEGO parts without damaging them?**
A: Start with warm water and mild dish soap, rubbing gently with a soft brush. For large batches, put the parts in a laundry bag, soak for ten to fifteen minutes, then rinse. Printed and stickered parts should be rinsed with water only — no rubbing, no soaking.

**Q: Can yellowed bricks be whitened again?**
A: Solid-colored parts can be treated with hydrogen peroxide plus UV light, but this only improves them and can't fully reverse the yellowing; results vary from part to part. It isn't suitable for stickered or transparent parts, and parts that are already brittle or deformed are better replaced.

**Q: Do I need to sort bricks by color before washing?**
A: Yes. Wash dark and light parts separately to prevent color bleeding, wipe transparent parts individually with a soft cloth, and never put electronic or spring parts in water. Lay everything flat to dry in the shade and store only once fully dry.

**Q: How long does washing a big batch take, and how should I dry it?**
A: A soak of ten to fifteen minutes is enough; rinse in clean water, then lay the parts flat on a large towel to dry in the shade. Never dry them in direct sun, since UV light speeds up aging and yellowing. Once dry, store them in sealed boxes sorted by type.
