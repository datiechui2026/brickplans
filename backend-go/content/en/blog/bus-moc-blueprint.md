# Bus MOC Blueprint: Build a 900-Piece Double-Decker City Bus

The short version: a double-decker bus MOC of roughly 900 pieces can be built in about 4 hours by a first-time builder if you follow the steps in order. The finished model measures 38 cm long and 16 cm tall, and with doors, seats and a bus stop sign worked in, it turns into an instant focal point on a city street layout.

## Step 1: Chassis and Running Gear

Start with the chassis and axles, about 150 pieces in total. Build one axle assembly at the front and one at the rear, then add a steering structure so the front wheels can swing left and right — the model rolls much better when you push it around. Leave space under the chassis for a battery box so you can add a motor later and make the bus drive itself. Don't forget the side skirts along both edges of the chassis: they hide the internal structure and clean up the silhouette.

## Step 2: Body Shell and Window Frames

The body is the face of the bus, and it accounts for about 380 pieces. A good approach is to frame the shell with 2x4 and 2x6 bricks first, then set transparent window elements into the openings to get that open, glazed transit look. Aim for a body height of 8 bricks — the proportions sit better that way. Give the front a large windshield and pair it with two round headlights, and the nose comes alive immediately.

## Step 3: Interior Seating and Details

The interior is what decides how refined the finished model looks, and it uses about 250 pieces. Fit 8 rows of double seats on the lower deck and 6 rows on the upper deck, building the backrests and armrests from small 1x2 pieces. Add a steering wheel, a fare box, handrail poles and a stop bell — the more detail you pack in, the longer the model holds up to close inspection. On the roof you can add an air conditioning unit and an escape hatch, and at the rear fit a license plate and a route sign.

## Color Scheme and Parts Tips

- Classic livery: red-and-white or green-and-white double-decker body over a dark gray chassis — high recognition at a glance.
- Route sign: use printed elements or stickers to show a route number such as "Route 3" for a stronger sense of realism.
- Parts budget: around 900 pieces runs 120 to 200 yuan in total, and missing pieces can be topped up from a second-hand brick library.

## FAQ

**Q: How many parts does a bus MOC need?**
A: The double-decker version is about 900 pieces, with the body and interior accounting for 70% of that, and a budget of roughly 120 to 200 yuan. If you build a simplified single-decker instead, about 450 pieces will do — a good way for beginners on a tight budget to practice first.

**Q: Can the bus drive on its own?**
A: The manual version rolls smoothly when pushed, and the front wheels steer. If you want it to move under its own power, install a motor kit in the battery bay you left in the chassis and convert it into an electric bus; it is also compatible with remote control modules.

**Q: How long does it take to build a bus MOC?**
A: About 4 hours for a beginner, split across the three stages of chassis, body and interior at a little over an hour each. Experienced builders finish in just over 2 hours. The tricky parts are mainly the front windshield and getting the seat rows aligned.

**Q: Where can I find bus MOC instructions?**
A: Search the "bus" tag on the brickplan platform and you will find a range of original designs — single-decker, double-decker and articulated — each with detailed step photos, a parts list and a color scheme, so you can just follow along and build.

**Q: How is a bus MOC different from an official city set?**
A: Official sets are mostly fixed vehicle types. With a MOC you set your own body colors, seat layout and route number, and you can build it as a double-decker or an articulated bus. It is more personal, and the finished model sits more harmoniously alongside street buildings.
