# Elephant MOC Blueprint Guide: Build a Realistic Brick Elephant

## What an Elephant MOC Blueprint Actually Is

An elephant MOC blueprint is a set of build instructions a MOCer draws up to recreate an elephant from ordinary bricks — and elephants are one of the most popular subjects in animal building. Unlike a retail set, an elephant MOC lives or dies on posture and character. Whether you are aiming for a long-trunked giant of the African savanna or a chunky, endearing Asian elephant calf, the muscles, skin folds and overall weight of the animal all have to come out of how the studs line up. A good blueprint usually includes a step-by-step parts list, structural diagrams for each stage, and magnified detail callouts, so you learn the underlying logic of animal shaping while you build.

## Key Building Techniques

A handful of core techniques separate a convincing elephant from a grey lump.

**Body curves** form the skeleton of the whole animal. An elephant's back is a rounded arch while the belly sags slightly, and you get that from a stepped up-and-down structure. The usual approach: lay a horizontal support frame on the base, then narrow it layer by layer with wedge plates and slope pieces until the torso transitions naturally into a full, round mass. Keep the weight distribution even — a heavy head on light hindquarters is the quickest way to watch a build topple.

**The trunk** is where the elephant's character lives. Standard bricks do not bend, so most blueprints segment the trunk with hinge or joint pieces, linking several sections so it can hang down naturally, tip slightly upward, or even curl. You can also use hose-style parts to fake the trunk's flexibility, then cap it with a round tile for the nostril opening.

**Ears** need to read as thin and lively. Elephant ears are wide and flat, so builders commonly use sideways building or SNOT (studs-not-on-top) to mount them perpendicular to the head, then add a hinge piece for a slight angle. That gives the ears both a hanging weight and a bit of life.

**Legs** have to balance stability against proportion. Elephant legs are thick and straight, so most builds use a solid brick column as internal support, wrap the outside in tiles to form the cylinder, and thicken the foot with a wider plate to keep the whole model standing.

## Where to Find Good Blueprints

When you go looking for an elephant MOC blueprint, start with the BrickPlans community, which collects a large number of animal blueprints that have been build-tested by players, with clear structures, detailed parts lists and build walkthroughs attached. Beyond that, well-known MOC sharing platforms, brick forums and the brick-building corners of social media are all good hunting grounds. When choosing, favor blueprints that list part numbers and quantities so you can check your inventory, and read other builders' finished-model feedback to steer clear of designs with unstable structure.

## Practical Building Advice

Before you start, sort your bricks by the categories in the blueprint's parts list — it saves a surprising amount of time. Follow an inside-out, bottom-up order: build the internal structure before applying the exterior, and finish the leg bases before raising the torso. If a step in the blueprint is ambiguous, cross-reference the finished-model photos and work it out from there. Once the main body is done, add props — a tan and brown baseplate for savanna ground, or a single palm tree — and the elephant suddenly has a scene and a story. Remember that the blueprint is only a starting point: bring in your own colors and ideas and you will end up with an elephant nobody else has.
