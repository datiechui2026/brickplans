# City LEGO Building Guide: Plan, Build and Expand a Mini City

Every builder has an ideal city in their head. It starts with one street, then one building, and before long you are laying out a whole miniature metropolis. This guide walks you through **city LEGO building** from scratch — how to plan, what to build first, and how to keep the city growing without painting yourself into a corner.

## Why a brick city is worth building

### The appeal of a LEGO city

**City LEGO building** is more than a hobby; it is a way of creating a world:

- **Endless expansion**: a city can always grow, and there is always a next possibility
- **Storytelling**: every corner has a story to tell
- **Achievement**: watching a city go from nothing to something is deeply satisfying
- **Community**: you can connect with other city builders and combine layouts into something bigger

### Three forms of a brick city

1. **Desktop city**: a compact layout squeezed into limited space
2. **Modular city**: standard modules that combine and expand freely
3. **Themed city**: built around a single idea, such as a Christmas town or a future city

## Step 1: City planning — lay the foundation

### Decide on scale

Before you start **city LEGO building**, be honest about your goals and your space:

**Small city (1–2 square meters)**
- Good for beginners
- 3–5 buildings
- Streets and a plaza
- A little greenery

**Medium city (2–4 square meters)**
- For experienced builders
- 8–15 buildings
- Complete blocks
- Rich landscaping

**Large city (4 square meters and up)**
- For veteran builders
- 20+ buildings
- Multiple districts
- A full transport system

### Zoning

A believable city needs sensible zoning:

**Commercial district**
- Shops, restaurants, cafés
- Office buildings, banks
- Landmarks and tall towers

**Residential district**
- Apartment blocks, houses
- Neighborhood parks
- Schools, hospitals

**Industrial district**
- Factories, warehouses
- Train stations, freight yards
- A harbor, if you have water

**Public facilities**
- City hall, police station, fire station
- Museum, library
- Parks, plazas

### Traffic planning

In **city LEGO building**, the road network is the skeleton of the city:

- **Main roads**: 4–6 studs wide, two-way traffic
- **Secondary roads**: 2–4 studs wide, connecting blocks
- **Sidewalks**: 1–2 studs wide, running along the buildings
- **Intersections**: traffic lights, crosswalks
- **Public transport**: bus stops, subway entrances

## Step 2: Buildings — the cells of the city

### Choosing an architectural style

**Modern**
- Glass facades: use transparent glass elements
- Clean lines: smooth tiles for flat walls
- Geometric shapes: boxy or curved forms

**Classical**
- Arched doors and windows: arch pieces as trim
- Decorative molding: specialty parts for ornament
- Spires and domes: distinctive rooflines

**Specialty styles**
- Chinese style: upturned eaves, red walls, glazed roof tiles
- Steampunk: gears, pipes, copper-colored parts
- Fantasy: whatever your imagination allows

### Building techniques

**The basics**
- Give each building its own baseplate
- Leave openings for doors and shop windows on the ground floor
- Separate floors with plates

**Exterior walls**
- Alternate bricks in different colors
- Line windows up in a regular rhythm
- Add signs, awnings, air-conditioning units and other small details

**Roofs**
- Flat roof: simple and practical, with room for equipment
- Sloped roof: built from slope bricks
- Combined roof: mix heights for a sense of depth

### Landmark buildings

Every city needs landmarks to make it recognizable:

- **City hall**: a large building with a clock tower
- **Train station**: an arched hall with multiple platforms
- **Museum**: classical style with a colonnade
- **TV tower**: tall, with an observation deck

## Step 3: City landscaping — bring it to life

### Street furniture

In **city LEGO building**, street furniture is what makes a city feel real:

- **Street lamps**: one every 2–3 studs
- **Benches**: around plazas and parks
- **Trash cans**: one on every corner
- **Phone booths and mailboxes**: classic city elements
- **Bus shelters**: a simple canopy is enough

### Greenery

**Street trees**
- Planted along the roads
- The same species throughout for a tidy look
- Evenly spaced

**Parks and plazas**
- Central plaza: fountain, sculpture, pigeons
- Neighborhood park: playground equipment, lawn
- Waterfront promenade: if you have a river or lake

**Planters and green strips**
- Road medians
- Small gardens in front of buildings
- Rooftop greenery

### Minifigures and vehicles

**Residents**
- Minifigures with different jobs
- Characters of all ages
- Everyday scenes: walking the dog, jogging, waiting for a bus

**Vehicles**
- Private cars, taxis, buses
- Bicycles, scooters
- Fire engines, police cars, ambulances
- Trucks and delivery vans

## Step 4: Modular expansion — let the city grow

### Module standards

Set a consistent module standard so the city can expand indefinitely:

- **Base module**: 32x32 baseplate as the standard unit
- **Road module**: 16x32 or 32x32
- **Corner module**: 32x32 with a corner road
- **Plaza module**: 32x32 or larger

### How modules connect

- **Technic pins**: connect underneath for stability
- **Alignment markers**: make sure modules line up
- **Consistent height**: roads and building footprints sit flush

### Expansion strategies

- **Linear expansion**: extend along a street
- **Depth expansion**: add depth to existing blocks
- **Vertical expansion**: increase building height
- **Thematic expansion**: add a new district

## Advanced techniques: a professional-grade city

### Lighting

Add LED lighting to the city:
- Interior lighting in buildings
- A street lamp network
- Headlights on vehicles
- Overall night-time atmosphere

### Terrain

Break away from the flat tabletop:
- Rivers and lakes
- Hillsides and steps
- Viaducts and interchanges
- Underground spaces

### Seasonal variation

Build modules for different seasons:
- Spring: cherry blossom, fresh green trees
- Summer: parasols, ice cream vans
- Autumn: yellow fallen leaves, pumpkins
- Winter: snow scenes, Christmas decorations
