# Lion King MOC Blueprints Review: Building the Pride Lands

The short version: a Lion King MOC lives or dies on character personality and the Pride Rock setting. Beginners should start with a young Simba figure; go for the big scene only if display impact is the priority.

*The Lion King* is one of Disney's most enduring animated films, and Simba's journey from careless cub to king of the Pride Lands still lands with audiences. Recreating that landscape in brick is a common goal for Disney builders. This review looks at Lion King MOC blueprints from three angles: character design, scene accuracy, and blueprint quality.

## Character Design: Capturing Simba and Scar

The heart of any Lion King MOC blueprint is character personality. Simba's rounded cub form needs curved pieces piled up to suggest a fluffy mane, while the adult version has to read as regal. Scar is the harder job: the sly, narrowed eyes and dark mane are what make him recognizable, and a few degrees off on the eyebrow angle changes the whole mood. The blueprints that perform best in this review all put effort into mane depth, layering brown pieces in several shades rather than using a single color. Faces are outlined with slope bricks, with features carried by printed or sticker pieces — and the character suddenly reads correctly.

## Scene Accuracy: Pride Rock and the Savanna

Scenery is where Lion King MOC blueprints really show their ambition. Pride Rock is the signature landmark, and it tests a designer's control of rock layering and overhanging structure. Savanna scenes lean on a dead tree, grass and sunset-toned color blocking to set the African mood. The most popular build in this review collects Pride Rock, the savanna and several character figures onto a single base, with the presentation of Simba held high reproduced precisely — strong storytelling and strong display value together. The catch is part variety: these scene blueprints are heavy on yellow and brown, so sort by color before you start building.

## Blueprint Quality and Build Difficulty

Difficulty across Lion King MOC blueprints varies widely. Single-character figures are structurally simple and a beginner can finish one within an hour. The large Pride Rock scenes involve a lot of SNOT technique and rock-texture construction, and are better suited to builders with some experience. When choosing a blueprint, check two things closely: whether the parts list is complete, and how hard the special pieces are to source (curved mane elements, printed expression heads). Both feed directly into your replication cost. Among the blueprints we examined, most divide steps clearly; a few of the large scenes require you to supply baseplates yourself.

## Advice for Disney Brick Builders

For a first attempt at a Lion King MOC blueprint, start with the young Simba figure: low cost, quick payoff. If display impact is what you want, take on the Pride Rock scene — the finished model is a showcase piece on any shelf. ## FAQ

**Q: Which Lion King MOC blueprint should a beginner build first?**
A: Start with the young Simba figure. The structure is simple, a beginner can finish it within an hour, and the low part count keeps costs down while giving you a quick win. Move on to the large Pride Rock scene once you're comfortable.

**Q: What makes the Simba and Scar figures difficult to design?**
A: Mane layering and the eyes. Manes need brown pieces in several shades stacked in layers, faces are outlined with slope bricks, and a small deviation in eyebrow angle completely changes the character's mood.

**Q: Why does a Pride Rock scene blueprint cost more?**
A: Mostly part variety. Yellow and brown elements are used in large quantities, and you also need special pieces such as curved mane elements and printed expression heads, which pushes replication cost up noticeably. Some large scenes also require you to supply baseplates.

**Q: How do I judge whether a Lion King MOC blueprint is worth buying?**
A: Check three things: whether the parts list is complete, whether the special pieces (curved mane elements, printed expression heads) are easy to buy, and whether the steps are divided clearly. Those three determine your replication cost.
