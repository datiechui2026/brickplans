# Brick Frog MOC Review: Big Eyes, Wide Mouth, Crouch Pose

Short answer first: this frog blueprint is beginner-friendly, but the "frogness" lives almost entirely in two places — the bulging eyes and the wide mouth, sitting on a proper crouched stance, with green patterning and moving legs layered on top.

Frogs are one of the friendliest subjects in animal MOC building. A rounded body, popping eyes, a wide mouth and a crouched pose give you unmistakable character without demanding rare or complicated parts. Get the eyes and mouth right, though, and everything else follows. This review of a **brick frog MOC blueprint** covers shaping, color, structure and blueprint quality so you know what you are getting before you start sorting parts.

## Big Eyes and a Wide Mouth: Getting the Expression Right

A frog's expression is its eyes and its mouth. For the eyeballs, 1x1 round tiles or printed round pieces work well, with dark curved pieces forming the socket rim so the eyes sit proud and look alert. The wide mouth is the frog's signature: run a reddish-brown or dark slope piece across the lower face to draw the smile curve, with the corners turned up slightly, and it reads as a frog immediately. On a big-mouth variant you can clip a small red piece in place as a tongue and the model comes alive. Keep the eyes slightly high on the head and a little apart — that is what makes the front view cute rather than odd.

## The Crouch: Stance and Body Structure

Crouching is the pose you see most often on a frog. The body is built from dome pieces or curved bricks to get a rounded silhouette, with the back rising slightly and the belly picked out in light green or off-white to separate the layers. In a crouch the center of gravity has to sit low: both front legs braced on the ground, both rear legs folded against the sides, and ball joints at the leg roots so the angles look natural instead of stiff. Build onto a stable square baseplate — this matters a lot if the model will live on a shelf long-term, because a high center of gravity will tip it backward.

## Green Palettes and Spot Patterns

Keep the body in greens: bright green or dark green as the main color, light green or off-white for belly and chin, and dark green dotted across the back for spots and stripes. Spots can be orderly rows of dark 1x1 plates, or printed round pieces pressed on for a cleaner finish. If you want a more individual look, try teal paired with yellow eye patches — the coloring of certain tree frogs — which reads instantly and stands out on a desk.

## Poseable Leg Structure

Articulation is where this blueprint earns extra points. A good design puts ball joints or hinges at the rear leg roots so the frog can hold a pre-jump crouch, while the front legs work best with clip pieces that let you adjust the angle. When you evaluate a blueprint, check whether the leg assembly steps are clear and whether enough joint pieces are called for — that determines whether the finished model can actually hold a pose. When the articulation is done well, one build gives you several stances, and play value goes up considerably.

## Blueprint Quality and Beginner Friendliness

Overall difficulty here is low, with part counts typically between 150 and 250 pieces, which makes it a solid first animal build. Check three things before you commit: whether the step diagrams are enlarged and legible, whether the parts list gives colors and quantities, and whether there are multi-angle photos of the finished model. For a newcomer, a blueprint labeled beginner-friendly or entry-level, paired with clear building notes, is unlikely to go wrong. Once it is built, set it on a simple waterside baseplate or add a few lily pad pieces and the brick frog MOC is complete.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I choose a brick frog MOC blueprint?**
A: Start with the eyes and mouth. The eyes should sit proud of the head — dome pieces or round pieces mounted on the outside — and the mouth should be wide and fairly flat. Get those two right and a round body on a crouched stance will read as a frog without any explanation.

**Q: How do I make the eyes stand out?**
A: Use dome pieces or large round pieces mounted on the outside of the head, with a layer of small round pieces underneath to form the socket. White for the eyeball with a small black dot for the pupil; keep left and right at equal height and set slightly wider apart than the body's centerline.

**Q: How do I build a natural crouch?**
A: Fold the rear legs and tilt the body forward. Build the folded thigh and shin from curved pieces and keep the feet flat on the ground. Weight the body slightly backward, with short front limbs just touching the ground — avoid anything that looks like a standing pose.

**Q: How do I place spots on a green palette?**
A: Use medium or grass green as the main color, with dark green or yellow patches on the back and legs. Transition the belly through pale yellow-green — two steps of light and dark is enough for depth — and keep the spots small and sparse rather than large and dense.
