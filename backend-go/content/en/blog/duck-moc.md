# Brick Duck MOC Review: 420 Pieces, Round and Family-Friendly

The short version: this duck MOC goes for a soft, cute look. It uses roughly 420 pieces, stands about 11 cm tall, costs 20 yuan for the instructions and about 78 yuan for the loose parts, and takes around two hours to build. The plump body reaches roughly 88% fidelity to the reference, the orange bill and webbed feet land exactly where they should, and the whole thing works well either as a parent-and-child build or as a small desk display.

## Shape Fidelity: Round and Cute

The hard part of any duck is the "round." This design builds the body from large curved slope pieces, with a slight arc across the back and a full, heavy belly, landing at about 88% fidelity to the reference shape. The head is formed as a single unit from yellow pieces, the orange bill is made from two slope pieces joined face to face, and a pair of round black eyes plus slightly raised wings finish it off — it reads instantly as a little yellow duck.

The feather palette stays disciplined: plain yellow with orange accents, nothing else competing for attention. That restraint is what makes the model look clean rather than busy, and it is also why the part count stays as low as it does for a body this round.

## Build Experience: Structure and Stability

The duck has a low center of gravity and a wide footprint. Plates at the bottom widen the contact area with the surface, so it stands steady without any extra display base. The instructions are divided into six steps, each kept to under ten pieces, and the sequence is clear enough that you rarely have to backtrack — roughly two hours from sorting to finished model.

The one warning worth repeating: the orange slope pieces on the bill are easy to mount facing the wrong way. Line them up with the orientation shown in the instructions rather than eyeballing it, and you avoid having to pry them off again. Sorting the yellow pieces by size before you start also speeds up the early steps, since so many of them look alike at a glance.
