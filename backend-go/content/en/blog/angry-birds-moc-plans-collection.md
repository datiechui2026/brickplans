# Angry Birds MOC Plans: Character, Scene and Part Guide

That red bird launched from a slingshot is a childhood memory for a whole generation. When a classic mobile game IP meets brick MOC building, designers turn out a huge amount of wildly creative work. I have collected and built a fair number of **Angry Birds MOC plans** over the years, so here is a roundup review split by character builds, scenes and parts, to help you pick the ones genuinely worth building.

## Angry Birds MOC plans: what's out there

Angry Birds plans fall into three main groups. First, single characters, with Red, Chuck, Bomb and the green pigs the most popular. Second, scene builds, where the slingshot, the pig castle and floating islands are common subjects. Third, characters worked into a larger scene, such as a battle board or a small story diorama. These three types of **Angry Birds MOC plans** differ a lot in parts demand, so beginners should start with a single character and move on to scenes once they have some experience. My own first build was a lone Red, and I was hooked the moment it was finished.

## Character builds: accuracy is everything

Character plans are the real test of a designer's skill. A good Red plan captures the round body, the angry white eyebrows and the pointed orange-yellow beak, using stacked curved pieces and a mix of wedge slopes to sketch out the silhouette. For the green pigs, the giveaway details are the green snout and the two little moustache tufts. In many of the plans I have built, the designers also made the eyebrows and wings poseable so you can set different expressions, which works both on the shelf and in hand. When choosing, look at front and side photos of the finished model rather than only the cover render. The more real photos a designer posts, the more confidence they clearly have in the build.

## Scene plans: fun and difficulty together

Scene plans have more story to them than single characters. A slingshot scene usually needs the launch mechanism built in, while a pig castle tests how well you handle layered brick walls and colour blocking. These plans generally use a larger part count, but the structural logic is clear and building from numbered bags is not hard. One thing to watch: on some plans the slingshot joints come out a little loose, so reinforce the key connections after the build to stop it coming apart in your hands. Overall the difficulty is moderate, which makes it good practice for intermediate builders, and the finished piece is a display model with real narrative pull.

## Parts and building experience: how to judge a plan

A decent Angry Birds MOC plan should have a complete parts list and clear step diagrams, ideally with the quantity of each piece labelled. Most plans today rely on common basic bricks that you can fill in from your own loose-parts stock, so beginners do not need to pay a premium or hunt overseas for one rare element. Because these builds use a lot of same-colour pieces, sort by colour before you start. If you spend time in the community, you will notice that some designers revise their steps after player feedback. Plans maintained like that are worth keeping, and they give you more building confidence.
