# LEGO Villa MOC Build Guide: Foundation to Garden

If you have been meaning to build your own brick villa, this walkthrough should save you a few wrong turns. Most builders start architectural MOCs by following someone else's instructions, but a genuinely good brick villa is more than a pile of parts clicked together. Blueprint selection, foundation structure, wall bricklaying, roof, and garden each have their own rules.

## Step 1: Pick the Right Blueprint First

The first move in any villa build is not buying bricks, it is choosing a plan. A good plan sets the proportions, floor heights, and detail density of the whole house. Beginners should look for plans that include **baseplate zoning** and a **parts list**, ideally with step-by-step rendered views so you can locate your place quickly if you get stuck. On BrickPlans you can filter by the architectural MOC category; plans with structural annotations are usually the ones where the author put in real effort, and they are worth saving.

## Step 2: Foundation and Interlocking Structure

The thing that ruins a brick build fastest is a loose overall structure. For a villa, lay a full layer of large plates and bricks 2x4 or bigger as the foundation, then close the perimeter upward using **staggered, interlocking bricks** so the walls and foundation actually grip each other. For a wide open living room, add a few columns or a beam across the middle as support, otherwise the weight of the upper floors will bow the floor plates. Keep one principle in mind: **every seam should be covered by the bricks of the next layer**. That is the underlying logic of all architectural MOCs.

## Step 3: Wall Bricklaying and Detail for a Real-Brick Look

Walls are where a villa build shows real skill. Offset the joints between courses as you go and the exterior starts to resemble the mortar lines of a real building. At corners, use 1x1 bricks and 1x2 bricks as transitions so no obvious patchwork appears. Reserve window and door openings before you lay the surrounding wall, and use window frame pieces or inverted plates for sills to add instant depth. If you want a premium exterior, apply **bricks with studs on the side** in places to suggest stone or wall-tile texture, then break it up with a few decorative lines. It reads far better than a large flat panel.

## Step 4: Roof and Garden, Where the Finish Decides the Quality

The roof is the easiest place to fail and the easiest place to shine. On a pitched roof, add a ring of anti-slip pieces along the eaves so the tiles stay put. On a flat roof, leave a drainage slope on the inside of the parapet wall; that kind of detail is what holds up to a second look. For the garden, start with a layer of green base plates as lawn, then dress it with 1x1 flower plates, low shrubs, and street lamp pieces, and run a small path to the front door. Suddenly the villa feels lived in.

## Step 5: Color Scheme and the Beginner Mistake Checklist

Keep the facade to three main colors at most. White paired with light gray or beige is the safest combination, with dark doors and windows as accents. And here is the checklist worth reading before you start: gather all the parts first so you do not run out halfway through; never force or bend a part, since the damage weakens the whole structure; on multi-story villas, reinforce each floor before stacking the next; and leave access points for disassembly so you can revise the MOC later.
