# How to Build a LEGO MOC Tank: Chassis, Turret and Tracks

Short answer first: proportions make or break a tank MOC. Keep the hull length-to-width ratio between 2.5:1 and 3:1, make the barrel roughly 0.8 times the hull length, and tension the tracks so they sit against the road wheels without being stretched tight.

Tanks are one of the most popular military MOC subjects, and also one of the most demanding on structural skill: the tracks have to run free, the turret has to rotate, and a slightly off ratio immediately reads wrong. This tutorial splits the build into four stages — chassis, turret, tracks, details — with the key techniques and common mistakes at each one.

## Before You Start: Decide the Scale

The first step isn't building, it's **choosing a scale**. There are three common options:

- **Mini scale (200–400 pieces)**: silhouette-focused, good for practice and for displaying a row of vehicles;
- **Standard scale (600–1200 pieces)**: enough detail to sit alongside minifigures, and the most popular choice;
- **Large scale (2000+ pieces)**: full internal structure, with working tracks.

Once the scale is locked in, your part selection and structural strength requirements have a basis.

## Step 1: The Chassis — Length-to-Width Ratio and Load Bearing

The chassis is the tank's skeleton and directly determines whether the finished model feels solid. Build a rectangular chassis frame first, line it with technic beams for load bearing, then lay plates over the bottom face. Key points:

1. **Length-to-width ratio**: WWII tanks mostly fall between 2.5:1 and 3:1 — too short looks fat, too long looks floaty;
2. **Load-bearing beams**: run two technic beams lengthwise along the hull, spanning the track area, to stop the middle sagging;
3. **Ground clearance**: leave 1–2 plate thicknesses between chassis and ground, so the tracks don't expose the hull bottom.

## Step 2: The Turret — Turntable First, Shaping Second

For a turret that rotates, you need a **turntable** between hull and turret. Mount the turntable base on top of the hull and the matching axle on the underside of the turret; join the two and you get 360° rotation.

For shaping, most turrets are wedge-shaped: sloped front armor, a narrowed rear bustle, built up with slope pieces and wedge pieces to create the angles. Use a long technic axle or cylinder for the barrel, and remember the **barrel should be about 0.8 times the hull length** — too short and it lacks presence, too long and it becomes top-heavy. Keep the turret's center of gravity over the turntable so it doesn't wobble when it turns.

## Step 3: Tracks — Tension Is the Key to Smooth Running

Tracks are the most visually effective part of a tank and the easiest place to jam. Two mainstream approaches:

- **One-piece molded track links**: less work and they roll smoothly, but with limited flexibility;
- **Segmented links + road wheels**: more realistic, but you need to adjust the number of links to get the tension right.

The rule for track tension: **it should hug the road wheels without being pulled tight**. Too loose and you throw the track; too tight and it locks up. Once installed, push it by hand — if it rolls smoothly one link at a time, it's right.

## Step 4: Details — Making It Read as a Tank

Details decide whether it looks the part: add fenders and tow hooks at the front of the hull, tool boxes and spare track links on the sides, hatches and an antenna on the turret roof, and exhausts at the rear. Keep the color scheme within a single family — dark green or sand is the safest bet, and avoid a rainbow of colors.

Finish with three checks: does the turret rotate smoothly, do the tracks roll, and does the hull sit flat without rocking.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I build a MOC tank chassis that doesn't sag?**
A: Run two technic beams lengthwise along the hull as load-bearing members, spanning the track area, then lay plates over the bottom face. The middle of the chassis won't sag, and the track area keeps enough ground clearance.

**Q: How do I make the tank turret rotate?**
A: Mount a turntable base on top of the hull and the matching axle on the underside of the turret; join them for 360° rotation. Keep the turret's center of gravity as close to directly above the turntable as possible so it doesn't wobble when it turns.

**Q: The tracks keep jamming or coming off — what should I do?**
A: Adjust the tension. The track should hug the road wheels without being pulled tight; once installed, push it along by hand and it should roll smoothly. Too loose throws the track, too tight locks it up — adding or removing one track link is enough to fine-tune it.

**Q: How do I set proportions so the tank doesn't look like a toy?**
A: Keep the hull length-to-width ratio between 2.5:1 and 3:1, make the barrel about 0.8 times the hull length, and sit the turret centered on the hull or slightly rearward. Get those three points right and it reads as a real vehicle rather than a toy.
