# Brick Scooter MOC Review: Lightweight Body, Smart Folding

The short version: this is one of the best value small builds you can drop into a street scene. It uses about 120 parts, takes a beginner roughly 40 minutes, and its folding mechanism is built from two hinge assemblies plus pins — folded up, it takes about half the space.

Cars and motorcycles are the usual street-MOC traffic, but more and more builders like to leave a scooter parked in a corner. This particular brick scooter MOC sells itself on a light frame and a frame that actually folds, and the overall execution is impressively complete.

## Accuracy to the Real Thing

The reference is the folding commuter scooter you see in any city: T-shaped handlebar, long slim deck, small wheels front and rear. The proportions are handled well — the ratio of handlebar height to deck length is close to the real vehicle, and it sits at a believable size next to a minifigure without looking bulky. Several color schemes are offered; the default white-and-grey mix is clean and pairs with most street buildings.

## A Genuinely Lightweight Body

The main frame leans heavily on Technic elements. Beams and pins replace conventional brick stacking, which keeps the overall weight down while preserving rigidity. The deck surface is made from thin plates with reinforcing beams hidden underneath, so it does not flex or sag under a minifigure. You can pick the whole scooter up with one hand, which is exactly what the lightweight brief calls for.

## The Folding Mechanism

This is the highlight of the design. The fold is achieved with two hinge assemblies working together with pins: the handlebar stem folds backward, and the middle of the deck folds in half as well. Folded, the scooter takes up roughly half its volume — small enough to go into a minifigure backpack or the trunk of a car. Hinge resistance is about right, and stop pieces lock everything in place once unfolded, so it does not flop around in hand. One caveat: folding it repeatedly will wear the pins faster, so it is worth keeping a few spares of the same size on hand.

## Parts List and Build Difficulty

The parts list runs to roughly 120 pieces, the vast majority common Technic elements and basic plates. There are no rare or discontinued parts, so sourcing them from the list is inexpensive. The instructions are clearly stepped out at 3 to 5 parts per step, with part numbers and colors labeled. Difficulty sits at the lower end of medium: a beginner can finish in about 40 minutes. The only part that demands patience is the wheel assembly — the front and rear wheels use small hubs with rubber tires, and you need to line them up on the axle centerline or they will wobble.

## Verdict

This brick scooter MOC strikes a good balance between lightness and playability. The folding mechanism is cleverly engineered, the parts are affordable and common, and the instructions are easy to follow — a low-cost, high-value piece of street dressing.
