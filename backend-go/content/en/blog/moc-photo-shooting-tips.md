# LEGO MOC Photography Tips: Shoot Pro Photos With a Phone

Start with the conclusion: you don't need professional gear to photograph a MOC. One phone, one backdrop cloth and one 45-degree side light source can lift the perceived quality of your build by 80%. Remember the three principles — clean background, soft light, shoot from multiple angles — and note that 90% of the highly upvoted builds on brickplan were shot on phones.

## Step 1: Set Up the Shooting Scene

The background is the first impression your build makes. A solid-color backdrop cloth or a plain white wall is the safest choice; keep cluttered desktops out of frame. A 60cm-square backdrop cloth costs around 20 yuan. Place the model at least 30cm in front of the backdrop while shooting, and use a wide aperture or portrait mode to blur the background and isolate the subject. Pair dark builds with light backgrounds and light builds with dark backgrounds for stronger contrast and better photos. If the build itself is already very colorful, try a light gray or beige backdrop instead — it keeps attention on the model without competing with it. For outdoor shooting, pick an overcast day or the soft light of early morning and dusk for the highest hit rate.

## Step 2: Control the Direction of Light

Light determines how the texture reads. Window light is the first choice; avoid the harsh direct sun of midday. A 45-degree side light creates a sense of volume and makes the edges and layers of the parts stand out clearly. Indoors, a single desk lamp plus a sheet of white paper as a reflector gets you about 70% of the effect of a softbox. Avoid overhead light — it blows out the top of the build and loses detail. Keep color temperature consistent at around 5000K for the most natural tones. Before you shoot, clear dust off the parts with an air blower or a soft brush; under macro conditions dust is magnified into obvious white specks, and dark parts show it worst. Cleaning up instantly moves the image up a level.

## Step 3: Multiple Angles and Post-Processing

Shoot three groups of angles: a 45-degree high angle for the overall shape, an eye-level close-up for details, and a macro shot for part texture. Take at least 10 frames per build, then pick the best. For post-processing, use your phone's built-in editing tools to adjust exposure, contrast and saturation — and be careful not to overdo filters. When cropping, follow the rule of thirds and place the subject at the one-third line of the frame. Upload your finished photos to the brickplan build section, where you can also give a thumbs-up to instructions you like. When sharing to social platforms, it's worth including the build's dimensions, part count and design reasoning; brickplan users most enjoy this kind of behind-the-scenes creation content, and it clearly gets a higher interaction rate.

## FAQ

**Q: What equipment should I use to photograph a LEGO MOC?**

A: A phone is entirely sufficient — flagship phones now come close to entry-level mirrorless cameras. What matters is lighting and background, not the device. If your budget allows, add a 100-yuan-class fill light, and night shooting becomes much more comfortable.

**Q: How do I choose a background for MOC photos?**

A: Go first for a solid-color backdrop cloth or a white wall; black and white are the most versatile. Keep busy desktops and clutter out of frame. A 60cm-square backdrop cloth costs about 20 yuan, folds away easily for storage and is big enough for most builds.

**Q: Do MOC photos need dedicated lighting?**

A: Yes — light is the core of image quality. Window daylight is best; indoors you can use a desk lamp with white paper as a reflector. A 45-degree side light brings out the three-dimensionality of the parts, so avoid overhead light and overly hard direct light.

**Q: What should I watch out for when editing MOC photos?**

A: Only make small adjustments to exposure, contrast and saturation with your phone's built-in tools, and avoid heavy filters that distort the model. Crop using the rule of thirds, place the subject at the one-third line, and keep the overall color tone consistent.
