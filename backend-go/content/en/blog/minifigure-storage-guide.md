# How to Store LEGO Minifigures: Boxes, Displays and Sorting

Once you own more than 50 minifigures, the best-value setup is a grid storage box with one figure per compartment paired with a glass-door display cabinet, sorted by series and rarity, and the whole thing can be kept to a budget of 200–500 CNY. If you have fewer than 30, a single drawer-style box is enough. The real enemy is not shelf space — it is dust and the UV fading that turns plastic yellow. That makes the two core rules simple: divide without squeezing, and block light and dust.

## 1. Take inventory: how many minifigures do you actually have

Count before you buy anything. Collections usually fall into three tiers. Under 30 figures counts as entry level, and one 24-compartment drawer box holds them all. Between 50 and 100 figures, go for a multi-layer grid box — at 3–5 figures per compartment you will need roughly 15–25 compartments. Above 100, combine storage boxes with a display cabinet.

A minifigure stands about 4 cm tall, and heads and hair pieces deform easily under pressure, so look for compartments at least 5 cm high.

## 2. Sort by series, then by rarity

Sorting comes first, before any box purchase. Split by series first, then subdivide by rarity. Keep City, Ninjago and Harry Potter in separate boxes, for example, then give retired, limited-edition and translucent-piece figures their own compartments inside each series and add a small label. You will find figures faster, and rare ones will not get worn down while sitting among common duplicates. If you are unsure which figures count as rare, check the release year and production numbers for each series against the minifigure index on BrickPlan.

## 3. Drawer boxes vs grid boxes: size comparison

Drawer-style boxes typically measure 30x20 cm per layer and are split by dividers into 4–8 compartments. They suit figures stored upright and let you pull out a whole layer at once. The downside is that each compartment is large, so small parts slide around and mix.

Grid-style boxes usually have 4x4 cm or 5x5 cm compartments, ranging from 12 up to 60 cells. One figure per cell means nothing presses on anything else, which makes this the most recommended option. The trade-off is that pulling a single figure out is less convenient than sliding out a drawer.

On price: a 24-compartment grid box runs about 30–50 CNY, while a 4-layer drawer box costs roughly 60–120 CNY.

## 4. Display cases: dust and UV protection

If you want your figures stored and displayed at the same time, a cabinet with glass or acrylic doors is the answer. The doors keep dust outside and save you constant wiping. Placement matters as much as the cabinet: keep it away from balconies and west-facing windows, because UV in sunlight will visibly yellow and fade plastic parts within six months to a year. A safer step is to apply UV-blocking film to the cabinet glass, which costs about 20–40 CNY and noticeably slows fading. For lighting, use cool-white LED lamps and avoid anything that runs hot and bakes the bricks.

## 5. Budget plans by collection size

- **Up to 30 figures:** one 24-compartment grid box, about 40 CNY.
- **50–100 figures:** two 24-compartment boxes plus one 4-layer drawer box, about 150–250 CNY.
- **Over 100 figures:** the same combination plus a display cabinet with doors, which ranges from 300–800 CNY, for a total of 500–1000 CNY.

Fill every compartment you own before buying the next layer. That way you avoid stockpiling boxes you never use.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the best way to store LEGO minifigures?**
A: Start with grid storage boxes sorted by series, one figure per compartment so nothing gets squeezed, then move them into a display cabinet with glass doors to keep out dust and light. Under 30 figures, a single-layer box is fine; above 50, add the cabinet. A budget of 200–500 CNY covers it.

**Q: Which storage box will not crush my minifigures?**
A: Choose a grid box with 4–5 cm cells and a depth of at least 5 cm, so heads and hair pieces keep their shape. Avoid large open drawers — figures topple over and stack against each other, which bends leg parts.

**Q: How should I sort minifigures so I can find them quickly?**
A: Split by series first, then by rarity, with rare figures stored separately and labelled. Keep frequently used figures in the upper boxes and retired ones below, and label each box with its series name — this can cut search time by more than half.

**Q: Will minifigures in a display cabinet still fade?**
A: Yes. Sunlight UV can yellow plastic in six months to a year. Keep the cabinet out of direct window light, apply UV-blocking film to the doors (about 20–40 CNY) and light it with cool-white LEDs, and the original colour will hold up long term.

**Q: What is the cheapest option on a tight budget?**
A: Buy two 24-compartment grid boxes first (about 60–80 CNY) to sort 50 figures, and substitute a plain shoe box with dividers for the display cabinet. Upgrade to a cabinet with doors once you pass 100 figures — spreading the cost out is the cheapest route.
