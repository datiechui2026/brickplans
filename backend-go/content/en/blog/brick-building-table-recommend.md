# Brick Building Table Guide: Size, Material, Storage

The hardest part of building with bricks is not running out of pieces. It is having nowhere to put a half-finished model. A table that suits the job lets a build sit safely overnight, keeps parts in their places, and saves parents from crawling around picking up bricks every evening. A good buying decision comes down to four things: size, material, height and storage. This guide walks through all four so you only have to choose once.

## 1. Sizing: Check Your Room, Then Your Child's Age

There is no single correct tabletop size, but there are two rules worth following.

First, **the surface has to hold a work in progress**. Kids should not have to dismantle a build every night, so allow at least 40 x 60 cm of clear space — enough for a small house plus a box of loose parts.

Second, **size follows age**. A tabletop around 60 cm is plenty for ages three to six. Older builders working on MOCs and technic mechanisms will be much more comfortable with 80 cm or more.

In a small home, look first at models with flip-top storage, where the play surface and the bins are one unit — that saves the floor space a separate storage cabinet would take. Measure the spot you have reserved before ordering, so the table does not become a regret the moment it comes through the door.

## 2. Material: Durability and Safety First

Most building tables on the market fall into three groups.

**Solid wood**: good feel, strong load capacity and a long service life. Best for a fixed spot in a child's room. Downsides are weight and price, and you should check that the corners are genuinely rounded.

**Plastic**: light, water-resistant and easy to clean, at a friendly price. A good fit for younger children, though load capacity and stability are average. When buying, check the wall thickness of the plastic and whether it smells of anything.

**Folding**: packs away when playtime ends, which suits tight spaces. The folding mechanism tends to loosen over years of use, so inspect the hinges and locking catches closely.

Whatever the material, confirm three points: whether the surface is anti-slip (a studded plate is better for building than a smooth panel), whether the corners are rounded, and whether the paint or finish is environmentally friendly and odour-free.

## 3. Height and Storage: The Details That Decide the Experience

Table height should match the child. The right height is where, seated, the elbows rest naturally on the surface with the back straight and shoulders relaxed. In practice that is roughly 40–45 cm for ages three to six and 50–55 cm for ages six and up. A height-adjustable growth table is the better value over time.

For storage, the ideal setup is **zoned tabletop plus drawers plus hanging pockets**. Use shallow trays on the surface for frequently used parts, drawers for sorting boxes, and side pockets for instructions and tools. Sort parts by colour or by shape, add labels, and children find pieces faster and tidy up faster too.

One more thing worth planning for: a low lip or guard rail around the table edge keeps parts from rolling off, which saves you the daily ritual of picking bricks off the floor.

## 4. Setups and Budget Guidance

A few reference combinations by need:

- **Starter setup (under 300)**: a folding plastic table plus two sorting bins. Good for younger children trying building for the first time.
- **Step-up setup (600–1,000)**: a solid wood or heavy-duty plastic table with drawer storage. Suits a permanent spot and daily play.
- **Builder setup (1,000 and above)**: a large growth table plus a dedicated parts cabinet and a baseplate fixing mat. Suits young MOC builders who want the tabletop to double as a building workbench.

If the budget is tight, put the money into the tabletop first and add storage gradually. A surface that is big enough and stable enough is the core of the purchase.
