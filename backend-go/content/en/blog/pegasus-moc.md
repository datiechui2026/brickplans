# Pegasus MOC Tutorial: 350-Part Winged Display Build

The short version first: this white mythical Pegasus MOC runs about 350 parts and measures 13 cm tall with a 22 cm wingspan. The wings use a ball joint at the root plus a two-layer wing panel, so you can pose the model mid-flight without a single sticker. It is an entry-level mid-size build that works equally well as a desk display piece or a gift.

**Step 1: Gather parts and plan the color scheme**

The all-white build totals roughly 350 parts, with white bricks making up more than 70 percent, rounded out by a small amount of light gray and gold accents. Sort everything into four bags before you start: body, wings, mane and tail, base. Building while digging through one pile is where mistakes happen. The parts list and step-by-step instructions are available as a free download on brickplan.cn.

**Step 2: Build the body and legs**

Start from the chest cavity and stack 2x4 and 2x2 bricks in a staggered pattern to create the curve of the horse's body. Pull the belly in with inverted plates underneath. The four legs connect to the shoulders and hips through hinge bricks: front legs stretched forward, rear legs slightly bent for the airborne pose. Make sure the left and right legs end up exactly the same length, or the finished model will not stand level.

**Step 3: Shape the neck and head**

The neck stacks three ball joints, which lets the head turn through a small range. Build the muzzle from slope bricks and use a 1x1 round tile for each eye. For the mane, insert 1x1 clip plates along the top of the head and the back of the neck, which gives the model a strong linear, flowing look.

**Step 4: The wing-opening mechanism (the key step)**

The wings are the heart of this build. A large ball joint at the root connects each wing to the shoulder, so it swings up and down and locks at the angle you choose. Each side splits into two panels, a front wing and a rear wing. The front wing uses long plates as its spar, while the rear wing widens progressively and tapers at the tip with slope pieces cut into feather points. Fully extended, one side measures about 11 cm, for a total wingspan of roughly 22 cm. To make the wings sturdier, add a support rod behind the wing root and clip it onto the studs on the side of the body.

**Step 5: Base and finishing touches**

Build a round base about 10 cm in diameter and stack transparent pieces on top to suggest swirling clouds. Fix the raised front hooves in place and anchor the rear hooves onto studs on the base, and the Pegasus reads as if it is rising off the clouds. As a final pass, check the tension on every hinge and shim any loose joint with a single 1x1 plate.

## FAQ

**Q: How do you secure the wings on this Pegasus MOC?**

A: The wing root attaches to the body with a large ball joint, backed up by a support rod that clips onto the rear of the wing root. Once the wings are spread they lock at a stable angle, so the pose will not collapse, and the wings still come off easily when you want to take the model apart.

**Q: How many parts does this Pegasus MOC need?**

A: Around 350 parts in total, with white bricks making up about 70 percent, plus light gray and gold accents and the transparent base pieces. That is a modest parts count, which makes it a good choice for anyone trying a mid-size MOC for the first time. Expect roughly 4 to 6 hours of building time.

**Q: Are white parts easy to buy?**

A: White is the most common brick color, so the second-hand and loose-part market is well supplied and prices are low. For the few less common pieces, such as the ball joints and the gold accents, the instruction page on brickplan.cn lists substitution suggestions, and almost everything is readily available.

**Q: Will the extended wings break easily?**

A: The wing structure combines long plates with hinges, and the load is designed to run along the horizontal spread, so the wings will not snap under normal display use. As long as you do not repeatedly force the ball joints at full strength, regular handling is completely fine.

**Q: Can I build it without instructions?**

A: Download the free step-by-step instructions from brickplan.cn first. They include the parts list and color callouts for every step, which raises your chances of a clean build and keeps you from ordering the wrong parts.
