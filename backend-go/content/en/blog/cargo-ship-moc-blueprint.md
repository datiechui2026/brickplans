# Cargo Ship MOC Blueprint Review: 4,200-Part Freighter

The short version: this cargo ship MOC blueprint is a strong piece of design work. The finished model measures 62 cm long and 28 cm tall, uses roughly 4,200 pieces, and captures the classic container-ship silhouette. Expect about 12 hours of building. It suits experienced ship builders best — if you are new to hulls, start with a smaller vessel first and come back to this one.

## Exterior Accuracy

The hull is built in dark blue and white, and the deck is fully equipped: superstructure, bridge, funnel and a container crane are all present, and the containers can be lifted off and repositioned. The rake of the bow and the height of the waterline are both scaled to match a real ship, so the model reads convincingly from a distance. The one letdown is the stern — the propeller sits hidden behind the hull, so that detail is largely lost to the viewer.

## Build Experience

The instructions are split into eight numbered step bags and move in a logical order: keel and hull bottom, then hull sides, then deck, then superstructure. Shaping the curved bottom takes patience, and some of the curved elements fit tightly — keep a brick separator within reach to save your fingertips. The manual itself is clearly drawn: no step asks for more than 20 parts at once and nothing is skipped. The roughly 12-hour build time is mostly eaten up by the repeated container modules rather than by anything technically difficult.

## Part Quality

The parts come from a high-precision Chinese factory, and the results are good: clutch is firm and the sprue marks are cleanly finished. Printed elements include the ship's name and the portholes, which lift the overall look considerably. Among the roughly 4,200 pieces there are a handful of rare curved elements, and buying those individually later is expensive — take an inventory before you start, and contact the seller for replacements straight away if anything is missing.

## Value for Money

Blueprint plus parts comes to about 1,200 yuan, which works out at under 0.3 yuan per piece — mid-to-upper territory for ship MOCs, but reasonable. Compare that with finished display models of the same size, which routinely cost 2,000 yuan or more, and self-building wins clearly on value. You also get to repaint or modify the model however you like.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this cargo ship MOC blueprint suitable for beginners?**
A: Not really. At 4,200 pieces and around 12 hours, it is a demanding first project. Build a ship model under 3,000 pieces first, get comfortable with curved hull techniques, then come back to this one.

**Q: How much does the whole cargo ship MOC cost?**
A: Blueprint plus parts runs about 1,200 yuan, and promotional pricing sometimes drops it below 1,000 yuan. The blueprint on its own is around 30 yuan; you can supply the parts from your own stock or order them individually from the parts list.

**Q: Can the finished freighter go in the water?**
A: No. This is a display model with no waterproofing and no reserved motor compartment. If you want a model that actually sails, look for a dedicated RC ship building set instead.

**Q: Where can I buy a legitimate copy of the blueprint?**
A: Digital versions are sold on brickplan.cn and the mainstream MOC platforms. Check the seller's reputation carefully and avoid scanned copies or files with missing pages.
