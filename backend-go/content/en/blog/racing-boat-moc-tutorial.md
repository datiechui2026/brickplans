# Racing Boat MOC Tutorial: Catamaran Hull and Rowing Linkage

Bottom line first: around 700 parts and a 200 yuan budget will get you a 40 cm racing boat, 8 studs wide, built on a twin-pontoon hull with a four-oar linked rowing mechanism that actually works in the water. A first-time builder should plan on about 3 hours. This tutorial splits the build into four stages: hull, seating, oar mechanism, and trim.

## Step 1: Build the Catamaran Hull

The whole model depends on the two pontoons being exactly symmetrical. Lay the base from 4x8 technic bricks, then build each pontoon from 2x6 curved slope bricks, stacking five layers from bow to stern and pulling in 1 stud per layer. That gives you the streamlined profile: a pointed bow and a flat stern. Four 12L cross axles run across the central bridge and lock the two pontoons into one hull.

Watch the part count. Each pontoon needs to use the same number of pieces, roughly 180 per side. If one side ends up heavier, the boat will list that way the moment it touches the water, which is the most common mistake builders make on this model. When the deck is finished, press down on both ends of the bridge. If anything flexes, fix it before moving on.

## Step 2: Lay Out the Seats

At 8 studs wide the hull takes four seats side by side, two per side in mirrored pairs. Build the seat surface from a 2x2 tile and use a 1x2 plate as the backrest. Leave a 2-stud gap between the front and rear rows so minifigure legs can bend naturally and rest on the hull floor. Raise every seat to 3 brick layers so the seat tops sit flush with the upper edge of the hull, which puts the rower's arms at the most efficient angle for the pull. Build one seat each in red, white and blue so the crew is easy to tell apart in photos.

## Step 3: Build the Four-Oar Rowing Linkage

This is the mechanical core of the boat and the step that eats the most time. Mount a 16L driveshaft under the central bridge and place the four oar mounts in pairs on either side of it, each driven by a meshing pair of 8-tooth gears. Turn the hand crank at the stern and all four oars sweep forward and pull back together, perfectly in sync.

Test the mechanism dry before you mount a single oar. If the gears grind or stutter, the axle centres are not in line, so raise or lower a mount by half a stud and try again. Set aside 40 minutes for this step and do not rush it.

## Step 4: Trim the Hull Edges and Fix the Oars

Run 1x3 curved slope bricks around the full top edge of the hull, swapping in 1x1 round corner bricks at the corners. The trim smooths the silhouette and keeps the edge from scratching your hands.

Each oar uses a 2-stud thin plate as the shaft and a blade piece as the face. Push the shaft through the hole in the oar mount, then cap the end with a 1x1 round plate as a stop so the oar cannot slide out mid-stroke. Finish with a 2x4 rudder piece at the stern for sharper turning. Once everything is on, do a 10-minute water test: four oars moving in sync and no water inside the hull means you are done.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this boat really go in the water?**
A: Yes. As long as the gear train is locked with cross axles and the oar mount holes line up, short sessions in shallow water are fine. Keep it out of choppy water, and if it does take on water, take it apart and dry it immediately so the cross axles do not rust and stiffen up.

**Q: Can the 700 parts be turned into a single-seat rowing boat?**
A: Yes. Remove two oar mounts and cut the seating down to two seats, which saves about 80 parts and lets you narrow the hull by 2 studs. Swap the 16L driveshaft for a 12L one and leave the rest of the structure alone. The conversion takes about half an hour.

**Q: Can a beginner finish it in 3 hours?**
A: Most first-timers land between 2.5 and 3.5 hours, and a first attempt at the gear train can add 30 minutes on its own. Sort the 700 parts into five colour groups in trays before you start; that cuts search time by roughly 40%.

**Q: Is a 200 yuan budget enough?**
A: Yes. At brickplan's average of 0.3 yuan per part, 700 parts comes to about 210 yuan, and gears and cross axles account for roughly 40 yuan of that. If you already own technic parts, you can bring it in under 150 yuan.
