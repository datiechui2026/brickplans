# Brick Assembly Step-by-Step: From Unboxing to Finished Build

Open a box with a thousand-plus pieces and the first reaction is usually paralysis — where do you even start? A clear, repeatable process solves most of that. What follows is the full workflow in the order you actually use it: unboxing, sorting, reading the instructions, building, and the final check.

## Preparation Before You Build

A good start is half the work. Do these things first and everything downstream gets easier.

### 1. Unbox and Check the Parts

- Work through the parts list (BOM) bag by bag and confirm the counts. If something is missing, contact the seller or platform right away.
- Do a rough sort by colour and by shape into several storage boxes or shallow trays.
- Keep the original box and instructions — you will want them for display or resale later.

### 2. Tools and Workspace

- Get a brick separator. It lifts wrongly placed parts safely instead of damaging the studs.
- Build on a flat, well-lit table, and lay down a build mat so small parts do not roll onto the floor and vanish.
- Load a high-resolution copy of the instructions on your phone, or keep a magnifier handy, so fine details stay readable.

## Three Things to Get Right When Reading Instructions

The instructions are the core reference for the whole build. Reading them correctly is half the battle.

### 1. Matching Colours

- Every part in the instructions is labelled with a number and a colour. Match both against the actual piece, one to one.
- Dark parts are hard to tell apart on a printed page. Use the part number rather than relying on colour alone.

### 2. Build Order and Orientation

- Each step highlights the newly added parts in a new colour while everything already built is shown in grey.
- Watch the stud direction and the insertion point. A part flipped the wrong way usually blocks the next few steps instead of failing immediately.

### 3. Finish One Step Before Starting the Next

- Complete each numbered step before moving on. Do not dump every same-colour part in at once.
- Build the habit of assembling a step and then verifying it. This cuts rework dramatically.

## The Six-Step Building Process

This sequence works for the overwhelming majority of sets.

1. Work through the numbered steps in order and check your work after each number.
2. For large structures, pre-build small sub-assemblies first, then combine them — it is far easier to adjust.
3. Symmetrical sections, such as the two sides of a fuselage, should be built in parallel so the details stay consistent.
4. Any moving part involving an axle or gear should be tested for free movement immediately after assembly.
5. If the instructions do not match reality, step back one stage and find the error. Never force a part in.
6. When the model is complete, compare it against the finished photo and add any decorative parts you skipped.

## Advanced Techniques for MOC Builders

Once official sets feel routine and you want to try harder MOC work, the same habits carry over.

- **Reverse engineering**: build an official model first, study how the key structures are held together, then improvise from there.
- **Modular design**: split your build into a base, a main body and detail modules, finish each on its own, then join them.
- **Part reuse**: break down idle sets and store everything in one sorted inventory so your own designs have a parts pool to draw from.

## Common Problems and How to Avoid Them

- **A part is facing the wrong way**: do not yank it. Use a brick separator and lever it gently from the side to avoid scratches.
- **Missing or wrong parts**: photograph the evidence, then contact after-sales support. Most platforms replace parts free of charge.
- **Instructions are hard to read**: enlarge them or open the electronic manual on the official site, which is sharper and clearer.
- **Loose connections**: check for a missed or misplaced part, then press the joint firmly back together.

## Final Thoughts

With this process in hand, assembly stops being a chore and becomes the creative part. From unboxing and sorting through reading the drawings to the last full inspection, every stage has a rule you can follow. Finishing a model you built yourself is a feeling nothing else quite matches.
