# Leaning Tower of Pisa MOC Review: 668 Pieces, 5.5° Lean

Bottom line first: this Leaning Tower of Pisa MOC uses 668 pieces, builds to roughly 42 cm tall with a 20 cm base diameter, works out to about 1:140 scale, and takes around 6 hours to assemble. The instruction file costs 128 yuan. The lean is achieved with clever offset wedge layers rather than a hidden frame, the model reads at about 9/10 for accuracy, and it makes a great first landmark build or desktop display piece. Strong recommendation.

## 1. How the Lean Is Built

The whole point of the tower is the tilt. This design skips the hidden support arm entirely. Instead, each storey of the tower body shifts about 0.5 cm off the one below it, and across six levels that accumulates into the real 5.5° lean. The layers are locked together with 1×6 bricks running crosswise, which holds the offset in place while keeping the whole thing rigid.

The shaft is mostly cylindrical, with 12 columns wrapping each level, and it uses two main colours — white and light beige — to suggest marble. The belfry on top is a separate module and detaches, which makes it easy to add lighting inside later. That is a genuinely nice touch.

## 2. Build Experience and Part Reuse

Six hours is a friendly length for a newcomer, and the manual runs to only 18 steps, each with a 3D view. The parts are mostly standard bricks and cylindrical elements, so repetition is high without ever feeling tedious. Of the 668 pieces, roughly 55% can be reused — buy a few extra copies of the parts list and you can expand the model into a larger Roman plaza scene.

On size, 42 cm sits comfortably on a bookshelf or a desk, and the footprint is under half a sheet of A4 paper, so it will not swallow space the way a large-scale landmark does. Sourcing parts from the brickplan.cn parts list works out to about 0.3 yuan per piece, or roughly 200 yuan in total parts cost.

## 3. Price and Value

The instructions are priced at 128 yuan on the brickplan.cn platform and include step-by-step animation plus a full parts list. If you would rather skip the sourcing work, a complete kit is available for about 420 yuan. Against comparable tower MOCs that run past 1,000 pieces, this one delivers noticeably more display value per unit of shelf space — especially for builders on a tight budget or trying a landmark subject for the first time.

Scores: design 9.0, structure 8.2, build experience 8.8, value 9.4. Overall, this is one of the rare small-format MOCs that pairs a low price with a high degree of finish, and it has no obvious weak spot.

## FAQ

**Q: With a lean like that, will it fall over once it is built?**

A: No. Each level shifts about 0.5 cm for a total of 5.5°, but the base is widened and thickened, so the centre of gravity stays inside the base circle. It stands stably in normal display conditions, and a shake test showed it holds roughly 2 kg of load.

**Q: Is a 600-plus-piece MOC worth buying instructions for, and is the price fair?**

A: Instructions are 128 yuan and loose parts about 200 yuan, so total spend stays under 330 yuan. Against official sets of the same type at 500 yuan and up, the accuracy here is higher, so it is well worth it.

**Q: I am a first-time builder — can I finish it in 6 hours?**

A: Absolutely. The manual has only 18 steps with 3D views for each. A newcomer will take about 7–8 hours; an experienced builder can finish in 5–6. It works well as a first landmark MOC.
