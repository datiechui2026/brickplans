# Which Glue to Use for LEGO MOCs: A Beginner's Guide

The short answer: it depends on what the model is for. Use model-specific glue or cyanoacrylate (superglue) for permanent display pieces, hot glue where you plan to take the build apart later, and acrylic-based adhesives as the default for ABS parts. Never use solvent-based glues that attack plastic.

Plenty of builders hit the same wall: the MOC is finished, but the structure feels loose. Should you glue it or not? Choosing the wrong adhesive ranges from a white haze on the surface to parts that are outright eaten away — and a single retired brick can be ruined for good. The approach below comes from hands-on testing shared by hundreds of builders in the brickplan community.

**Step 1: Decide what the build is for.** Work out first whether the model is a permanent display piece or something you will keep rebuilding. Permanent displays can take a strong adhesive without worry. Builds you plan to modify should use hot glue or sticky putty instead, so you can take them apart and rebuild later.

**Step 2: Match the glue to the material.** Almost all LEGO parts are ABS plastic, so reach for an acrylic-based adhesive (cyanoacrylate superglue is the common choice) or a model glue made specifically for ABS. Most model glues priced under 20 yuan will do the job, but avoid anything containing benzene-based solvents — those will corrode the surface of the part.

**Step 3: Apply small dots of glue.** Pick up the glue with a toothpick or a needle tip and place it on the inside of the part, at the clutch points. Keep each application to 1-2 drops. Spreading glue over a large area is more likely to squeeze out, and once it reaches a visible face it is very hard to clean off, which hurts the look of the finished model.

**Step 4: Hold the part and let it cure.** Press the part in place with your fingers for around 30 seconds. Superglue reaches about 80% strength within 5 minutes, but full curing takes 24 hours. Do not move the model while it cures, or parts can shift and take on a permanent warp.

**Step 5: Test before you commit.** Take 2-3 parts you do not use often, glue them, and check for hazing or corrosion before you touch the real model. As a reference point, a mid-sized MOC of around 2,000 parts takes roughly 1 hour to glue at all the key joints.

One important warning: minifigures, transparent parts and chrome-plated parts should not be glued. Transparent elements tend to fog when glued, a minifigure that is glued solid can never be swapped out, and the coating on chrome parts is destroyed by adhesive. These three are the mistakes builders make most often.

## FAQ

**Q: Which glue holds a LEGO MOC most securely?**
A: Cyanoacrylate superglue and ABS-specific model glue give the strongest bond. Once cured, the strength is close to that of the part itself, which makes them suitable for permanent display models. Use only small dots, and after a 24-hour cure the joint reaches maximum strength — normal handling will not pull it apart.

**Q: Does superglue corrode LEGO parts?**
A: Standard cyanoacrylate superglue does not corrode ABS parts, but low-quality products may contain extra solvents. Buy from a reputable brand, test on a scrap part first, and stop immediately if you see hazing or softening — switch to an ABS-specific glue instead.

**Q: Can I use glue on transparent LEGO parts?**
A: Not recommended. Glue on transparent parts very easily produces a white haze and bubbles, which ruins the light transmission. For transparent elements, use sticky putty or hot glue, or rely on the part's own clutch geometry. If you really must glue them, use as little as possible.

**Q: Can a glued MOC still be taken apart?**
A: Builds joined with hot glue or sticky putty can be taken apart; builds joined with superglue essentially cannot. Cyanoacrylate cures to a very high strength, and forcing it apart usually damages the parts, so use a reversible adhesive for anything you plan to rebuild and save the strong glue for permanent displays.

**Q: Is it worth gluing an entire MOC?**
A: It is more worthwhile to glue only the load-bearing joints and the spots that work loose. Gluing a whole model wastes time and adhesive. On a 3,000-piece build, gluing roughly 50 key points takes about 40 minutes and gives essentially the same result as gluing everything, while leaving room for small adjustments later.
