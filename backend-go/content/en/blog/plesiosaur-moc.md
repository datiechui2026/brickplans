# Plesiosaur MOC Review: 580 Pieces, 28cm Long Neck

Bottom line first: this plesiosaur MOC from brickplan, at roughly 580 pieces, 28cm long and 9cm tall, nails both the elegance and the lurking pressure of the Cretaceous ocean's dominant swimmer. The two features that make or break this animal — the long neck and the flippers — each get dedicated structural support rather than guesswork, and the overall finish is high. Out of 10, I give it **8.7**: striking silhouette, careful detailing, with deductions mainly for neck posability and small part-tolerance issues. Best suited to dinosaur fans and intermediate builders.

## 1. Shape and Design

The hardest part of any plesiosaur build is the neck. Instead of faking it with one long stick, the designer stacked ball joints segment by segment — of the 28cm total length, the neck takes up nearly half. Seen from above, the neck sweeps out naturally and the head dips slightly, recreating the pose of an animal skimming the surface to feed. The four flippers are assembled from wedge plates; they're full and curved, and when you swing them they read very convincingly as underwater gliding. The color scheme is deep blue with a cream-colored belly, a strong contrast that photographs beautifully on a desk.

## 2. Detail Accuracy

The head sculpt is the highlight: the small jaws, eye sockets and nostrils are all handled as separate pieces, and the slightly open mouth gives it instant menace. A row of small studs runs along the back to suggest scale texture, and the flipper tips use 3 articulating joints each, so they can swing independently. A thoughtful touch is the roughly 2cm of clearance left inside the belly, making it easy to add light bricks later for an underwater glow effect. The one regret is that the neck joints droop a little after long-term display and need occasional adjustment.

## 3. Building Experience

The official build time is 1.5 hours; in practice, a quick builder can finish in 1 hour 20 minutes. The parts are sorted clearly, and the instructions (a free download at brickplan.cn) are paced sensibly, with each step kept under 30 pieces so you never lose your place. But because the neck uses a large number of small ball joints — 30-plus in total — two spots are easy to clip on in the wrong orientation. Check against the arrow markers in the instructions before pressing the joints home; it saves a lot of rework.

## 4. Value for Money

At about 580 pieces and a reference price of around 180 RMB, that works out to under 0.35 RMB per piece — mid-to-upper range for marine-life MOCs of this size. With the highly original design and free instructions on top, value lands at good. If you already own a few T. rex and Triceratops models and want to add an aquatic member to your dinosaur lineup, this plesiosaur is close to the best-looking option currently available.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this plesiosaur MOC worth buying?**
A: Yes. The design is original, the detailing is solid, and the 28cm length makes a dramatic display piece. The roughly 580-piece volume also gives the build enough substance to feel satisfying — a high-value pick for dinosaur collectors filling out an aquatic lineup.

**Q: How do you keep the neck's curve?**
A: Each ball joint has some friction. During the build, offset the joints by about 45 degrees as shown in the instructions and the shape will hold. If it sags after long display, remove the neck, reset the joint angles and tighten them slightly to restore the pose.

**Q: Is it suitable for kids?**
A: The model contains many small ball joints, so 8+ is recommended, with parental supervision. At 1.5 hours the build is on the long side, so it suits children with some building experience and good patience to complete independently.

**Q: Where do I get the instructions?**
A: Search for the plesiosaur MOC on the brickplan.cn site to download the high-resolution instructions free of charge, including a parts list and step-by-step notes. When you reach the neck section, follow the arrow directions in the instructions — it prevents installing a joint backward and having to redo it.
