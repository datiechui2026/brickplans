# Ship MOC Blueprint Review: Sail, Steam and Container

## Introduction

Nautical themes hold a particular place in the brick MOC (My Own Creation) world. Whether it is a classic wooden sailing ship or an Industrial Revolution-era steamer, ship blueprints give builders a very different kind of experience from the usual castle or city project. This review looks at three selected **ship MOC blueprints**, breaking each one down by parts inventory, build difficulty and how the finished model actually looks on the shelf.

## Product Overview

The three blueprints reviewed here cover three distinct styles:

1. **Classic pirate sailing ship** — modelled on Golden Age pirate vessels, a three-masted design with adjustable sails and stern cabin windows.
2. **Victorian paddle steamer** — a side-wheeler whose two oversized paddle wheels are the headline feature, paired with a retro funnel and passenger cabin.
3. **Modern container freighter** — minimalist in style, with a long, slender hull and container modules lined up neatly on deck for a very contemporary look.

All three blueprints come from well-known community designers. The parts requirements are mostly standard bricks and plates, and the small number of specialised pieces can be handled with substitutions.

## Build Difficulty

| Model | Part count | Estimated time | Difficulty |
|------|--------|----------|----------|
| Pirate sailing ship | 1,200+ | 4-5 hours | ★★★☆☆ |
| Paddle steamer | 2,000+ | 6-8 hours | ★★★★☆ |
| Container freighter | 800+ | 3-4 hours | ★★☆☆☆ |

Beginners should start with the container freighter. Its structure is comparatively regular, the hull uses very little SNOT (Studs Not On Top) technique, and the build flows smoothly from start to finish. The paddle steamer's wheel transmission, by contrast, is what advanced builders will enjoy most: the internal gears have to mesh with precise alignment, and the difficulty steps up noticeably.

## Parts Review

### Pirate Sailing Ship

The palette is dominated by dark brown, tan and white, with curved bricks used heavily to shape the hull's lines. The masts call for long cylindrical pieces; if you do not have the original parts to hand, several shorter cylinders stacked together work as a substitute. The sails are cloth, which feels good in hand but collects dust quickly.

### Paddle Steamer

Dark red and black set the tone, recreating the weight of an iron hull. **The paddle wheel assembly is the highlight of the whole blueprint** — a crank and connecting-rod mechanism keeps the paddles rotating in sync, and the smoothness of that rotation is genuinely satisfying. The funnel combines slope bricks with round bricks for a detailed finish.

### Container Freighter

Grey and blue dominate, and the coloured containers on deck (each one 6-12 pieces) are designed as standard modules. The rake of the bow is achieved with wedge plates, while the propeller assembly at the stern is small and neatly detailed.

## Building Experience

### Pirate Sailing Ship

The keel is the part that really tests your patience. The blueprint uses a layer-by-layer stacking method, narrowing gradually from the bottom of the hull up to the deck, so you need to read each layer's position carefully. The stern windows and the cabin interior are not large, but there is room enough for a small table and a nautical chart. Once it is complete, the sail arrangement photographs beautifully — this one is made for display shots.

### Paddle Steamer

This is the most rewarding of the three. **The paddle wheels are the climax of the build** — when the paddles on both sides are seated and turn freely, the sense of achievement is hard to describe. The instrument panel in the wheelhouse, the portholes and staircase inside the cabin: every detail shows the designer's care. The one thing to watch is that some small parts have similar colours that are easy to confuse on the instructions, so build in a well-lit space.

### Container Freighter

The most relaxed build of the three, ideal for unwinding. The modular containers can be recoloured freely, which gives builders some room to put their own stamp on the model. The hull is solid and holds up well to handling.

## Finished Models

The three ships measure as follows when complete:

- Pirate sailing ship: approx. 35cm long × 28cm high
- Paddle steamer: approx. 45cm long × 22cm high
- Container freighter: approx. 50cm long × 15cm high

Placed side by side, these two very different styles of vessel look like a conversation between nautical civilisations from different eras. For display, a blue baseplate standing in for the sea makes the whole thing read much better.

## Verdict

Each of these three **ship MOC blueprints** has its own strengths. The pirate sailing ship suits builders who like a vintage look, the paddle steamer suits advanced builders chasing mechanical detail, and the container freighter is the clear entry point. Broadly speaking, nautical MOCs have a natural advantage in sheer looks: the streamlined hull profile and the vertical elements — masts, funnels — make them natural desktop display pieces.

If you are looking for **ship MOC blueprints**, pick one of these three and start your own nautical build. Whether for a personal collection or for building together with kids, all three deliver plenty of building enjoyment.
