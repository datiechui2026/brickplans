# Brick Mars Base MOC: Airlock, Dome and Red Terrain Guide

What makes a space-themed **brick Mars base MOC** work is believability. It has to read as a fully functioning research outpost, yet still carry the emptiness of a red planet. This guide breaks the build down piece by piece, from the airlock and the dome habitat to the solar array and the ground colours, so you can turn a limited parts supply into a base that looks lived in, and pick up a few tricks for using transparent parts along the way.

## 1. The Airlock: the Seal Is the Soul of the Base

A Mars base lives or dies on airtightness, so the **airlock** is a structure you have to explain visually. Use two openable double-layer door pieces to suggest an entry chamber: a red outer gate, a grey inner gate, and a short sealed compartment between them. Add a round transparent viewing window and a red warning-light piece on the side. Run a pressure line across the top, connecting both walls with a silver hose piece, and the model instantly reads as "pressurising". Build the gates as moving assemblies if you can. Every time they swing open, a minifigure walks in or out of the base and the scene tells its own story.

## 2. The Dome: Balancing Transparent Parts and Frame

The dome habitat is the visual centre of the base and the hardest part of a **brick Mars base MOC**. Assemble transparent curved pieces into a hemisphere, then run white or silver long bars outward from the centre to form a radial frame. That transparent-plus-frame combination is the classic dome look. Inside, place a lab bench, an incubator and computer screen pieces so that busy science minifigures are visible straight through the shell. Close the dome base with ring pieces, and wrap a band of blue warning stripes around the outside to flag it as a high-security research zone.

If you do not have enough curved transparent pieces, swap the shell for transparent flat plates as large glazed windows. The result holds up just as well.

## 3. Solar Array and Comms: Making the Base Feel Alive

Function is what sells a base, and a solar array is non-negotiable. Lay out photovoltaic panels from dark blue plates, mount them on rotating bracket pieces above a base, and line them up facing the "sun". If you worry about keeping the angle consistent, draw a sun-direction marker on the baseplate. Build the comms dish from a round dish piece plus an antenna rod, with a red indicator light on top.

Beside the array, park a Mars rover and a set of storage tanks: white round barrel pieces with a silver waistline read nicely as fuel reserves. Once these functional details are in place, the model stops being a display piece and starts looking like an outpost that is actually running.

## 4. Martian Terrain and Overall Colour Scheme

The base colour of Mars is **rust red**. Lay red-brown and orange-red plates in staggered rows to build rolling dunes, scatter a small amount of dark red studs across the surface as grit, then dot a few grey pieces around as exposed rock layers. The barren feel arrives immediately.

Keep the architecture itself white, silver and grey, with transparent parts for the dome. Red sand against cool-toned buildings is the colour logic that makes a Mars base MOC stand out. Watch the balance: hold the ratio of "base white" to "surface red" at roughly four to six, which is the most comfortable proportion to look at.

## 5. Beginner Tips and Parts List

Start small: build the airlock plus a single dome module first. Once you have the double-layer gate and the transparent dome structure under control, expand into the solar array and the surrounding terrain.

Parts list:

- Plenty of white, silver and grey plates for the habitat shells
- Transparent curved or flat pieces for the dome and viewing windows
- Red-brown and orange-red plates plus dark red studs for the ground
- Dark blue photovoltaic plates and a handful of silver hose pieces
- Accessories: Mars rover, storage tanks, warning lights, scientific instruments

Store transparent parts separately to avoid scratches. Sci-fi builds in particular cannot survive a scuffed, cloudy canopy.

Airlock, dome, solar array, rust-red ground: get the sealed structure, the transparent shell and the warm-cool colour contrast right, and this **brick Mars base MOC** will have both sci-fi tension and research-station logic.
