# Flower and Bird Market MOC: A 3,200-Part Street Scene Guide

Here is the short version before we start: this flower and bird market MOC takes roughly 3,200 parts and about 300 CNY, finishes at around 36 × 24 × 16 cm, and runs 9–11 hours of building time. Three things carry it — pack the flower stalls with color, get the birdcage bars right, and give the fish tank real water shine. Hang a printed sign over the gateway and the street-life atmosphere arrives on its own. For a street-scene subject, it is one of the better value builds around. The instructions live on brickplan.cn; search "flower and bird market" to find them.

## Step 1: The Street Gateway and Flower Stalls

Build the gateway first: two dark brown columns carry a crossbeam, a printed sign element hangs from it, and a street lamp stands on each side.

Then the stalls, two or three of them lined along the street face. Light-colored plates make the counter surfaces; four short columns make the legs. Cover each counter with green leaf and foliage pieces so it reads as a dense flower bed, then push red, pink and yellow flower pieces into it — the denser the color, the better the stall looks. Put two flower buckets in front of each stall: dark cylindrical pieces with long-stem flower pieces standing in them, set at different heights so the eye keeps moving. That is where the color weight of the whole street comes from.

## Step 2: The Birdcages and the Fish Tank

The birdcage is the detail people notice. Stand brown thin bar pieces (the rail or fence type) in a vertical ring to form the cylinder, cap it with a round plate and a round stud with a hook, lay a light round plate in the bottom, and drop a small bird minifigure inside. The cage-bar look appears immediately. Hang two or three outside the door at staggered heights rather than in a neat row.

For the fish tank, box in transparent panels on all four sides. Lay transparent light blue pieces flat inside to read as water, scatter colored round studs on the floor as gravel, and run a ring of white pieces around the rim. Set one tank beside the flower stalls: the water shine and the flower colors lift each other, and it becomes the small scene people photograph first.

## Step 3: Street-Life Atmosphere Details

Pave the ground with gray plates and leave a lighter stone-slab path down the middle of the street. Line both sides with potted plants and bamboo baskets, the baskets built from brown rail pieces for the rims. Along the base of the wall, add a run of green wall: fill a backing panel with green foliage pieces and dot flower pieces through it here and there.

Minifigures do the storytelling, and this is where the build is won or lost. The flower seller holds a bouquet, a customer carries a birdcage, and a child crouches in front of the fish tank watching the fish. Those three groupings tell the whole market story without a word. Finish by hanging a string of red lanterns from the gateway and putting a few coin pieces on the stalls, and the everyday-market feel is complete.

## FAQ

**Q: How many parts does this MOC need, and what does it cost?**
A: About 2,800–3,600 parts and a budget of 260–340 CNY. Flowers and greenery eat most of it, and if you source the parts yourself from the list you can bring it in around 280 CNY, which is considerably cheaper than buying equivalent sets.

**Q: How do you get the birdcage bar effect?**
A: Stand brown thin rail pieces vertically in a ring to make the cylinder, add a round stud with a hook on top, lay a light round plate in the bottom, and put a small bird minifigure inside — the bar look appears at once. Make two or three and hang them by the door at different heights.

**Q: How is the water in the fish tank done?**
A: Box in transparent panels on four sides, lay transparent light blue pieces flat inside, put a few colored round studs at the bottom as gravel, and add a ring of white pieces around the rim. The water shine comes through clearly and photographs beautifully next to the flower stalls.

**Q: How is this different from a normal street-scene build?**
A: The market is mostly small stalls with no large building walls, so it leans harder on plant color and cage detail. The result has more layers and more minifigure interaction, which gives it a livelier, more photographic street feel.
