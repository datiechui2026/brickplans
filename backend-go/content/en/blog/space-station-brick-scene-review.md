# Space Station Brick Scene Review: Build Your Own Orbital Base

## Introduction

Space is a subject that sells itself, but plenty of sets still get it wrong — once you start adding module after module, the proportions drift. Chinese-designed space station brick scenes have multiplied over the past couple of years, so we built one all the way through on the table, working from the core module and lab module out to the solar panels and the docked spacecraft. Below we break the review into three parts: design, parts and the build itself.

## 1. Design Highlights of the Space Station Scene

### 1.1 Overall Structure

The first thing you judge a space station brick scene on is whether the overall structure holds together. The mainstream approach is modular, built from these core modules:

**Core module**: the hub of the station, usually containing the living quarters, the control centre and the docking ports. A good core module uses a round or hexagonal cross-section, combining curved bricks and angled slope bricks to imitate the cylindrical look of a real pressurised module.

**Lab module**: fitted out with scientific equipment, with viewing windows made from transparent bricks and lab accessories such as microscopes and test-tube racks inside.

**Docking module**: multi-directional ports that can take a cargo ship and a crewed spacecraft. The multi-port layout is the key detail that makes the station feel functional.

**Solar panels**: large gold or dark blue panels spread out on both sides of the station, and visually the most eye-catching part of the whole build.

### 1.2 Detail Fidelity

On details, the better space station brick scenes almost always deliver these:

- **Interior living area**: sleeping pods, a dining table, exercise equipment and other daily fittings, so the station reads as a home for the crew and not just a workplace
- **External robotic arm**: a posable arm for outside work and cargo handling
- **Communications antennas**: a parabolic dish plus omnidirectional antennas to add a technical feel
- **Attitude control thrusters**: small thrusters mounted on the various modules

### 1.3 Creative Design

A few ideas in brick space scenes genuinely make you pause:

- **Rotating gravity ring**: a technic-driven ring that spins to simulate artificial gravity
- **Airlock**: an airlock door that opens, so astronaut minifigures can be posed on a spacewalk
- **Plant cultivation room**: a growing system built inside a transparent module, showing off space agriculture

## 2. Part Quality

### 2.1 Brick Precision

The space station scene we reviewed uses roughly 1,800 parts, covering basic bricks, technic elements and specialised pieces. On precision:

- **Basic bricks**: accurate dimensions, compatible with LEGO standards, so they mix into an existing brick collection
- **Clutch power**: medium to firm — the finished structure stays solid and does not come apart when you move it
- **Transparent parts**: the clear bricks are properly transparent, and the gold finish on the solar panels looks excellent

### 2.2 Specialised Parts

The scene leans heavily on specialised elements:

- **Curved bricks**: used for the module shells; the curvature is accurate and the seams between pieces are small
- **Transparent window frames**: they fit tightly against the main bricks with a good seal
- **Technic elements**: used for the robotic arm and the rotating structure, and they turn smoothly
- **Printed parts**: control panels and markings are printed rather than stickered, so the colour holds up and does not wear off

## 3. The Building Experience

### 3.1 Instruction Design

The instructions directly shape how a space MOC scene feels to build:

- **Step breakdown**: eight main steps, roughly 200-250 parts each — logical, and never dragged out
- **Diagram clarity**: colour printing with high contrast, and new parts highlighted in each step
- **Part list**: a complete inventory at the front of the manual for checking everything off

### 3.2 Build Time

For an intermediate builder, the whole space station brick scene takes about 6-8 hours. We'd suggest spreading it over 2-3 days at 2-3 hours a session — long enough to enjoy it, short enough to avoid fatigue.

### 3.3 Best Moments Along the Way

- Assembling the solar panel deployment mechanism, where the mechanical linkage design is a genuine highlight
- Finishing the core module interior, where the little household fittings raise a smile
- The final assembly, when the separate modules join into one complete station — a real sense of achievement

## 4. Display and Play

### 4.1 Visual Impact

Finished, the station measures roughly 40cm x 30cm x 25cm, which takes up a reasonable amount of desk space. On display:

- **Overall shape**: strongly technical, clean silhouette, instantly recognisable
- **Colour scheme**: a white body with gold solar panels and blue accents — a professional palette
- **Depth**: from core module to lab module, from solar panels to the docked spacecraft, there is plenty of layering

### 4.2 Playability

- **Moving parts**: the robotic arm poses, the solar panels fold and unfold, and the docking hatches open and close
- **Minifigures**: 3-4 astronaut figures are included, enough to stage working scenes on the station
- **Expansion**: the modular design lets you add more function modules of your own

## 5. Pros and Cons

### 5.1 Pros

- Modular design with a clear structure and strong display presence
- High part quality and good compatibility
- Rich interior detail and convincing living scenes
- Moving structures add play value

### 5.2 Cons

- Some transparent parts pick up fingerprints during the build
- The solar panel connectors can loosen after repeated folding
- Fitting an LED light kit requires your own modification

## 6. Buying Advice

This space station brick scene suits:

- **Space fans**: builders with a strong interest in space subjects
- **Scene builders**: anyone who enjoys putting together large dioramas
- **Parents and kids**: a good build to complete together, with plenty to learn along the way
- **MOC builders**: a useful parts bank and inspiration source for space MOCs

## Closing Thoughts

Half the fun of a space station brick scene is in the building and half is in the display: the modular structure can keep growing, and the robotic arm and solar panels are at their most enjoyable when they move. If space is your theme, this one is a safe buy.
