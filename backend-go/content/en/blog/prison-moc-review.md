# Brick Prison MOC Review: Details, Play Value and Build Tips

Among the many subjects covered by brick street scenes, a prison is an odd one out. It has none of the warmth of a shop and none of the lived-in clutter of a house, yet its strict sense of order and story potential draws in a lot of builders. I recently finished a complete brick prison MOC, working from loose parts up to the finished model and looking closely at both the exterior and the interior. Here is what I found.

## Tall Walls, Guard Towers and the Gate: Accuracy That Surprises

What impressed me most is how closely this brick prison MOC follows real prison architecture. The facade is built from large runs of grey and white bricks, and the walls keep a classic mortar-joint texture. The razor wire along the top of the perimeter is suggested with transparent pieces and thin rods, and the effect reads as genuinely convincing. The four corner guard towers are raised on a higher level, each with observation windows and a searchlight, plus a simple rain eave on the roof.

The main gate is one of the highlights of the whole model: a double-layer gate with a metal look, paired with an access-control room, and a printed "POLICE" sign above the lintel with crisp detail. The overall presence is a close match for a real prison entrance.

## Cell Bars and Police Details: More Play Value Than Expected

If the exterior is the face of the build, the interior is its soul. The cell block in this brick prison MOC uses a modular structure across two floors. Every cell has a barred door and a window that opens and closes, with the bars built from layered black long bars that let just the right amount of light through. The cells are also furnished with mini pieces: a bed, a small table and a toilet, all reproduced at a high level of accuracy.

The police details are what surprised me most. The duty room holds a walkie-talkie, a police cap and a document folder. At the end of the corridor there is a fingerprint-access door and a surveillance camera, and even the exercise yard comes with a water dispenser and a bench. These touches make the brick prison MOC more than a static display piece. You can set up escape attempts, arrests or routine patrol scenes however you like, which gives it far more play value than an ordinary street set of the same size.

## Accuracy, Play Value and Building Advice

Taken as a whole, this brick prison MOC strikes a good balance between accuracy and play value. The bricks clutch firmly and the wall structure is solid, so repeated disassembly and rebuilding does not loosen it. Printed pieces are plentiful and stickers are used only sparingly, which cuts down on the annoyance of crooked decals.

On the building side, the walls and guard towers follow clear, logical steps and suit a confident beginner. The tiny furniture and police equipment inside the cells demand more patience, so they are a better fit for experienced builders who like digging into details. If you have a minifigure collection, put police officers, prisoners and guards inside together and park a police car in front of the gate, and the storytelling of the whole street scene appears immediately.

A few suggestions if you plan to pick this one up:

- Build the high walls layer by layer following the instructions, putting up the wall surfaces before adding the razor wire, so you avoid redoing work.
- The connectors on the guard towers and the access-control room are fairly tight. Use a brick separator when taking them apart to avoid scratching the brick surfaces.
- For a stronger street scene, place the brick prison MOC at the end of your row of buildings. The height contrast against shops and houses adds a richer visual layering.
