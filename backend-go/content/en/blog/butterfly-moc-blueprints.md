# Brick Butterfly MOC: Symmetrical Wing Building Guide

The short version: build one wing, then mirror it. Give the wings a double-layer face with transparent parts, and keep the main color count to three or fewer.

Butterflies are one of the most popular insect themes in brick building. The wings are light and colorful, and the model pairs well with flowers or a display frame. But butterflies have one specific difficulty: **symmetry**. The left and right wings must be exact mirrors. Add one stud too many or too few on one side, and the moment the wings open, the error shows. This guide to **brick butterfly MOC blueprints** covers choosing a plan, building symmetrically, and fixing the wings so you get that light, ready-to-fly look.

## Choosing a plan: start with how the wings open

Butterfly MOC blueprints vary a lot, so first check how the wings "open." There are three common styles: flat-spread, with wings level and horizontal, good for a specimen frame; raised, with wings lifted upward for a flying pose; and folded, with wings closed over the back, which is compact and saves parts. Beginners should pick flat-spread, since it is the simplest to build and display. Also confirm the blueprint marks the left and right mirror steps. A proper plan lists the left and right wings as separate steps so you cannot go wrong following along. On finished size, a butterfly with a wingspan of about 15 cm sits nicely on a desk.

## Symmetrical building: one side first, then mirror it

Symmetry is the make-or-break point of a brick butterfly MOC, and the trick is "build one side, then copy it." Build the left wing completely first, then work through the same steps for the right wing, comparing left against right after every layer to check that colors and positions mirror correctly. To cut down on errors, use parts that are distributed symmetrically: with the wing's centerline as the axis, keep the same number of bricks on the inner and outer sides, and place pattern elements in mirrored pairs of the same type. One more practical tip: lay both wings flat on the floor and confirm they match exactly before attaching them to the body. That is far easier to check than building in mid-air.

## Double-layer wings: where the lightness comes from

Butterfly wings look light because they are "thin and translucent." A double-layer wing structure works best: a base layer of tiles, then a top layer of transparent or open-framed parts forming the pattern, with a gap of one plate between the layers so light passes through and the wings instantly read as translucent. On pattern design, the forewing and hindwing can use different colors as long as the overall tone stays unified. Orange forewings with cream hindwings, for example, look far more harmonious than giving each wing its own separate color scheme. Finish the wing edges with curved bricks; a rounded outline reads as more delicate and insect-like than straight lines.

## Body and antennae: small details set the quality

The body is small but it is the finishing touch of a brick butterfly MOC. Build the torso from 2 to 3 layers of dark bricks in a spindle shape, slightly thicker in the middle and tapering at both ends, mounted horizontally at the seam between the two wings. Use a 1x1 round part for the head and small black round parts for the two compound eyes. For the antennae, bend two thin rod parts forward and upward, with a small round bead at each tip. The curve and length of the antennae must be symmetrical; that is the key to a convincing butterfly. Fix the wing-to-body connection with hinge parts or technic pins so the wings can be adjusted slightly in angle and posed in different open positions.

## Part advice: pairing transparent and smooth parts

Transparent parts are the key to quality in a brick butterfly MOC. Transparent tiles and transparent curved parts both let the wings "pass light," so stock several sizes if your budget allows. Tiles get used heavily too, on wing surfaces, base colors and edging. Keep the main colors to three or fewer and add dark outlines so the wing pattern stays crisp. ## FAQ

**Q: For a first brick butterfly MOC blueprint, which wing pose should I choose?**
A: Flat-spread, with wings level and horizontal. It is the simplest to build and display, and it fits straight into a specimen frame. Raised suits a flying pose, and folded saves the most parts.

**Q: How do I keep the wings perfectly symmetrical and avoid crooked wings?**
A: Build the left wing completely, then follow the same steps for the right, comparing colors and positions after each layer. You can also lay both wings flat on the floor before attaching them to the body.

**Q: How do I make the wings look light and translucent?**
A: Use a double-layer wing: a base of tiles, a top layer of transparent or open-framed parts for the pattern, and a gap of one plate between them so light passes through. Finish the edges with curved bricks.

**Q: What size is a brick butterfly, and how many transparent parts should I stock?**
A: A wingspan of about 15 cm is right for desktop display. Transparent tiles and transparent curved parts see heavy use; keep main colors to three or fewer with dark outlines for clearer patterns.
