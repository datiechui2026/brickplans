# Brick Parts Storage and Sorting: A Complete System

Emptying an entire bin to find one red 2x4 brick. Reaching the halfway point of a build and discovering the key part is missing. Finally finding it — and not having enough. I have been through all of it, and the root cause was always the same: the parts were never sorted properly in the first place.

Below are four things to fix — sorting methods, storage tools, a label system and ongoing upkeep — with one standard to meet: you can start today.

## Why Bother Sorting Your Brick Parts?

Plenty of builders figure that dumping everything into one big box and rummaging when needed is fine. For a small set of 50–100 pieces, it is. Once your collection passes 500, 1000 or 5000 parts, an unsorted bin stops being storage and becomes a disaster zone.

Sorting your parts properly pays off in concrete ways:

- **5–10x faster part retrieval**: from tearing the shelves apart to grabbing it on the first reach
- **A much better building experience**: no more broken rhythm while you hunt for a piece
- **No duplicate purchases**: you know exactly which parts you own and how many of each
- **Better part condition**: separated storage cuts the friction and collisions that scuff and dull parts

Many experienced builders in the [BrickPlans](https://brickplan.cn) community have shared their own storage setups — worth a look if you want more reference points.

## Sorting Methods for Brick Parts

### Sort by Color (Beginners)

The simplest approach: one container per color. It is intuitive and fast to set up, but parts of the same color in different sizes still end up mixed together, so the efficiency gain is limited once the collection grows.

**Who it suits**: beginners with 500–2000 parts
**Rating**: ★★★

### Sort by Part Type (Recommended)

The most recommended approach: group parts by type (bricks, plates, slopes, technic parts and so on), then subdivide by size within each type. This is the system most experienced builders run today.

**Who it suits**: intermediate builders with 2000–10000 parts
**Rating**: ★★★★★

### Hybrid: Type + Color (Advanced)

The most efficient method: sort by type first, then by color within each type. It combines the strengths of the previous two and gives the fastest retrieval, but it demands more storage space and more management effort.

**Who it suits**: veteran builders with more than 10000 parts
**Rating**: ★★★★

### Sort by MOC Project

For a specific MOC, pack that project's parts separately, then return them to the main collection by type when the build is finished. This works well for builders who regularly take on large creations.

**Who it suits**: builders who frequently work on large MOCs
**Rating**: ★★★★

## Choosing Storage Tools

### Drawer Units: The Top Pick

Drawer units are the most mainstream storage tool for brick parts right now. Their advantages:

- **Easy access**: pull a drawer open and take the part, no digging
- **Flexible sorting**: different drawer sizes hold different part sizes
- **Expandable**: modular designs stack as your collection grows

Recommended brands: IKEA's Kallax line paired with insert boxes, or dedicated parts storage brands such as Akro-Mils and Stack-On.

### Clear Storage Bins: For Large Parts

For oversized parts such as baseplates and large plates, or for **bulk storage of common parts**, clear bins are a good choice. The upside is capacity and low cost; the downside is that retrieval is slower than with drawers.

### Compartment Boxes: For Small Parts

The best option for small parts — 1x1 bricks, round plates, small technic pieces. One compartment holds one part type, neat and orderly. Choose models with **removable dividers** so you can resize compartments as needed.

### Zip Bags and Self-Seal Bags: Best Value

For builders on a tight budget, self-seal bags are the cheapest route. Bag parts of the same type, then drop the bags into a larger bin by category. Stick a label on each bag noting the part type and size.

## A Label System for Your Parts

### Why Labels Matter

A good label system can double the efficiency of your whole sorting setup. Even if you have not touched the parts for a month, labels get you to the right drawer fast.

### What Goes on a Label

Each label should carry:

- **Part type**: e.g. "2x4 brick"
- **Color**: e.g. "red" (use standard color names)
- **Quantity**: e.g. "×50" (optional, useful for inventory tracking)
- **Part number**: e.g. "3001" (BrickLink or LDraw number, for precise lookup)

### How to Make Labels

1. **Handwritten labels**: simplest, fine for a small collection
2. **Printed labels**: use a label printer for a clean, legible result. Brother and Seiko label printers are good options
3. **QR code labels**: an advanced route — scan the code to see part details, stock counts and usage history

## Advanced Techniques

### Tip 1: Build a Parts Inventory List

Before you start reorganizing, create an **electronic parts inventory**. Use Excel, Google Sheets or dedicated parts management software to record each part's name, number, color, quantity and storage location.

### Tip 2: Create a Fast-Access Zone

Keep your most-used parts — 2x4 bricks, 1x2 plates, 2x2 bricks — in the most convenient spot, such as a small compartment box on the desk. That cuts down on getting up mid-build.

### Tip 3: Pre-Sort Parts for Big MOC Projects

Before starting a large MOC, pull that project's parts out of the main collection and store them separately. The search space shrinks dramatically during the build, and efficiency climbs with it.

### Tip 4: Audit and Tidy on a Schedule

Every 3–6 months, do a **full sort and stock check**:

- Clear out surplus parts and fragments
- Update your inventory records
- Refine the sorting scheme (it may need adjusting as the collection grows)

### Tip 5: Use MOC Instructions to Plan Purchases

If you build MOCs often, export the parts list when you browse plans on [brickplan](https://brickplan.cn/zh/plans) and buy everything in one go — it saves about half the shipping compared with topping up piece by piece later. Check the list against your inventory and buy only what is missing, which saves money and storage space at the same time.

## FAQ

### Q: There are too many parts — where do I even start?

**A**: Start with "sort first, organize second." Separate everything by broad category (bricks, plates, technic parts and so on), then refine step by step. Do not try to finish in one day; working in batches keeps it manageable.

### Q: Are clear containers better than opaque ones?

**A**: Clear containers let you see the contents instantly, so they are recommended. If you are sensitive to light or your parts need to be kept dark, go with opaque.

### Q: Small parts always end up mixed together — what do I do?

**A**: Use compartment boxes with one part type per compartment. For very small parts like 1x1 round plates, add a soft liner at the bottom of each compartment to reduce collisions.

### Q: During a MOC, parts are scattered in different places and it slows me down.

**A**: Use a project tray. Before each step, pull the parts that step needs onto one tray. When the step is done, load the next step's parts. You only ever face one tray of parts at a time, which makes retrieval extremely fast.
