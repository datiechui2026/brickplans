# Penguin MOC Review: 428-Part Round-Body Build

The short version: this is a genuinely charming animal-themed MOC that uses 428 parts to capture a penguin's rounded silhouette surprisingly well, and its black-and-white parts use is textbook-level. The blueprint runs 96 steps, holds a 4.9/5 rating on the brickplan community, and works equally well as a desk display piece or a parent-and-child build.

## 1. Round Shape: Curved Slopes and Chamfered Pieces Working Together

The biggest risk with a penguin MOC is ending up with a blocky duck. This blueprint uses 6 kinds of curved bricks and 3 kinds of chamfered pieces to build the arcs of the body and head, with the belly stepped inward layer by layer using 1x2 curved slope bricks, so the overall outline reads round and natural. The head connects to the body through a 2x2 turntable, letting it rotate roughly 30 degrees left and right — that alone delivers most of the cute factor.

## 2. Black and White Parts: The Logic Behind the Ratio

Black to white across the model runs about 3:7. The back mixes deep black with light gray so it does not look flat, while the belly stays uniformly bright white for a clean read. Black and white pieces make up 85% of the parts list; the rest are accent pieces — an orange beak and pink flippers. The color logic is clear and easy to swap if you want a different palette.

## 3. Cute Details: Eyes, Wings and Flippers

The eyes use 1x1 round tiles with printed pupils, and combined with the slightly forward tilt of the head they give a permanent slightly-heartbroken expression from the front. The small wings stack two 1x3 slope bricks and can flap up and down a little. The flippers are orange 2x2 slope pieces, and standing stability is good — in testing it stayed upright on a 15-degree incline.

## 4. Build Difficulty and Kid-Friendliness

The full blueprint is 96 steps at an average of 4.5 parts per step. An adult finishes in about 1.5 hours, and a child aged 6 or over can complete most of it independently with a parent nearby. Color callouts in the instructions are clear; the only real sticking point is the stacking order of the curved belly bricks, and that step includes a magnified diagram, so following along will not lead you astray.

## 5. Blueprint and Buying Advice

The blueprint sells for 6.9 yuan on the brickplan platform and includes the PDF instructions, a parts list and 3 alternative expression options. Black and white parts are all common stock; sourcing the BOM costs roughly 60 yuan. If you already have plenty of black and white bricks at home, just pick from the list — you will barely spend anything extra.

## FAQ

**Q: How big is this penguin MOC?**

A: The finished model is about 14 cm tall and 10 cm wide — roughly the height of a medium coffee cup. It sits well on a desk, shelf or display cabinet, taking up little space without looking small.

**Q: Is it too hard for a child to build alone?**

A: No. With only a few parts per step across the 96 steps, the only spot needing adult help is the layered curved belly. A child aged 6 or over can finish independently in about 2 hours; building together takes roughly 1.5 hours.

**Q: Can it be rebuilt as a different-colored bird?**

A: Yes. The main structure is generic — swap the black and white parts for a yellow and blue palette and the penguin becomes an oriole. The brickplan blueprint page also offers 3 alternative expression options, so there is plenty of replay value.

**Q: Are missing parts a problem?**

A: About nine-tenths of the 428 parts are common standard pieces, and black and white bricks are in most people's stock. The BOM lists the part number and quantity for every element, so check it against your collection before building and missing pieces are basically a non-issue.
