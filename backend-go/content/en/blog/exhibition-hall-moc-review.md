# Modular Exhibition Hall MOC Review: A Gallery You Can Re-curate

Cultural buildings are a smart subject for a MOC. A museum is built on empty space — the emptier the hall, the more premium it reads — which makes it far harder to get right than a detail-packed little shop. For this review we built a modular city exhibition hall straight from the instructions to see how it turns "empty" into "grand."

## 1. Instructions and Parts: An Elegant Large-Scale Build

The model is drawn at **1:48 architectural scale**, comes in at roughly **2,800 pieces** split across **9 numbered bags**, and the total workload sits somewhere between a concert hall and a standard street scene. The manual is laid out cleanly, and the sections packed with same-colour parts get colour-separated highlighting, so you are not squinting at a sea of white while hunting for one plate.

The palette is dominated by **pure white, light grey, ink black and natural wood tones**, which pushes the whole thing toward the minimalist art-gallery look. The small details — exhibit plinths, wall labels and the like — are printed elements rather than stickers, and they read as a premium touch. Missing-part rate was low. **Instructions and parts: 9/10.**

## 2. Structure and Facade: The Atrium Is the Star

The biggest risk with a museum MOC is that it ends up looking like a warehouse. This **exhibition hall MOC** avoids that with a few clean moves:

- **Double-height atrium:** the entrance is a full-height glass atrium, with the floor-to-ceiling curtain wall built from transparent elements. Daylight pours through during the day and the transparency is excellent.
- **Modular galleries:** three separate exhibition rooms on each of the two floors. Every floor is a **plug-in plate assembly** — pull it out and the entire display can be swapped as one unit.
- **Spiral staircase:** the stair at the side of the atrium is built from curved step elements. It saves floor space and doubles as structural bracing for the wall, which is a genuinely clever bit of engineering.

Overall the build is solid, and the curtain wall clips together tightly with no visible gaps between panels. **Structure: 9/10.**

## 3. Play Value: Swap the Floor, Swap the Show

What won me over is that this **exhibition hall MOC** turns curating into a play feature. The gallery floors are pre-drilled with standard hole positions, so different exhibits can be dropped in at any time: an abstract sculpture built from geometric parts, photography pieces using printed frames on mini easels, or a mechanical installation with gears you can actually turn. Want a new show? Pull the exhibits, swap the modules, and the whole theme of the building changes.

Each gallery also has its own spotlight position and information plaque, which adds a real curatorial feel. The cafe module on the second floor is removable and interchangeable too, so the "stop for a coffee after a lap of the galleries" slice of daily life is built in rather than implied. **Play value: 9.5/10.**

## 4. Accuracy and Lighting

On accuracy, the all-white massing, the glass atrium and the modular galleries nail the "less is more" character of a contemporary art museum.

Lighting is the finishing touch. The gallery track spotlights are recreated with small warm-white light bars, so individual exhibits are lit while the rest of the room stays dim — a professional-looking contrast. The atrium glazing adds a cool-white base light, and at night the whole building looks like a glowing crystal box. The one mark against it is that the number of exhibit pieces included is on the low side. **Accuracy and atmosphere: 9/10.**

**Verdict: strongly recommended — curatorial tinkerers and street-scene landmark collectors will both be very happy.** This **exhibition hall MOC** sits at medium-to-high difficulty (roughly 8-9 hours), so builders with some experience can buy with confidence. It suits players who like the "one building, many shows" idea and expect to re-dress the galleries repeatedly; players who want an elegant cultural landmark to raise the tone of a city layout; and players who enjoy minimalist modern architecture and know how to use negative space.

The weak points are the modest number of exhibits included and a build that is on the large side. For creative builders chasing a building they can actually re-curate, though, it is one of the most inventive cultural structures released recently.
