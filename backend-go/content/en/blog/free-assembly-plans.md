# Free MOC Instructions: Where to Download Them Legally

![Finished brick model built from free MOC instructions](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/free-assembly-plans/free-assembly-plans-1.jpg)

"The instructions cost more than the bricks" — plenty of MOC builders have said it. Yet there is a huge amount of genuinely free building-plan material online; most people just look in the wrong places. Here is where the good channels are and how to read the licensing before you download, so you can skip the trial-and-error.

## 1. What MOC instructions actually are

![Step-by-step pages from a brick building instruction booklet](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/free-assembly-plans/free-assembly-plans-2.jpg)

MOC stands for My Own Creation — a model designed by a builder rather than by a set manufacturer. Compared with official sets, the sharing culture around free MOC instructions is remarkably strong, and many experienced builders are happy to publish their designs openly. A typical plan includes a parts list (BOM), step-by-step build diagrams and a colour scheme. For builders moving past official sets, these are the main source of inspiration.

## 2. Where to find free building plans

When you are hunting for free instructions, start with dedicated plan-sharing platforms and community forums — that is where the hit rate is highest. Platforms such as BrickPlans collect a large amount of downloadable brick plan material in one place, so you can browse and grab what you need directly. Brick-design software communities and builder forums also see regular free MOC sharing posts. Browse often, save what you like, and your own library fills up quickly.

## 3. Watch the license and the commercial boundary

![Close-up of individual bricks in a MOC build](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/free-assembly-plans/free-assembly-plans-3.jpg)

"Free" does not mean you can do whatever you want with it commercially. Before downloading, read the designer's license terms carefully: personal use only, sharing allowed, or open for commercial use. Respecting the original creator is what keeps the free-instruction ecosystem alive. If you build on someone else's plan to make your own version, credit the original designer.

## 4. How to get the most out of free instructions

Once you have a free MOC plan in hand, first check whether the parts list matches what you already own — many platforms also list a difficulty rating and a part count. When building, sort parts by step and section; if a diagram is blurry, zoom in rather than guessing. Save multiple copies of downloaded plans locally so a dead source file can't cost you the build. And you can always add your own ideas on top of an existing plan to make the model genuinely yours.

## Wrapping up

Free resources are not in short supply — knowing how to pick them is. Find the right platform, read the license, and keep commercial use out of it unless you have permission.
