# Woma Airplane Series Review: Jets, Airliners, Helicopters

Woma has built its reputation in Chinese brick circles on military and vehicle subjects, and its aircraft line has always been one of the most popular corners of the catalogue. I recently built one set each from the **Woma airplane series** — a fighter, an airliner and a helicopter — and this write-up covers the actual building feel, parts quality and which sets I would recommend, so anyone thinking of buying has a clearer picture.

## Fighter jet: the most solid structure, and the most playable

Start with the fighter, the set with the strongest word of mouth in the **Woma airplane series**. Part counts here are mid-range, and the design leans on moving features: the wings fold, the underwing stores detach, and the landing gear retracts. On building feel, Woma bricks clutch on the tight side, and the wing hinges and rotating elements move smoothly — fold the wings back and forth repeatedly and they still don't go loose, so posing holds up well. On parts precision, the fighter's fuselage lines are crisp and the sprue marks are clean, to the point where you barely need to trim anything; once finished, it reads as one coherent object. The weak point is the transparent canopy: light transmission is so-so, fine from a distance but a step behind first-tier brands up close.

## Airliner: the collector's pick for detail-minded builders

The airliner is the larger and more detailed set in the **Woma airplane series**, with a cabin interior, passenger windows and printed livery. It's a longer build, and the instructions are sensibly split across numbered bags, so following the steps in order keeps things organized. The printed elements are a genuine plus — fuselage livery and window patterns come out sharp and look properly professional. The catch is that the airliner is built for display: the wing structure carries load only averagely, so it isn't a set to handle constantly. For anyone who wants a large airliner sitting on the shelf, it's a very good fit.

## Helicopter: the beginner-friendly entry point

If this is your first contact with the **Woma airplane series**, the helicopter is the safest place to start. Part count is low and the steps are simple, so it's done in roughly two hours; the main rotor turns, and the finished model both moves and displays, which makes for a strong sense of accomplishment. Clutch is moderate — easier on beginner fingers — and it's the cheapest of the three, so the cost of finding out you don't like it is low. It's about as good a practice build as you'll find.

## Pros, cons and who these sets are for

Overall verdict on the **Woma airplane series**: on the plus side, the moving structures are well executed, the subject matter is properly hardcore, printing is fine, and prices stay friendly — the value for money holds up well against other vehicle brick sets. On the minus side, transparent parts are only average for light transmission, the airliner is display-oriented rather than play-oriented, and a few of the long, thin pieces are a little tight. Anyone who likes military and vehicle subjects, or who wants a big aircraft as a centerpiece for the room, can buy with confidence.
