# LEGO Missing Parts Replacement: Get Free Official Pieces

![Finished LEGO model after a replacement parts request](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/lego-missing-parts-replacement/lego-missing-parts-replacement-1.jpg)

LEGO has a strong reputation for quality control, but no standard is tight enough to stop the occasional set from arriving with a piece missing. The good news is that the official replacement service is free, and the process is straightforward once you know the steps. Here is the full LEGO missing parts request workflow, from confirming the shortage to tracking your delivery.

## 1. Confirm the part is really missing

![LEGO building steps during a parts check](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/lego-missing-parts-replacement/lego-missing-parts-replacement-2.jpg)

Before you file a request, make sure the part is genuinely absent. **Count every piece in the set carefully** and check them one by one against the parts list in the instructions. Many "missing" parts turn out to be packed into the wrong bag, lodged at the bottom of a bag, or sitting on a corner of your desk. Sort in a **flat, open, well-lit space** so nothing rolls away or hides under a printout.

Only move on to the replacement request once you are sure the piece is gone. Note that the service mainly covers sets bought through **official, legitimate channels**, so keeping the box and your proof of purchase is worth the effort.

## 2. Find the official replacement portal

LEGO handles replacement requests **mainly through its website**. Go to the official LEGO site and look under "Help" or "Support" for the Missing Bricks / Replacement Parts entry. Some regional LEGO sites also accept requests through online chat or by phone.

Once you are on the replacement page, the system walks you through **the set number, the element number of the missing part, and the quantity**. All of this appears on the first page of the instructions or in the parts list, so having it ready makes the form much quicker to complete.

## 3. Fill in the required information

A **LEGO replacement request** typically asks for three groups of details. First, **set information**: the set number (for example, 75301) and the set name. Second, **part information**: the element number, colour and quantity of the missing piece. Third, **personal and shipping information**: your name, address and contact details.

Some regional forms let you **upload a photo of the missing part** so the team can verify it. Take the picture in good light and make sure the element number is legible. Read everything over before you submit, since an error in the details can cause the request to fail.

## 4. Understand the processing time

![Close-up of LEGO part details](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/lego-missing-parts-replacement/lego-missing-parts-replacement-3.jpg)

After you submit, LEGO reviews and processes the request **within a set window**. Timing varies by region and by stock availability, and usually falls somewhere between **a few working days and two or three weeks**. Once approved, the parts ship free to the address you entered, with nothing to pay.

If you hear nothing for a long stretch, use the **request status lookup** on the website to check progress, or contact customer service to follow up. While you wait, you can start on the sections of the set that do not need the missing piece.

## 5. What to build while you wait

While the replacement is on its way, **build the sections that do not depend on the missing part**. Many LEGO sets are designed in modules, so you can finish the main frame and other sub-assemblies first and leave the gap for last. That puts the waiting time to good use.

If the missing piece sits in a critical structural spot and cannot be skipped, **store the half-finished sections safely** and pick them up again when the part arrives. Avoid forcing in a piece that does not match, since that will hurt the finished model.

## 6. A few things to keep in mind

The LEGO replacement service is a **free, official channel**, and there is no need to buy the missing piece from a third party. Always apply through the official route so you avoid exposing personal information or ending up with a non-genuine part. The service also has reasonable quantity limits, so request only what you actually need and describe it accurately.

The **LEGO missing parts process** really comes down to four steps: confirm the shortage, go through the official portal, enter the set and part details, then wait for review and shipping.
