# LEGO Modular Buildings Review: Is the Series Worth Collecting?

Short answer first: the Modular Buildings line wins on expandable modular design plus a three-floor structure. One set runs about 2,500-3,000 pieces and 8-15 hours of building, and the best way in is to start with a currently available set.

Among adult LEGO fans, there is no product line with a steadier reputation or better value retention than Expert Creator Modular, known to builders simply as the Modular Buildings series. From Cafe Corner (10182) in 2007 to the 2024 release, the line has held to one rule: a three-storey building every year. Thanks to a mature **modular design**, it has built up a whole generation's nostalgia and shelf space.

## Modular design: why it can extend forever

The soul of the series is modularity. Every building is a three-part structure of **ground floor + middle floor + top floor**, and the levels lock together with anti-studs on the underside and studs on top, so you can lift off a whole floor to play inside. Better still, each set ships with **standard connector pieces**, so different sets can be joined side by side into one continuous street. That "buy one now, add another later" expandability is the core appeal that separates it from ordinary building sets.

## Ground floor, middle floor, top floor: every level has its own story

The layering is very clear. The **ground floor** is usually a shop or street-facing unit with an opening door and a full interior — the level with the most going on. The **middle floor** holds living space or offices with dense display detail. The **top floor** is typically an attic or terrace, finished with a roof chimney and decoration. All three together come to roughly 2,500-3,000 pieces and 8-15 hours of building time, which makes it a weekend-immersion experience and strong value for money within LEGO's adult range.

## Minifigures and scenes: making the street feel alive

The **minifigure interaction** is handled with real care. Each set includes 5-8 minifigures whose jobs and outfits match the function of the floor they belong to — a barista in the cafe, readers in the bookshop, a butler in the detective's office. Add scene pieces like opening windows, street lamps and sidewalk trees, and any arrangement becomes a street photo with a story in it. For players who like to tell stories, this narrative density is the single biggest point in the set's favour.

## Collector value and advice for getting started

On **collector value**, the Modular line is LEGO's closest thing to a financial product: retired sets generally climb in price, and the earliest releases (Cafe Corner, Grand Emporium) routinely trade at double their original cost on the secondhand market. If you want in, start with a **recently released set that is still on sale** — pricing is transparent and there is no risk of aged or brittle parts. If you are waiting for appreciation, watch the sets retired two or three years ago.

Specific picks: **Detective's Office (10246)** has a rich interior and top-tier interactivity, and is widely agreed to be the essential step up; **Bookshop (10270)** is clean in structure and friendly to newcomers; if your budget is tight, starting with a small-to-mid store-style set is perfectly reasonable. One warning: popular sets attract a lot of counterfeits, so buy through official or authorised channels and check for the "LEGO" stamp on the studs.

## Wrap-up

The Modular series wins because modularity makes a collection expandable, the three-floor structure gives the build real layering, and the minifigures give the scene warmth. Whether you are buying your first building or filling out a street, it rarely disappoints.
