# Cafe MOC Guide: Counter, Seating and Corner Atmosphere

Among all the street-scene subjects you can build, a **cafe MOC** is reliably the most lovable. Warm-toned walls, a doorway that seems to smell of coffee, minifigures sitting face to face in the window — the whole thing arrives with a built-in sense of comfort. It is small enough to be a great first project for practicing color schemes and interiors, yet it is the piece that gives an entire street layout its human warmth. Below is a complete build sequence, working from the outside in.

## 1. Corner Placement: Give the Cafe Instant Atmosphere

The most charming part of a cafe is often not the interior but its relationship to the street. Building on a corner lets you show a front window display and a side wall at the same time, and the structure immediately reads as three-dimensional. Stay on the warm side of the palette: dark brown, off-white and brick red, with wood-tone window frames, finished with a shallow eave or awning so the whole facade stays soft without losing depth.

The sidewalk space in front of the door is where the mood really gets made. Put out a small round table with two chairs, add an outdoor parasol, then prop a handwritten chalkboard menu beside the entrance. A customer minifigure sits outside with a coffee while a passerby stops to read the board — two small actions and the street is alive. This corner treatment is exactly what separates a **cafe MOC** from a generic building.

## 2. The Counter: Stage Center of the Cafe

The bar is the undisputed focal point, usually set facing the door or running along the window. Start with dark plates and tiles as the counter base, then line up the equipment on top: espresso machine, grinder, cup rack and cash register. The espresso machine can be built from small silver and black parts for the body and spout, topped with a red indicator-light piece. For the cup rack, arrange inverted small round pieces in a neat row to suggest cups waiting to be used.

The back wall behind the counter deserves extra attention. Fill shelves with coffee tins, cake stands and glasses, then hang a row of cup rails above them. Place a busy barista minifigure on the inside of the counter, a coffee "in hand," leaning slightly forward — the scene picks up motion instantly. Remember: the more professional the bar of your **cafe MOC** looks, the more convincing the whole shop becomes.

## 3. Seating and Interior Structure: Comfort Comes First

Seating should feel comfortable and slightly irregular. Put two-person tables with booth seating or bar stools along the window, where natural light does the most work; in the middle and against the walls, mix long tables, sofas and single seats so the layout reads as both open and private. Tabletop props are the cheapest way to raise the polish level — coffee cups, a slice of cake, a vase, an open book. A handful of small items tells the story of a leisurely afternoon.

For the interior structure, use a combination of floor-to-ceiling glass and half-open partitions. Glass keeps sightlines open between inside and out, making the interior feel roomy and bright; a low half-wall or a bookshelf then divides zones naturally while giving you wall surface for decorations. Add a few warm pendant lights or track spotlights overhead, pair them with a wood-tone floor, and the cozy atmosphere appears on its own.

## 4. Details and Story: Making the Cafe Feel Warm

Details decide the soul of a cafe. Build the sign from letter tiles or a printed piece and add a small wall lamp beside it for a nighttime feel. Door handles, the awning, planters on the windowsill and an umbrella stand by the door are all low-cost, high-reward finishing touches. Stack paper cups on the takeaway counter and hang a hand-drawn style menu board on the wall — every one of these says something about the shop.

Narrative matters just as much: a customer taking a coffee across the bar, a couple chatting by the window, a child outside pointing at the cake display. Arrange minifigures in different poses and the **cafe MOC** stops being a static model and becomes a small world with comings and goings, noise and warmth. And do not forget the background-music detail — a cat stretched out asleep on the windowsill.

## 5. Beginner Tips and Parts List

If you are new to cafe MOCs, start with a small scene: one counter plus one table and two chairs. Focus on stacking small pieces cleanly and on getting a warm color scheme to work; once that feels comfortable, expand to a full corner storefront. On color, remember that less is more — dark brown and white as the base, with one or two bright accents (a red sign, a yellow pendant lamp) doing the highlighting.

Suggested parts list: plenty of warm-colored bricks and plates plus wood-tone pieces; a generous supply of transparent window elements and glass doors; silver, black and white small parts for building the espresso machine and cup rack; decorative items such as vases, books, planters and an umbrella stand in quantity; an outdoor parasol, tables and chairs, bar stools as the key to the corner atmosphere; and several minifigures in varied poses plus one small animal. Sorting small parts into boxes by color family will make the whole build far more relaxed.

From choosing the corner plot and shaping the bar to filling in seating and details, all it takes is tuning the atmosphere one step at a time — and this cafe becomes the one building on the street everyone wants to push the door open and walk into: warm, quiet and full of ordinary life.
