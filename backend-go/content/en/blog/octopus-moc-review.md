# Brick Octopus MOC Review: Poseable Tentacles in Deep Red

The short version first: this octopus MOC recreates deep-sea coloring with a dark red and purple-magenta two-tone scheme, and each of its eight tentacles is a chain of multiple ball joints that bends in any direction and holds its pose. For a sea-creature subject, that puts it firmly in the high-articulation bracket.

Among ocean-themed MOCs, the octopus has always been a designer favorite and a designer headache. Eight arms, a soft body, sucker detail — every one of those natural features is awkward to reproduce in a material as rigid as brick. The model reviewed here has been drawing a lot of attention on https://brickplan.cn, so let's take it section by section.

## Shape and Silhouette: Eight Arms Done Right

Start with the look. This octopus MOC pairs dark red with purple-magenta, close to the shifting body tones a real octopus shows in deep water, and the transition between the two reads naturally rather than patchy. The head is round and full, with curved elements pieced together into a dome-shaped mantle. The two large eyes sit at just the right height, giving the model an expression that is realistic but with a touch of cartoon charm. The eight tentacles are not simple repeats of one another: varying lengths and segment counts create front-to-back layering, so the whole animal reads as mid-swim rather than splayed flat on a shelf.

## Articulation: Tentacles and Suckers

Poseability is the soul of a creature MOC like this. Every tentacle here is built from a run of ball joints, so it can bend in any direction and hold its position firmly — wrapping, gripping, stretching out, all of it without strain. Rarer still, the builder used small plates at the tips of some tentacles to suggest the sucker structure, and as you rotate an arm the suckers rise and fall with the segments. That is real attention to detail. There is also a small gear assembly hidden where the head meets the tentacles: turn it and the arms sway as a group, a nice little easter egg in the articulation design.

## Parts and Build Experience

The part count is friendly. This octopus MOC uses very few specialized elements — the overwhelming majority is standard bricks, ball joints and curved pieces, which means the barrier to rebuilding it yourself is low. The build order is sensibly designed: torso first, tentacles second, so each stage gives you a complete piece of the model and you are never left staring at a pile of loose parts wondering whether to quit. The one thing to watch is the sheer number of tentacle segments; check the instructions carefully when aligning them, and note that a few of the ball joints seat rather tightly — a brick separator helps during assembly.

## Verdict

Overall this is a remarkably complete octopus MOC: strong shape accuracy, genuinely playable articulation, accessible parts and a smooth build. ## FAQ

**Q: What skill level is this octopus MOC aimed at?**
A: Intermediate builders with some basics behind them. Joining the curved elements on the head is not hard, but the eight tentacles are repetitive and demand patience; following the instructions, expect roughly 5-8 hours to finish. Complete beginners should start with a smaller creature build first.

**Q: Can the eight tentacles actually move, and how do they hold a pose?**
A: Yes. Each tentacle is a chain of multiple ball joints that bends in any direction and stays firmly at a fixed angle, so wrapping, gripping and stretching poses are all possible. Giving the joints a light press now and then keeps the tension consistent.

**Q: How is the sucker detail on the octopus MOC achieved?**
A: The builder offsets small plates at the ends of some tentacles to form the sucker structure, working with the dark red to purple-magenta transition so that it suggests suction from a distance. The detail sits on the inner side of the tentacles, so mind the orientation when you position them.
