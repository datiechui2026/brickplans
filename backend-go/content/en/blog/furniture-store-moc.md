# Furniture Store MOC Tutorial: Windows, Displays and Layout

The short version: around 3000 parts plus one 32x32 baseplate (25.6cm square) is enough for a two-storey street furniture store MOC. Loose parts run about 200-350 yuan, and a beginner following the four steps below can finish in 6-8 hours. What makes or breaks a furniture store MOC is not how refined the walls are — it is whether the window display makes people want to walk in.

## Step 1: Baseplate and Wall Skeleton

Use a standard 32x32 baseplate, reserving 8x32 for the sidewalk in front of the store and the inner 24x32 for the shop itself. Build the walls with staggered 1x4 and 1x6 bricks, reinforcing corners with 2x2 bricks. The ground floor is 12 bricks tall (about 9.6cm) and the upper floor 10 bricks, so furniture placed later has proper headroom. Leave a 6-wide, 3-high mounting slot at the entrance so the sign can be installed afterwards.

## Step 2: Window Display

The shop window is the face of a furniture store. Build three floor-to-ceiling window bays from 1x2 transparent bricks and transparent glass panels, each 4 wide and 6 high. Inside, add a display platform 1-2cm tall and set the hero products on it: a 6x3 bed (tiles for the mattress, curved bricks for the pillow), an L-shaped sofa (roughly 8x5) and a 4x3 TV cabinet. Lay dark tiles on the window floor and light it with two 1x1 transparent light bricks — the nighttime effect shows up immediately.

## Step 3: Small Furniture and Interior Layout

Divide the interior into three zones — living room, bedroom and storage — with a 4-wide aisle running between them. The living room zone gets a two-seat sofa, coffee table and floor lamp; the bedroom zone gets a double bed, nightstand and wardrobe (use 1x2 hinge bricks so the doors actually open); the storage zone gets two 8-wide display cabinets with a mini ornament on each shelf. The universal formula for small furniture: a 2x2 or 2x4 base, a body built from tiles plus sideways bricks to form the "legs", and a 1x1 decorative element on top. That keeps pieces detailed without burning through parts.

## Step 4: Storefront Sign and Finishing Touches

Spell out "FURNITURE" on the storefront with 1x2 white bricks (or apply the printed tile downloaded from brickplan). The sign backing uses dark red 2x6 bricks, with a 4-high lamp post standing on each side. The entrance is a double sliding door made from 1x4 transparent glass panels plus rail elements. Finally, dress the sidewalk with 2-3 small trees built from 1x1 round bricks, one street lamp and a 2x2 mailbox — and the whole street suddenly feels lived in.

## FAQ

**Q: Where can I download plans for a furniture store MOC?**
A: brickplan.cn hosts plenty of furniture store and street plans. Search "furniture store" and you will find builds in the 2700-3500 part range, filterable by part count; the downloaded plans can be followed directly while building.

**Q: Is this MOC hard? Is it beginner-friendly?**
A: It sits on the easy side of medium. The walls are standard brickwork, and only the transparent window parts and the hinged cabinet doors need a little technique. Anyone who has built a set of 1000+ parts can manage it, in roughly 6-8 hours.

**Q: What will 3000 parts cost?**
A: About 200-350 yuan using loose domestic bricks; buying part-by-part from a marketplace against the brickplan parts list usually works out at 0.06-0.1 yuan per piece. LEGO parts would push it past 1500 yuan, which is not recommended.

**Q: How big is the finished model? Will it connect to other street MOCs?**
A: Roughly 25.6x25.6cm and about 20cm tall. Because it sits on a standard 32x32 baseplate, it joins any 8-wide street layout seamlessly — line it up with a restaurant, bookstore or appliance store MOC and you have a complete commercial street.
