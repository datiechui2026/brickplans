# Crayon Shin-chan MOC: Nohara House & Futaba Kindergarten

Bottom line first: the heart of any Crayon Shin-chan MOC is the Nohara house, roughly 2,500-3,500 pieces, and once you add the kindergarten and the park you have a complete Kasukabe neighborhood block.

Anyone who has watched the show knows its appeal isn't epic plotlines — it's the small, everyday stuff. That's exactly where a **Crayon Shin-chan brick MOC** shines: rebuilding the routines of the Nohara house, Futaba Kindergarten and the local park one brick at a time. The more mundane the scene, the more it delivers.

## The Nohara House: the core of the build

Almost every **Crayon Shin-chan MOC** plan starts with the Nohara house. The two-story Japanese home with its small yard anchors the whole display. Focus on the entryway, the living room and Hiroshi and Misae's bedroom: the genkan needs a shoe cabinet and a step up, the living room absolutely needs the TV and sofa, and it's worth giving Misae an open kitchen — after all, "Mom cooking while Shin-chan watches Action Kamen" is the most frequently replayed scene in the series. Use dark blue pieces for the roof and off-white walls with wood-tone trim. The curve of the Japanese eaves comes from offsetting slope pieces layer by layer; once it sits in a display case, it reads as Kasukabe at a glance.

## Futaba Kindergarten and the park: the best stages

Futaba Kindergarten (the Action Kindergarten) is the other landmark you can't skip. The building shape isn't complicated — the job is capturing the kindergarten feel: bright yellow exterior walls, a rounded arch entrance, and open ground in front. Leave space on that front apron for a slide and a sandbox, and you have natural standing spots for Kazama, Nene, Masao and Bo. The park is even better as a little theater: a bench under the cherry trees, a swing set, low shrubs, and the accidental-encounter scene between Shin-chan and Miss Nanako can start any time. Build both of these on 32x32 baseplates so they connect directly to the Nohara house and form one continuous Kasukabe block.

## Character builds: capturing Shin-chan and Shiro

The characters are the soul of the set. Shin-chan's likeness comes down to three things: the trademark thick eyebrows, the red T-shirt and yellow shorts, and that perfectly round head. Brown curved pieces laid flat against the face are the most convincing approach for the brows; stack round plates for the head silhouette, add a cheeky mouth sticker, and the personality appears instantly. Shiro is a marshmallow blob built from pure white small pieces, with ears dotted on using 1x1 round studs — the curled tail is the finishing touch. If you build a minifigure-scale version, remember to give Shin-chan his Action Kamen cape; it makes photos look instantly better.

## Bright color scheme: Kasukabe at a glance

The palette of a **Crayon Shin-chan brick MOC** is the most recognizable part of the whole build: warm yellow for the Nohara house, bright orange for the kindergarten, vivid green for the park, plus the big red and yellow on Shin-chan himself. Overall saturation runs very high. When picking parts, favor pure primary colors over dull mixed shades — that sunny feel is the base tone of Shin-chan's world. If your color-part stock is well stocked, scenes like these barely require any extra purchases.

## Play value and difficulty

On difficulty, the Nohara house sits at the upper-intermediate end: many structural layers, and the windows and balcony need precise alignment. The kindergarten and park are friendlier for beginners. For part count, the Nohara house needs roughly 2,500-3,500 pieces, and the two smaller scenes add about 2,000 more combined. Don't pack it straight into the cabinet when you're done — swap minifigure positions, change the seasonal stickers, and you can shoot a whole "A Day in the Life of Shin-chan" series. It also works well as a family build: kids place the characters, adults handle the main structure.

## Closing thoughts

The biggest appeal of a **Crayon Shin-chan brick MOC** is that everyday feel. You don't need a grand landmark — once the Nohara genkan, the kindergarten slide and the park bench are in place, childhood memory comes back on its own. ## FAQ

**Q: How many parts does a Crayon Shin-chan Nohara house MOC need?**
A: The Nohara house runs about 2,500-3,500 pieces, with the Futaba Kindergarten and park scenes adding roughly 2,000 more each. Build on 32x32 baseplates so they connect to the house easily.

**Q: Is a Crayon Shin-chan MOC hard to build? Can a beginner manage?**
A: The Nohara house is upper-intermediate — windows and balcony need precise alignment. The kindergarten and park are much friendlier for newcomers. For family builds, adults take the main structure and kids place the characters.

**Q: How do I get Shin-chan and Shiro to look right?**
A: Nail three things on Shin-chan: brown curved pieces for the thick eyebrows, a red T-shirt with yellow shorts, and round plates stacked into a round head, finished with a cheeky mouth sticker. Shiro uses pure white small pieces with 1x1 round studs for the ears.

**Q: What should I watch out for with the color scheme?**
A: The whole set runs highly saturated — use pure colors like warm yellow, bright orange and vivid green rather than dull mixed shades. With a good stock of colored parts, scenes like these need almost no extra buying.
