# Cable Car MOC Review: 2,600-Piece Mountain Gondola Set

Short version first: this cable car MOC is a remarkably complete design. The finished model stands about 35 cm tall and spans 40 cm, uses roughly 2,600 pieces, and the gondola cabins actually slide along the steel cable — recreating how a real alpine cable car station operates. Expect about 6 hours of building, and the value is solid. It is an easy recommendation for anyone who builds scenery and dioramas.

## 1. Exterior and Accuracy

The set includes two cable car stations, three cabins and a stretch of cable track. The stations mix timber tones with white, and the details hold up at every level: roof, waiting area and ticket gate are all accounted for. Each cabin has transparent windows and a hanging arm, and once hooked onto the cable it slides back and forth. The mountain base is built from a large number of slope pieces piled up to create the gradient, and the overall composition reads very strongly as a picture.

## 2. Build Experience

The build order runs mountain base, then stations, then cable, then cabins — a sensible sequence. The mountain section uses a lot of slope pieces, so you will want to sort parts by colour into zones before you start. Installing the cable is the hard part: tension has to be even at both ends, otherwise the cabins will snag and stutter as they slide. The instructions are split into six numbered step bags at about 400 pieces each, and the full build takes around 6 hours at a comfortable pace.

## 3. Part Quality

The parts are mainly high-precision bricks from a domestic manufacturer, with a snug, firm fit, and the transparent window pieces are clear and free of scratches. The cable is a custom soft cord that resists wear and is not prone to snapping. One small gripe: the mountain relies heavily on same-colour slope pieces, so a mistake means reworking a whole section — sort by zone against the instructions before building. Missing parts were rare, and the replacement service responded quickly.

## 4. Value for Money

The set costs about ¥700, which works out to roughly ¥0.27 per piece, and that includes the custom cable and the printed elements — a fair price. Compared with city street sets at the same price point, the cable car's moving play feature gives it a distinctive edge. If you already own mountain parts, you can also buy just the instructions and source the pieces yourself, bringing the cost down to around ¥500.

## FAQ

**Q: Do the cabins actually move once the cable car MOC is finished?**
A: Yes. Each cabin hangs from the cable by its arm and slides with a light push, and both stations have fixed stop positions. It works well for both display and handling.

**Q: What age is this cable car set suitable for?**
A: 10 and up is the sensible recommendation. At 2,600 pieces and 6 hours of building time it is a long project with plenty of small parts, and younger builders are more likely to lose pieces or misbuild the mountain structure.

**Q: Is the cable car MOC worth the asking price?**
A: Around ¥700 for the full set is reasonable, and a sale price near ¥650 is a good moment to buy. Instructions alone are about ¥25, and sourcing the parts yourself can bring the total under ¥500.

**Q: Can it be combined with other scenery sets?**
A: Yes. The mountain base is stylistically compatible with street scenes and train station builds, so it combines nicely into a mountain tourist town layout and is easy to extend.
