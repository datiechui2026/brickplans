# LEGO MOC Tutorial: Build Your Own Custom Brick Model

MOC stands for "My Own Creation" — a LEGO model designed entirely by the builder rather than taken from an official set. This tutorial walks you through the full creative process, whether you have just picked up your first brick or you already have a few builds behind you.

## Step 1: Choose a Theme and Gather Inspiration

Every good MOC starts with a good idea. The subject can be architecture, mecha, vehicles, natural scenery, or even an anime character. Start with something you know well: recreate your own living room, a scene from a film you love, or a corner of an imagined future city. Browse the outstanding builds on brickplan.cn, scroll through social platforms, and save the compositions that catch your eye — those saved images become raw material when you start designing.

## Step 2: Plan the Size and the Part List

Once the theme is set, decide roughly how large the model will be. The base is usually a baseplate or a foundation of standard bricks, with everything stacked upward to scale. Sketch the idea roughly or write out a part list and estimate which brick types and how many of each you will need. This is the moment to check your inventory with the part lookup on brickplan.cn so you do not run dry halfway through the build.

## Step 3: Learn the Core Building Techniques

Technique sets the ceiling on what a model can be. A few essentials:

- **SNOT (Studs Not On Top)**: attach bricks sideways or upside down to get much finer surface detail.
- **Stacking and interlocking**: offset bricks between layers so the structure holds together and resists falling apart.
- **Internal bracing**: large models need support structures inside so the top does not sag under its own weight.
- **Color scheme**: limit yourself to two or three main colors and lift the model with a small amount of accent color — the result reads as far more cohesive.

Beginners should start small: a car or a little house is enough to learn the common brick sizes (1x1, 2x2, 2x4) and how they connect. A few practical habits prevent most disasters — sort parts by color and size before you begin, keep the table clear while building, and check your proportions against the sketch as you go. MOC building is not about hoarding rare parts; shaping something clever out of ordinary bricks shows far more skill.

## Step 4: Build from One Section Outward

Do not try to finish everything in one pass. Build a core module first — the main body of a building, the torso of a mecha — and confirm that the structure and proportions work before extending outward. When you hit a wall, take apart an official set to study how it is braced internally, or look at how builders on brickplan.cn handle the same subject. The build itself is a process of trial and error: dismantle boldly, change boldly, and many classic techniques exist only because someone rebuilt the same section over and over.

## Step 5: Document Your Steps and Share the Build

After the physical model is finished, photograph it stage by stage. Where useful, add a part list and building notes so other builders can recreate it. Publish the result to communities such as brickplan.cn, swap notes with other MOC fans and take the feedback on board — that is the fastest route to improvement.

## Final Thoughts

With the methods in this LEGO MOC tutorial, an idea in your head can become an object in your hands, one step at a time. The pleasure of creating lies in the finished model, but just as much in the repeated taking apart and rebuilding along the way. Open your brick box now, start with a small scene, and begin your own MOC journey.
