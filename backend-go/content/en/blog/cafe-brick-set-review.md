# Cafe Brick Set Review: A Detailed Street Scene Build

## Opening

A street scene without a cafe always feels like it is missing something — the corner where people linger. Stand it on its own and it has character; slot it into a full row of buildings and it fits right in. We built this cafe brick set all the way through, taking apart the ground-floor service area, the second-floor roasting workshop and the third-floor terrace to look inside each one. Here is how it actually performs, split into four areas: exterior, interior, parts and the build itself.

## 1. Exterior Design

### 1.1 Architectural Style

This cafe brick model uses a classic European corner-cafe layout, mixing vintage and modern elements:

- **Walls**: off-white and dark brown bricks with a red awning, creating a warm European street feel
- **Roof**: a pitched roof built from dark grey slope bricks, with dormer windows and a chimney for extra detail
- **Frontage**: large glass display windows in wooden frames, giving the building a very open, see-through look
- **Signage**: a printed coffee-cup logo hangs above the entrance — eye-catching and neatly done

### 1.2 Size and Scale

The set measures roughly 25cm wide x 20cm deep x 30cm tall, and it is compatible with standard street scene modules, so it lines up easily with other buildings. The structure has three levels:

- **Ground floor**: the cafe service area, including the counter and seating
- **Second floor**: the coffee roasting workshop, showing the bean-to-cup process
- **Third floor**: a rooftop garden planted with flowers and greenery

### 1.3 Color Scheme

Colour is one of the strengths of this brick set:

- The main palette is warm (off-white, brown, red), which gives the building its cosy feel
- Green plants and gold accent details lift the overall quality
- Interiors use lighter tones so the rooms read as bright and open

## 2. Interior Details

### 2.1 Ground Floor — Cafe Service Area

Step inside the ground floor and you can almost smell the coffee. Interior details include:

- **Counter**: built from dark brown bricks with a smooth tile surface, holding a cash register and an espresso machine
- **Espresso machine**: a professional-style machine built from silver and black parts, unusually realistic at this scale
- **Seating**: two to three small round tables, each with two chairs
- **Display case**: a glass case displaying cake and dessert models
- **Menu board**: a blackboard menu made from a 1x2 printed tile

### 2.2 Second Floor — Coffee Roasting Workshop

This level shows coffee going from green bean to finished product:

- **Roaster**: a rotating roasting drum built with technic parts
- **Green bean storage jars**: transparent cylinder bricks holding raw beans, simulated with 1x1 brown round plates
- **Tasting area**: a small tasting counter with coffee cups and a tasting notebook
- **Display shelving**: packaging from different coffee-growing origins

### 2.3 Third Floor — Rooftop Garden

The rooftop garden on top is the place to relax:

- **Wooden floor**: brown plates laid across the terrace surface
- **Parasol**: an opening and closing sunshade with lounge chairs and a table underneath
- **Planters**: flowers in a range of colours plus greenery
- **Railing**: a white ornamental fence — safe and good looking

## 3. Part Quality

### 3.1 Brick Quality

How does this brick set hold up on parts?

- **Moulding precision**: clean edges, no flashing, complete studs
- **Clutch power**: moderate — tight enough to hold, loose enough that you are not fighting the bricks
- **Colour consistency**: no shade variation within the same colour, and the colours are rich
- **Transparency**: the clear parts are genuinely clear, with no bubbles or scratches

### 3.2 Printed Parts and Stickers

- **Printed parts**: around 15 of them, including the signage, menu board and coffee cup graphics; printing is sharp and the colours are vivid
- **Stickers**: a small number used for detail work such as bean labels and price cards; the sticker material is good and they stay put

### 3.3 Special Parts

The set includes several useful special pieces:

- Curved window frames for the shop windows
- Awning components, including a foldable fabric awning
- Plant parts: multiple flower and greenery elements
- Coffee cups and tableware in miniature

## 4. Building Experience

### 4.1 Instructions

The instructions are printed in full colour with clear steps. New parts in each step are highlighted so they are easy to spot. The manual is divided into four main stages, one per floor plus the finishing work, which keeps the logic easy to follow.

### 4.2 Build Time

An experienced builder will need roughly 4-5 hours to finish the cafe brick model. Newer builders should budget 6-7 hours. Splitting it across two evenings is the better approach — take your time and enjoy it.

### 4.3 Build Highlights

- Attaching the fabric awning material to the brick frame is a clever bit of construction
- The espresso machine uses some non-traditional techniques that genuinely stand out
- The rooftop garden at the end acts as the finishing touch, bringing the whole building to life

## 5. Display and Playability

### 5.1 Display

Once finished, the cafe brick set displays very well:

- On a desk or shelf it is a genuine focal point
- Paired with other buildings from a street scene range, it forms a complete commercial block
- Photographers can use it as a prop for macro shots

### 5.2 Playability

- The modular design means each floor lifts off independently, which makes the interiors easy to show and handle
- Four minifigures are included: a barista, a customer, a roaster and a server
- Opening doors and windows add interactivity

## 6. Pros and Cons

### 6.1 Pros

- Beautiful exterior with a harmonious colour scheme
- Rich interior detail — every floor has its own function and story
- Excellent part quality and a smooth building experience
- Modular construction that is easy to display and to play with

### 6.2 Cons

- The third-floor terrace is on the small side and could be larger
- The minifigure count is low; another two or three customers would help
- Some stickers need very precise placement, which is a challenge for beginners

## 7. Buying Advice

This brick set suits the following builders:

- **Street scene fans**: anyone looking to expand an existing layout
- **Coffee culture enthusiasts**: builders drawn to a coffee theme
- **Detail obsessives**: those who want refined interiors
- **Beginners**: the difficulty is moderate, making it a reasonable entry-level street scene set

## Final Thoughts

This cafe brick set clears the bar on all three counts — exterior, interior and build. Calling it a miniature display piece you assemble by hand is not an exaggeration. If you want a building with some everyday life in it for your street scene, it deserves a place on the shortlist.
