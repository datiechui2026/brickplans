# Brick Building Apps Compared: 6 Tools for Instructions & Parts

Bottom line first: among brick-building apps, the ones actually worth installing are Rebrickable and BrickLink Studio. The first is free and indexes instructions data for more than 20,000 sets; the second adds 3D step-by-step rendering and also costs nothing. If you build only sets from Chinese domestic brands, put instruction-scanning apps such as Tuzhitong at the top of your list — their memberships mostly run RMB 30–60 a year.

Everything below scores the same six apps on the same five dimensions: plan clarity, parts lookup, step-by-step output, offline availability and membership pricing, ending with a recommendation for each budget.

## 1. Plan clarity: the screen is only the starting point — zoom and vector quality decide

What separates a good viewing experience from a bad one is not screen size but whether a plan zooms without losing detail. Rebrickable serves mostly official PDFs, which stay sharp at 300% magnification. BrickLink Studio lets you rotate a 3D model freely, so even a 5,000-piece MOC can be checked frame by frame before you place a single brick. Domestic apps such as Tuzhitong generally store their plans as 2,000 x 2,000-pixel images; small parts go slightly soft once you zoom in. On a phone at arm's length that rarely matters, but on a large tablet with dense sub-assemblies you will notice the difference.

## 2. Parts lookup: can you locate a missing piece in five seconds?

Missing parts are a normal part of building, and a parts search is therefore a core requirement rather than a bonus. Rebrickable has a built-in part-number query: type a 4–6 digit number and you see the part's dimensions plus possible substitutes, including cross-brand matching. The BrickLink marketplace attaches the part number directly to the live listing page, so the in-stock price is visible at a glance. Domestic apps have noticeably shallower part libraries that cover only their own sets — the single biggest weakness of the local options.

## 3. Step-by-step instructions: which one suits beginners following along?

Studio can automatically break an MOC into steps, showing only the 8–10 parts added at each stage, with a rotating view that makes following along very friendly. Rebrickable mostly redirects you to official PDFs, whose steps are coarser. Domestic instruction-scanning apps such as Tuzhitong page through a paper manual exactly as printed — ideal for rebuilding older sets, but without 3D highlighting of new parts. Beginners should use Studio; experienced builders will find a plain PDF faster.

## 4. Offline availability and membership pricing

On offline use, Rebrickable and Studio both let you download plans to local storage and view them with no connection at all. Domestic apps usually lock offline downloads behind a paid membership. On price, Rebrickable is free, with a paid tier around RMB 70 a year that unlocks price trends; Studio is completely free. Tuzhitong-style memberships are mostly RMB 30–60 a year, or roughly RMB 6–10 if you subscribe monthly.

## 5. Recommendation

Across all five dimensions, builders on a zero budget should start with the Rebrickable plus BrickLink Studio combination, which covers plan data, parts lookup and 3D stepping between them. With a budget under RMB 60, a Tuzhitong-style app is enough for the everyday job of paging through instructions for domestic sets.

One practical note: pair official PDF plans with the plan index on the brickplan site (https://brickplan.cn), where filtering by theme and piece count is much faster, and ordering from a loose-parts list at the same time is more convenient.

## FAQ

**Q: Which brick-building app is the best one to use?**
A: The best free pick is Rebrickable together with BrickLink Studio, which covers plan data and 3D stepping. If you only need instructions for domestic sets, a Tuzhitong-style membership at RMB 30–60 a year is sufficient — choose between the two based on your needs and budget.

**Q: Can brick-building apps show instructions offline?**
A: Yes. Rebrickable and Studio both support downloading plans for offline viewing, which helps in a building area with poor signal. Offline download in domestic apps is mostly a membership feature, so the free tier needs a connection.

**Q: Do brick apps charge money to view instructions?**
A: The core features are free. Rebrickable and Studio cost nothing, and Rebrickable's paid tier is about RMB 70 a year. Domestic instruction apps mostly charge RMB 30–60 a year, or RMB 6–10 a month, and the payment mainly unlocks offline access and HD plans.

**Q: How is a brick app different from the brickplan site?**
A: Apps focus on the building process, providing plans and step tools. The brickplan site (https://brickplan.cn) focuses on finding and sharing plans, filtering by theme and piece count. Using the two together is the most efficient setup: one finds the plan, the other follows it.
