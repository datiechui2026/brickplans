# Medieval Castle Brick MOC: Gatehouse, Drawbridge and Moat Build

A medieval castle brick MOC that can hold its own on a shelf rarely fails because of a brick shortage. It fails on likeness. Real fidelity is not about covering every tower with spires and flags — it comes from getting the gatehouse proportions, the drawbridge mechanism and the moat layers right. What follows is the build order I use, from the foundation to the last detail, with the exact parts I reached for and the mistakes I made along the way.

## 1. Set the Footprint First: The Golden Ratio of the Baseplate

Most medieval castle brick MOCs go wrong at the very start: the builder piles up towers immediately, then discovers the base cannot hold everything and the main structure is squeezed into a lump. Instead, join two 32x32 baseplates into a 64x32 footprint and place the main gatehouse about one third of the way in from the left, leaving the right side for the outer wall and the drawbridge entrance. That composition reads best from any angle. When you sketch it out, reserve a channel at least 2 studs wide and 1 brick deep for the moat, then close the edges with 1x2 slope bricks — the water line appears instantly.

## 2. Gatehouse Structure: Square Towers Beat Round Ones

Build the main gatehouse as a square tower. Use 2x2 corner bricks at the four corners, alternate half-brick offsets on every course, and fill the middle with 2x4 bricks so the seams stagger; the result is far stronger. Around 16 brick courses tall — roughly 14 cm — gives the most comfortable proportion. Lower than that looks childish, higher steals the show from the curtain wall. One pitfall worth flagging: **every 3 courses, sandwich a row of 1x2 bricks with a stud on top as a tie course**. Without them the tower tends to snap in half the moment you carry it to a display table. That is exactly how my first version died.

For the crenellations, alternate 1x1 bricks with 1x1 bricks with a cross axle hole, and level the gaps with 1x2 plates so minifigures can stand on top securely. Finish the tower top with a ring of 1x1 bricks with a stud on top, and when you cut 1x2 arched windows, turn 1x4 curved slopes upside down to frame them — the medieval feel arrives immediately.

## 3. The Drawbridge: The Best Feature and the Easiest to Jam

The drawbridge is the highlight of the whole castle and also the part most likely to be rebuilt. My approach: sink two 1x2 bricks with a round hole into the gate opening as pivots and run a long axle through them. Build the deck from 4x8 plates reinforced with 2x6 plates, and connect the sides to the axle with 1x2 bricks with a hole. When lowered, the deck should land exactly on 1x1 stud bricks on the far bank, giving it a natural resting point.

Three pitfalls to watch. First, the pivot holes must line up dead centre with the gate — one stud off and the deck hangs crooked. Second, do not make the deck too thick; two plates is enough, and three or more will catch on the threshold when lowered. Third, leave half a stud of clearance on each side of the bridge, or friction will make it stick as it moves. If you want a genuinely raising bridge, run a long axle through the pivots on the inside of the gate opening and fit handle bricks at both ends — turning them lifts the whole span.

## 4. The Moat and the Overall Atmosphere

Do not build the moat as one flat sheet of dead blue; it looks like plastic. Lay dark blue bricks as the base course, scatter 1x1 trans-light-blue bricks over it to create random depth variation, and dot the edges with plant pieces and 1x1 dark green bricks for reeds. The visual layering jumps straight away. On the far bank, add a short stretch of rocky embankment mixing 2x2 slope bricks with 1x1 irregular bricks so it reads as natural rather than poured.

For the finish: wrap the wall corners with 1x2 slope bricks, lay a dark red brick axis down the centre of the great hall, stand 1x1 round bricks as columns on either side, set the throne on 1x4 steps, and post 6 to 8 minifigures with swords and bows along the crenellations and the gatehouse. The narrative of the medieval castle brick MOC is now complete. The full build runs to roughly 3000 pieces and took me about two evenings of on-and-off work solo.
