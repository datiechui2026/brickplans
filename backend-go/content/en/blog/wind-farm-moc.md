# Wind Farm MOC Review: Three Spinning Turbines, 1,400 Pieces

The short version: this wind farm MOC hangs its whole scene on three working turbines, and it works. The blades turn smoothly, the shapes stay clean and simple, and the build difficulty stays low enough that it makes a great parent-and-child project or a friendly entry point into renewable-energy builds.

## Appearance and Design

Three white turbines are staggered across the terrain rather than lined up in a row, which keeps the layout from looking like a diagram. Each one is built from a tower, a nacelle and three blades.

The towers use a stepped, tapering brick arrangement to suggest the narrowing profile of a real turbine mast, so the silhouette tightens as it rises instead of running straight up like a cylinder. On the side of each nacelle there are small cooling grille details, a nice touch at this scale. The blades are assembled from long bricks combined with airfoil-shaped elements, which gives them a natural curve rather than a flat paddle look.

The base is laid out as rolling hill terrain and dressed with trees, a road and a small substation, so the scene reads as a complete site rather than three models on a plate.

## Structural Stability

A technic cross axle runs through the inside of each tower, locking the base and the nacelle into a single straight line. The result is very rigid — there is no wobble where the tower meets the ground or where the nacelle sits on top.

The blades attach to the front of the nacelle through a rotating connector, so they spin freely without working themselves loose. In testing, holding the nacelle and turning the blades by hand felt smooth, with no rattling or grinding. The center of gravity sits low, so the whole thing stays put on a desk.

## Playability and Value

Every turbine can be spun by hand, and all three can also be linked through gearing in the base to simulate wind sweeping across the whole farm at once. A small switch at the rear of each nacelle lets you reverse the direction the blades turn.

The full model comes in at roughly 1,400 pieces. Sourcing the parts against the brickplan.cn parts list costs about 480 yuan, and the build runs around 4 hours — strong value for the size of the scene you end up with.
