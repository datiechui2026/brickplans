# Star Wars MOC Plans Review: Millennium Falcon & Star Destroyer

Star Wars is one of the most popular IPs in the brick MOC world. From the classic Millennium Falcon to the massive Imperial Star Destroyer, Star Wars replica plans let fans recreate those iconic ships and scenes in brick. This review looks at three vehicle plans side by side — part count, difficulty, and how much of the original ship survives the transition into bricks.

## 1. Classic Star Wars Vehicle MOC Plans Reviewed

### 1. Millennium Falcon

- **Plan name**: Millennium Falcon, 1:50 UCS version
- **Part count**: about 3,500
- **Difficulty**: High
- **Rating**: ★★★★★

This Star Wars replica plan reproduces the Falcon's disc-shaped hull and cockpit detail closely. The circular hull is the biggest challenge in the whole build: getting a smooth curved surface means working with SNOT (Studs Not On Top) techniques throughout, so you are building sideways and upside down rather than straight up. Budget time for the sub-assemblies — the hull comes together in sections before it ever looks like a ship.

### 2. Imperial Star Destroyer

- **Plan name**: Imperial Star Destroyer, 1:100
- **Part count**: about 2,800
- **Difficulty**: High
- **Rating**: ★★★★☆

The Star Destroyer's signature triangular silhouette is the focus of the build. The plan asks for a gradual transition from bow to stern, and that taper only works if the parts are lined up precisely — a single offset plate in the middle of the hull shows up as a visible step in the finished wedge. Expect to check and re-check alignment as you go rather than fixing it at the end.

### 3. X-wing Starfighter

- **Plan name**: X-wing Starfighter, 1:35
- **Part count**: about 1,200
- **Difficulty**: Medium
- **Rating**: ★★★★★

The X-wing's opening wings are the highlight of this plan. A clever gear linkage structure lets the wings spread and fold back, so the model can sit in either attack position or landed configuration. At around 1,200 parts it is the smallest of the three, which makes it the sensible starting point before you commit to a 3,500-part hull.

## 2. Build Difficulty Analysis

### 1. Curved Surfaces

Star Wars ships are full of curved shapes, and those shapes need special building techniques rather than standard stacking. Learning SNOT and curved slope bricks before you start is the difference between a clean hull and a staircase. Practise the technique on a small section first.

### 2. Scale Control

Ships built at different scales cannot be displayed together convincingly. If you plan to combine models into a full Star Wars scene, keep the scale consistent across every plan you build — the 1:50, 1:100 and 1:35 ratios above are not interchangeable.

### 3. Detail Reproduction

Cockpit, engines and weapon systems are what sell the finished model. How faithfully those details are reproduced directly determines how the build reads on the shelf, and it is usually where the part count goes.
