# Dessert Shop MOC Blueprints: Build a Corner Sweet Shop

Here's the short version: for a dessert shop, choose a 32x32-baseplate street-scene blueprint, build the display counter from transparent parts, and if you're new, start with a compact 16x16 design.

A dessert shop is one of the most popular storefronts in any street-scene builder's portfolio. It doesn't need a complicated structure, yet color and detail let it grab attention instantly. A good dessert shop MOC blueprint asks more of its "atmosphere" than a standard building blueprint of the same size — a pink awning, a clear cake display case, a cute ice-cream-cone sign above the door. Put those elements together and you have a corner shop people would stop to photograph.

## Why the dessert shop theme is so popular

Dessert shops are one of the few genuinely good-looking subjects in the street-scene genre. Compared with a serious bank or police station, a dessert shop carries a warm, sweet character, and its color palette is wide open — anything from macaron pink to mint green works. Many builders treat a dessert shop as their entry point into street scenes: the parts list isn't exotic, the structure is mostly walls, counter and roof, a beginner can finish it, and an experienced builder can mod it. On top of that, dessert shop MOC blueprints exist in wildly different styles — Japanese sweet shops, French macaron stores, American ice-cream parlors — so there is always a plan to match. That's why the theme stays near the top of the popularity charts.

## The core design highlights of a dessert shop

Three areas matter most when you read a dessert shop MOC blueprint. First, the display counter: build a glass case from transparent parts and stack macarons and cakes inside with 1x1 round tiles — the sense of depth appears immediately. Second, the storefront and awning: a striped awning can be built row by row from slope bricks, and cream white paired with pink, or red-and-white stripes, are the most recognizable choices. Third, the roof decoration: a large ice cream cone, a strawberry or a piped-cream shape on the roof is the "signature element" that separates a dessert shop from an ordinary storefront. Strong blueprints also add small scenes — potted plants, a cat — on window sills and in corners so the building feels alive.

## How to choose the right blueprint

Before you pick a dessert shop MOC blueprint, confirm three things: whether the size matches your existing street-scene modules (usually a 32x32 stud baseplate), whether the parts list is complete (color codes and quantities listed), and whether the instructions are clearly broken into steps. Favor blueprints that include renders and a parts count so you don't run out of pieces halfway through. If your parts stock is limited, look for designs that lean heavily on common basic bricks — those are also easy to recolor. For more on choosing street-scene plans, see our [brick blueprint tutorial section](https://brickplan.cn/en/blog).

## Common parts and color advice

The most-used palette for a dessert shop is pink, white, cream yellow and mint green, with deep pink or chocolate brown as accents. Transparent parts go to shop windows and display cases; 1x1 round tiles are the universal part for macarons and cake rolls; curved slopes and round plates suit ice cream cones and piped cream. For the floor, alternate light and dark 1x1 bricks in a checkerboard — cheap and effective, and a standard move in many dessert shop MOC blueprints.

## Beginner vs. advanced approaches

Beginners should start with a compact 16x16 or 32x32 dessert shop blueprint and get the walls, door, windows and counter right first, copying the colors straight from the plan. Advanced builders can shift the focus to interiors and lighting: SNOT techniques for a cake stand, transparent parts plus light boards to simulate window lighting, or even a hinged facade so the interior can be opened up for play. At any level, comparing a few dessert shop MOC blueprints before you start will make your corner shop more your own.
