# Football Stadium MOC: Pitch Size, Grass, Goals and Stands

Start with the conclusion: the first decision on any football stadium build is the size of the pitch. A small scale at 32x48 studs works for desktop display, a medium 48x64 fits a properly proportioned centre line, penalty area and goal area, and only then do you start stacking stands on top.

## Plan the pitch dimensions before anything else

A football stadium is the most classic subject in the sports scene MOC category. The first step is settling the size: a small scale at 32x48 studs suits desktop display, a medium scale at 48x64 studs leaves room for a correctly proportioned centre line, penalty area and goal area, and a large scale can run to 64x80 studs or more for maximum detail. Before you build, mark the line positions on the baseplate with pencil or stickers — the centre line, centre circle, penalty area and corner arcs all need their space reserved. Leave the width of a running track around all four sides of the pitch, otherwise stands sitting right against the touchline look cramped.

## Grass texture: getting the look of a real pitch

The grass treatment is critical. A single shade of green reads flat, so use two or three different shades of green to create a striped mowing pattern — the way a real pitch is cut. The method is simple: lay alternating light and dark green in bands 8 studs wide, with the stripes running along the long axis of the pitch. Use lighter green pieces to distinguish the centre circle and penalty areas. Add a line of white plates between the grass edge and the running track for the touchline, and mark the goal area with white pieces as well. For extra realism, drop a small white round plate at the centre spot for the kickoff point.

## Goal structure and netting

The goal is the core structure of the whole stadium. Build it in white: two posts plus a crossbar, with proportions that hold together — the post height should be roughly 0.75 times the goal width. There are two ways to handle the net. The simple version hangs a transparent mesh piece or a piece of gauze behind the frame. The detailed version weaves white thin bars into a grid texture. The goal must be anchored to the pitch, so add a connector underneath that plugs into the baseplate. You can also build one or two spare balls from a white round piece with a black pentagon sticker, placed in the centre circle or beside the goal, which gives the scene a bit of story.

## Stand structure: from a single tier to multiple decks

The stands decide how imposing the build feels. For a small scene, build a single tier: lay out the steps with slope pieces and seat a few rows of minifigures on top. For a large scene, go double deck — dark pieces for the VIP boxes on the lower level, slope pieces for ordinary seating above. Use bright colours such as red and blue for the seats so they contrast with the green pitch. Minifigures are the key to atmosphere: ordinary spectator figures can repeat with different hair colours and outfits, and put a few players in kit in the front row. Cap the stands with a roof made from curved pieces or large panels to get that covered-arena feel, and the whole thing instantly looks bigger.

## Lighting and supporting facilities

Lighting lifts the night-time effect. Build a floodlight pylon at each of the four corners, use transparent pieces for the lamp housings, and fit light bricks or LED strip inside so the pitch lights up like a real ground. For supporting facilities, add a dugout bench with a canopy at the side of the pitch, advertising boards (stickers or printed pieces) running around the perimeter, and a scoreboard spelling out 0:0 with number pieces. Outside the pitch you can add a running track built from dark red plates circling the field. You do not have to do all of this at once — finish the pitch, goals and stands first, then add lighting and accessories gradually.

## Atmosphere details and display tips

To give the build more atmosphere, add movement: a player minifigure in the centre circle ready to kick off, a referee with a raised flag on the touchline, and waving flags in the stands made from a bar and a small flag piece. For display, put the stadium in the middle of a table and cover it with a simple acrylic case to keep dust off. If you want a playable version, make the goals removable so they are easy to reposition. A football stadium scene works both as a display piece and as something to build and play with alongside kids, and once finished it looks impressive whether it sits in a study or a living room.

## FAQ

**Q: How big should a brick football stadium MOC be?**
A: Decide by purpose. A small scale of 32x48 studs suits desktop display; a medium scale of 48x64 studs fits a correctly proportioned centre line, penalty area and goal area; go bigger and you need to think about how many tiers of stands and roof you are adding.

**Q: How do you get real texture into the grass?**
A: Alternate light and dark green plates in stripes running along the long axis of the pitch. Set the centre line and penalty area lines into the grass surface with white plates at the same height so nothing sticks up — visually that is the cleanest result.

**Q: How do you give the stands depth?**
A: Raise them step by step from a single tier to multiple decks: each row of seating rises by one plate thickness, slope pieces form the steps, and mixed-colour minifigures fill the seats. Once you go several tiers high, add internal support so the middle does not sag.

**Q: How do you build the goal and the extras?**
A: Use thin cylindrical pieces for the goal frame and hose or mesh pieces for the net. Then add corner flags, pitch-side advertising boards and lighting pylons — once those details are in place, the atmosphere of the whole stadium comes through.
