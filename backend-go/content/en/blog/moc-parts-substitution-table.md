# MOC Parts Substitution Guide: Rare and Odd Parts

The short version first: the core of any MOC parts substitution plan comes down to three matching rules — same connection, same dimensions, same strength. Roughly 80% of missing parts can be swapped directly for Gobricks or domestic clone parts, at about one third of the price of the original LEGO element. The list below sorts substitutions into three groups — rare-colour parts, special-angle parts and general shortages — so you can check against it before you build and save most of the time you would otherwise spend hunting for pieces.

## 1. Three Things to Confirm Before You Substitute

Before you start swapping, confirm three points. First, the connection type: studs, technic pins or ball joints. Second, the actual dimensions — measure with a caliper, and keep the error under 0.2 mm. Third, the load position: clone parts are not recommended for load-bearing structures. Get these three right and the substitution will almost always work.

## 2. Step 1: Substituting Rare-Colour Parts

Rare-colour parts — sand green, dark orange, translucent fluorescent elements — are often long out of production in the LEGO system, and single pieces get resold for several yuan or even tens of yuan. The approach here is to keep the shape and change the colour:

- LEGO 30414 (1x2 curved plate) missing in sand green → Gobricks 30414 in any colour, around 0.3 yuan per piece;
- LEGO 30374 (4x4 transparent light cover) → Gobricks 30374 in transparent, with light transmission close to the original and good general compatibility;
- For transparent parts, Gobricks is the first choice: the price is only one fifth of LEGO's, and the visual difference is very small.

## 3. Step 2: Substituting Special-Angle Parts

Special-angle parts — 45-degree slopes, 135-degree direction changers and similar — come up constantly in MOC building. When you run short, swap them like this:

- LEGO 43710/43711 (1x2 slope 45 degrees, left and right pair) → the matching Gobricks part number, which clutches tightly with no wobble;
- LEGO 32034 (technic angle arm with holes) → clone 32034, but watch the hole tolerance; a slightly tight pin is normal;
- When LEGO 47457 (1x2 with ball joint) is out of stock, you can substitute a 4733 combined with a ball joint pin — lower cost, same function.

## 4. Step 3: General Shortages and Verification

For general shortages — 1x1, 1x2 and 2x4 basic bricks — simply top up with Gobricks, but keep an eye on colour difference: Gobricks and LEGO vary slightly even at the same colour code. On visible surfaces, replace a whole area at once so you do not end up with one dark patch next to a light one. Once all substitutions are done, test-fit the load-bearing nodes and moving joints against the instructions and confirm there is no looseness and no interference before you carry on building. That way you avoid discovering a problem halfway through and having to take the model apart again.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I choose a MOC parts substitution so it will actually fit?**
A: Confirm the connection type and dimensions of the original part first, then find a substitute with the same connection and the same size. For studded parts, check the stud count; for pin parts, check diameter and length. Keep the error within 0.2 mm and it will almost always fit normally.

**Q: Can Gobricks and LEGO parts be mixed?**
A: Yes. Gobricks moulds to LEGO specifications, the 8 mm stud pitch matches LEGO, and pins and cross-axle connections are interchangeable — mixing does not affect the build. Only transparent parts and special colours are best sourced from Gobricks first, to keep the overall look consistent.

**Q: Is it worth buying original parts for rare colours?**
A: No. Out-of-production colours like sand green are often resold at over 5 yuan per piece, while a Gobricks part in the same shape costs around 0.3 yuan. You can barely see the difference on a finished model, so the value is far better.

**Q: How big is the difference between clone parts and LEGO parts?**
A: The difference is mainly in tolerance and material. Clone parts have slightly looser tolerances, so load-bearing structures may end up a little loose or a little tight, but for ordinary visible parts the difference is very small. Use LEGO or Gobricks for load-bearing and drivetrain positions, and clones are fine for exterior details.
