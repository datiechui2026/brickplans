# Brick Tanker Truck MOC: Blueprint and Build Guide

Here is the short version first: the hard part of a brick tanker truck MOC is the entire cylindrical tank. Curved elements need to come from the same batch number to sit flush, and the chassis should be built as longitudinal beams first, with cross members added afterwards. A small model at roughly 1:20 scale comes in around 600–700 pieces, which makes it the right place for a beginner to start.

Tankers are one of the most recognizable subjects in road-transport building: a smooth cylindrical barrel, bold hazmat markings, and a silhouette that stands out instantly next to an ordinary truck. But because the tank is one continuous curved surface, a tanker is actually harder to build in brick than a standard truck — get it wrong and you end up with a square box dragging a round drum. This guide covers where to find blueprints, how to build the tank and chassis, what to check on the parts list, and the questions beginners ask most.

## Where to Find Blueprints

When you go looking for a tanker truck MOC blueprint, there are a few directions worth prioritizing. The overseas Rebrickable community produces rigorously structured English instructions that suit experienced builders. Chinese-language brick forums and Tieba threads are friendlier to read and share player write-ups, but check the resolution of the instructions before you commit. Digital blueprint bundles sold on e-commerce platforms often pack in a large number of truck subjects, which makes them good value. Whichever route you take, favor versions that include multi-angle renders and a clearly itemized parts list.

## Tank Structure: The Soul of the Tanker

The tank is one long cylindrical curved surface, and that is the core difficulty of the build. There are two common approaches. Small-scale models use round bricks and curved bricks to form the barrel directly — simple and efficient. Large-scale builds use SNOT side-on construction finished with curved plates, which produces a smoother curved shell. Both ends of the tank need to close off into a domed head, usually with wedge elements and curved bricks forming the transition; skip this and the ends look harshly flat-cut. Don't forget the pressure-relief valve and the filler port on top of the tank — these are the signature details that separate a tanker from a plain box-bodied truck.

## Chassis and Saddle Mount Structure

The chassis is what decides whether the tanker stands up properly. Tankers generally split into two types: the rigid truck, which carries its own cab and cargo chassis in a compact layout, and the semi-trailer, where a tractor unit pulls the tank body and the tank section needs its own chassis beams plus a saddle connection. When building, lay the longitudinal beams first, then reinforce with cross members so the tank doesn't sag once it is mounted. If the blueprint includes suspension, fit the springs in the specified order so the compression and rebound action stays smooth.

## Parts List and Scale

When you check the parts list, watch three categories closely: curved bricks and wedge elements (the main tank material), transparent or white cylindrical elements (to simulate sight glasses for the oil level), and red warning stickers or elements (the hazmat markings). On scale, a small tanker at around 1:20 uses roughly 600–700 pieces and suits beginners; a large 1:10 build can include opening cab doors and a tank that detaches separately, but the parts count may double. Confirm the finished dimensions before you order and plan where the display cabinet will go in advance.

## FAQ

**Q: Where do I find brick tanker truck MOC blueprints?**
A: Overseas community plans are rigorous but mostly in English; Chinese-language forums and Tieba threads are easier to read; e-commerce blueprint bundles often group several truck subjects together; and on BrickPlan you can search the tanker keyword for Chinese-labelled instructions and parts lists.

**Q: What do I do if the tank surface isn't flat?**
A: It is usually a mix of mismatched curved element types. Try to use the same batch number across one section of curvature, and press each layer fully home as you go. If a step appears, pull the misaligned layer back to the previous ring and re-lay it — that is easier than forcing it down locally.

**Q: How do I reinforce a loose connection between tank and chassis?**
A: Add locating pins and reinforcement beams under the tank, and supplement the longitudinal beams with a cross member on each side to grip the saddle. Keep the tractor-to-trailer joint removable so it stays easy to take apart without losing rigidity.

**Q: How many parts does a tanker MOC need, and which scale should I pick?**
A: A small model at around 1:20 runs about 600–700 pieces and suits beginners; a 1:10 build can include opening cab doors and a removable tank, but the parts count may double. Confirm the finished dimensions before ordering.
