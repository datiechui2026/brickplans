# Brick Glider MOC Tutorial: Wing Curves and Build Guide

Bottom line first: the make-or-break point of a glider MOC is the wing curve. Run slope bricks into hinge pieces segment by segment and you get a smooth airfoil. A glider with about 900 parts and a 52 cm wingspan costs roughly CNY 110 for the plan plus the parts, takes about 4 hours to assemble, and suits intermediate builders well.

## Step 1: Pick a Plan by Wingspan and Scale

Start with wingspan and scale when you choose a glider plan. A wingspan around 50 cm is the sweet spot: much smaller and the details get cramped, much larger and display space becomes a problem. This 900-piece glider runs 36 cm long with a 52 cm wingspan, roughly 1:48 scale, which sits nicely on a bookshelf. When buying, check whether the plan includes a parts list so you don't discover a rare part is missing after checkout.

## Step 2: Fuselage and Cockpit

Build the fuselage from the nose backward. Wedge bricks taper the nose to a point, and curved transparent pieces form the canopy, which opens so you can seat a minifigure inside. Use 2x4 bricks under the cockpit as the load-bearing structure, and reinforce the point where the wings attach; adding one technic beam to each side of the fuselage is the usual fix for wings that sag. You can leave the fuselage interior hollow to cut weight — the finished model comes in around 0.6 kg.

## Step 3: Shaping the Wing Curve

This is where a glider MOC shows real craft. Lay the upper wing surface with 1x4 slope bricks, segment by segment, offsetting each segment by half a stud so the curve reads smoother. Build the lower surface from plates, which produces the classic cambered-top, flat-bottom airfoil. Turn the wingtips up with hinge pieces to form the glider's winglets. Keep the left and right wings symmetrical: build one side, then match the other against it and hold the error within 2 studs.

## Step 4: Tail and Details

Assemble the horizontal and vertical stabilizers from thin plates plus slope bricks, fixed with hinge pieces so the angle can be adjusted slightly. Finish with an antenna, nose livery and similar details, and apply 2-3 decorative stickers across the wing surfaces to lift the overall finish. Total build time is about 4 hours, and the glider's lines come out clean enough that a static display reads close to the real aircraft.

## FAQ

**Q: Where can I download brick glider MOC plans?**
A: Search "glider" on brickplan.cn and you'll find several plans. PDFs cost about CNY 15-25 with permanent download after purchase, and include a parts list plus instructions of roughly 120 steps.

**Q: Is a glider MOC hard to build?**
A: Medium. The difficulty concentrates on making the wing curves symmetrical; the fuselage and tail are simpler. About 900 parts take roughly 4 hours, which suits builders with 2-3 sets of experience who want to level up on wing technique.

**Q: Can a brick glider actually glide?**
A: No. A brick glider is a static model, shown on a stand or display cradle. A few plans allow a linked propeller, but there is no real gliding ability — don't test it with weights or throw it.

**Q: How much does a glider MOC cost?**
A: The plan is about CNY 20 and the parts about CNY 90, so roughly CNY 110 total. If your spare-parts bins are well stocked, buying only the plan and sourcing bricks yourself can bring it down to around CNY 70; the number of rare parts drives the final cost.

**Q: How is a glider MOC different from an airliner MOC?**
A: A glider is unpowered, has a proportionally huge wingspan and a long, slender fuselage, with the wing curve as the focus. An airliner has engine nacelles, a thick fuselage and far more detail. A glider uses fewer parts and builds faster, which makes it the better subject for practicing wing techniques.
