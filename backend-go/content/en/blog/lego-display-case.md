# LEGO Display Case Guide: Dust, UV, Shelf Heights and Lighting

The short version: get a case with UV-filtering glass doors, measure your tallest, deepest and heaviest models before you fix shelf heights and load ratings, and light it with 4000K LED strips.

Once your built sets and MOCs start piling up, where to put them becomes a real problem. Leave them on an open bookshelf and a layer of dust shows up within months; leave them in direct sun and the ABS parts slowly yellow. A **LEGO display case** is not just storage — it protects your builds and lifts the look of the room. This guide covers dust and UV protection, shelf height and load planning, lighting, and budget so you get it right the first time.

## Why You Need a Display Case: Dust and UV

LEGO parts are made mainly of ABS plastic. Long-term direct sunlight oxidizes it and turns it yellow, and white bricks show it worst — once yellowed, the change is essentially irreversible. Dust dulls the surface, and dust packed into gaps is especially hard to clean out. The first job of a **LEGO display case** is to block both: choose glass doors with a UV-filtering coating where possible, and keep the case away from west-facing windows and the direct throw of spotlights. In practice, builds behind closed doors for two years show a white-brick color difference you can see with the naked eye. If you have cats, a doored case matters even more — it keeps bricks off the floor.

## Shelf Height and Load Planning: Measure First, Buy Second

Before buying, measure your three extremes: the tallest, the deepest and the heaviest build. Modular street buildings sit on a 32×32 stud footprint, so the case needs at least 40 cm of depth; long models like the Titanic need 60 cm and up. Allocate shelf heights to your builds: 35-45 cm for standard street scenes and vehicles, 60-70 cm for tall structures like castle towers, and the top shelf for your flagship set. Load ratings matter just as much: particleboard cases should carry no more than 15 kg per shelf, solid wood can go to 30 kg. Don't force large models onto one shelf — use acrylic risers to lift shorter builds so one shelf does the work of two.

## Glass Doors or Open Shelving

Open shelving photographs well and is easy to reach into, but a week without dusting puts a layer back on and all your dust-proofing is wasted. For a **LEGO display case**, favor glass doors: at least 4 mm glass thickness and smooth, durable hinges. Sliding doors save space compared with hinged doors but seal slightly worse — add a dust strip along the gap to compensate. If you already own open shelving, cover your most valuable builds with individual acrylic dust covers; at a few dozen yuan each, they are excellent value.

## Lighting and Backdrop Setup

Lighting is the soul of a display case. Fit an LED strip on every shelf, with a color temperature around 4000K neutral white — it renders brick colors faithfully without glare. Avoid ordinary spotlights: some fixtures emit UV and accelerate plastic aging over long exposure. Use a dark grey or black acrylic back panel to make brick silhouettes pop, which is especially noticeable with light-colored builds. Glass doors reflect easily, so mount the strip on the inside front edge of the door and angle it downward to cut reflections. Add a timer switch so the lights come on for a few hours a day — low power, good atmosphere.

## Budget Tiers and Brand Advice

The market splits into three tiers. **Entry, ¥300-600**: an IKEA Billy bookcase (40 cm deep) with an add-on glass door and dust strip — fine for getting started. **Mid-range, ¥1000-2000**: a ready-made cabinet with glass doors, or a local custom build with shelf heights and depths set to your models — one step, done properly. **Flagship, ¥3000 and up**: a full-glass locking cabinet with a dedicated lighting system, suited to collectors with a hundred-plus builds. Domestic custom cabinets generally cost 30-50% less than imported brands with little difference in board quality and finish; the key checks are adjustable shelves and durable hardware.

## FAQ

**Q: Why does a LEGO display case need doors?**
A: Because ABS plastic oxidizes and yellows under long-term direct sunlight — white bricks worst of all, and almost irreversibly — while dust dulls the parts. A case with UV-filtering glass doors, kept for two years, shows a color difference visible to the eye.

**Q: What dimensions should I measure before buying a display case?**
A: Measure your tallest, deepest and heaviest three builds: modular street buildings have a 32×32 stud footprint and need at least 40 cm of depth; long models start at 60 cm; allow 35-45 cm of shelf height for standard street scenes and 60-70 cm for towers.

**Q: What lighting won't damage the parts?**
A: An LED strip on each shelf at around 4000K neutral white, which renders brick colors faithfully without glare. Avoid ordinary spotlights that emit UV and accelerate plastic aging; mount the strip on the inside front edge of the door, angled downward.

**Q: How should the budget tiers break down?**
A: Three tiers: ¥300-600 entry, a bookcase plus add-on glass doors and a dust strip; ¥1000-2000 mid-range, a ready-made glass-door cabinet or a local custom build; ¥3000 and up, a full-glass locking cabinet with dedicated lighting.

**Q: Do cheaper custom cabinets cut corners?**
A: Domestic custom cabinets usually cost 30-50% less than imported brands without a big gap in board quality or finish. Check that shelves are adjustable and the hardware is durable before ordering.

**Q: What if I only have open shelving right now?**
A: Use individual acrylic dust covers over your most valuable builds. They cost a few dozen yuan each and deliver a lot of protection for the money.

**Q: How do I stop LED strips from reflecting in the glass?**
A: Mount them on the inside front edge of the door and point them diagonally downward, and use a dark grey or black acrylic back panel so the light reads as a glow around the bricks rather than a mirror image.

**Q: How do I fit more models into a fixed-height case?**
A: Use acrylic risers to lift shorter builds so they sit above the taller ones on the same shelf. That turns one shelf into two usable levels without exceeding the 15 kg particleboard or 30 kg solid-wood limit.

**Q: Where should the case not go?**
A: Away from west-facing windows and out of the direct beam of spotlights. Even with UV-filtering glass, a case in constant harsh sun will shorten the life of the ABS parts inside.

**Q: Can I keep a case in a room with cats?**
A: Yes, and a doored case is the reason to do it. Glass doors keep paws out and stop bricks ending up scattered across the floor.

**Q: What glass thickness should I look for?**
A: At least 4 mm, with smooth, durable hinges. Sliding doors save space but seal less tightly, so add a dust strip along the door gap.

**Q: Is a full-glass locking cabinet worth the flagship price?**
A: For collectors with a hundred or more builds, yes — the lock and dedicated lighting system are the main draws. Below that scale, the mid-range tier with adjustable shelves and local customization usually gives better value.

**Q: What else should I check before paying?**
A: Confirm the shelves are adjustable, the hardware is durable, the depth clears your deepest model (40 cm minimum, 60 cm for long sets) and the shelf load rating suits your heaviest build.
