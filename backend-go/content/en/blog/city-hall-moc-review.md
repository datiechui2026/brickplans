# Brick City Hall MOC Review: Classic European Civic Building

Of all the single buildings that define a modular street, the city hall is the one many builders dream of putting at the end of the block. This review takes a close look at a brick city hall MOC instruction set to see how well it balances a grand exterior against the fine detail inside.

## Exterior Design: A Grand European Facade

The MOC gets its overall proportions right, and that is what sells the look. A tall central tower rises above a symmetrical two-wing layout, reproducing the formal, imposing character of classical European civic architecture. The dome and clock tower are the standout passages: curved bricks and slope pieces are combined so the roofline steps back in layers, giving the silhouette real depth. Even tucked into a corner of a street diorama, it reads as the visual anchor of the scene.

## Interior Details: Craftsmanship in Every Room

If the facade is the public face, the interior is where this instruction set earns its reputation. The ground-floor hall is built as a double-height open space, and the floor-to-ceiling windows let light through so the room feels airy rather than cramped. The functional zones are all accounted for: a council chamber, a mayor's office and an archive room. Each room is furnished to match, with a writing desk, bookshelves and heraldic emblem decorations on the walls, so the building feels inhabited rather than hollow.

## The Build Experience and Part Usage

The steps are divided clearly and the instruction diagrams are accurate, so even a first-time builder can work through them without confusion. The designer is adventurous with parts, leaning heavily on upside-down building and side-mounted connections. That approach keeps the structure rigid while reducing reliance on rare or specialised elements, which matters if you plan to source the parts yourself. The model totals roughly 5,000 pieces and takes about 10 to 14 hours to assemble. That puts it in the upper-middle range of build commitments, but there is something interesting in nearly every step.

## Verdict: A Centerpiece Worth Collecting

This brick city hall MOC strikes a solid balance between a monumental exterior, a carefully furnished interior and an enjoyable build. Displayed on its own, it holds up as a standalone piece; dropped into a full modular street, it brings instant recognisability and a lot to look at. If you are after a civic building instruction set that is imposing without sacrificing interior detail, this one is easy to recommend as a centrepiece for a street-scene collection.
