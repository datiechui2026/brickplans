# Chinese Mecha Brick Series Guide: Mould King, SEMBO & More

![Finished mecha model from a Chinese brick series](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/mecha-series-guide/mecha-series-guide-1.jpg)

Mecha is the one brick category where poseability decides everything. A kit that can't hold a pose, or whose joints go limp after a week on the shelf, is a kit you stop looking at. Chinese brands have pushed hard in this direction over the past two years, and Gobricks, SEMBO, Mould King and Qiji all now run their own mecha lines. This guide works through the main series by articulation and accuracy, so you spend your money once.

## Why Chinese Mecha Bricks Are Worth a Look

![Building steps for a Chinese mecha brick kit](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/mecha-series-guide/mecha-series-guide-2.jpg)

Compared with street scenes and architecture, mecha kits put much heavier demands on structure design, working joints and part tolerance. Every limb is a load-bearing assembly, and every joint has to hold a pose under the weight of the model. Previously, if you wanted to build mecha, the only real option was expensive imported brands. Domestic part precision and original design have now caught up, while prices stay a fraction of what imported kits cost. That gap is the fundamental reason these Chinese mecha series have taken off.

## Popular Chinese Mecha Brick Series

![Close-up of brick and part detail on a mecha build](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/mecha-series-guide/mecha-series-guide-3.jpg)

### Mould King: The Technic-Style Mecha Pick

Mould King is known for its technic parts, and its mecha line leans into mechanical feel and poseability, with heavy use of gears, linkages and power components. Some flagship machines accept motors, so the finished model can walk and swing its arms under remote control. Recommend these to advanced builders who want a demanding build and dynamic play rather than a static display piece. Mould King mecha kits typically run CNY 300-900, with part counts generally above 2,000 pieces, which makes the value for money stand out.

### SEMBO: Popular Mecha and Licensed Designs

SEMBO's mecha series sells on shape accuracy and a broad IP roster, covering several classic anime mecha designs. Printed parts are crisp, the color choices are bold, and the finished model looks strong on a shelf. SEMBO sits in the mid-range: CNY 200-500 buys you a mid-size mecha. It suits collectors who display their builds and care about how faithful the source design is.

### Qiji: The Friendly Entry Point

Qiji focuses on kids and the beginner market. Its mecha sets keep part counts moderate, building difficulty approachable and instruction manuals clear, with prices mostly in the CNY 50-150 range. That makes them well suited to newcomers, students, or parents building alongside their children. Structure and articulation won't match what Mould King offers, but as a first mecha, Qiji is more than enough.

### StarBricks: Original Design First

StarBricks mecha sets lead with original mechanical designs and striking looks, deliberately taking a different route from licensed kits. The silhouettes are exaggerated, the detailing is dense, and they're popular with younger builders who want something distinctive. Prices land around CNY 100-400, which suits anyone who doesn't want to see their model sitting on everyone else's shelf.

### Other Brands Worth Watching

Beyond those four, Gobricks, Pantasy and Double Eagle all put in solid work in the mecha space. Gobricks is known for part precision and suits finely detailed mecha builds; Pantasy focuses on large collector-grade mecha; Double Eagle has real technical depth in technic-based mecha. Weigh budget and requirements and pick accordingly.

## How to Choose in One Paragraph

Judge a Chinese mecha kit on four dimensions: budget, build difficulty, poseability and subject preference. Beginners go with Qiji, advanced builders with Mould King, collectors with SEMBO, and anyone chasing something new with StarBricks.
