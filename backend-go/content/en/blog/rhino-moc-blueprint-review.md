# Brick Rhino MOC Review: Armor-Plate Hide and Accuracy

Animal builds remain one of the most popular themes among brick MOC builders, and the rhino is a favourite challenge: its silhouette is unmistakable and its bulk gives it real presence. This review looks at one widely shared brick rhino MOC blueprint and asks how well the designer turns individual bricks into the spirit of a walking armored vehicle.

## Recreating the Heavy Body and Armored Hide

The rhino's defining feature is its natural armor of thick, folded skin. Working through the blueprint, you can see the designer leaning heavily on slope bricks, curved slopes and sideways building techniques, layering them up to suggest the raised contours of the shoulders, ribs and hindquarters. The cleverer move is the color work: light and dark gray bricks are alternated across the body to create highlights and shadows in the folds, so the finished animal reads as if it is genuinely wearing a plated hide.

Many animal MOCs chase a smooth, sculpted surface. This blueprint deliberately keeps the corners and seams of the bricks visible, which actually sits closer to the coarse, heavy texture of real rhino skin. For builders who enjoy studying technique, the transition from neck to shoulder alone is worth the price of admission.

## Horn, Skull and Load-Bearing Legs

The head carries the character of the whole animal, and the signature one or two horns matter most of all. In this blueprint the horn is built as a tapering cone, stepping down layer by layer with the stud orientation rotated as it rises so the tip can curve naturally upward. The skull below it is assembled from small plates combined with hinge elements to give a heavy, downward-hung shape, and its proportions connect cleanly to the body.

Legs are where large animal MOCs most often fall apart. This blueprint puts a complete internal skeleton inside the legs and uses widened plates and grip studs at the feet, so the rhino holds a standing pose securely. Even if you nudge it or change its angle slightly, the centre of gravity holds and nothing collapses, which matters a great deal for a display piece.

## Accuracy, Difficulty and Who It Suits

On overall accuracy, the blueprint scores highly on body outline, skin texture and the animal's attitude. The one compromise is the hip area, which looks a little boxy in exchange for structural rigidity; it does not hurt the overall impression. The main building challenges are the curved transitions in the skin folds and locking the head angle in place, both of which demand careful step-by-step checking. If it is your first time through the build, keep a sorting tray or compartment box within reach.

That makes the blueprint best suited to intermediate and advanced builders who already have some experience and want a bigger animal MOC as their next challenge. If you are a display-focused builder who chases fine detail, it is still worth your time. Beginners would do better to start with a simpler animal blueprint and come back to this beast later.
