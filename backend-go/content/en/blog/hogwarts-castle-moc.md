# Hogwarts Castle MOC Review: 2,450-Piece Modular Build

**Difficulty**: ★★★★☆ (intermediate)
**Piece count**: 2,450
**Recommended brands**: Gobricks, Panlos
**Model type**: micro-scale full panorama | **Estimated build time**: 8–10 hours

Hogwarts is one of the most tempting subjects in brick-built architecture MOCs, and also one of the most abandoned. Too many towers, too many fragmented roofs, and one wrong colour choice turns the whole thing muddy. This Hogwarts Castle MOC solves the problem with micro scale: it keeps the full-skyline readability while holding the part count at 2,450, which is a size an intermediate builder can realistically finish.

## 1. Accuracy: what micro scale keeps and what it drops

The logic of micro scale is simple — protect the silhouette, sacrifice the detail. The instructions keep the four most recognisable structures: the main tower, the astronomy tower, the Great Hall and the courtyard, using height differences and roof pitch to create the castle's staggered profile. Window grids and brick-line texture are handled with printed elements and slope pieces instead of individual bricks. The payoff is a model that stands on a 40cm square baseplate and still looks commanding in a display cabinet.

## 2. Modular design: build in parts, display as one

The standout feature here is modularity — each building can be completed on its own and then joined. That gives you three advantages. You can work through the 2,450 pieces in stages instead of one marathon session. You can separate sections when moving or cleaning the model. And if you later want to extend the courtyard or add another tower, you simply add a module rather than tearing the build apart.

For castle projects, modularity is close to mandatory. If you want the general techniques for going from foundation to tower, see [How to Build a MOC Castle: A Step-by-Step Guide from Foundation to Tower](/en/blog/how-to-build-moc-castle). The layered foundation, offset wall coursing and round-tower handling described there apply directly to this micro-scale design.

## 3. Parts and colour: the grey system is the soul of it

Roughly 80% of a castle's character comes from colour. This plan uses Gobricks light grey and dark grey as the base, dark grey for roofs and light grey for walls, with white trim lines to lift the edges. The courtyard adds a small amount of sand and green elements for ground and planting. The reference scheme is "three greys plus one warm tone" — go past three greys and the model starts to look muddy.

One caveat: grey is a mid-tone, so batch-to-batch and brand-to-brand variation is relatively visible. Try to source the exterior faces from one brand and one batch, and when ordering replacements check against a photo of the finished model rather than going by the colour name alone.

## 4. Key build points

1. **Start with the main tower.** It sets the height reference for the whole model; build it first and work outward, and proportions stay under control.
2. **Keep roof pitch consistent.** Match slope direction and angle across the model — once roofs disagree, the build falls apart visually.
3. **Leave play in the module joints.** Connect modules with pins and connectors rather than forcing a hard fit, so you can disassemble repeatedly.
4. **Place the scene dressing last.** Minifigures, lighting and vegetation go on at the end; get the architecture structurally solid first.
5. **Pre-route any lighting.** If you want illumination, leave cable channels during the build — retrofitting wiring afterwards is genuinely painful.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this Hogwarts Castle MOC have a lot of parts?**
A: 2,450 pieces, which is intermediate territory. Because it is modular, you can build it in 4–5 sections by building rather than in one go; measured total time is around 8–10 hours.

**Q: Which brick brand should I use for this plan?**
A: Gobricks and Panlos are recommended. Gobricks has stable colour control and suits large wall surfaces, while Panlos is better value and works well for the courtyard and interior filler — mixing the two is the most cost-effective route.

**Q: Will micro scale look too blurry?**
A: No. Micro scale preserves the architectural silhouette and roof outline, and the tower height differences, roof pitch and Great Hall are all still recognisable. From a distance it reads as more of a castle than a brick-by-brick replica.

**Q: Can I add lighting?**
A: Yes. The plan leaves space for scene dressing and lighting components, and it is best to route the wiring during the build itself. One light point in the tower and one in the Great Hall gives the best effect.
