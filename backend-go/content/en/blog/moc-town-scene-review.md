# MOC Town Scene Review: 5,200 Pieces, 12 Buildings

The short version: this town MOC from the brickplan catalogue uses roughly 5,200 pieces to build a miniature town with 12 buildings and one main street. The overall level of finish is very high, and it ranks among the street-scene MOC instructions most worth picking up in 2026. The catch is a long build and a big footprint, so it suits patient, experienced builders rather than first-timers.

## Scene Scale and Accuracy

The finished town measures about 90 cm long and 35 cm wide. It contains 12 buildings in distinct styles, including a cafe, a bookstore, a flower shop and a police station, plus around 200 scene accessories such as street lamps, trees and cars. The facades are richly detailed: signage, windows and doors, and rooftop chimneys are all reproduced, so viewed from the front it reads almost like a miniature commercial street. The road is built from gray plates and includes printed crosswalk and manhole cover elements, which lifts the realism considerably.

Where the design shines is in the small decisions rather than the headline count. Each shopfront has its own identity instead of repeating one facade with different colors, and the accessories are distributed along the street rather than clustered in one display corner. That distribution is what makes the finished build look like a place people live in, not a row of display models on a shelf.

## Build Experience and Structural Design

A full build takes about 20 hours. The instructions are split into 12 modules, one per building, at 2-3 hours each, so the project can be spread across many sessions without losing track of where you are. Every building sits on its own base, and the modular approach makes storage and transport genuinely easy.

The buildings connect to each other through locating pins on the baseplate, and the assembled town holds together firmly rather than falling apart when nudged. The instructions are digital, and brickplan supports online zoom, which is noticeably clearer than a printed booklet when you are hunting for a single part in a dense wall of bricks.

The baseplate is built in two sections. To move the town, you separate it into two halves first and carry them one at a time, which means a single person can reposition the whole layout without help. For anyone who dusts and cleans on a schedule, that detail matters more than it sounds.

## Value and Who It's For

At 5,200 pieces for 468 yuan, the cost works out to roughly 0.09 yuan per piece. Add the roughly 5% share of printed and special elements and the value sits at the upper end of comparable street-scene MOCs.

Budget around 20 hours of build time. A sensible pace is one module per day, finishing within two weeks. The set is aimed at intermediate builders with one or two MOC projects behind them; beginners would do better to start with a smaller street scene to learn the workflow before committing to a town this size.

The town looks considerably better at night with a string of lights added. Builders share their lighting modification approaches in the brickplan comments, and those comfortable with wiring can add their own street lamp circuits.

## FAQ

**Q: How much space does the finished MOC town take up?**
A: The completed build is about 90 cm long and 35 cm wide. Plan for roughly one square meter of display space. An acrylic dust cover or a glass display cabinet is worth considering, both to keep dust off and to show the detail at its best.

**Q: How long does the town MOC take to build?**
A: Around 20 hours in total. The instructions are divided into 12 modules of 2-3 hours each. One module a day gets you finished in about two weeks, which works well for evenings or weekends.

**Q: Can I build only a few of the buildings?**
A: Yes. Each building is a separate module with a complete base, so any of them can be built and displayed on its own. If you want to work up gradually, start with a smaller structure such as the cafe or the flower shop.

**Q: Is the town MOC suitable for beginners?**
A: It is not recommended as a first MOC. 5,200 pieces and 20 hours is a demanding introduction. Build a small scene of around 1,000 pieces first to get comfortable with the process, then take on the town.
