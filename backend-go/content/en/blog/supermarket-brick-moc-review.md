# Supermarket Brick MOC Review: Shelves, Checkout and Signage

I recently picked up a supermarket-themed brick MOC, and once it was built and slotted into my street scene, the whole block suddenly felt lived in. Supermarket subjects have long been popular with MOC builders because they come with a natural supply of small sub-scenes and a lot of playable detail. This review walks through the model from the shelves to the checkout counter and covers what works well and where it falls a little short.

## Shelf and Product Display Details

This is my favourite layer of the build. The shelving is modular, and each row can be lifted out on its own and repositioned, so you can rearrange the product layout whenever you like. Most of the "stock" on the shelves is built from bricks and printed elements: drink bottles, bread, cans and fruit baskets are all accounted for, and they are set at slightly irregular angles that read as a real supermarket display. The only small disappointment is that some of the individual products use very few pieces, which makes assembly a test of patience, though the finished result looks excellent.

## Checkout Counter and Shopping Cart Structure

The checkout counter is the visual centre of the whole scene. The countertop carries a scanner gun, a cash register and a row of small divider panels, and even the section where a customer pays and receives change is modelled. The accompanying mini shopping cart is solidly built, all four wheels turn, and the basket comfortably holds several of the small product pieces, so handling it feels natural. A cashier minifigure stands behind the counter, and with queuing customer minifigures added, a busy checkout scene appears straight away.

## Signage and Storefront Design

The frontage uses large transparent elements for the display windows, paired with a bold projecting sign. It reads as open and airy in daylight, and with light bricks fitted it looks especially good at night. The lettering on the sign is done with stickers, cleanly printed in a simple, crisp style. The building connects seamlessly to a standard street baseplate, and the connections on all four sides line up well, so it works equally well as a gap-filler in a street layout or as a standalone display.

## Value and Playability Assessment

The part selection across this supermarket MOC covers standard elements, printed pieces and a good number of special small parts. The amount of content matches the positioning of the set, and the value sits at a mid-to-high level. In terms of play, it works both as a static display and as a role-play scene where you stage opening hours and checkout transactions, and it pairs well with minifigures for storytelling. If you enjoy street scenes and diorama building, you can find more supermarket MOC instructions and sharing at https://brickplan.cn.

## Who It Suits

If you are a street-scene builder who wants to add a bit of everyday life to a neighbourhood, or a MOC fan who likes high-detail interiors, this supermarket theme is worth a try. As a practical note, it is worth checking the parts list and the finished dimensions before you buy to make sure they suit your plans. Overall this is a set that holds up both to looking at and to playing with, and I would recommend it to anyone who loves street-scene builds.
