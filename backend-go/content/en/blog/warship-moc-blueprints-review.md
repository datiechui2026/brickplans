# Warship MOC Blueprint Review: Battleships, Carriers, Destroyers

## Product Overview

Warships are the most imposing branch of military brick building. From the big-gun giants of World War II to modern stealth destroyers, from carrier groups to submarines, a lot of builders got their start by taking a blueprint and putting a piece of naval history on the desk. This review covers five warship MOC blueprints with solid reputations, spanning different eras and ship classes, so you can see which one suits you.

## Part Review

Warship MOC blueprints demand a lot from parts, especially **color consistency** and **shape accuracy**. Hulls are almost always built in **dark gray or naval gray** as the base tone, with lighter gray on the deck to separate the layers visually.

The **Bismarck-class battleship** blueprint is remarkable for how it captures the hull's curves. A heavy use of wedge plates and curved parts reproduces the angular-yet-sweeping lines typical of German warship design. Its main turrets use a precise gear linkage so all four guns elevate together. The **Type 055 destroyer** leans on sloped parts and smooth panels instead, nailing the faceted, low-observable profile of a modern warship.

Complete parts lists for these warship MOC blueprints can be downloaded at [brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn), and the site also carries companion MOCs for embarked aircraft and ship's boats, which makes it easy to build out a full fleet formation.

## Building Experience

The defining traits of a warship MOC build are **sheer size** and **detail density**. Putting the Bismarck-class battleship together is a marathon: from the keel up to the deck, from the superstructure to the anti-aircraft mounts, every subassembly needs careful placement and alignment.

The Liaoning aircraft carrier is even more involved, because beyond the hull itself you also build a large number of carrier aircraft. The angled flight deck is a real test of spatial thinking, but the payoff when it's finished is hard to match. The Type 093 nuclear submarine is comparatively forgiving thanks to its streamlined hull, which keeps the build flowing and makes it a good entry point for anyone new to warship MOCs.

## Finished Model Showcase

As display pieces, warship MOCs sit at the **top tier** of brick building. The finished Bismarck-class battleship measures over 80cm long, with four twin 380mm main turrets that traverse and elevate, secondary and anti-aircraft batteries throughout, and deck detail that keeps your eyes moving.

The Liaoning aircraft carrier comes in at close to 100cm long, with dozens of J-15 carrier aircraft parked on deck, working elevators, and a richly detailed island. It is a genuine centerpiece. The Type 055 destroyer is smaller, but its stealth shaping and vertical launch system are reproduced with real precision.
