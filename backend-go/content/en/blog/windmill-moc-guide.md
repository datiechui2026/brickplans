# Windmill MOC Build Guide: Plans, Parts List and Axle Setup

The short answer first: you do not need a flagship-grade replica to build a windmill MOC. Nail three things — a tapered tower, blades that actually turn, and a proper cap — and roughly 800-1200 pieces plus 6-8 hours will get you a Dutch-style windmill. With the right plans in hand, the first-attempt success rate is above ninety percent, so a beginner can start here directly.

## 1. Pick the Plans First: Where to Find a Reliable Windmill MOC

Plans decide half the outcome. Start by searching a plan community like brickplan.cn for the "windmill" tag, then check three things: whether the total part count is stated, whether the instructions run past 60 steps, and whether anyone in the comments has posted a photo of the finished build. A plan with a built-model photo has been verified by someone else, which lowers the odds of hitting a trap. Skip the ones that show only a cover image and no interior preview — nine times out of ten the parts list will not match.

## 2. Put Together the Parts List: Don't Order Yet

Running short mid-build is the classic windmill failure. I budget the main parts plus a 10% buffer. For an 800-piece plan that means roughly 150 brown bricks and 150 sand bricks for the tower, 100 white bricks for the cap and base, and 40-60 dark gray Technic pieces for the axle frame. Hinges, 1x2 bricks with holes, cross axles and bushes: at least 20 of each. Those last few are what makes the blades turn — miss any one of them and the whole model stays static.

## 3. Build the Tower: The Tapered Top Is the Whole Point

Step one: frame the base on a 16x16 baseplate, build up the four corners with staggered 2x4 bricks, and step the walls inward by one stud every two layers, so the tower shifts naturally from square to octagonal. Step two: stop at around 70% of the final height and leave the door and window openings; frame the door in dark brown bricks so it reads as timber and looks closer to a real windmill. Step three: reserve a ring of 1x2 bricks with holes at the top of the tower — that is the mounting point for the cap. Get that ring off-center and the entire windmill sits crooked.

## 4. Make the Blade Axle: Whether It Spins Comes Down to This

Use Technic for the axle, not regular bricks. Run a single 16L cross axle out through the center of the cap, fix it on the outside with two bushes, connect four blades to cross connectors on the axle using 1x4 Technic liftarms, then extend each blade with 2x4 plates to about 24 studs. Do not close the roof yet — spin it by hand for a couple of turns first. If it runs smooth with no binding, keep going. Ten minutes spent here saves the two hours it takes to tear the tower down and rebuild it.

## 5. Color Scheme and Scene: Make the Windmill Come Alive

Sand and dark brown for the tower, red-brown for the roof, white for the blades — that three-color combination ages best. Ring the base with green plates for grass, add two trees, a fence and a sheep minifigure, and the scene immediately reads as Dutch countryside. Finish with a small rotating chimney on the cap and the level of detail jumps.

The difficulty in a windmill MOC really sits in one place: the axle. Everything else is patience. Follow the order above and 800 pieces takes about 6 hours — a display piece that looks great at home or on an office shelf, and very photogenic.

## FAQ

**Q: Where do I find windmill MOC plans?**
A: Search a plan community such as brickplan.cn for the "windmill" tag. Favor plans with step previews, a stated part count and photos of built models — plans someone else has already verified succeed more often.

**Q: How do I make the windmill blades actually turn?**
A: Build the axle from Technic parts: a cross axle plus bushes, with the blades attached through liftarms onto cross connectors. Do not stack regular bricks. Test the rotation by hand before you close the top; if it binds, adjust the bush positions.

**Q: How many parts and how much budget does a beginner need?**
A: A small windmill runs about 800 pieces and CNY 200-400; a large one is 1500+ pieces and CNY 500-800. Beginners should start at the 800-piece level — 6-8 hours to finish and a higher success rate.

**Q: How is a windmill MOC different from a house MOC?**
A: Mostly the axle assembly. A windmill adds blades, a cross axle, bushes and liftarms — about 8-10% of the part count — plus a rotation test during the build, so it is a step above a static house.

**Q: How should I display a windmill MOC?**
A: Lay green plates around the base for grass, add trees, a fence, a sheep minifigure and a stone path, and use the three-color scheme of white blades, sand tower and dark brown roof. It photographs well in a home display cabinet.
