# Brick Deer MOC Review: 1,200-Part Forest Stag Build

The short version: this brick deer MOC cuts a slim, elegant silhouette, uses roughly 1,200 parts to build a standing buck about 17 cm tall, takes around 4 hours, and sits at the easy end of the difficulty scale — which makes it a strong first animal MOC for newer builders. Brown-tan as the main color with a white belly, about 90% accurate to the real animal, good value for money, few specialized parts, and replacements are easy to source. The instructions come from the brickplan platform, and the parts list is split up by body section.

## Exterior Accuracy

The pose is what sells this deer. Four long, powerful legs, the neck tipped slightly forward, as if it has just lifted its head to check the treeline. The antlers are built symmetrically from slender brown elements into a multi-branch shape, and they are the standout part of the whole model. The head uses two shades to separate the face from the muzzle, and the eyes are small but expressive. A brown-tan body with a white neck-to-belly transition puts the color scheme close to a real sika deer — roughly 90% faithful to the animal.

## Build Experience

The build moves through four stages: legs, torso, neck and head, then antlers. Difficulty is low throughout. There is no tricky SNOT work anywhere, and the model is mostly standard bricks. The four legs attach to the torso with hinge pieces, so you can fine-tune the stance. The antlers branch a lot, so the only real thing to watch is keeping the left and right sides symmetrical. The instructions are clear step by step, and following them gets you to the finish in about 4 hours with almost no rework — a beginner can get it right on the first pass.

## Value and Verdict

The model runs about 1,200 parts, which works out to roughly 280 yuan if you source domestic parts. The brown-tan elements and the slender antler pieces account for most of that. Given the finished height and how refined the shaping is, that price is very good value; there are few specialized parts, so replacing anything you are missing is painless. Overall it is an elegant, good-looking, approachable build, and a genuinely good pick as a first animal MOC. Paired with brickplan's tree and grass display bases, it recreates a complete forest scene.

**Q: Is this deer MOC suitable for beginners?**

**Q: Do the antlers break off easily?**

A: No. The antlers use slender brown elements fixed with connector pieces. As long as you seat the clips properly per the instructions, they hold up fine in normal display and handling — just avoid forcing the branches apart.
