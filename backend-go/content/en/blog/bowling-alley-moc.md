# Bowling Alley MOC Review: Lanes, Scoreboard and Street Display

The short version first: this bowling alley MOC uses roughly 2,980 pieces, measures 36 cm long, 16 cm wide and 24 cm tall when finished, and the instruction file costs 29 yuan. Sourcing the parts loose at typical BrickPlan rates puts the build at about 550-650 yuan, or roughly 0.19 yuan per piece, and assembly takes around seven hours. Overall polish is high — the recessed lane channel and the scoring screen are the biggest highlights — and the model is aimed at street-scene builders with some experience behind them. If you are new to the hobby, start with the simplified lane section rather than committing to the full building straight away.

## Design and Accuracy

The lane is assembled from slotted slide bricks laid end to end, and the channel is exactly wide enough to hold one standard brick-built bowling ball. Push the ball into the groove and it rolls down toward the pin area smoothly — the whole mechanism captures close to 80% of the feel of a real lane. The scoring screen is built from transparent bricks with a printed sticker on top, and paired with the stepped seating area it gives the ground-floor entertainment space a real sense of depth rather than a flat, boxy interior.

Upstairs, the shopfront follows classic street-scene module dimensions. The 16x32 baseplate underneath means it butts up cleanly against other street-scene MOCs on brickplan.cn, so you can drop it into an existing row without modifying either building. That compatibility is worth noting: a lot of entertainment-themed builds look great on their own but fight with neighbouring modules over pavement height and corner alignment, and this one does not.

## Building Experience

The instructions are split into four booklets — lane, scoring screen, seating area and exterior walls — and each module runs about 40-50 minutes, for roughly seven hours in total. That modular split is the single most useful thing about the document, because it gives you natural stopping points instead of one seven-hour slog.

The symmetrical construction of the lane channel is the part that demands care. It is very easy to lay a mirrored row in the wrong direction, and the error only becomes obvious several steps later, so work row by row following the arrows in the diagrams and check the symmetry every few courses. Everything else is straightforward. Parts are bagged by step, which keeps finding time low; only a handful of small dark bricks need a second look under good light. If you plan to motorise or handle the model regularly, test-roll the ball through the channel before you close the side walls in — getting your fingers in afterwards is awkward.

## Value and Recommendation

At 29 yuan for the instructions and an average of 0.19 yuan per part, the loose-parts cost lands around 550 yuan. Against a retail street-scene set of a comparable footprint, that is strong value — and it is the main reason the model is worth considering if you already own a decent parts stock, since the effective cost drops further the more of the 2,980 pieces you can pull from your own bins.

Both core functions, the working lane and the scoring screen, are designed to be played with rather than just looked at, so once the building is slotted into a street row it becomes a genuinely interactive corner. It is recommended for builders who like entertainment-themed street scenes. On a tighter budget, buy the instructions first and test-build the lane on its own before ordering parts for the rest of the structure — that way you find out whether the slotted construction suits you before spending the full amount.
