# Brick F1 Car MOC Plans Reviewed: W13, SF-23, RB19, Lotus 49

## Introduction

Formula 1 is the ultimate symbol of speed and engineering. In the brick world, building an F1 car MOC is a bucket-list project for a lot of builders. Whether you want to capture the streamlined bodywork of a classic racer or replicate a modern engine and suspension setup, the quality of the **brick F1 car MOC plan** you start from decides how the finished model looks on the shelf. This article reviews four popular F1 MOC plans so you can pick the one that matches your parts bin and your skill level.

## 1. What Makes a Good F1 Car MOC

### 1.1 Aerodynamic Shape

Aerodynamics is the core design language of an F1 car. A strong **race car MOC** plan has to solve these problems:

- **Low bodywork**: F1 cars sit extremely low, so building a body that is both low and structurally solid is the key challenge
- **Front and rear wings**: Complex front wing endplates and multi-element rear wings demand plenty of slope bricks and curved slopes
- **Sidepods**: The flowing transition from sidepod to floor takes clever building techniques
- **Diffuser**: The multi-layer structure at the rear of the floor

### 1.2 Mechanical Detail

- **Engine detail**: Piston linkage for a V6 turbo engine
- **Suspension**: A faithful pushrod suspension setup
- **Steering**: Steering wheel connected to the front wheels
- **Gearbox**: A simplified 8-speed gearbox

### 1.3 Livery and Colour Scheme

A car's livery is a big part of how it is recognised. A good **brick MOC plan** should give you a clear colour scheme and guidance for the sticker design.

## 3. F1 Car MOC Building Techniques

### 3.1 Shaping the Bodywork

Getting the bodywork to flow is the hardest part of an **F1 car MOC**. A few techniques that help:

1. **Use wedge plates**: Wedge plates are the best tool for a streamlined nose and tail
2. **Stack slopes**: Layering slope bricks over several rows produces a smooth curved transition
3. **SNOT techniques**: Studs Not On Top lets you mount bricks sideways for a cleaner surface
4. **Curved slope edges**: Finish body edges with curved slopes to cut down the hard corners

### 3.2 Front and Rear Wing Design

- **Front wing**: Combine thin plates and tiles, stacking them into multi-element wing profiles
- **Endplates**: Build endplates from sideways-mounted plates for extra depth
- **Rear wing**: Use Technic parts as supports and large plates for the wing surface
- **DRS**: Some advanced plans include a working DRS (drag reduction system) flap

### 3.3 Wheels and Tyres

- **Front wheels**: Narrow tyres using standard racing tyre parts
- **Rear wheels**: Wider tyres using the broader tyre elements
- **Hubs**: Silver wheel hubs, or a colour matched to the car's livery
- **Brake calipers**: Tuck small red or yellow parts inside the hub to suggest brake calipers

## 4. Choosing and Sourcing a Plan

### 4.1 How to Pick a Plan

When browsing **brick F1 car MOC plans** on BrickPlans and similar platforms, check these points:

1. **Complete parts list**: Make sure the plan includes a full inventory so you can gather parts
2. **User reviews**: Look at build feedback and photos of finished models from other builders
3. **Difficulty rating**: Pick a level that matches your experience
4. **Instruction clarity**: Preview the step screenshots and judge how readable they are

### 4.2 Substituting Parts

If you are missing parts, try these alternatives:
- **Colour substitution**: Use the same element in a different colour
- **Function substitution**: Find an element with a similar function
- **Simplified design**: Simplify non-critical areas where it will not show
