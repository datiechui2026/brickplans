# Brick Tricycle MOC Review: Three-Wheel Chassis and Cargo Bed

Verdict first: this tricycle's parts list is friendly to anyone building on a budget. Around 460 pieces, no retired or rare elements, roughly 28 cm long overall, and a front-1 / rear-2 wheel layout that stands firmly on its own. The cargo bed lifts off, and the whole model is a high-value small build for a city layout.

The MOC reviewed here is based on a vintage cargo tricycle, about 28 cm long, scaled to match minifigures in a street scene. The designer put real effort into accuracy: the rake of the front fork, the curve of the two-person seat and the texture of the cargo bed's side panels all sit close to the real vehicle, and smaller touches like the headlight, mudguards and toolbox are all present. Finished and parked in a corner of a city block, it adds a strong slice-of-life feel.

## Three-Wheel Chassis

The three-wheel layout is the highlight of this design. The front-1 / rear-2 track width, paired with a low centre of gravity in the chassis, keeps the model standing very steadily — even fully loaded it does not tip sideways. The front wheel is linked to the handlebars through a steering arm, so the turning circle is small, which suits narrow alley scenes in a city layout. The chassis rails are built from technic elements, and the overall rigidity is excellent: pick the model up and nothing feels loose. That is genuinely rare for a MOC in this size range.

## Cargo Bed and Loads

The cargo bed is a removable design, clipped to the chassis at four studs so you can swap in a different bed shape easily. The side panels fold down toward the rear, and the interior has room for city accessories such as milk crates and bundles of newspapers. The designer also left pre-moulded holes in the bed floor so builders can add their own canopy. The loading play value interacts well with a street scene, and it photographs beautifully when you set up a shot.

## Parts List and Build Difficulty

The instructions come with a complete parts list of about 460 elements, all common pieces with no rare or out-of-production parts, so recreating it is cheap. The build guide uses step-by-step 3D renders, split sensibly, with no more than six parts per step and clear callouts. The drivetrain uses a technic gear set to connect the rear axle, so when you spin the rear wheel the pedals turn with it — a lot of fun. On difficulty, the design rates 3 stars out of 5, which suits builders with a little experience; newcomers should sort the parts into bags following the instructions before starting.
