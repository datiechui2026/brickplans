# Xingzuan Brick Review: Part Quality and Series Lineup

Short answer first: Xingzuan is an entry-level, high-value brand. Big military sets run a little over 100 RMB, parts meet the bar, and the minifigure printing is a genuine plus. If you want tight tolerances, budget higher.

Xingzuan is one of the long-standing names in Chinese bricks, focused on small-scale building sets, best known for military subjects and city scenes, and priced to stay friendly. This review looks at part quality and the series lineup to work out whether the old standby is still worth your money.

## 1. Brand Positioning and Series Lineup

Xingzuan positions itself as an **entry-level, high-value brand**. Its most famous line is **military**: tanks, armoured vehicles, fighter jets and warships, covering land, sea and air — a low-cost option for military fans. The **city series** covers police, fire and construction scenes, with moderate set sizes that suit kids and parent-child building. There are also dinosaur and ocean science-themed sets with larger parts and simple builds, clearly aimed at younger builders. The catalogue is broad rather than deep — the appeal is that there is something for everyone.

## 2. Part Quality: Hands-On Findings

Part quality is both Xingzuan's weak point and its strength. Start with the good news: **prices are very low but the clutch is basically acceptable** — main structural parts connect firmly and a finished model displays without problems. Bricks are compatible with LEGO, so you can mix them in. Now the weak points: **tolerances on small parts vary noticeably**, with the occasional piece running too tight or too loose; dark-coloured parts sometimes show surface scratches, and transparent parts are only average in clarity. Colour consistency is middling — parts from the same batch generally match, but mixing batches needs care. For a price under 100 RMB, that is an acceptable showing.

## 3. Building Experience and Instructions

Xingzuan has improved its instructions in recent years: **step diagrams are clear and bag numbering is logical**, so beginners can build straight from the manual without trouble. Military sets lean on repetitive structures — a slightly tedious build, but the finished model has real presence. City sets sit at a moderate part count with a brisk building rhythm, making them a reasonable first brick set for a child. Minifigure printing is a highlight: face and clothing detail stand out at this price point.

## 4. Value and Who It Suits

The verdict is blunt: Xingzuan is the classic "you get what you pay for, and then some" brand. Large military sets often come in at just over 100 RMB, which makes the price-per-part extremely strong. It suits **military fans on a tight budget, beginners testing the water cheaply, and parents looking for a low-cost set for their kids to practise on**. If part precision and building feel matter most to you, spend more and choose a brand using Gobricks moulds. If subject matter and price are what you care about, Xingzuan will rarely disappoint.
