# Hotel Brick Scene MOC: From Lobby to Guest Rooms

Every brick builder carries some version of a road-trip dream, and a hotel scene is one of the best ways to put that romance into a few square studs. Whether you lean toward a vintage European inn or a sleek high-rise business hotel, a well-built hotel MOC usually ends up as the landmark of an entire city layout. This walkthrough takes you from an empty baseplate to a finished, lit hotel, one stage at a time.

## 1. Plan First: Settle the Style and the Footprint

Before you reach for bricks, decide what kind of hotel you are actually building. A warm, rustic country inn and a glass-curtain-wall modern tower demand completely different color schemes and brick selections, and switching halfway through is expensive. Beginners should start on a 32x32 baseplate: it is large enough to hold a full set of functional zones without letting the parts budget spiral. Sketch the lobby, front desk, guest rooms and terrace on paper first. Ten minutes with a pencil saves hours of rebuilding later.

## 2. The Lobby and Front Desk: The Heart of the Scene

A successful hotel scene needs a lobby that people remember at a glance. The front desk is the visual anchor, so build the counter from dark-colored bricks, then pair it with two symmetrical small pendant lamps and a band of gold trim to create that formal welcome. Do not skip the corner grouping: a sofa, a coffee table, and ideally a potted plant. These small details are precisely what make a hotel scene feel inhabited rather than empty. Transparent bricks and light bricks used together will give the lobby a soft, warm glow even before you add wiring.

## 3. Guest Rooms: Build Daily Life Into Every Room

The guest rooms are the most patience-testing and the most rewarding part of the project. A single floor can hold two or three standard rooms side by side, each distinguished by a different bedspread color so the room types read clearly. Miniature nightstands, bedside lamps, a TV cabinet, even a framed picture on the wall can each be built from one or two specialty pieces. Working at a small scale? Use 1x1 bricks to suggest pillows and folded blankets. Prefer a larger scale? Give each room opening floor-to-ceiling windows and a balcony.

## 4. Public Areas and Supporting Facilities

A hotel scene should also include the spaces guests pass through: the elevator lobby, corridors, restaurant, and a rooftop observation deck. Run a straight band of bricks down the corridor and flank it with door frames and room-number plaques. In the restaurant, set out a small round table with a few chairs and some plate pieces, and it reads as though a guest will arrive for breakfast any second. If your baseplate still has room to spare, add an entrance driveway with a fountain; it lifts the overall completeness of the build immediately.

## 5. Lighting and Final Polish: Make It Glow at Night

One good light beats ten good bricks. Fit the hotel with a lighting kit and light up the lobby chandelier, the corridor wall sconces, and the bedside lamps in each room, and the whole building takes on a completely different character in low light. Finish by laying a "walk of stars" at the entrance with 1x1 transparent studs, then spell out the hotel name on the sign. At that point your hotel scene is done, and it can go straight into your city layout with confidence.

The real pleasure of building a hotel scene is the sense of achievement that only shows up after a few rounds of trial and error. Whether you are recreating an old inn you remember or imagining an interstellar hotel that does not exist yet, push the idea as far as you like.
