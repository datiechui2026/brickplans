# Pokémon Brick Building Guide: From Pikachu to Charizard

Pokémon is one of the most popular IPs on the planet, and pairing it with brick building sends fans into overdrive. This guide walks you through the strange, satisfying overlap between Pokémon and bricks: how to capture the classic silhouettes, what parts to keep on hand, and how to build a pocket monster world of your own.

## Why Pokémon Suits Brick Building

### What Makes These Characters Such Good MOC Material?

Pokémon and bricks are a natural match. The roster is enormous and the shapes are wildly varied, from the round softness of Pikachu to the angular bulk of Charizard, and every character has a silhouette you can recognize at a glance.

The advantages of building Pokémon from bricks:

- **High recognizability**: each Pokémon has a distinctive shape, so even a rough build reads correctly
- **Clean color schemes**: most Pokémon use only 2-3 main colors, which makes part sourcing simple
- **Flexible scale**: you can build a micro version or a large display model of the same character
- **Playable combinations**: you can build full world scenes and add minifigure trainers

### Three Formats for Pokémon Builds

1. **Brick figures**: minifigure scale, good articulation, made to be handled
2. **Display models**: focused on accurate shape, meant to sit on a shelf
3. **Scene models**: Pokémon plus environment, telling a story

## The Basics: Building Your First Pokémon

### Tools and Parts to Prepare

Before you start building Pokémon, stock the following parts:

**Base colors:**
- Yellow family: Pikachu, Raichu and friends
- Red family: Charizard, Cyndaquil and friends
- Blue family: Squirtle, Blastoise and friends
- Green family: Bulbasaur, Sceptile and friends
- White and black: Eevee, Misdreavus and friends

**Essential part types:**
- Slope bricks: for shaping rounded heads and bodies
- Curved plates: smooth transitions, ideal for organic shapes
- Hinge parts: for building posable joints
- Eye parts: a Pokémon's eyes are its soul
- 1x1 round bricks: for small-scale detail work

### Starter Project: Building Pikachu

Pikachu is the face of the franchise and the best entry point for Pokémon brick building.

**Step 1: Build the body**
Use yellow bricks to build an oval body, with curved plates to soften the bottom edge.

**Step 2: Shape the head**
Stack slope bricks to create a rounded head profile and leave space where the ears will attach.

**Step 3: Ears and tail**
Build the ears from yellow plates with black tip parts, and use hinge parts to form the lightning-bolt tail.

**Step 4: Face details**
- Eyes: black and white round tiles
- Mouth: a 1x1 curved slope for the smile
- Cheeks: small red round tiles as accents

**Step 5: Arms and legs**
Use short cylindrical parts for the limbs to keep Pikachu's cute proportions.

## Advanced: Techniques for Complex Pokémon

### Charizard: The Challenge of a Large Build

Charizard is the most iconic dragon-type character in the franchise. It is a hard build, but the payoff is dramatic.

Key points for building Charizard:

**Wings**
- Use large plates as the wing frame
- Combine orange and yellow parts for a flame effect
- Make the wing joints posable so you can adjust the pose

**Head shape**
- Use inverted SNOT techniques to build the snout
- Add white parts for the teeth
- Use cone parts for the horns

**Tail flame**
- Use orange, yellow and red transparent parts
- Layer them to suggest a rising flame
- A transparent rod inside can support the structure

### Mew: Expressing Round Shapes in Bricks

Mew's difficulty lies in the round body and the long, slender tail.

**Technique notes:**
- Use curved plates and slopes heavily
- Build a pink gradient across the body
- Use hose or chain parts so the tail can curve
- Keep the eyes large and lively

## Scenes: Building the Pokémon World

The end goal of Pokémon brick building is a complete Pokémon world. Pallet Town is where every trainer's dream begins.

### Pallet Town Layout

**Scene elements:**
- **Oak's laboratory**: a white building with a Poké Ball logo
- **Ash's house**: a typical Japanese-style home
- **Grassland**: green baseplates dotted with wildflowers
- **Pokémon Center**: the classic building with a red roof

### Battle Scenes

Build a Pokémon battle scene and your brick Pokémon come alive:

- **Battle arena**: use a specialized baseplate to make the battle platform
- **Gyms**: design each gym in a different style based on its type
- **Spectator seating**: minifigure trainers watching the match

## Creative Tricks: Making Your Builds Stand Out

### Lighting Effects

Install LEDs inside the model:
- **Charizard**: an orange LED inside the mouth to simulate flamethrower breath
- **Pikachu**: red LEDs in the cheeks to suggest an electric attack
- **Gastly**: a fully transparent body plus purple LEDs for a ghostly glow

### Articulation

Poseable design makes a build far more fun to own:

- **Ball joints**: use ball-joint hinges for the limb sockets
- **Spring mechanisms**: a simple spring lets a Pokémon "jump"
- **Rotating base**: a turntable base for 360-degree display

### Micro Pokémon

Capture a Pokémon's key features with the minimum number of parts:
- Use a 1x1 round brick as the body
- Build signature features, like Pikachu's ears, from 1x1 plates
- Ideal when you want dozens of Pokémon to populate one scene
