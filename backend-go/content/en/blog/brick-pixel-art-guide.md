# Brick Pixel Art: Beginner Tutorial, Grids and Baseplate Guide

The short answer: brick pixel art needs no design talent at all — if you can count squares, you can build it. A beginner can take one 32x32 baseplate, round up 300–500 basic bricks, and follow four steps (choose the image, grid it, pick colors, build row by row) to finish a 25x25 cm pixel picture in 2–3 hours, with total cost held between 60 and 150 yuan.

The logic behind it is simple: slice a picture into small squares, then replace each square with one brick in the matching color, and you get a mosaic effect. It is far easier than a normal MOC because there is no structural engineering involved, yet the result photographs beautifully — it looks striking on a wall or standing on a desk.

## Step 1: Pick an Image and Grid It

Image choice decides how the finished piece looks. Favor patterns with clean outlines and strong color contrast: pixel game characters, fruit, animals and flags are all good material. The image resolution has to match the grid count of the baseplate you choose — a 32x32 baseplate means a 32x32 pixel image. You can use an online pixel art tool or image editing software to pixelate a photo, or simply sketch the design by hand on graph paper.

## Step 2: Plan Colors and Parts

The biggest mistake in pixel art is using too many colors. Keep the palette to 6–8 colors or fewer: let black, white, red, yellow, blue and green carry the main areas, and use in-between shades such as light gray and light blue for transitions. 1x1 and 2x2 plates do the heavy lifting. A 32x32 piece takes roughly 400 pieces in total — about 200 1x1 plates, 100 2x2 plates, with 1x2 plates filling in the edges. If the budget is tight, start with domestic compatible bricks, where a single piece costs under 0.1 yuan.

## Step 3: Build Row by Row

Work in rows from the bottom up. First fix the four corners at the edges of the baseplate, then lay plates row by row. After each row, press it down gently with your thumb so the studs and the baseplate are fully engaged. Don't keep shifting your working position mid-build, or you will knock loose the sections you have already laid. If a few colors come out wrong, note the positions and swap them all at the end — that is much more efficient than fixing each one as you go.

## Step 4: Secure and Display

When the build is finished, stack two baseplates together to add thickness, or mount it directly on a brick picture-frame stand. If it will be on display long term and you want to stop children from knocking it apart, you can apply a small amount of brick-specific glue to the back — but glued pieces can no longer be taken apart, so photograph the finished piece first as a record. The result can hang on the wall as a decorative picture or go into a clear display case to keep dust off.

On baseplate choice, remember: 32x32 is right for a first practice piece; 48x48 allows finer detail but doubles the part count and takes about 6 hours; only consider display baseplates with a built-in stand once your budget allows. ## FAQ

**Q: How do I make brick pixel art? Can a total beginner do it?**
A: Yes. Pixel art requires no structural design skill — you count squares against a chart and match colors. Choose a 32x32 baseplate, keep to 6–8 colors, and your first piece takes 2–3 hours.

**Q: Which baseplate should I use for brick pixel art?**
A: For beginners, a standard 32x32 baseplate, about 25x25 cm, with 300–500 bricks is enough. If you want finer detail, go to 48x48, but both the part count and the build time double. Pick a baseplate with flat edges and a tight fit.

**Q: How do I keep a finished pixel art piece from falling apart?**
A: Press each row down firmly as you lay it, then stack two baseplates for extra thickness or fit a frame stand. For long-term display you can apply brick-specific glue to the back, but glued pieces cannot be modified, so photograph the piece first.

**Q: What does one brick pixel art piece cost?**
A: Around 60–100 yuan with domestic compatible bricks, or 150–300 yuan with original LEGO bricks. Most of the money goes into 1x1 and 2x2 plates. Holding the palette to 6–8 colors saves a noticeable amount, and a single piece can cost as little as 0.1 yuan.
