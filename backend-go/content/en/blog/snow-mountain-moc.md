# Snow Mountain MOC: Layered White Bricks, Peaks and Pine Trees

Here's the short version: a snow mountain MOC on a 30×30 baseplate with roughly 2,200 parts runs about CNY 450 in materials and takes around 8 hours of building time spread across a weekend. Three things carry the whole build: layering white bricks in five tones for the snowpack, stacking slope bricks into a convincing peak, and planting a few snow-covered pine trees. Follow the order below and a first-time builder can get it right in one pass. The full instructions are available at brickplan.cn.

## Step 1: Lay the Base and Set the Terrain Skeleton

Start with a 30×30 light gray baseplate. Build up a rocky foundation from dark gray and brown basic bricks in loose, staggered layers, 2 to 4 bricks high. Don't make it too flat — keep the front-to-back height difference within 6 bricks so the mountain body has a gentle slope facing southwest. Leave a flat area at the foot of the mountain; that's where the pines and the snowfield will go. Don't chase tidiness here. The more random the stacking, the closer it gets to the natural irregularity of a real mountain.

## Step 2: Build the Snowpack with White Bricks

Layering is the whole game with snow. Sort your white bricks into five brightness levels: light gray-white for the lowest layer, cream white in the middle, pure white at the top, plus a small number of transparent pieces mixed in to suggest ice crystals. Moving from the foot of the mountain upward, white coverage climbs from 30% to 100%, and the summit is pure white throughout. Run 1×2 slope bricks along the mountain's faces to get that soft, snow-covered roundness; along the ridgelines, dot 1×1 round plates to mimic the trails wind leaves in snow. This layer eats the most time, but it's also the dividing line between a convincing snow mountain and a pile of white bricks.

## Step 3: Shape the Peak and Plant the Snow Pines

Close the summit with 2×2 and 2×4 slope bricks, pulling in from all four sides toward the center. The highest point sits about 18 cm above the base, forming a triangular cone. Leave two protruding rock walls on the flanks to add depth. For the snow pines, stack three tiers of green 2×2 bricks in a tower shape, sandwiching a white round plate between each tier as snow load. Each tree is 6 to 8 cm tall; plant 5 to 7 of them at the foot of the mountain, staggered in height. Finish with a scatter of white 1×1 round plates as falling snow and the scene is done. Advanced option: run a string of warm yellow lights around the midsection — lit up at night, the reflection off the snow surface reads much more real.

## FAQ

**Q: How many parts does a snow mountain MOC need?**
A: About 2,200 as described here, with white-family parts making up roughly 55%. If you build only the mountain without the pines and lights, you can get down to around 1,500. Short on parts? Substitute light gray for white in the lowest layer — the visual difference is minimal.

**Q: Is a snow mountain MOC instruction set worth buying?**
A: Yes. Working out colors and structure on your own means several rounds of disassembly and rebuilding, while a set of instructions locks in both the parts list and the build order in one go. The time saved far exceeds the price. Most snow mountain instructions on brickplan.cn also include substitution suggestions for parts.

**Q: How is a snow mountain MOC different from a normal street scene MOC?**
A: The biggest differences are color layering and structural center of gravity. Street scenes depend on walls that are plumb and square; snow mountains rely entirely on slope bricks and staggered stacking to build natural silhouettes. The gradient of white bricks decides whether it ultimately reads as snow — a technique rarely needed in street scenes.

**Q: What does it cost to build a snow mountain MOC?**
A: With loose parts already on hand, materials run around CNY 450, dominated by white and light gray. If you're buying everything new from the parts list, budget CNY 600 to 800, because white is a scarce color in many sets.

**Q: How do I make a snow mountain look more realistic?**
A: Remember one word three times: layering, layering, layering. White bricks transition through five levels from light gray-white to pure white, with 30% coverage at the foot and 100% at the summit; then use slope bricks to close the faces and round plates to dot the snow, and finally add 5 to 7 pines of varying heights. The effect is immediate.
