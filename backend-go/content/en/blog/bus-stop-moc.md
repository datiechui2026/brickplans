# How to Build a LEGO Bus Stop MOC for Your City Street

Here is the short answer first: build your bus stop on a 32x32 baseplate, leave about 8 studs of clear waiting space, and keep the whole structure 10 to 12 bricks tall so it lines up with the ground floor of your street buildings.

In city street MOCs, the bus stop is often the corner nobody thinks about, yet this small slice of everyday life is exactly what gives a street its energy. A well-built **LEGO bus stop MOC**, paired with a few waiting minifigures and a bus pulling in, instantly gives the whole layout a story. This guide breaks the build down step by step, from baseplate layout and shelter structure to the small accessories that sell the scene.

## Baseplate and Modular Street Layout

The first decision is where the stop actually goes. Most street MOCs use a 32x32 baseplate, and the bus stop can be either a standalone module or built straight into the sidewalk. Put the stop at the edge of the baseplate, flush with the sidewalk, and leave a waiting area roughly 8 studs wide. Line the platform edge with dark grey plates as a curb, so it contrasts with the lighter grey road surface. The advantage of a modular approach is that you can shift the stop whenever you like and recombine it freely with other street buildings.

## Shelter Canopy Structure

The shelter is the visual anchor of the scene. The classic approach is two columns plus one canopy: use 1x1 round bricks or technic axle pieces for the columns, then a 2x8 or 2x10 plate for the roof, with transparent panels underneath as glazing so the result stays light and see-through. If you want more refinement, run a border of white or blue trim along the canopy edge to suggest corrugated panelling; let the roof overhang slightly toward the front for a cantilevered look, which gives the structure far more character.

## Benches, Signs and Details

Inside the waiting area, four items do most of the work: a bench, a stop sign, a trash can and a bike rack. Build the bench from two 1x4 plates on four small feet, which seats two minifigures comfortably. For the stop sign, use a printed 1x2 transparent tile or a sticker showing the route map, mounted on a lamp-post-shaped column. The trash can is a brown round brick with a dark lid, and next to it park a bicycle built from small wheels and a frame. None of these details cost many parts, but they add a huge amount of realism.

## Minifigures and the Bus Arriving

The soul of the scene is the act of waiting. Place two or three minifigures on the platform: a student looking down at a phone, an office worker with a briefcase, and an older woman carrying a shopping bag, each in a different pose. Build the bus with a 6-stud-wide body, nose aligned with the platform, door side open, and keep a half-stud gap from the platform edge to suggest passengers boarding and alighting. If you have the space, add a speed bump or road markings so the arrival reads more clearly.

## Lighting and Day-to-Night Atmosphere

Add one street lamp and the bus stop comes alive at night. Build the lamp from a transparent rod with a 1x1 round dome shade, and tuck a micro LED inside. You can also channel light through transparent pieces under the canopy to mimic the shelter's own fixtures. For a daytime scene, use a light-coloured baseplate and bright minifigures; for a night scene, shift the light warmer and swap in a dark blue night-sky baseplate with a few stars, and the waiting mood lands immediately.

## Matching Scale to Your City Street MOC

Finally, check your proportions. A minifigure stands about 4 bricks tall, so keep the bus stop 10 to 12 bricks tall overall, matching the ground floor of the neighbouring buildings. The stop should take up roughly one third of the baseplate width, no more, so it never steals the show. For style, echo the colours of the sign and shelter with the rest of the street: a European street suits dark green or black cast-iron styling, while a modern street works with white and grey plus a bright accent.

A bus stop is small, but it is the most human corner of a city street. Apply this **LEGO bus stop MOC** approach and your layout gains a spot that tells a story. When you are done, upload it to the BrickPlan community and share your own little waiting-room drama.

## FAQ

**Q: Where should a LEGO bus stop MOC go on a street layout?**
A: Place it at the edge of a 32x32 baseplate, flush with the sidewalk, leaving a waiting area about 8 studs wide. Lay dark grey curb plates along the platform edge so the module is easy to move and reconfigure.

**Q: How do I make the shelter canopy look good?**
A: Use two 1x1 round bricks as columns, a 2x8 or 2x10 plate for the roof, transparent panels as glazing, and a white trim along the edge. Let the roof overhang forward for a cantilevered look.

**Q: Which details add the most realism to a bus stop scene?**
A: The bench, stop sign, trash can and bike rack deliver the most impact for a low part count. Add two or three minifigures in different poses and the scene instantly gains a story.

**Q: How do I keep the bus stop in scale with a city street MOC?**
A: A minifigure is about 4 bricks tall, so keep the stop 10 to 12 bricks tall to match a ground floor, and let it take up about one third of the baseplate width so it does not dominate the street.
