# Brick Aircraft MOC Plans: Fighters, Airliners and Build Tips

Brick aircraft are one of the most popular directions for MOC (My Own Creation) builders. Whether it is a aggressive fighter, a graceful airliner or a vintage twin-wing propeller plane, a good set of **brick aircraft MOC plans** is the key to a successful build. This guide collects the most useful plan sources and walks through a practical tutorial for building an aircraft MOC from scratch.

## 1. Why Brick Aircraft MOCs Are So Popular

Aircraft are a symbol of humanity's conquest of the sky, and they carry a natural mechanical beauty. The appeal of building them in brick comes down to a few things:

- **Structural variety**: everything from the airfoil curve of a wing to the mechanism of the landing gear is a challenge
- **Adjustable scale**: from minifigure scale up to 1:1 large-scale models, there is a size for every collection
- **Part versatility**: plenty of standard bricks and plates find a use in an aircraft MOC
- **Display value**: a finished aircraft model makes an excellent desk piece

And having a high-quality set of **brick aircraft MOC plans** is the fastest route from idea to finished model.

## 2. Recommended Brick Aircraft MOC Plans

### 2.1 Fighter Plans

Fighters are the most popular subject in brick aircraft MOCs. These classic airframes are the ones most worth collecting plans for:

**F-22 Raptor**
The diamond wing and vector nozzles of this stealth fighter are the hard parts of the build. Recommended **brick aircraft MOC plans** usually use slope bricks and wedge plates to capture the stealth shape, and the mid-fuselage needs a lot of SNOT (Stud Not On Top) work to get smooth transitions.

**Su-57**
The Russian fifth-generation fighter's movable foreplanes and distinctive intake shape make interesting demands on the brick structure. Many good plans use movable joints so the foreplanes and tail surfaces can be set at different angles.

**J-20**
As a Chinese-designed fifth-generation fighter, the J-20's canard layout and all-moving vertical tails are genuinely challenging to reproduce in brick. The **brick aircraft MOC plans** circulating online tend to focus closely on the relative position of the canards and the main wings, as well as the detail of the weapons bay on the belly.

### 2.2 Airliners and Transports

**Boeing 747**
The iconic double-deck hump and four engines make the 747 one of the most recognisable airliners. When building, pay attention to the smooth curve of the fuselage and the structural strength where the wing meets the body.

**C-17 Globemaster**
This large military transport's high-wing layout and stubby fuselage mean the landing gear structure needs particular attention in brick. High-quality **brick aircraft MOC plans** include a detailed retractable landing gear mechanism.

### 2.3 Creative Aircraft

Beyond realistic aircraft, there are plenty of inventive fictional designs:

- **Steampunk airship**: copper-tone parts combined with gears for a fantasy flying machine
- **Futuristic sci-fi fighter**: trans-blue parts and streamlined shaping for a science-fiction feel
- **Cartoon aircraft**: rounded curved bricks to reproduce the cute planes from animation

## 3. How to Build a Brick Aircraft MOC

### 3.1 Preparation: Tools and Parts

Before starting an aircraft MOC, get these tools ready:

- **Sorting boxes**: sort parts by colour and type to speed up the build
- **Brick separator**: essential when you place a part wrong, and it avoids damaging parts
- **Digital design software**: such as BrickLink Studio or LDD, so you can design on screen before building

**Basic parts list**:
- Wedge plates (all angles) — for wings and tail surfaces
- Slope bricks — for nose fairings and fuselage transitions
- Tiles — to cover studs and create smooth surfaces
- Hinges — for movable control surfaces and landing gear
- Transparent parts — for the canopy

### 3.2 From Plan to Finished Model

Once you have a set of **brick aircraft MOC plans**, this order of work is the most efficient:

**Step 1: Check the parts list**
Compare the plan's part list against what you have. If a key part is missing, substitute it or order it from an online parts marketplace.

**Step 2: Build the internal frame**
Start with the internal skeleton of the fuselage. Structural strength is critical in an aircraft MOC, especially where the wings join the body. Use Technic parts as the internal frame and cover them with regular bricks on the outside.

**Step 3: Nose and fuselage**
The streamlined nose transition is one of the hard parts of an aircraft MOC. Work through slope bricks of different sizes, moving from narrow to wide, to achieve a smooth nose curve.

**Step 4: Wings and tail**
Wing construction has to balance appearance and structural strength. On large aircraft, add Technic beams inside the wing as support. For the horizontal and vertical stabilisers, take care to reproduce the correct angles.

**Step 5: Details and livery**
Finish with the canopy, engines and landing gear. For the livery, use parts in different colours to reproduce the paint scheme of the real aircraft.

### 3.3 Advanced: From Following Plans to Original Design

Once you have built several sets of **brick aircraft MOC plans** confidently, try your own original design:

**Reverse engineering**
Find three-view drawings of the real aircraft (top, side and front), draw a grid over them, then convert the proportions into brick units. This is the most reliable method for original design.

**Modular design**
Break the aircraft into separate modules — nose, forward fuselage, wings, rear fuselage, tail — design each one, then combine them. This makes adjustment and revision much easier.

**Aerodynamic fidelity**
Even though it is a brick model, reproducing the aerodynamic features of the real aircraft — wing dihedral, winglets, blended wing-body details — makes the model far more convincing.

## 5. Common Questions and Fixes

**Q: What if I find parts missing halfway through the build?**
A: Check the plan's parts list first and confirm whether you simply did not order something. In the short term, substitute a part in a similar colour — it will not affect the overall structure.

**Q: What if my aircraft MOC keeps falling apart?**
A: Build a rigid frame inside the fuselage from Technic beams and pins, especially at high-stress points such as the wing roots and the landing gear mounts.

**Q: What if I cannot find the aircraft plan I want?**
A: Design it yourself in BrickLink Studio, or buy a paid plan on Rebrickable, where quality is usually higher.
