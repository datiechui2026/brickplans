# Greenhouse MOC Review: Is This Botanical Garden Worth It?

Short answer first: this greenhouse MOC is worth picking up. The look is faithful and the build experience sits above average — but the price is on the high side, so it suits builders who care most about display value and fine detail. As a miniature botanical garden under a glass dome, it uses roughly 1,800 pieces to recreate a tropical greenhouse scene, putting its value-for-money firmly in the upper-middle range.

## 1. Exterior Accuracy: The Glass Dome Steals the Show

The dome is the headline feature. It combines trans-clear parts with frame pillars to mimic the daylight performance of a real glasshouse, throwing clean, bright light through the structure when the sun hits it. The roof vents open and close, and overall accuracy lands at around 90 percent. If you build for the shelf rather than for play features, this is the section that sells the model.

## 2. Build Experience: About Six Hours of Work

At roughly 1,800 pieces, the build takes around six hours — a medium-to-heavy workload rather than a weekend afternoon. The instructions break the steps down clearly. Trans-clear elements need a firm, even press to avoid loose panels, and closing the dome requires precise alignment of the frame sections. Difficulty is moderate overall, and it fits builders with six months of experience or more.

## 3. Part Quality: Clean Clear Parts, One Sourcing Problem

The trans-clear elements are free of bubbles and hold with a sensible amount of grip — tight enough to stay put, loose enough to disassemble without stress. Colour matching between the base and the planter green is very close, and every part has smooth edges with no flashing and no missing pieces in the box. The one weak point: curved dome parts are in short supply, so replacement channels are limited if you ever need spares.

## 4. Value for Money: 0.38 Yuan per Piece

The reference price is about 680 yuan, which works out to roughly 0.38 yuan per piece — a little above comparable street-scene MOCs. If you already collect garden themes or a modular street layout, it earns its place as a landmark build. On a tighter budget, wishlist the instructions first and buy parts later.
