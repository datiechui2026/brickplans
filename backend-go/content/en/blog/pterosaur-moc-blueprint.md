# Brick Pterosaur MOC Review: A 50cm Wingspan Build

## Product Overview

Pterosaurs ruled the skies of the dinosaur era, and their enormous wingspan, long pointed beak and distinctive head crest have long made them a favorite subject for paleontology fans and brick builders alike. This **brick pterosaur MOC blueprint** is modeled on an adult pterosaur and focuses on three signature features: the spread wings, the slender neck and the unmistakable head crest. The instructions were drawn up by a dedicated MOC designer and include a complete parts list, step-by-step diagrams and scale references. The finished model reaches a wingspan of over 50cm, which makes it striking whether you display it on its own or use it as a centerpiece for tabletop gaming scenery. On [brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn), large flying-animal MOCs like this one are consistently one of the most popular categories.

## Build Difficulty

Overall difficulty sits at **advanced-plus (★★★★☆)**. The blueprint's core challenges come in two areas. The first is the **wing membrane structure**: building wings that read as both thin and taut takes a lot of slope bricks working together with hinge pieces. The second is **body balance**: the head and long neck push the center of gravity forward, so the designer has to distribute enough counterweight inside the torso. Because the wings are a symmetrical structure, the build tests your patience and precision. Beginners should lock down the base and torso first, then extend outward to the wings step by step.

## Parts Review

The **brick pterosaur MOC blueprint** is unusually careful about parts selection. The main palette is dark gray, off-white and brown, which matches the look of pterosaur skin and bone. Key parts include **hinge pieces** (for folding and spreading the wings), **long rods** (supporting the neck and wing ribs), **slopes and wedge bricks** (shaping the streamlined body curves) and **claw-shaped specialty pieces** (recreating the wing fingers). The parts are compatible with mainstream brick systems and are easy to source as loose elements. In our test the color consistency was well controlled, and we especially recommend Gobricks dark gray and off-white parts: the color layering is clear and gives the finished model a much more organic, living feel.

## Building Experience

The build breaks into three clear stages: first the body skeleton and the weighted base, then the neck and head with the crest angle adjusted, and finally the symmetrical wing structure. The whole process takes roughly 4 to 6 hours, which makes it a good immersive weekend project. The most pleasant surprise is the wing joint design. Thanks to a clever hinge arrangement, the wings can spread and fold freely, letting you pose the model gliding, hunting or roosting, so play value goes well beyond a static display piece. Every step in the blueprint is marked with clear arrows and part numbers, and following along you will almost never get stuck.

## Finished Model

The completed pterosaur MOC has serious visual impact: the spread wings paired with the streamlined torso create a sense of a creature about to launch into the air. Adding a simple rock base or a clear stand to lift it off the ground makes the suspended pose even better. Whether it sits in a study, on a display shelf, or serves as a keepsake from a parent-and-child build, this **brick pterosaur MOC blueprint** produces a model that will be the focal point of any space it occupies.
