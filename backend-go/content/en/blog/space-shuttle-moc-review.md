# Space Shuttle MOC Review: 1:110 Scale, 6-8 Hour Build

## Introduction

The space shuttle is one of the most recognisable vehicles ever built for human spaceflight, and building one out of bricks is a bucket-list project for a lot of space fans. This review walks through a space shuttle MOC instruction set in detail — the parts it calls for, the substitutions the designer allows, and what the build actually feels like from the first frame to the display stand.

## Product Overview

The design is based on the American space shuttle and is drawn at roughly 1:110 scale. The finished model measures about 45 cm long with a wingspan of around 28 cm. The designer reproduces all three core components of the launch stack: the orbiter, the external tank, and the solid rocket boosters.

The instruction set runs to roughly 1,200 part steps, and the level of detail is genuinely impressive for a fan-designed model.

## Build Difficulty

The difficulty sits in the medium-to-hard range, split into four main stages:

1. Lower frame and landing gear
2. Fuselage and payload bay
3. Wings and tail
4. External tank and boosters

Each stage comes with a clear parts list and well-broken-down steps, so even someone new to MOC building can finish the model in 6-8 hours. The instructions specifically call out how to reinforce the key connection points, and the result is a structure stable enough to pick up and handle without worry.

## Parts Review

The parts selection shows real thought from the designer:

**Rare parts:** The build uses about 15 dark grey curved panels and 8 transparent dark blue 1x1 bricks to simulate the cockpit glass. These pieces are not common, but they can be sourced through BrickLink or domestic brick parts retailers.

**Substitutions:** The designer lists several alternative parts. For example, a 2x4 tile can stand in for the combination of a 2x2 tile plus a 1x2 brick, which lowers the barrier to sourcing everything you need.

**Colour scheme:** White, light grey, and black carry the model, with orange accents picking out details — a combination that nails the shuttle's real-world look. The red-brown external tank against the white orbiter is especially striking and gives the finished stack strong visual contrast.

## Build Experience

A few things stand out during the build. The **openable payload bay** can hold a small satellite inside, which adds a lot of play value. The **retractable landing gear** uses a clever mechanical arrangement to reproduce the shuttle's landing stance. The **rear thrusters** combine cylindrical parts with transparent orange bricks to suggest engine flame, and the effect lands hard.

The **fuselage curves** are the design's biggest highlight: the designer pairs hinge bricks with slope bricks to reproduce the shuttle's streamlined silhouette almost perfectly.

## Finished Model

Once complete, the shuttle is a genuine desk piece. The transparent cockpit windows work beautifully with the pointed nose, and the smooth fuselage lines flow into the externally mounted tank and boosters for a launch-ready pose. The model sits on a matching display base with a NASA-style printed nameplate sticker, which lifts the overall finish.

Compared with the official LEGO space shuttle sets, this MOC goes further on detail accuracy — particularly in the wing angle and the shape of the tail, both of which sit closer to the real orbiter.

## Verdict

This space shuttle MOC instruction set is a well-made piece of work. The designer shows a high standard across structural design, parts selection, and display presence, and the build manages to be both challenging and rewarding. For space fans and brick MOC builders alike, it is absolutely worth keeping in the collection.

If you are looking for a high-quality space shuttle MOC to build, this one is worth your time — whether it ends up as a desk display or a gift for a child, it delivers.
