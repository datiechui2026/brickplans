# MOC Player Communities: Where to Share Your Brick Builds

## Introduction

MOC — My Own Creation — is the part of the brick hobby where you design without instructions. Building alone is satisfying, but the culture only comes alive when builders trade work, compare techniques and answer each other's questions. Below is a rundown of the communities where that conversation happens, what each one is best at, and how to join in without lurking forever.

## Why MOC Builders Need a Community

### Inspiration and Creative Cross-Fire

A good community shows you styles and subjects you would never have tried yourself. Seeing another builder's brick-built version of a landmark, a sci-fi ship or a mechanical device is usually what triggers your next idea. That exchange of ideas is the core value of talking to other MOC builders.

### Technical Support and Troubleshooting

Unstable structures, missing parts, instructions you cannot follow — every MOC builder runs into these. Asking in a community usually gets a fast answer from someone who has already solved the problem, which is how you get past a creative block instead of abandoning the build.

### Showing Work and Getting Feedback

Once a model is finished, what you want most is a reaction. Community platforms give you a stage for that, and likes and comments from people who understand the build are the best motivation to start the next one.

## The Main MOC Communities Compared

### 1. BrickPlan.cn Community

A community platform focused on sharing MOC instructions, brickplan.cn makes it easy to stay in touch with other MOC builders.

**Core features:**
- Instruction upload and download, with support for multiple file formats
- Comment threads under each build, with image and text replies
- A tag and category system for finding similar models
- Likes and favourites for saving ideas you want to come back to

**Who it suits:** all MOC builders, especially those looking for instructions and for people to discuss build techniques with.

### 2. Baidu Tieba — Brick Bar and LEGO Bar

Baidu Tieba is one of the longest-running gathering places for brick fans, and its Brick Bar and LEGO Bar forums hold a large population of MOC hobbyists.

**What the community is like:**
- Free-form posting, well suited to long, in-depth discussion
- Many veteran builders with deep technical knowledge
- Dedicated threads for showing off MOC builds
- Occasional themed building events

**Strengths:** a huge user base, so questions get answered quickly.
**Weaknesses:** moderation is loose and good posts are easily buried.

### 3. Xiaohongshu MOC Hashtags

Xiaohongshu has become an important showcase for MOC builders in recent years. Hashtags such as #MOC and the brick-creation tag make it easy to find a large amount of good content.

**Platform traits:**
- Photo plus short-video format, so work looks its best
- A young, active user base with high interaction rates
- Good for finished-model photos and step-by-step build logs
- You can attach a link to where the instructions were bought

**Who it suits:** builders who enjoy documenting the process and care about visual presentation.

### 4. WeChat and QQ Groups

Instant-messaging groups are the most active arena for MOC chat. There are many brick-themed WeChat and QQ groups, and the atmosphere is usually more private and friendlier.

**Group types worth joining:**
- **General MOC groups:** for all kinds of builders, with a wide range of topics
- **Themed MOC groups:** modular street scenes, mecha, military and so on, where discussion stays focused
- **Regional groups:** handy for offline meetups and swapping parts in person

**How to join:** through community posts, a blogger's recommendation or a friend's invitation.

### 5. The Bilibili MOC Creator Scene

Bilibili has a large number of MOC video creators, and their comment sections, fan groups and feeds form a distinct ecosystem of their own.

**What you get:**
- Build-process videos to learn technique from
- Finished-model showcases and reviews that explain the details
- Livestreams for real-time chat about design decisions
- Joint projects between creators, with builds crossing over into each other's worlds

## How to Take Part Efficiently

### A Beginner's Guide to Joining In

If you are new to MOC and want to fit into the scene quickly, these steps help:

1. **Watch more than you post:** learn the tone and the rules of a community first
2. **Start with a question:** ask politely when you are stuck — most builders are happy to help
3. **Share your first MOC:** even a simple model is worth posting
4. **Comment on other people's work:** leave honest, specific feedback

### Strategies for Experienced Builders

If you already have a body of work, try going deeper:

- **Start a themed discussion:** raise design questions or building techniques as topics in their own right
- **Join community events:** many communities run regular building contests or themed challenges
- **Build a portfolio:** keep updating your own thread so your name becomes recognisable
- **Post across platforms:** publish the same build in several places to reach a wider audience

## Chinese-Language vs International Communities

### Strengths of Chinese-Language Communities
- No language barrier, so discussion flows easily
- Better knowledge of Chinese brick brands and where to source parts
- Shared cultural background and closer aesthetic preferences
- Buying instructions and parts is more convenient

### International Platforms Worth Watching
- Rebrickable: the largest MOC instruction platform worldwide
- Flickr brick groups: high-quality build photography
- Eurobricks forum: deep technical discussion

Chinese builders are best served by making a Chinese-language community their main base while following international platforms for ideas at the leading edge of the hobby.

## Building Your Own MOC Circle

### From Online to Offline

MOC chat should not stay online. Brick exhibitions, player meetups and building workshops let you meet people who share your interests in person. Major cities including Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou and Shenzhen all host regular brick fan gatherings.

### Starting Your Own Group

If you have the energy and enthusiasm, consider starting a group of your own. Pick a clear theme, write a few rules, run activities on a regular schedule, and a like-minded crowd will gradually form around it.

## Closing Thoughts

Whether you have just started building or already have a shelf full of finished models, the right community makes the hobby richer. BrickPlan, Tieba, Xiaohongshu, chat groups, Bilibili — each platform offers something different. The part that matters is taking the first step: post a build, leave a comment, introduce yourself to someone new.
