# How to Build a Train Station MOC: Platform, Hall, and Track

## Introduction

A train station is the transport hub of any brick city, and one of the most satisfying subjects you can build. Whether you want a transport centre for an existing street layout or a standalone station diorama, this **train station MOC tutorial** takes you from an empty baseplate to a finished model, one step at a time.

## 1. Planning and Design

Good planning before the first brick saves a lot of rework. Start by deciding the size of your station: a compact intercity stop or a large terminus? For newcomers, a **medium-sized single-platform station** is the best starting point. You still get the full building process, but the project stays small enough that you actually finish it.

### 1.1 Setting Size and Scale

A **brick train station** has to be in scale with your minifigures. A standard minifigure is about 4 bricks tall, so keep the platform at **2 bricks high**, level with the floor of the train car. The platform should be long enough to hold **4-6 cars**, which works out to roughly **60-80 studs**.

### 1.2 Collecting Reference Material

Before you build, gather photos of real stations and study them for:
- The overall architectural style (modern, classic, industrial)
- The structure of the platform canopy
- The layout of the concourse
- The way the tracks are laid

## 2. Base and Platform

### 2.1 Building the Base

Start with a **32x32 or 48x48 baseplate**. Doubling the thickness adds stability: lay a layer of plates underneath and cover it with a second layer on top. The structure becomes much more rigid, and the gap between the layers is a handy place to hide wiring if you plan to add lighting.

### 2.2 Platform Design

The platform is the core of a **brick train station**. Keep these points in mind:

1. **Platform height**: 2 bricks, matched to the height of the track
2. **Platform edge**: use slope bricks or 1x2 tiles for the safety line, usually marked in yellow
3. **Platform surface**: lay dark grey bricks as the floor and mix in a few lighter bricks to suggest a tiled texture
4. **Platform furniture**: add benches, timetable screens, trash cans and ticket machines

### 2.3 Laying Track

Track is the part of a **MOC tutorial** that trips people up most. You can use LEGO's official track components, or build the track yourself from ordinary bricks:

- **Official track components**: LEGO train track, including straight and curved sections, lets you lay standard track quickly
- **Brick-built track**: use 1x2 plates with 1x2 tiles on a dark grey baseplate to create the visual effect of rails

If you go the brick-built route, add a ballast effect along both sides of the track: scatter light grey and dark grey 1x1 round plates at random around the rails.

## 3. The Main Building

### 3.1 Concourse

The concourse is the visual focus of the station. When designing it:

- **A tall space**: support the roof on columns at least **8-10 bricks high** to create a sense of openness
- **Large glazed areas**: use window frames combined with transparent bricks so daylight reaches the hall
- **Waiting seats**: line up rows of seating in the waiting area; a 1x2 brick topped with a 1x2 plate is all you need
- **Information board**: a black plate with transparent red bricks imitates an electronic departure screen

### 3.2 Ticket Hall

Set the ticket area to one side of the hall, including:
- Ticket windows (a 1x2x2 window frame combined with a counter)
- Self-service machines (1x2 tiles with 1x1 transparent bricks)
- Queue barriers (small railing pieces)

### 3.3 Roof Design

The roof is where a **brick scenery build** really shows off its design work. Three reliable options:

- **Arched canopy**: curved bricks for a vaulted roof, ideal for an old European station
- **Flat roof with skylights**: transparent bricks set into a flat roof, ideal for a modern station
- **Sawtooth roof**: slopes alternated in rows to create an industrial sawtooth profile

## 4. Details and Scenery

### 4.1 Exterior Details

- **Station sign**: spell the station name in bricks across the front of the building
- **Clock**: fit a round clock face to the main facade
- **Canopy**: build a rain canopy above the entrances
- **Greenery**: add trees and flower beds to the forecourt

### 4.2 Interior Details

- **Kiosk or convenience store**: tuck a small shop into a corner of the concourse
- **Luggage lockers**: a row of lockers made from 1x2 door panels
- **Restroom signs**: use printed 1x1 tiles for the signage
- **Trash cans**: a 1x1 cylinder brick topped with a 1x1 round plate

### 4.3 Movement and Life

To make your **train station MOC** feel alive, add:

- A moving train: place a train on the track, ideally with a power system fitted
- Arriving passengers: minifigures carrying luggage
- Staff: a conductor and station crew on the platform

## 5. Advanced Techniques

### 5.1 Lighting

LEDs inside the station dramatically improve the atmosphere. Fit small LEDs in the ceiling pendants of the concourse, in the platform lamp posts and at the ticket windows.

### 5.2 Multi-Level Structure

For a large station, design an underground level (a metro interchange) and an upper level (retail space) to give the building real depth.

### 5.3 Fitting into a Street Layout

If your **train station MOC** has to sit inside an existing street layout:
- Keep the style consistent with the neighbouring buildings
- Provide an entrance to the station from the pavement
- Reserve a taxi drop-off zone in front of the station

## 6. Final Thoughts

Building a **brick train station** is a demanding but hugely rewarding MOC project. Planning, base, platform, main building, track and detailing each need careful design and patient assembly, and the order in section 2 through 4 is the one that keeps the build manageable. Builders of any level can find a technique here that fits their collection, so pick up your bricks and get started, then share the result on BrickPlans so other builders can see it.
