# Mario LEGO MOC Plans Reviewed: Minifigure to Bowser's Castle

The Mario Bros. are among the most recognizable characters in gaming history. From the red cap to the blue overalls, Mario LEGO plans let builders recreate the plumber's classic adventures in brick form. Below is a review of several popular Mario-themed MOC plans, covering what each build includes, how hard it is to put together, and what you end up with on the shelf.

## 1. Popular Mario MOC Plans Worth Building

### 1. Classic Mario Minifigure
- **Plan name**: Super Mario classic minifigure-scale model
- **Part count**: about 450 pieces
- **Difficulty**: Low
- **Rating**: ★★★★★

This Mario LEGO plan is a very good entry point for newcomers. The red cap is built from slope bricks, the blue overalls are paired with brown shoes, and the mustache is recreated with a printed element. The compact footprint makes it easy to display on a desk, and at roughly 450 pieces it is a weekend project rather than a long commitment. If you are building your first Mario MOC, start here: the shape is forgiving, the color blocking is simple, and the finished model still reads instantly as Mario from across the room.

### 2. Question Block and Coin Scene
- **Plan name**: ? Block scene set
- **Part count**: about 600 pieces
- **Difficulty**: Low
- **Rating**: ★★★★☆

The classic question block and coin combination is one of the most identifiable elements in the entire Mario series. This plan uses a spring mechanism so the coin pops out when you press the block, which gives it far more play value than a static display piece. Around 600 pieces keeps the build approachable, and the mechanism is the part worth studying closely — it is a neat piece of functional brick engineering that transfers well to other MOC ideas. The half-star deduction is simply because the model is more of a vignette than a full scene; it looks best paired with a character build.

### 3. Bowser's Castle Level
- **Plan name**: Bowser's Castle scene MOC
- **Part count**: about 1,800 pieces
- **Difficulty**: High
- **Rating**: ★★★★★

This is a complete level scene, including Bowser's boss room, a lava pool and a brick bridge. At roughly 1,800 pieces the build is demanding — expect to spend real time on the castle walls and on getting the lava section to sit cleanly — but the result is imposing once finished. This is the plan to choose if you want a centerpiece rather than a desk model, and it pairs naturally with the smaller character builds above if you want a full display.

## 2. Key Building Points

### 1. Character Proportions
Mario's super-deformed body shape is the single most important thing to get right. The oversized head and small body are the signature feature, with the head taking up roughly two-thirds of the total height. If you compress the head or lengthen the legs, the model stops looking like Mario even when every individual section is built correctly, so check the proportions before you commit to the torso.

### 2. Pipe Design
Green pipes are the defining visual element of the Mario games. Cylindrical bricks combined with curved bricks reproduce the pipe shape cleanly, and the plan shows how to stack the sections so the opening reads correctly from the front. Get the diameter consistent between the vertical shaft and the rim and the whole thing looks like it was molded in one piece.

### 3. Pixel-Art Style
Building an 8-bit pixel-style Mario out of 1x1 bricks and tiles is a completely different creative direction from the sculpted character builds, and it is a great way to use up small parts. Tiles on the front face give the flat, screen-like finish the style needs, while the 1x1 bricks behind them handle the color changes.
