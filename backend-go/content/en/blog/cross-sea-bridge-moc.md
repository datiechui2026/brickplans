# How to Build a Cross-Sea Bridge MOC: Towers, Cables, Deck

The short version: a cross-sea bridge MOC stands or falls on three assemblies — tower, stays and deck. Lock the towers together with standard bricks and plates, render the cables with fishing line or chain links, and split the roadway into 5-8 sections. At 1:100 scale, an 80 cm bridge needs roughly 1,500-2,000 parts.

## 1. Design: Fix the Scale and Dimensions First

Lock the deck width at 6 studs (about 4.8 cm) and set the tower height at one-half to two-thirds of the deck length. An 80 cm bridge, for example, pairs with a 40-50 cm main tower — that ratio reads as the most balanced visually.

Deck segmentation: 3 sections for the main span plus 2 approach sections on each side, 7 sections in total, each 10-12 cm long.

## 2. The Tower: Stability Comes First

**Step 1, build the base.** Stack three layers of 6x6 plates to form the tower footing, then sink 4 standard 2x4 bricks into it as the tower columns.

**Step 2, add crossbeams.** Every 6 brick courses, add a 2x8 plate crossbeam that ties the two columns into one rigid unit. Bending resistance improves noticeably.

**Step 3, taper the top.** Use slope bricks to shape the triangular silhouette of the tower cap, and leave openings for the cable saddles at the same time.

One caveat: once a tower passes 40 cm in height, run 2 technic axles vertically through the tower body to stop it from swaying.

## 3. Stay Cables: Two Routing Options

**Option 1 (fishing line).** Thread 0.5 mm fishing line out of the saddles at the tower top and fan it down to the anchor points on the deck, 6-8 lines per side, secured with 1x1 bricks with holes.

**Option 2 (chain links).** Use off-the-shelf chain links to imitate steel cable. It is stronger, but the curve is stiffer, so it suits small bridges under 60 cm.

Aim for a cable angle of 25-35 degrees. Steeper angles look sparse; flatter angles lose the sense of tension.

## 4. Deck Sections and Assembly

Lay the deck surface on 2x8 plates, and join each section to the next with hinge pieces or technic pins so the bridge can be taken apart and moved. Add 1x2 railing bricks along both edges of the deck and reserve a streetlight position every 4 studs — that is where the detail level comes from.

## 5. Instructions and Budget

## FAQ

**Q: How many parts does a cross-sea bridge MOC need?**

A: An 80 cm twin-tower structure takes about 1,500-2,000 parts, half of them standard bricks and plates. Buy loose parts against the brickplan.cn list and expect a budget of CNY 800-1,200 and 8-12 hours of building.

**Q: What material should I use for the stay cables?**

A: 0.5 mm fishing line is the recommendation — run it out of the saddles at the tower top, fan it down to the deck and fix it with 1x1 bricks with holes. Chain links also work, but the curve is stiffer. With the fishing line approach, 6-8 lines per side at 25-35 degrees looks the most natural.

**Q: What if the tower is so tall that it wobbles?**

A: Above 40 cm in height, run 2 technic axles through the tower body and add a 2x8 plate crossbeam every 6 brick courses to lock the columns together. With three stacked layers of 6x6 plate at the base, the wobble is essentially eliminated.

**Q: Why split the deck into sections instead of building it in one piece?**

A: A single deck section longer than 20 cm tends to deform and sag. Once it is divided and joined with hinges or pins, it is easier to dismantle and transport, and the load is spread across the joints. Go with 3 sections for the main span plus 4 approach sections, each 10-12 cm.
