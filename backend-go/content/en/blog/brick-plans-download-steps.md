# How to Download Brick Plans on BrickPlan in 4 Steps

Short answer: downloading brick instructions on BrickPlan takes four steps — search for the plan, open its detail page, hit download, then unzip and open the file. The whole process runs about 3 minutes, and more than 80% of the plans on the site are free. Below is the detail for each step, plus the snags that trip people up.

## Step 1: Search BrickPlan for the plan you want

Open the BrickPlan site and type a set number or a model name into the search box — for example a theme like "modular street" or the name of a MOC designer. Results show each plan's part count, price and rating. Beginners get the best success rate by picking plans with under 500 parts and a rating of 4.5 or higher.

## Step 2: Read the plan's detail page

Once you click into a detail page, focus on three things: the parts list, the step preview images, and user comments. The parts list tells you exactly how many pieces you need — a 2,000-piece plan, for instance, will be itemized clearly. Comments from builders who had to redo sections help you steer clear of plans with vague steps before you commit.

Go through every preview image on the page. Check that the step diagrams are legible and that the parts added at each step are easy to tell apart. If a plan only has a finished-model render and no step photos, think twice before downloading. Also look at the difficulty level shown on the page: BrickPlan generally uses three tiers — beginner, advanced and challenge. New builders should start with beginner plans of 300 parts or fewer, which take 1 to 2 hours to build and are much less likely to be abandoned halfway.

## Step 3: Click download and choose a format

The download button sits on the right side of the detail page. The usual formats are PDF and io source files. PDF is what you follow directly while building; io files are what you open in Studio software if you want to modify the model later. Check the file size before downloading — plans typically run from 5MB to 30MB. Anything much larger is probably a high-resolution multi-page version that will be slow to open on a phone.

On mobile, download the PDF first: a small screen still lets you zoom and page through freely. On a computer, grab both formats — PDF for building, io for future edits. If clicking the download button does nothing, the browser has almost certainly blocked the pop-up download. Look for the blocked-pop-up notice at the right end of the address bar, allow it, and click again. Don't keep refreshing the page.

## Step 4: Unzip the file and open the plan

Always unzip the archive before use. Android phones can do it with the built-in file manager; on a computer, 7-Zip is the recommended tool. Once the PDF opens, flip to the parts list page and check it against your stock before you start building — that avoids the classic situation of discovering a missing piece halfway through.

After unzipping, put your plan files in one dedicated folder and rename them in a "set name + part count" format, such as "Modular Coffee Shop 800 pcs.pdf", so you can find them instantly next time. While building, load the PDF on a tablet or a second screen: plan on the left, parts bins on the right. It beats printing, since you can zoom freely. If you find a mistake in the steps, go back to the detail page and leave a comment — you'll be notified when the author updates the file, and you can simply download the latest version.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I download brick plans to my phone?**
A: Open the BrickPlan site in your phone browser and download directly. On Android, unzip with the built-in tool; on iPhone, use the Files app. The PDF opens in the built-in reader.

**Q: How much does it cost to download plans on BrickPlan?**
A: More than 80% of plans are free. Paid plans are usually high-resolution step versions or original MOCs, mostly priced between 5 and 30 yuan. Payment gives you permanent download access with no hidden charges.

**Q: The downloaded plan won't open — can it be fixed?**
A: First confirm the file was fully unzipped, then check whether you have the right reader. If a PDF won't open, try another browser or Adobe Reader. io files require the free Studio software.

**Q: What's the difference between a PDF plan and an io plan?**
A: A PDF is a finished step-by-step diagram you follow as-is. An io file is an editable source file — you can change colors and add parts, which suits builders who want to remix. Both are downloadable on BrickPlan.
