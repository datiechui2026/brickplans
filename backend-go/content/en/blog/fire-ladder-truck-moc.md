# Fire Ladder Truck MOC: Build a Working Telescoping Ladder

The heart of a brick fire ladder truck is not the red bodywork — it is the ladder extension mechanism. Build it from two nested sliding-rail Technic pieces and the whole thing works. The model below uses roughly 750 parts and takes about 2.5 hours to complete, producing a truck 30 cm long whose ladder reaches 22 cm when fully extended. The full parts list and plans are available at brickplan.cn.

Step 1: Build the chassis and body. The chassis uses 2x6 plates in a double layer for rigidity. Add a steering linkage to the front axle, and give the rear axle a dual bearing setup so it carries the weight. The body is built in red brick: a driver's cab plus an equipment box. Use curved bricks at the front of the cab to shape the nose, sitting on a black bumper as the base layer.

Step 2: Build the ladder turntable. Mount a turntable piece at the rear of the truck as the ladder base, then add two support arms, one on each side, on top of it. Cross-brace the arms with Technic pieces so the ladder cannot tip sideways when raised. The turntable rotates a full 360 degrees.

Step 3: Build the telescoping ladder. Nest two 1x8 Technic pieces with sliding grooves, with the inner section cut 4 cm shorter than the outer one; pulled out, the ladder measures 22 cm in total. Add a work basket at the front end, with 1x2 plates forming the railing. Fit a hydraulic rod piece at the middle of the ladder to simulate the real lifting action.

Step 4: Add the fire-fighting details. Fit red warning lights to both sides of the body, built from transparent red pieces on 1x1 round plate bases. Stock the equipment box with small accessories such as a fire axe and a water nozzle. Spell out "119" on the body using white 1x1 pieces. Finally, add two fire extinguishers at the rear, each a red cylinder piece with a white top cap.

When the build is finished, extend and retract the ladder repeatedly to test how smoothly it runs. A thin smear of petroleum jelly on the sliding rails will help it glide. The complete parts list is on brickplan.cn; you will need around 8 Technic sliding-rail pieces, and these are the key to the extension mechanism, so buy good-quality original parts rather than clones.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I build a fire ladder truck MOC?**

A: Work in this order: chassis and body, then ladder turntable, then telescoping ladder, then fire-fighting details. Test the ladder sliding rails on their own for smoothness before mounting them on the truck — otherwise you risk having to take the build apart if they jam. The whole model takes about 2.5 hours.

**Q: Does the ladder actually extend?**

A: Yes. Two Technic sliding-rail pieces are nested, with the inner section sliding inside the outer one. Extended it measures 22 cm; collapsed it measures 14 cm. Leave a 0.5 mm gap between the sliding rails — too tight and the ladder jams, too loose and it sags.

**Q: Is the MOC instruction plan worth buying?**

A: Yes. The telescoping structure demands precise part specifications, and the plans list the exact part numbers for the sliding rails — buy the wrong size and it will not fit. The brickplan.cn plans also include the mounting angle for the hydraulic rod, which saves a lot of trial and error.

**Q: How is a ladder truck MOC different from a standard fire truck brick set?**

A: The ladder on a standard fire truck set is usually fixed or only swivels a little. On this MOC the ladder extends in two sections, the turntable rotates 360 degrees, and it has a work basket and hydraulic rod. Playability is far higher, but the sliding-rail parts also cost more.
