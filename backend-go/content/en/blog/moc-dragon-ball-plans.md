# Dragon Ball LEGO MOC Plans Review: Super Saiyan Builds in Bricks

Dragon Ball is one of the most influential anime franchises ever made. From Goku to Vegeta, from Super Saiyan to Ultra Instinct, **Dragon Ball LEGO plans** give fans a way to rebuild those characters in brick form. This review walks through the most popular plans, what each one demands from a builder, and the techniques that decide whether the finished model looks right.

## 1. Recommended Dragon Ball MOC Plans

### Super Saiyan Goku
- **Plan name**: SSJ Goku, 1:20 scale poseable model
- **Piece count**: about 900
- **Difficulty**: Medium
- **Rating**: ★★★★★

This **Dragon Ball LEGO plan** captures the signature blonde hair and blue eyes of Goku's Super Saiyan form. The jointed design lets the character hold the classic Kamehameha pose, which is the main reason it earns a full five stars — the model is not just a statue but something you can re-pose on the shelf.

### Vegeta
- **Plan name**: Vegeta, Prince battle armor version
- **Piece count**: about 800
- **Difficulty**: Medium
- **Rating**: ★★★★☆

Vegeta's Saiyan battle suit is the focus of this build. Slopes and tiles carry most of the work here, used to reproduce the sheen of the armor plating. Because the armor is made of large curved surfaces, the plan rewards careful part sorting more than raw technique.

### Shenron
- **Plan name**: Shenron, 1:50 scale
- **Piece count**: about 1500
- **Difficulty**: High
- **Rating**: ★★★★★

Shenron's winding body is the biggest challenge of the build. It calls for a large stock of green parts and a lot of joint connectors, and the long serpentine shape means every section has to hold its position once the dragon is standing. At roughly 1500 pieces it is also the most demanding plan in this group.

## 2. Key Building Points

### Hair Design
Super Saiyan hair is the defining feature of the character, and it is where most builds succeed or fail. Wedge bricks and pointed roof bricks are the standard approach for reproducing the spiked silhouette. Getting the spikes to read clearly from every angle matters more than adding extra detail — a sharp outline in the right shade of gold does more work than dense greebling.

### Reproducing the Gi
Goku's orange gi and blue belt are handled through color blocking and brick arrangement rather than special parts. The plan relies on clean color transitions so the belt reads as a separate garment and the folds of the gi stay visible without breaking the smooth surface of the torso.

### Poses and Articulation
Ball joints are what make multi-angle posing possible, and they allow the character to hold a range of fighting stances. When building poseable figures, check the tension at each joint as you go — a joint that is too loose will not hold a raised arm, and retrofitting it later usually means rebuilding the surrounding section.
