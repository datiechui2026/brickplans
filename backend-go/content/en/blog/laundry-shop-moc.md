# Laundry Shop MOC Tutorial: Washer, Drying Rack and Signage

The short version first: a laundromat is one of the most grounded subjects you can pick for a street scene, and it is genuinely beginner-friendly. A single drum washer, a line of hanging clothes and a plain signboard are enough to make the build feel lived in.

There is nothing flashy about a laundry shop. It earns its charm from everyday clutter rather than ornament, and that is exactly why it holds up to repeat viewing. It is also a great first MOC: the structure is simple and the parts are ordinary, yet you still get to practice interior detailing, which is where most street-scene builds succeed or fail. The tutorial below runs in three layers: storefront, equipment, details.

## Storefront Design: Start With the Sign

Keep the frontage simple. Lay light-colored wall bricks as the base, then build the signboard from a blue or green baseplate with white lettering on top, finished with one small lighting fixture. A display window made from transparent glass pieces works well here, letting you show off a corner of the machines inside.

At ground level, add a doormat at the threshold, and place plants on both sides of the door frame — or park a drink vending machine beside the entrance to give the street scene some interaction. For the overall palette, aim for clean and fresh: white, light blue and light gray, which quietly signals the "cleanliness" the business is selling.

## Washers and Equipment: The Heart of the Interior

The washer is the centerpiece of the whole MOC. For a front-loading drum machine, lay cylindrical bricks on their side, build the front panel from tiles and set a round transparent window piece into it, then add knob details along the top. For a top-loading machine, use a square body with a round lid plate on top.

Place two or three units side by side and vary the colors slightly — white, silver, light blue — so the row reads as real equipment rather than a matching set. Add a dryer along the wall with a vent grille detailed on top, and the equipment zone is complete.

One detail worth the effort: build the observation window from a transparent round plate ringed with a silver border. Look through it and you can just make out the "clothes" inside. Small mechanisms like this are what play-focused builders appreciate most.

## Drying Racks and Details: Where the Life Comes From

The drying rack is the finishing touch that ties the scene together. Span bar pieces across the interior or out front, then represent garments with 1x1 tiles held by clip parts. Stagger the colors and tilt each piece slightly — perfectly level clothes read as a diagram, not a shop.

For smaller props: a laundry basket on the counter (hollow lattice pieces filled with clothes), a price list on the wall, and a dirty laundry hamper on the floor all earn their place. The life in a laundry shop comes from these small objects, not from the architecture itself.

At the entrance, try a flipping "open" sign, or park a small delivery cart with a few folded garments piled in the bed. Either one lets a passerby read the story of the shop at a glance.

## Color Palette and Parts List

Stick to white, light blue and light gray as the base, then accent with bright small props in red or yellow. The core parts are:

- Cylindrical bricks (the washer drum)
- Round transparent window pieces
- Tiles (front panels, folded clothes)
- Clip pieces (hanging garments)
- Hollow lattice basket pieces (laundry baskets)

None of these are exotic. Most builders already have them in their parts bins, so you can start almost without sourcing anything — another reason the laundry shop MOC works so well as a first project.

## Common Mistakes and Next-Level Ideas

The mistakes beginners make most often: packing the equipment zone so tightly that minifigures have nowhere to walk, and hanging the drying rack so high that nobody can reach it. Sketch a rough layout before you build — equipment against the wall, an open aisle down the middle, counter on one side of the entrance.

For builders ready to go further, try a rotating drum with an axle run through the interior, or doors that genuinely open and close. You can also place the laundry shop next to a bubble tea shop and a barber to form a small neighborhood strip, which gives the whole street more of a lived-in feel.

## FAQ

**Q: Is a brick laundry shop MOC suitable for beginners?**
A: Very much so. The structure is simple and the parts are common, and although the shop is small it still trains three separate skills: the signboard, the interior and the small details. Work through storefront, equipment and details in that order and you can finish it in one session.

**Q: How do you build the front-loading washer?**
A: Form the drum opening with cylindrical or curved pieces, set a transparent piece into the front as the glass door, and add small knob pieces on the side. Close off the top with tiles, and two machines side by side immediately read as a self-service laundromat.

**Q: How do you make the drying rack and details feel lived in?**
A: Build the rack from thin bar pieces and hang a few garments made from slope and plate pieces. Then add a laundry basket, detergent bottles and a folding chair — once the details pile up, the scene instantly feels like people use it.

**Q: How should you choose the laundry shop's colors?**
A: Light blue, white and light gray is the reliable combination: white walls, light blue equipment, light gray floor, for an overall clean look. Use a contrasting color for the signboard, but avoid mixing too many colors across the whole shop.
