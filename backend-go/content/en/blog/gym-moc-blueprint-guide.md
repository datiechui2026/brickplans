# LEGO Gym MOC Blueprint: Equipment Area and Locker Room

A gym is one of the most distinctive themes you can drop into a street scene: barbells, treadmills, a punching bag, a wall of mirrors — a single building can tell a story about discipline and sweat. The key to giving your block that burst of energy is choosing the right **gym MOC blueprint** and getting the two functional zones — the equipment floor and the locker room — working together. This guide walks from plan selection down to the details, giving you a reusable build recipe.

## 1. Choosing a Plan: From Flat Layout to Two Storeys

### Layout and Footprint

A two-storey design works best for a gym: equipment area plus front desk on the ground floor, locker room and showers above. A 24×16 stud footprint gives you comfortable room to fit the core machines without feeling cramped. If you are short on space, a 16×16 single-level "neighbourhood gym" is a good compromise.

### Checking the Parts

The equipment needs a lot of dark grey, black and metallic parts, with a small amount of red for accents. Make sure you have enough pulleys, axles and pins, and note that black tread links can stand in for a treadmill belt. A good blueprint should spell out the part combination for every machine so you don't mis-build a structure halfway through.

## 2. The Equipment Area: The Gym's Face

### Barbells and the Squat Rack

Build the squat rack frame from 2×2 posts with crossbar pieces, then hang metallic weight plates on both sides (round plates stacked to thickness). For the barbell itself, use a long axle with dark grey round plates on each end, and rest it on a floor stand in front of the rack — the sense of weight comes through immediately.

### Treadmill and Spin Bike

For the treadmill, use a baseplate plus black tread links for the running belt, with a transparent panel standing at the front as the screen. The spin bike combines two wheel pieces with a frame assembly, and you can add a small motor to the front wheel for a spinning effect. Leave one stud of clearance between machines so the equipment doesn't crowd itself.

### Mirror Wall and Floor

Use a full run of silver or light blue transparent panels along one wall of the equipment area to suggest mirrors and stretch the sense of space. For the floor, lay large dark grey plates to imitate rubber gym matting — practical and good-looking at the same time.

## 3. The Locker Room: Small but Complete

### Lockers

The heart of the locker room is the locker bank: line up 1×2 door panels into a full wall of cabinets, keep the doors consistent in blue-grey or dark green, and leave a few doors openable for play value. Put backpacks and towel pieces on top of the lockers to add a lived-in feel.

### Showers and Rest Area

Screen off the shower area with half-height partitions plus light blue transparent panels, and add a drain grate piece to the floor. Beside it, the rest corner takes a folding chair and a water cooler (a cylindrical piece with a water bottle on top). The space is small but fully functional, with a complete circulation path.

## 4. Signage, Front Desk and Colour Mood

### Sign and Reception

A dumbbell-shaped 3D sign works well here: build it from cylindrical pieces with round plates at both ends and hang it over the shopfront. The front desk is a three-piece setup — a long counter, a computer screen and a logbook — and you can hang a shop flag on the wall behind it to reinforce the branding.

### Colour and Lighting

Go for a "cool grey plus energetic orange" palette: light grey walls, dark grey equipment, orange accents. Add a strip of top lighting and the mirror wall's reflections multiply, which makes the building especially photogenic in a night-time street scene.

## 5. Build Order and Common Pitfalls

### Recommended Order

Start with the baseplate and the two-storey walls, then the upper floor slab and the locker room, then place the equipment on the ground floor, and finally attach the signage and minifigures. Watch the load on the upper floor: add posts under the floor slab as support so it doesn't collapse under pressure.

### Common Problems

Gym equipment uses many small parts, so sort them into bags following the blueprint. Don't install the tread links the wrong way round, or your treadmill will "run backwards". Locker door panels are easy to mix up, so use colour to distinguish different cabinet bodies.
