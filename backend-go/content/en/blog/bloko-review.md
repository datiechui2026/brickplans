# Bloks Review: Big Bricks vs Small Bricks Compared

Here is the bottom line first: the two Bloks lines do different jobs. For ages 3-6, buy the big-brick range — safe and easy to build. For ages 6+ or adults who want faithful detail, buy the small-brick range, where mecha and space themes live. The two lines do not share parts, so do not buy across them expecting to mix pieces.

Among Chinese brick brands, **Bloks** is one you cannot skip. It broke into the young-kid market with big bricks, then moved into teen and adult territory with small bricks, and the two product lines sit in very different places. Plenty of first-time buyers stall on the same question: which line should I actually get? This Bloks review lays out both.

## 1. Brand positioning and product lines

Bloks positions itself as an **all-ages Chinese building brand**, and its core selling point is the big brick: larger than a standard brick, easy to click, hard to swallow, which makes it a natural first brick set for young children. In recent years the company added a **small-brick series** with parts sized to the mainstream standard, covering mecha, space and city themes, aimed at upper-primary kids through adult builders. Put simply, **big bricks sell safe early learning, small bricks sell fine detail.**

## 2. Representative sets

On the big-brick side, the **buildable cars, zoo and farm themes** are the classics: rounded parts, bright colors, few but large pieces, buildable by a two- or three-year-old working alone. There are also **licensed IP preschool ranges** with high recognition value that kids take to quickly. On the small-brick side, **mecha and space** are the most representative: mecha sets have plenty of articulated joints and sharp silhouettes, while the space sets reproduce rocket and space-station structure. Detail and play value are noticeably more hardcore, and the audience skews older.

## 3. Build experience and quality

Experience differs sharply between the lines. **Big bricks** are close to zero-threshold: large parts, moderate clutch that a child can pull apart and press home, and rounded corners that do not hurt hands. Safety is the biggest advantage. The downside is **cartoonish shapes and simple detail** — an adult will find it thin. **Small bricks** clutch tighter and carry finer parts, and mecha articulation and scene accuracy step up clearly, so a build feels like a model. For a young child, though, the parts are small and the steps are many, so it demands focus and patience.

## 4. Value for money and who should buy

On price, big bricks run slightly high for the piece count and play value, but they win on **durability and rebuildability** — one set lasts a long time, which suits safety-first families with young kids. Small bricks sit closer to mainstream brick pricing, and the big mecha and space sets are genuinely good value by piece count, making them a fit for **students and adults who enjoy detailed building**. If a child is just meeting bricks, take the big-brick line without hesitating. Once they are in primary school, or if you want to build yourself, the small-brick series is the better match.

## 5. Pros and cons

To sum up this Bloks review: the **big-brick strengths** are safe, easy building, rounded and durable parts, and strong early-learning value; the weaknesses are simple detail and limited adult appeal. The **small-brick strengths** are high accuracy, hardcore subject matter and a real sense of achievement; the weakness is that the parts are too small for young kids. Each line has a clear audience, so **choose by age and purpose and you cannot go far wrong**. If you want freer building beyond both lines, BrickPlan carries plenty of standard-brick MOC instructions, and paired with loose parts they open up a much bigger world — the two hobbies double the fun together.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the difference between Bloks big bricks and small bricks?**
A: Big bricks are larger with rounded corners, aimed at ages 3-6 for safety and easy handling. Small bricks follow mainstream sizing with mecha and space themes, aimed at upper-primary and above, and chase accurate detail.

**Q: What age is Bloks suitable for?**
A: The big-brick line fits ages 3-6, where a child can finish simple models alone. The small-brick line is best from 6 and up, with independent building going more smoothly after 8; younger builders need a parent reading the instructions alongside them.

**Q: Is the Bloks small-brick line worth it for adult builders?**
A: Worth a try, especially the mecha and space themes, which have plenty of articulated joints and solid accuracy, with most sets running 100-400 yuan. Part tolerance and clutch feel still trail the top-tier brands by a clear margin.

**Q: First Bloks purchase — big bricks or small bricks?**
A: Decide by age. For a 3-6 year old, pick a big-brick buildable car or zoo theme. For yourself or a child 6+, pick small bricks, starting with a 200-500 piece set to test the feel before expanding the line.
