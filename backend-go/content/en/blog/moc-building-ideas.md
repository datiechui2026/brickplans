# MOC Building for Beginners: A 6-Step Framework

The short version: a MOC plan is really "set the skeleton first, add the flesh later." Work through six stages — inspiration, sizing, structure, parts audit, modules, publish — and even a first-timer can finish an original build in 2–3 weeks, with a success rate roughly 70% higher than building whatever comes to mind.

MOC (My Own Creation) means a model designed entirely by you, with no official instructions. Plenty of builders spend a few hundred yuan on bricks, then sit staring at the pile. The problem usually isn't clumsy hands — it's having no building plan. Here is the process, step by step.

**Step 1: Build an inspiration library.** Keep a dedicated album or notes file on your phone and photograph buildings, mechs and animals whenever you see them. brickplan's inspiration board lets you file reference images by theme. Aim to collect 10+ images a week and don't start building until you have around 50 — the volume of reference material often sets the floor for how good the finished model can be.

**Step 2: Fix the size and the budget.** Decide the finished size first: a micro build is about 200–500 pieces, a mid-size build 800–1500 pieces, a large build 3000+ pieces. Estimate cost at roughly CNY 8–12 per 100 pieces, so a mid-size MOC usually lands at CNY 100–200 in materials — more flexible than buying official sets.

**Step 3: Sketch the structure.** Draw three views on graph paper or a tablet and mark the key connection points. For mechanical models, favour the beam-and-pin system; for architecture, favour brick-and-plate stacking. Spending 1–2 hours here saves the 3–4 hours you would otherwise lose tearing things apart and rebuilding — this is the most valuable part of the whole approach.

**Step 4: Audit parts and fill the gaps.** Sort what you own by colour and type, then search brickplan's parts library for substitutes where something is missing. Keep plenty of common parts on hand (2x4 bricks, standard pins) and hold rare parts (special prints, rare colours) to under 5% of the total part count, or the budget gets away from you.

**Step 5: Build in modules.** Split the model into a base, a main body and details, and finish each one separately, keeping every module small enough to pick up with one hand. Modularity cuts the cost of revisions by roughly 60%, and it makes the model easier to transport, display and light up later.

**Step 6: Test and publish.** Once it's together, do a handling test: give it a shake to check how tight it is, then take it apart and rebuild it three times to see how the parts wear. Shoot 9+ detail photos, note the part count and build time (brickplan generates a build profile in one click), then post it to the community for feedback — the next model will come together faster.

Common mistakes: making the first build too big by jumping straight to 5000 pieces, which usually ends up abandoned; chasing looks without thinking about structure, so the model falls apart at a touch; and setting no budget, so rare parts drain your wallet. Remember: finishing one 500-piece model beats starting three 3000-piece ones.

## FAQ

**Q: Can a complete beginner build a MOC straight away?**
A: Yes. Practise by taking apart and rebuilding 2–3 official sets first to get familiar with common connections, then follow the six steps here on a micro build of 200–500 pieces — your odds improve a lot.

**Q: How is a MOC plan different from building from instructions?**
A: Instructions train your hands; MOC trains your design decisions. A building plan gives you a framework for planning before you touch a brick, so you don't hesitate over part choice and structure — overall efficiency goes up by roughly 70%.

**Q: How much does a MOC usually cost?**
A: A micro build runs about CNY 20–50; a mid-size build of 800–1500 pieces costs roughly CNY 100–200 in materials. Parts can be reused from old sets, which can halve the real spend.

**Q: What if I run out of ideas halfway through?**
A: Pause for 24 hours, go back to the reference images in your inspiration library, or break the half-finished model into modules and recombine them. The comment sections on brickplan community builds often contain rescue ideas from other builders worth scrolling through.

**Q: How do I know when a MOC is finished?**
A: Three standards: the structure survives three rounds of disassembly and rebuilding, the theme is readable at a glance from a metre away, and you're happy to show it to a friend. Meet two of the three and you can publish — done beats perfect.
