# Drum Tower MOC: 7,200-Piece Three-Tier Chinese Build Guide

The short version: this Chinese drum tower MOC runs about 7,200 parts, stands roughly 55 cm tall, and takes around 26 hours to finish at 2-3 hours a day. Budget around 1,250 CNY. The tower is built as three tiers of double eaves, the ground-floor hall houses a great drum about 8 cm across, every level is wrapped in a wooden railing, and the roof is finished with ridge beasts. The result captures the heavy, grounded presence of a northern Chinese drum tower. Instructions come from the brickplan platform, with the parts list split floor by floor, so it suits intermediate and advanced builders ready to tackle traditional Chinese architecture.

## Step 1: Build the base and platform

Start with a 30x30 stud baseplate and lay dark gray bricks into a platform about 8 cm tall. Add stepped stairways on all four sides, then pick out the layered lines of a Sumeru-style pedestal along the platform edge using light-colored pieces. Reinforce the inside of the base with a cross-braced frame, because the finished tower is 55 cm tall and the load at the bottom matters. This step eats roughly 1,800 parts and is the foundation of the whole structure's stability.

## Step 2: Build the ground-floor hall and the great drum

The ground floor is an open or semi-open hall, with the drum standing inside it. Stack two large cylindrical pieces for the drum body, cap them with dark red round plates for the drum heads, and ring the rims with small gold pieces as studs. The stand uses brown pieces in a three-legged arrangement. Put columns at the four corners of the hall, close the outer walls with off-white pieces plus wooden door frames, and finish the entrance with a printed plaque element. This level uses about 2,000 parts.

## Step 3: Build the second and third tiers of double eaves

The second and third floors step inward as they rise. On each level, dark brown and red-brown pieces form the wooden railing, with even gaps left between the posts. Between levels, slope tile pieces build the double eaves and their upturned corners, while inverted slope pieces create the lifted curve at the eave line. The third tier pulls in again from the second, giving the tapering proportion of a pagoda. This step uses roughly 2,200 parts and takes the most time of any stage.

## Step 4: Finish the roof and ridge beasts

Top the tower with a four-sided hip roof, densely tiled in dark gray or black-gray tile pieces. Fit ridge beast elements at both ends of the ridge and set a finial at the center of the main ridge. Before you call it done, check that every level's railing is solid and verify parts against the floor-by-floor lists on the brickplan instruction page. If you want a night-time look, tuck lighting pieces inside the eave line; switch on the warm yellow light and the tower's silhouette comes straight out.

## FAQ

**Q: How tall is this drum tower MOC, and will it look out of place at home?**

A: The finished model is about 55 cm tall, close to half a meter, so it is a clear focal point. It works well on a living room display cabinet or on top of a study shelf. With lighting pieces added it looks best, and it is slightly tall for an ordinary desk, so a riser base is worth planning for.

**Q: How long does the whole build take?**

A: About 26 hours at 2-3 hours a day. Laying the tiles across the three tiers of double eaves is the slowest part at roughly 10 hours, the ground-floor drum and hall take about 6 hours, and the platform and the final detailing take about 5 hours each.

**Q: If I get a level wrong or run short of parts, can I substitute something else?**

A: Yes. Appearance parts like tiles and railing elements can be swapped as long as the color is close. Load-bearing columns and internal frame parts should stay as specified in the instructions, though, or the upper floors will not be stable. For anything missing, check the part numbers against the brickplan parts list and buy those pieces separately.

**Q: How do I get the night lighting effect?**

A: Run LED strip inside the eave line on each level, routing the wiring from the bottom up to the roof, and choose a warm yellow light source. You can also place a single light inside the tower so it glows out through the doors and windows, which gives a more restrained effect.

**Q: Can the ground-floor drum be removed and handled on its own?**

A: Yes, the drum is a separate module held to the hall floor by a few studs, so you can lift it out whenever you like. The drum heads, stand and body also break down into smaller pieces, which makes display or modification easy.
