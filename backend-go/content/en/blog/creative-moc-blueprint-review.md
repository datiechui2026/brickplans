# Creative MOC Blueprint Review: Airship, Izakaya and More

Building has no borders — if you can imagine it, you can build it out of bricks. Creative MOC is where that spirit lives: no fixed theme, no house style, no rules about what belongs together. In this review we look at a hand-picked set of creative MOC blueprints to see what the free-form side of the hobby actually builds like.

## Product Overview

The selection covers four very different ideas: a **Steampunk Airship** (850 pieces), a **Mini Japanese Izakaya** (620 pieces), a **Retro Game Console** (450 pieces) and a **Rotating Music Box** (1200+ pieces). Between them they span steampunk machinery, Japanese architecture, nostalgic electronics and mechanical assemblies. Piece counts run from roughly 300 to 2000 across this category, so there is something here for builders at every experience level.

## Build Difficulty: ★★★☆☆

Difficulty in creative MOC ranges widely, but the overall level here sits in the middle. The challenge is not usually complicated mechanisms — it is figuring out how to realise a distinctive design idea with a limited parts pool. The Mini Japanese Izakaya leans heavily on SNOT (Stud Not On Top) technique, turning bricks sideways to reproduce wooden lattice screens and eave details from Japanese architecture; beginners should expect a short adjustment period before the sideways building clicks. The Retro Game Console is the simplest of the four and mainly tests colour blocking and how faithfully you can capture a recognisable shape. The Steampunk Airship and the Music Box are a step up structurally, since both include moving components that have to actually work.

## Parts Review

Creative MOC demands variety rather than volume, and **Qiji, SEMBO and Gobricks** are the brands we would recommend for this kind of build. Qiji stands out for unusual shaped elements — its steampunk gear pieces, copper-coloured trim and curved window frames open up a lot of design options. SEMBO offers exceptional colour range, from cherry-blossom pink to bamboo-leaf green, and the rarer shades are exactly what a small, carefully colour-matched scene like the izakaya needs. Gobricks supplies the high-precision generic bricks that form the structural backbone of any creative MOC, with a comfortable clutch and easy disassembly when you inevitably rebuild.

## Building Experience

The build itself is the creative journey. The Steampunk Airship blueprint is especially well thought out: round bricks and transparent elements form the cockpit dome, chain and gear pieces decorate both flanks of the hull, and a brown-and-copper palette pulls the whole thing into a vintage industrial look. The Mini Japanese Izakaya packs an enormous amount of detail into a tiny footprint — noren curtains, lanterns, a counter, sake bottles and even a miniature oden pot, so every corner has a story. The Music Box blueprint is the biggest surprise: a simple gear train produces the rotation, and with a transparent base the finished piece is as good to look at as it is to play with. ## Finished Models

These are the kind of builds that are made to be displayed and handled. The Steampunk Airship comes out around 30 cm long and, on its display stand, looks like it is genuinely in flight. The Mini Japanese Izakaya includes its own base, and the lanterns glow convincingly through translucent parts — a superb desktop diorama. The Retro Game Console uses stickers to recreate a classic game screen, and both the buttons and the joystick move. When the Music Box spins, the small decorations on its base turn with it, which gives the whole piece a real sense of ceremony. Displayed alone or combined into a scene, all four hold up visually.
