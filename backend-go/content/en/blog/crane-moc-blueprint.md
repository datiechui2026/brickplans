# Brick Crane MOC Blueprint: From Chassis to Boom

The short version first: a crane MOC of roughly 1,200 parts, built in the order laid out below, is doable for a first-time builder in about six hours. You finish with a complete engineering crane that steers and raises its boom, standing around 35 cm tall — a genuinely imposing piece on a desk.

## Step 1: Chassis and Drive System

The chassis is the load-bearing base of the whole crane, so start with either a tracked or a six-wheel platform underneath it. The tracked version uses about 180 parts, grips better and looks more realistic; the wheeled version uses about 140 parts and steers more nimbly. Add mudguards and running boards to both sides of the chassis — they lift the level of detail noticeably. If you plan to motorise the model later, reserving space for the battery box at this stage saves you the most work.

## Step 2: Turntable and Cab

The turntable is the critical joint between the upper and lower body. Use a turntable part to get 360-degree rotation; this section runs to roughly 150 parts. For the cab, a two-seat layout works best, with opening doors and a windscreen — transparent elements for the windows read as far more convincing. Fit a warning light and a horn on the cab roof and the engineering-site feel arrives immediately.

## Step 3: Boom and Counterweight System

The boom telescopes in three sections. Build the base section around a technic frame so it carries the load; the assembly is about 350 parts and reaches roughly 40 cm when fully extended. The hook runs off a pulley block and can hold small items of around 200 g. The counterweight sits at the rear of the vehicle, uses about 60 parts, and stops the model tipping forward under load. Joints between the boom sections let you pose it three ways: lifting, held level, and stowed.

## Building Tips and Parts List

- Common parts: beams, pins, gears and pulleys make up about half the total part count, so sort them before you start building.
- Colour scheme: classic yellow-and-black hazard colours give the strongest result, with a little red and white as accent.
- Testing: after each section, turn the drivetrain by hand to confirm it runs smoothly — this avoids rebuilding later.

## FAQ

**Q: How many parts does a brick crane MOC need?**
A: The full version needs about 1,200 parts, half of them technic elements such as beams, pins and gears, plus transparent and decorative pieces. Budget roughly 150 to 300 RMB. Beginners can warm up on the 600-piece basic version first.

**Q: Can the brick crane actually lift things?**
A: Yes. With a manual drivetrain built from gears and a pulley block, the hook lifts small items of around 200 g steadily and gives you both raising/lowering and 360-degree slewing. If you want powered lifting, you will need to add a motor and remote-control kit separately.

**Q: How long does it take to build a crane MOC?**
A: About six hours for a newcomer following the steps, around three hours for an experienced builder. Build it in three sessions — chassis, turntable, boom — and test the drivetrain at the end of each one to avoid mistakes and rework.

**Q: Where can I get crane MOC blueprints?**
A: Search the crane tag on the BrickPlan platform to find original designs and parts lists from several builders. Work through the diagrams step by step, and if you get stuck you can talk to the author in the community comment section.

**Q: How is a crane MOC different from a regular engineering-vehicle brick set?**
A: A crane MOC is a fan's original design, so structures like the telescopic boom, the slewing turntable and the counterweight sit much closer to a real engineering vehicle. That means more play value, no overlap with mass-produced sets, and stronger building and collecting appeal.
