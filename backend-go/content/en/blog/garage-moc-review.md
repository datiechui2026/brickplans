# Garage MOC Review: Roller Door, Lift and Tool Wall Worth It?

The short version: this garage MOC delivers all three of its headline features — a working roller door, a knob-driven lift platform and a fully loaded tool wall — for about 1,800 pieces at roughly 120 RMB. The build runs around two hours, the finished model measures 20 x 16 cm, and it sits in the top 20% for value among small scene builds in the same price band. It makes a solid desktop display piece.

## Roller Door: Eight Hinged Panels on Rails

The door is built from eight 1x6 grey plates hinged to each other and seated in guide rails, so it pulls up and down by hand. Damping is on the light side, but travel is smooth and the door opens or closes cleanly roughly 95% of the time.

The one weak spot is light bleed: under strong lighting a little comes through the seams between panels. If that bothers you, a black backing plate behind the door blocks it without touching the mechanism.

## Lift Platform: Rack and Knob Drive

The lift uses a rack-and-knob transmission. One full turn of the knob raises the platform about 0.5 cm, and the maximum load is around 300 g — a standard brick-built car sits on it without sagging.

In testing the platform rose smoothly with no slipping, and repeated up-and-down cycles stayed consistent. This mechanism is the strongest element of the whole build and the main reason the model is worth the money.

## Tool Wall: 12 Tools Across Six Bricks

The tool wall stands six bricks tall and holds 12 small tools — wrenches, screwdrivers and the like — on 1x1 clip pieces. Tools go on and come off smoothly, so you can actually rearrange the wall rather than just look at it.

The wall also accepts three 2x2 sign tiles, which makes an "under repair" scene easy to stage and very photogenic.

## Verdict

Build difficulty lands in the moderate range, and the mechanism parts are well made for the price. If your budget is under 150 RMB, this is an easy yes.

If you want a more refined look, browse brickplan.cn for plans in the same theme and recolour your own version — swapping the roller door to a black-and-white two-tone makes it read much better on the shelf.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this garage MOC worth buying?**
A: Yes. Around 1,800 pieces for roughly 120 RMB, with two working mechanisms — the roller door and the lift platform — and a two-hour build. The finished model is compact and the mechanisms run smoothly, which puts it in the top 20% for value among small scene builds at this price. It suits newer builders well.

**Q: Can the lift platform really hold a brick-built car?**
A: Yes. Maximum load is about 300 g, and one turn of the knob raises the platform roughly 0.5 cm, so a standard brick car is held steady. The rack drive does not slip, and repeated raising and lowering stays smooth — durability looks reassuring.

**Q: What do I do if the roller door jams?**
A: First check both ends of the guide rails for debris or a misplaced brick. Then put a drop of petroleum jelly in the rail channel to lubricate it. If the panel hinges have worked loose, replace those 1x6 plates; after that the door should open and close cleanly more than 95% of the time again.

**Q: How different is this from a Technic build?**
A: The difference is mainly the transmission. This garage uses manual knob-and-rack drive, while Technic sets use gears and motors. The garage is simpler and quicker to pick up, which makes it a good two-hour entry project; if you want powered, moving play features, Technic is the better fit.
