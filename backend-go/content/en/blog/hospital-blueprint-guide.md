# Hospital MOC Blueprint Guide: How to Pick and Build One

Among the classic brick city themes — hospital, fire, police — the hospital is the building that does the most to make a layout feel like a complete city. A good hospital blueprint instantly adds both professionalism and everyday street life to your block. But hospital buildings are structurally complex with a lot of scene elements, and blueprint quality in this niche varies widely. This guide walks you through how to choose one, how to build it, and how to play with it.

## Three things to check before you choose a hospital blueprint

Don't judge a blueprint by its cover render. Look at three things: structural completeness, part count versus budget, and scene richness. A qualified hospital blueprint should at minimum include three major sections — the main tower, the entrance lobby and the ambulance entrance. Before you start building, check whether the parts list is clearly labeled and whether the common parts are easy to source. Hospital builds routinely run two to three thousand pieces, and if the design leans on too many obscure parts, buying them is both expensive and slow — you're often better off modifying the design slightly than chasing every rare element.

## Main structure: zoning emergency, outpatient and ward floors

The biggest difference between a hospital and an ordinary residential building is functional zoning. A well-designed hospital MOC puts the emergency area on the ground floor facing the street, so an ambulance and stretcher fit naturally; the outpatient area sits in the middle with a registration desk and waiting chairs; the upper floors become wards, with a neat row of beds and bedside tables. From the outside, each floor hints at its function; looking through the interior, the build tells a story. When I evaluate a hospital blueprint, the detail I care about most is the emergency entrance — a lit "EMERGENCY" sign is often the soul of the whole building.

## Detail scenes: small tricks that bring the hospital to life

Once the structure is up, details are what separate a good build from a great one. A solid hospital blueprint usually ships with a healthy set of scene accessories: a white nurse station, IV stands, a wheelchair, an operating lamp, even a microscope in the lab. If you already have minifigures on hand, add doctors, nurses and patients yourself so the emergency lobby, wards and pharmacy all feel staffed. These small changes need no extra blueprint, yet they massively improve how immersive the model feels.

## Building tips: don't let small parts slow you down

Hospital buildings are packed with small parts, especially white and light gray ones, which are the hardest to spot in a loose pile. My approach is to sort by color and size first, then enlarge the step diagrams on a tablet or computer. Many high-quality hospital blueprints provide high-resolution step-by-step images; paired with a manual stand and sorting trays, they save a huge amount of digging time. Also, build a foundation layer on baseplates before going upward — this prevents the whole structure from shifting in the later stages, a habit that matters even more on large hospital builds.

## From blueprint to your own hospital: modding ideas

After finishing a hospital, try some light modification: turn the second floor into a surgical area and add a translucent partition; put an "H" marker and a helipad on the roof; or lay a crosswalk at the emergency entrance so the traffic scene reads properly. The same hospital blueprint, with a different color scheme, extra signage and a rearranged floor plan, becomes a completely different hospital — and that is exactly what makes MOC building so addictive.
