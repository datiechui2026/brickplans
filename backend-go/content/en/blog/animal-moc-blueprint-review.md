# Animal MOC Blueprint Review: 5 Builds From Cute to Fierce

The brick world is not only skyscrapers and mech armor — it is also full of living, breathing animals. From round-faced cats and dogs to lions and tigers with real presence, recreating the animal kingdom in brick has become a favorite direction for MOC builders. In this review we look at five animal blueprints in depth to help you pick the one that suits you best.

## Product Overview

This review covers five animal MOC blueprints that draw a lot of attention: the **British Shorthair Blue Cat** (350 pieces), the **Golden Retriever** (420 pieces), the **Green Iguana** (280 pieces), the **African Elephant** (680 pieces) and the **Tyrannosaurus Rex** (1,200+ pieces). Together they span the full range from miniature pets to large beasts, with part counts between 200 and 1,500 — well suited to a parent-and-child project or a relaxed weekend build.

## Build Difficulty: ★★☆☆☆

Animal MOCs sit at the easy end of the hobby and make an excellent entry point. These blueprints are designed around surface accuracy first: unusual bricks and slopes are used to shape the animal's body curves, and complex mechanical structures rarely come into play. Small pets like the blue cat and the golden retriever take an experienced builder roughly an hour; even the larger African elephant and T-Rex need only two to three hours. If you are new to MOC building, an animal theme is a good way to build confidence before tackling something harder.

## Parts Review

For parts, we recommend **SEMBO**, **Panlos** and **Gobricks**. SEMBO has strong color expression, and its dedicated curved bricks and slopes reproduce the roundness of an animal's body very well. Panlos is a good source for rare colors — pink cat paws, or brown parts with an elephant-skin texture. Gobricks keeps up its usual quality control on general bricks, with clean sprue marks and well-judged clutch. All of these brands are fully compatible with LEGO, so you can mix them freely.

## Build Experience

The building process itself is a calming one. Take the blue cat blueprint: the designer stacks rounded corner bricks and smooth tiles of varying curvature to recreate the cat's round face and fluffy body. On the golden retriever, the ears and tail use hinge pieces, so they stay posable. The biggest surprise is the T-Rex blueprint — only 1,200+ pieces, but the clever skeletal structure and scale texture give the finished model real visual impact. On [BrickPlans](https://brickplan.cn), every animal blueprint comes with high-resolution step-by-step diagrams and a complete parts list, so you can gather everything before you start.

## Finished Models

Animal MOCs work well as desk pieces or gifts. The finished blue cat stands about 12 cm tall, a neat size for a bookshelf. The African elephant opens out to more than 30 cm with rich detail — even the curve of the tusks is reproduced accurately. The T-Rex reaches 25 cm tall, its open jaw and sharp teeth giving it a genuine sense of menace. None of these need a display case; set one down and it holds its own in the room.
