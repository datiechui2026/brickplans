# Cinema MOC Review: Structure, Parts and Value

**Bottom line up front:** this cinema MOC plan is above average overall. Accuracy to the reference building sits around 85%, the model runs about 1,500 pieces and measures 35cm wide when finished. It suits builders who have completed at least three sets already, and the value is reasonable rather than outstanding.

### 1. Structural Design

The plan uses a three-level layout: a ground-floor lobby with a ticket counter, a screening room on the second floor, and an equipment room on the third. The floors are carried on 2x2 brick columns, which hold up well and do not feel wobbly once the walls are closed in. The facade uses 1x2 slope bricks to suggest decorative trim lines, and the front sign is built to light up.

The weak point is ceiling height in the lobby. It is on the low side, and once you place a minifigure inside, the head sits almost flush against the ceiling. If you plan to populate the ground floor with a crowd, expect to either raise the floor level or leave figures seated.

### 2. Part Quality

Working from the parts list, the model comes to roughly 1,500 pieces, including 120 transparent elements and 18 printed pieces. The printed patterns are sharp and cleanly registered, with no visible offset, which is the part of a MOC plan most likely to disappoint and here does not.

The catch is in the parts table: three rare colours are mixed in, and sourcing those separately adds about CNY 60 to the bill. Check the list against brickplan before you order anything, so you do not end up with a build stalled halfway through waiting on a colour that nobody stocks locally.

### 3. Build Experience

The instructions run to 220 steps, with 4-8 pieces placed per step. That is a sensible rhythm, and the steps rarely feel either starved or overloaded. Measured build time was about 6 hours.

The dullest stretch is the screening room seating area, which is 40 units of 1x2 bricks laid out in rows. It is not difficult, just repetitive, and it is the point where most builders will want to take a break. Note also that the plan is drawn at roughly 1:45 scale, which is slightly off standard minifigure scale. Keep that in mind when you place the model in a street or display scene next to other buildings, because the difference is visible up close.

### 4. Value for Money

The plan itself is CNY 30. Buying the parts as loose elements runs about CNY 400-500, so the whole project lands under CNY 500. Comparable official sets of the same footprint routinely start above CNY 800, so the value holds up reasonably well.

The real barriers are not the price but the rare colours and the six hours of build time. New builders should think twice; anyone with a stocked parts bin will find this an efficient way to get a large building onto the shelf.

## FAQ

**Q: How much does this cinema MOC plan cost?**
A: The plan is about CNY 30, and the 1,500 loose parts run roughly CNY 400-500, so the total stays under CNY 500. If your parts collection already covers most of the list, you only pay the CNY 30 for the plan.

**Q: Is this cinema MOC suitable for beginners?**
A: Not really. A 220-step instruction book and rare-colour parts are unfriendly to new builders. Three or more completed sets of experience is a sensible baseline, and budget around 6 hours of build time.

**Q: Can I substitute domestic parts for the original ones?**
A: Yes. Swapping item by item against the parts list with domestic bricks saves roughly 20% of the cost. Transparent and printed elements are the exception, where the original parts are worth keeping, since domestic versions fall slightly short on light transmission and print accuracy.

**Q: What is the difference between this cinema MOC and an official set?**
A: A MOC plan gives you more design freedom and a larger footprint, but you have to source the parts yourself. An official set is buildable straight out of the box with everything included, at a higher price. Each has its trade-offs.
