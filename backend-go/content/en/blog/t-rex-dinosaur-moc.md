# T-Rex Fossil Skeleton MOC: 760-Piece, 1:35 Build

**Build difficulty**: ★★★☆☆ (beginner)
**Piece count**: 760
**Recommended brands**: SEMBO, Qiji
**Scale**: 1:35 | **Finished height**: approx. 28 cm | **Estimated build time**: 2–3 hours

Dinosaur MOCs fall into two camps. One recreates the animal as it looked alive; the other recreates the mounted fossil skeleton you would walk past in a museum. This T-Rex fossil skeleton design is the second kind — 760 pieces, 1:35 scale, fully articulated — and it is a great first project if you want to learn how skeletal structure actually translates into bricks.

## 1. Accuracy: Building a Skeleton Out of Bricks

The appeal of a fossil mount is the structure itself. The instructions work through the skull, cervical vertebrae, spine, ribs, pelvis and tail vertebrae in that order, and the key is getting the proportion and curvature of each section right: the skull has to be square and thick enough, the ribs need a genuine arc, and the tail vertebrae should taper node by node. At 1:35 the skull keeps its detail without the piece count running away from you.

The color choice deserves a mention too. Real fossils are a mottled bone white rather than a clean laboratory white, so this design does not build everything in pure white. Off-white carries the main mass, with a small amount of light sand on the shadow-facing surfaces. Under overhead lighting that contrast reads much more three-dimensionally than a single flat white would.

## 2. Articulation: Posing Is the Whole Point

"Fully articulated" is the most useful thing about this design. The neck, forelimbs, hind limbs and tail can all be set at different angles, which means one skeleton can hold several poses: head raised in a roar, lowered to sniff the ground, or forelimbs extended in a hunting stance. For a display model, that adjustability is worth more than a fixed skeleton — change the pose each season and it reads like a new build.

## 3. Parts and Color: Notes on the Off-White System

Mid-tone colors like off-white and light sand are exactly the range where brick color variation is most visible — you can spot the difference between brands, and even between batches, with the naked eye. For visible surfaces, try to source parts from the same brand and the same batch. SEMBO's off-white and Qiji's light sand are both stable as a main body color, but compare swatches before you mix them.

A skeleton build also leaves its structure exposed, so molding flaws and flash have nowhere to hide. When buying, favor brands with flat, clean brick faces.

## 4. Build Notes

1. **Build the spine first**: the spine is the axis of the whole skeleton. Get it straight and level first, then hang the ribs off the sides and attach the skull at the front.
2. **Do not lock the joints**: use pins and axle sleeves at the joints and leave some play. Lock everything down and you lose the ability to pose the model.
3. **Counterweight the head**: the skull is a cantilevered structure, so the neck needs thickening or multi-point support, otherwise the head droops.
4. **Taper the tail vertebra by vertebra**: make each tail segment slightly shorter than the one before it so the tip closes naturally.
5. **Anchor it to the base**: fix the finished skeleton to the baseplate with technic supports or transparent support pieces so it cannot tip over.
