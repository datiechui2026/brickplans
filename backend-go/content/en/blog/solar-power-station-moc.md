# Solar Power Station MOC Review: PV Array and Inverter Room

The short version: this Solar Power Station MOC nails its two core modules — the photovoltaic array and the inverter room — with far more polish than the part count suggests. It rides the clean-energy trend nicely, works as a teaching prop or a desk display, and the brickplan instructions hold up. Worth a build.

## Exterior Design

The PV array is made from 24 dark blue panels, all set at a uniform 15-degree tilt and facing south to mirror how a real plant is laid out. Each panel sits on a black support frame underneath, and the cable runs between rows are recreated with thin gray strips standing in for cable trays.

The inverter room uses white walls with a red roof. Inside you get a transformer and distribution cabinet details, and the roof lifts off so you can actually reach in and pose things. Overall the model measures roughly 45cm long and 22cm wide — a compact footprint for a desk or shelf.

## Structural Stability

The panel supports attach to the base with a double fixation of cross axles plus pins, so nothing falls off when you move the model around. The inverter room walls are stacked in double-layer brick, which gives plenty of strength.

The weak point: the panels connect to each other at a single point only, so a light knock can shift them out of alignment. Press each one firmly home while building and it holds fine. Overall stability is good, and everyday desktop display is no problem at all.

## Value for Money

The instructions sell for 29 yuan on brickplan. The model uses about 1,580 parts, which comes to roughly 180 yuan if you source them yourself, and the build takes around 5 hours. Compared with the hydroelectric station MOC, this one has fewer parts and a lower difficulty curve, making it a solid entry point for energy-themed MOCs. The included parts list can be sorted and counted by color, which makes filling any gaps very convenient.

## FAQ

**How do I download the Solar Power Station MOC instructions?**
Register on the brickplan site, buy them, and download. Both PDF and online step-by-step formats are supported, so you can follow along on a phone or tablet — handy for building step by step.

**Can I add lights to simulate power generation?**
Yes. The inverter room has a pre-drilled routing hole for a light kit, so once you thread an LED string through, the roof glows warm and mimics the plant running at night. The modification is easy — about ten minutes of work.

**How is this different from a real solar power station?**
This MOC is a static display model. It focuses on recreating the PV array layout and the inverter room structure, and it does not generate real power. As a teaching prop for explaining how the technology works, though, it is very well suited.

**Is it worth building for a beginner?**
Yes. At 1,580 parts the scale is manageable, and the instructions break the build into 40 steps — roughly an hour a day gets you finished in about a week. It is a cost-effective way for a newcomer to try an energy-themed MOC.

**How much does the whole thing cost?**
The instructions are 29 yuan and sourcing the parts yourself runs about 150 to 200 yuan, keeping the total under 250 yuan. That is noticeably cheaper than comparable photovoltaic-themed sets — very good value.
