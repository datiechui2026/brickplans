# Chinese Brick MOC Guide: Brands, Parts and Instruction Sources

Over the past few years the domestic brick industry has grown fast, and "Chinese brick MOC recommendations" has become a search term plenty of builders type into the box. Compared with imported brands, domestic bricks stand out on value for money, topic variety and part innovation, which has pushed MOC (My Own Creation) building out of specialist circles and into the mainstream. Here is a practical checklist covering brands, buying and instruction sources in one pass.

## Why Build MOCs with Chinese Bricks?

The biggest advantages are cost and playability. On one hand, domestic parts are affordable, and the range covers big bricks, technic elements and minifigures, so it is realistic to stock up in bulk for a build. On the other, more and more domestic brands sell individual parts separately, which gives MOC builders an excellent material library. The core value of buying Chinese bricks for MOC work is exactly this: turning ambitious ideas into plastic at a much lower cost.

## Chinese Brick Brands Worth Watching

Brand choice matters more than almost anything else. These are the names with the strongest reputations for quality right now:

- **Gobricks**: known above all for part quality — saturated colours and reliable clutch power. Many hardcore MOC builders treat it as their default parts supplier, and it suits large models that need precise structure.
- **Double Eagle**: focused on technic sets. Gears, motors and transmission parts are solidly made, making it a good fit for mechanical MOCs, drivetrain modifications and powered models.
- **StarBricks**: strong on architecture and street-scene subjects with plenty of specialty parts, ideal for builders who like architectural MOCs and Chinese courtyard themes.
- **SEMBO**: broad subject coverage and a complete series line-up, plus good-looking minifigures. A sensible starting point for beginners attempting a first MOC.
- **Panlos**: stands out in military and sci-fi territory with many special parts, aimed at more advanced builders working on mechs and vehicles.

## What to Check When Buying Parts for a MOC

Getting the purchase right is what decides whether a build actually finishes. Keep these points handy:

1. **Check part compatibility**: favour brands that stay compatible with the international mainstream brick standard, so you can mix brands and widen your material pool.
2. **Buy in batches against a list**: work out the parts list from the instructions first, then buy by colour and type in stages on e-commerce platforms. Blind stockpiling wastes money.
3. **Watch clutch power and colour batches**: parts from different production batches can show colour differences, so for a complex model it is better to buy everything in one go. Prioritise parts with moderate clutch — too loose and the model falls apart, too tight and assembly becomes a struggle.
4. **Use the single-brick sections**: many brand flagship stores run loose-part sections, which is the efficient route to special and rare elements.

## Where to Find MOC Instructions

Instructions are step one of any MOC, and the part beginners ask about most. The usual channels are:

- **Official instruction libraries**: some brand websites publish electronic manuals for their own sets, which work well as reference material for modifications and for learning techniques.
- **Communities and forums**: brick forums, tieba groups and QQ groups at home and abroad regularly host builders sharing homemade MOC instructions and build logs.
- **Instruction sharing platforms**: dedicated platforms such as BrickPlans collect a large amount of quality instruction material, with search by theme and by part type — a fast way to find both inspiration and a workable plan.

## Advice for First-Time MOC Builders

If this is your first attempt at a Chinese brick MOC, start with a small or mid-sized model. Get familiar with part types and the logic of how a build goes together, then work up to more complex structures. Study good builds, take models apart and rebuild them, and ideas keep coming. Treat this guide as your starting point, pick up the bricks and enjoy creating something from nothing.
