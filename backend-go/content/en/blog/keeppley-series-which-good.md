# Keeppley Series Guide: Which Line Is Worth Buying?

The short answer first: for adults, the Space series is the one to buy — it delivers the most detailed build and the highest collectible value. For children, go with the Pokémon series: 300-800 pieces per set, prices starting at 129 yuan, and the best balance of value and age-appropriateness in the range.

## 1. Series Positioning

Keeppley is Qiji's mid-to-high-end brick brand, and its mainstream catalogue is divided into six lines: Space, Pokémon, Forbidden City culture, Retro Street Scene, Military and Flower Language. The positioning of each is easy to read. Space focuses on faithful reproduction of real hardware; Pokémon runs on IP affection; Forbidden City takes the Chinese-national-style route. That separation is deliberate — each line is aimed at a different kind of builder, so the right question is not "which is best" but "which is best for you."

## 2. Part Quality

Across the whole range, Keeppley uses high-precision ABS moulds, and clutch power sits in the top tier of domestic brands at the same price point. In testing, the Space series holds part tolerances within 0.1 mm, so the build feels tight and does not fall apart afterwards — an important point for models with long, thin structural sections. The Flower Language series uses transparent elements with high light transmission, and the finished display piece has a texture close to imported brands. Quality control is consistent across lines, so you are not gambling on which series you pick.

## 3. Value for Money

On value, the Pokémon series averages about 0.12 yuan per piece, which is genuinely reasonable for officially licensed IP bricks — licences usually push the per-piece cost up, and here they do not. The Forbidden City series is finely made but sits at a higher price point, with most sets above 400 yuan; that is a display-piece purchase rather than an impulse one. Flower Language and Retro Street Scene are entry-level lines: you can get in for under 99 yuan, which makes them the natural choice if you want to try the brand before committing to a large build.

## 4. Age Suitability

The official recommendation is 6 and up, but the real differences between lines are significant. Flower Language and Street Scene have simple structures, so children aged 6-8 can complete them independently. The Space series uses small parts and runs to many steps, with a build time of roughly 5-6 hours — better suited to builders aged 10 and over, or to a parent-and-child session. Pokémon is fine from age 8, with a moderate difficulty curve that keeps a younger builder engaged without frustrating them.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q: Which Keeppley series is the best gift for a child?**

A: The Pokémon series. Piece counts run 300-800, prices start at 129 yuan, the IP is popular, and the difficulty suits children aged 8 and up building on their own.

**Q: Can Keeppley be mixed with LEGO?**

A: Yes. Keeppley uses standard stud connections compatible with mainstream brick systems, so its parts can be mixed with LEGO and Qiji elements of the same specification — the dimensions are fully interchangeable.

**Q: Which Keeppley series holds its value best for collectors?**

A: The Space series. It offers a high degree of accurate reproduction and plenty of limited editions, and it is well regarded among collectors; some out-of-print sets have doubled to twice their original price on the secondhand market.

**Q: What if I lose the instruction manual?**

A: Search on brickplan for the same model to find digital instructions, or apply for a replacement through the brand's official WeChat account. For popular lines such as Space, the corresponding step-by-step diagrams are usually easy to find.
