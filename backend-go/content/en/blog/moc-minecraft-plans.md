# Minecraft MOC Plans Reviewed: Build the Blocky World

Minecraft and brick building are a natural match — both are creative worlds built from cubes. Minecraft MOC plans let builders recreate the game's structures and creatures in real bricks, and the results look startlingly close to the screen.

## 1. Recommended Minecraft MOC Plans

### 1. Grass Block Scene
- **Plan name:** Minecraft Grass Block, 1:1 scale
- **Part count:** about 200 pieces
- **Difficulty:** Low
- **Rating:** ★★★★★

The classic grass block is Minecraft's signature element. Green and brown bricks, combined with SNOT (studs-not-on-top) techniques, reproduce the pixel look of the block cleanly — the top studs read as grass, the sides as dirt.

### 2. Steve Model
- **Plan name:** Steve, 1:4 scale posable model
- **Part count:** about 500 pieces
- **Difficulty:** Medium
- **Rating:** ★★★★☆

This one rebuilds Steve's blocky silhouette by stacking bricks in the same cube logic the game uses. It is one of the most enjoyable projects in the Minecraft MOC plan catalog, and the 1:4 scale leaves room for real articulation.

### 3. End Castle
- **Plan name:** End Castle, 1:100 scale
- **Part count:** about 3,000 pieces
- **Difficulty:** High
- **Rating:** ★★★★★

A large diorama build that includes the end stone castle, obsidian pillars and the Ender Dragon. This is the plan to pick when you want a display centrepiece rather than a quick weekend build.

## 2. Key Building Points

### 1. Pixel Style

The core of any Minecraft MOC is holding the pixel style. Use small bricks to reproduce pixel-level detail — a 1x1 tile or a small slope does more for the look than a large, smooth panel ever will.

### 2. Colour Accuracy

Match the game's palette strictly and use bricks in the corresponding colours. Grass is not one green but several; dirt is not one brown. Getting the shades right is what separates a convincing build from a generic one.

### 3. Interactive Features

You can add mechanism designs — openable doors, pushable pistons and similar functions — so the model does something when someone picks it up, not just when they look at it.

## 3. Choosing a Difficulty Level

- **Beginner:** grass block, creeper model (200–400 pieces)
- **Intermediate:** Steve model, animal models (500–1,000 pieces)
- **Advanced:** village, castle (1,000+ pieces)

A sensible progression is to start with the grass block, move to a creeper or Steve, and only then commit to a village or castle. The large plans are not technically harder per step, but they demand far more patience and a much deeper parts supply.
