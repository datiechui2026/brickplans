# Brick Repair Shop MOC Review: Twin Lifts and a Project Car

After enough street-scene builds, you start to notice that the functional buildings are what make a block feel alive. A repair shop — with lifts, a tool wall and a car waiting on service — turns a display row into a place where something is happening. For this **brick repair shop MOC review** we built a corner garage with two service bays from the instructions, and here is the bay-by-bay breakdown.

## 1. Instructions and Parts: Unboxing Joy for the Technically Minded

The model is designed at **1:48 street-scene scale** and runs roughly 2,400 pieces, on the large side for this genre, split across 8 numbered bags. The manual is properly detailed: every step involving mechanical parts marks the connection direction, so there is very little chance of installing something backwards. The palette leans on **light grey, dark grey, brick red and iron grey**, which gives the box that cold, industrial workshop feel the moment you open it. A nice touch: the small tools — bolts, wrenches and the like — are all printed elements rather than stickers, and they will outlast any decal. Missing pieces were minimal. Instructions and parts score **9/10**.

## 2. The Structural Core: Twin Lifts That Actually Move

The biggest risk for a garage build is ending up as a hollow shell. This **brick repair shop MOC** solves that cleanly:

- **Twin lifts**: each of the two bays gets its own column lift, with platforms built from slide-rail pieces that **genuinely slide up and down**, letting you raise a car into mid-air for maximum visual impact.
- **Load-bearing design**: each lift is reinforced with two lined columns per side, so the rails run smooth without binding and stay firm through repeated play.
- **High-window lighting**: three tall windows are cut into the side wall of the workshop, glazed with transparent panels in dark frames for an industrial look, and the top level stays solid with no bulging.

The structure is sound and the mechanical workshop personality comes through immediately. Structure scores **9/10**.

## 3. Playability: Tool Wall, Project Car and Robotic Arm

This is where the **brick repair shop MOC** really shines. The tool wall uses 1x1 round-hole bricks as a backing board, hung with printed wrenches, pliers and similar small pieces that lift off and clip back on freely. Beside it, a robotic arm is built on ball joints, and all three arm sections rotate — enough to hoist an "engine" over to a bay. Parked at the door is a project off-roader MOC: the hood lifts to reveal small engine parts inside, and the front wheels steer. The whole arrive–lift–inspect flow is complete and coherent. Playability scores **9.5/10**.

## 4. Accuracy and Atmosphere

On accuracy, nothing is missing: oil-stained flooring (dark plates and tiles laid in as "oil marks"), high windows, lifts, tool wall and the car awaiting repair — at a glance it reads as a standard auto shop. Add cool-white and warm-yellow lighting and the tall windows glow while the tool wall picks up real depth in photos. The one deduction: the oil stains are stickers rather than printed elements, and they will curl at the edges with heavy handling. Atmosphere scores **8.5/10**.

**Verdict: strongly recommended — a must for mechanical fans.** This **brick repair shop MOC** sits at medium-to-high difficulty (about 7 hours) and suits builders with some experience. It is a particularly good fit for anyone who likes functional buildings and enjoys working the lifts and robotic arm; for scene builders who want a "service in progress" storyline on their street; and for builders who already own vehicle MOCs and want somewhere to park them. The weak points are the sticker oil stains and the footprint, which needs a large baseplate. Even so, it is the most finished mechanical street-scene build we have seen recently.
