# Spaceship MOC Blueprints: Build Your Own Star Cruiser

Straight to the verdict: spaceship MOCs give you more creative room than almost any other theme, but hull silhouette and centre of gravity are what make or break the build. Beginners are better off starting with a small ship of a hundred-odd pieces.

## The creative space that space themes open up

Spaceships are one of the most liberating subjects in brick MOC building: with no real-world scale to obey, the hull outline, cockpit and engine array can go wherever you want. **Spaceship MOC blueprints** therefore come with an unusually wide creative range, from realistic space shuttles to sci-fi star cruisers. MOC stands for My Own Creation, meaning a design built by a fan rather than a set designer; in space themes, builders lean heavily on distinctive slope bricks and transparent pieces to create a sense of the future that official sets rarely attempt. Sci-fi fans and brick builders find common ground here. If you are new to this, start with the basics of MOC building over on the [brickplan blog](https://brickplan.cn/en/blog).

## Hull design: balancing silhouette and colour

The hull is the soul of a spaceship MOC. A good blueprint uses large slope bricks and curved pieces to shape a streamlined fuselage, then layers dark grey, light grey and white to read as "high tech", with transparent orange or blue pieces for portholes and power indicator lights. Swept-wing designs need particular care over the centre of gravity: wings that are too heavy make the finished model tip forward, so strong builders bury ballast or reinforcing beams inside the fuselage. On brickplan (https://brickplan.cn) you can find spaceship blueprints ranging from mini scouts to large motherships, with piece counts from a hundred or so up to several thousand.

## Details and thrusters: bringing the ship to life

Details decide how complete a set of **spaceship MOC blueprints** really feels. Thrusters are usually built from transparent flame pieces or a combination of cone pieces to mimic engine exhaust, while the cockpit uses large transparent elements for the canopy, with room inside for a minifigure pilot. More advanced blueprints add retractable landing gear, opening hatches and even a detachable escape pod, which lifts the play value considerably. These mechanisms demand accurate parts, so follow the blueprint's parts list closely to avoid size mismatches that cause jamming.

## Choosing a blueprint and building tips

When picking spaceship MOC blueprints, check three things. First, whether the preview images are complete from multiple angles, so you can see the back and underside structure clearly. Second, whether a parts list with part-number cross-references is included, which makes sourcing pieces much easier. Third, whether the difficulty level is clearly labelled, since beginners do better starting with a small ship. When building, work in stages: fuselage, then wings, then details, and check the strength of every connection at the end. ## FAQ

**Q: Which kind of spaceship MOC blueprint should a beginner start with?**
A: A small ship such as a mini scout is the safer start, with a few hundred pieces and a clearly labelled difficulty level making it easier to pick up; move on to a large mothership once you are comfortable, where piece counts run into the thousands and both fuselage and wing structures are more complex.

**Q: How do I handle the centre of gravity so the ship does not tip forward or back?**
A: Swept-wing ships need particular attention to the centre of gravity: wings that are too heavy tip the model forward, and good blueprints bury ballast or reinforcing beams inside the fuselage. Build in stages, fuselage then wings then details, and check all connections at the end.

A: Use transparent flame pieces or a combination of cone pieces to mimic engine exhaust, and transparent orange or blue pieces for portholes and indicator lights. Some advanced blueprints also add retractable landing gear, opening hatches and even a detachable escape pod.
