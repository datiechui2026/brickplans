# Customizing Minifigure Faces: Hand-Drawing, Decals and UV Print

Here is the short version: there are three routes to a custom minifigure face. Hand drawing has the lowest barrier to entry, decals carry zero risk, and UV printing gives the most faithful result. Beginners should start with hand drawing.

Most MOC builders hit the same wall. The character in your plans has real attitude, but when you dig through your minifig heads, every expression is either the standard smile or one of the same few official faces. The mood simply does not match. That is when **minifigure face customization** becomes the step that makes a character come alive. Below are three approaches I settled on after making my share of mistakes: hand drawing, decals and printing.

## 1. Hand drawing: the lowest-barrier entry point

Hand drawing is the easiest way into **minifigure face customization**. You need a 0.05 mm or 0.1 mm extra-fine oil-based marker (Sakura or STA fine-liners are the usual picks), a pencil, a toothpick and a bottle of alcohol.

The process is simple. Lightly sketch the expression on a blank head in pencil first, then ink over the lines once you are happy with the draft. When you draw the eyes, keep the pen tip vertical and your wrist off the surface so the line comes out in one continuous stroke. That looks far cleaner than going back over a line again and again. When you are done, leave the head alone for half an hour and let the ink cure completely before you handle it.

## 2. Stickers and waterslide decals: the zero-risk option

If you are worried about a shaky hand ruining the head, decals are the safer route. Waterslide face decals made specifically for minifigures are widely available: cut out the design you want, soak it in water for a few seconds, slide it gently onto the face, chase out the air bubbles with a cotton swab, and let it dry. The drawback is thickness. A decal sits slightly proud of the surface and shows an edge up close, so it works best for display at a distance or for photos. If you print your own stickers, choose a waterproof material, otherwise the print smears the moment you touch it.

## 3. UV printing and swapping printed heads: the one-step advanced route

If you want a perfect reproduction, find a shop that offers UV printing and have your finished design printed directly onto a blank minifig head. The color holds firmly and the processing marks are almost invisible. There is also a shortcut: many Chinese brick brands sell loose heads with printed expressions, and swapping one in is far less work than drawing your own. This is exactly how many MOC builders change faces in bulk.

## 4. When you mess up: cleaning and rescue

While oil-based ink is still wet, a cotton swab dipped in alcohol will wipe it away. Once it has cured, cover the area with an ink-remover pen and then wipe, or work slowly with an eraser and a small amount of alcohol. Do not scrape at it with a fingernail. A scratch on the ABS surface traps the color when you paint over it, and the damage shows. Blank heads are cheap, so if a face really cannot be saved, swap in a new one instead of mourning it.

## 5. Protecting the finished face: sealing and storage

A finished expression is most vulnerable to rubbing. Spray on a coat of matte clear sealer from about 20 cm away, in two thin passes. That locks the ink down and kills the shine, which also makes the head photograph better. For storage, give each customized minifigure its own zip bag so it cannot scrape against other parts, and the ink layer will not wear out prematurely.

## 6. Let the expression serve the character

The end goal of **minifigure face customization** is not a pretty drawing — it is giving a character personality. Angry eyebrows, a resigned dead-fish stare, a smug little smirk: a few strokes can double the storytelling in an MOC scene. Study how animation and comics exaggerate expressions, practice each one on paper until it is second nature, and only then put the pen to a head. Your success rate goes up considerably.

I hope this guide helps you build a character nobody else has. If you have a better method, share your work with the BrickPlan community.

## FAQ

**Q: For a first attempt at customizing a minifigure face, which method is safest?**
A: Hand drawing has the lowest barrier. Use a 0.05 mm or 0.1 mm extra-fine oil-based pen, sketch in pencil first and then ink the lines, and let the head sit for half an hour until the ink is fully dry before handling it.

**Q: If I draw the expression wrong, can I erase it and start again?**
A: While the ink is still wet, a cotton swab with alcohol wipes it off. Once it has cured, cover it with an ink-remover pen and wipe, or use an eraser with a little alcohol. Never scrape with a fingernail — the scratch on the ABS surface will trap color later.

**Q: What is the difference between hand drawing, decals and UV printing?**
A: Hand drawing is the most flexible but depends on a steady hand. Decals are zero risk but add thickness and a visible edge, so they suit viewing from a distance. UV printing holds color firmly with almost no visible processing marks, and gets it right in one step.

**Q: How do I protect a customized face so it does not wear off?**
A: Spray a matte clear sealer from about 20 cm in two thin coats to lock it in, and store each customized minifigure in its own zip bag so it does not rub against other parts.
