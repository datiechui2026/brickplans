# Pirate Ship MOC: Curved Hull, Masts, Sails and Skull Flag

The short version first: build the hull curve in segments with SNOT, lock the masts down on technic axles, keep the palette to red, black, brown and white, and always add weight along the keel so the ship does not capsize.

A pirate ship is one of the most photogenic subjects in brick MOC building. The curved hull, the tall masts, the brash skull flag — set it on a shelf and it already tells a story. This guide walks through the whole build in order: hull, masts and sails, figurehead and flag, then colors and the traps that catch first-time ship builders.

The hull curve is the first hard problem. The ship runs in a smooth line from bow to stern, and the cross-section goes from pointed to round and back to pointed. The most practical approach is segmenting plus SNOT. Split the hull lengthwise into 5 to 7 segments, and give each one a base of beams at a different width: narrowest at the bow, widest amidships, slightly drawn in at the stern. Join the segments with hinges so the run of the hull forms a natural arc. Then skin the outside of the bulwarks with sideways SNOT plates to give the hull its thickness and a waistline. If you want an even smoother curve, layer wedge plates over the transitions — the line softens considerably.

Masts and sails decide how commanding the ship looks. Technic axles or long rod pieces such as lightsaber blades make the stiffest masts; seat the base on the deck with a cross-axle holder. The bow, main and mizzen masts step down in height at roughly 1 : 1.2 : 0.9. Sails take one of two routes. For texture, cut cloth and clip it to the yardarm with clip pieces. For rigidity, build the sail from plates: line up 2x4 plates in a row and use hinges to give them a slight belly, as if the wind is filling them — far more lively than a flat panel. Attach the yards to the masts with hinges so you can also adjust the sail angle.

The figurehead and skull flag are the soul of the decoration. A minifigure works well as a figurehead: a cutlass-armed figure standing on one leg on the bowsprit reads instantly. For the skull flag, use a printed piece or sticker where you have one. If not, build an abstract skull from 1x1 white bricks — two black round plates for eyes and a white plate for teeth — and hang it at the top of the main mast. The pirate mood arrives immediately.

The stern castle and broadside guns make the ship feel alive. Add a two-level stern castle, framing it in dark brown plates and putting the captain's cabin with a window on the upper level. Hang a lantern or a small flag from the top of the castle — it is the most photogenic spot on the whole ship. Mount 3 to 4 small cannons along each side, built from 1x1 cylinders with black barrel pieces poking out of the gunports, muzzles facing outward; the sense of a fighting ship is right there. Hang the anchor below the bowsprit from a gray hook piece and chain links so it falls naturally with the curve of the hull. Detail-minded builders can stare at that for a long while.

On color, mix dark brown and black for the hull, use tan for the deck, white or off-white for the sails, and add a little red as an accent on flags and stripes. Red, black, brown and white is the classic pirate combination — steady but eye-catching.

Common traps. First is center of gravity: heavy masts and sails will roll the ship over, so weight the keel with a few heavy plates or add ballast at the stern. Second is left-right asymmetry — mirror the segments as you build them. Third, sails that are too large block the view; keep the highest point of the rig no more than three times the ship's length above the hull. If you want to study proven structures, search BrickPlan for pirate ship MOC instructions and pay attention to how other builders handle the hull curve and the mast mounts.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I build the curved hull of a pirate ship?**
A: Use segmenting plus SNOT. Split the hull lengthwise into 5 to 7 segments, give each one a base of beams at a different width (narrowest at the bow, widest amidships, slightly drawn in at the stern), join the segments with hinges to form the arc, then skin the outside of the bulwarks with sideways SNOT plates.

**Q: What is the strongest way to build masts and sails?**
A: Technic axles or long rod pieces make the stiffest masts; seat the base on the deck with a cross-axle holder and step the bow, main and mizzen masts down at 1 : 1.2 : 0.9. For sails, either cut cloth and fix it with clip pieces, or line up 2x4 plates and use hinges to create the wind-filled belly.

**Q: How should I handle the skull flag and figurehead?**
A: A cutlass-armed minifigure standing on one leg on the bowsprit makes a striking figurehead. For the skull flag, prefer a printed piece or sticker; if you do not have one, build an abstract skull from 1x1 white bricks (two black round plates for eyes, a white plate for teeth) and hang it at the top of the main mast.

**Q: What is the most common mistake on a pirate ship MOC?**
A: Center of gravity first — heavy masts and sails will capsize the ship, so weight the keel with heavy plates or add ballast at the stern. Next is left-right asymmetry, so mirror the hull segments. Finally, do not oversize the sails: keep the highest point of the rig within three times the ship's length.
