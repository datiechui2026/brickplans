# LEGO Ninjago Buying Guide: Best Sets by Budget

Short answer first: Ninjago sells on two hooks, minifigures and mechs. Entry sets start around ¥80, mid-to-large mechs sit in the ¥300–¥800 sweet spot, and chasing every new release on day one is rarely worth it.

Ninjago is a stop most builders hit sooner or later. Launched in 2011, the theme pairs a ninja team with mechs and giant beasts, and it holds attention from kids to adult collectors with sharp minifigures, striking mech designs and a long-running story. If you are thinking about getting in, or adding to an existing collection, this breakdown will help you find your direction fast.

## Why Ninjago Is Worth Collecting

The appeal is the three-in-one formula: minifigures, vehicles and locations. The core cast is six ninja with different elements — fire, water, earth, lightning, ice and energy. Each minifigure comes with its own mask and weapon, which makes them highly collectible. The main play pattern is rebuilding the mechs, dragons and giant monsters from the show, and build quality is generally high, with better articulation than you get in a standard City set. On price, small sets mostly land between ¥80 and ¥300, while mid-to-large mechs and locations run about ¥500 to ¥1500 — a theme that balances value and collecting pleasure unusually well.

## Classic Sets at a Glance

For beginners, start with a small ninja minifigure set: usually one ninja plus a small vehicle or weapon rack, ¥80–¥200, a good way to learn the characters and test how the build feels. The next tier is the character mechs — the fire ninja's red dragon mech, the lightning ninja's azure dragon mount and similar — clustered at ¥300–¥800 with strong mechanical detail and plenty of features. These are the backbone of the theme. At the top are the flagship location builds such as the Ninjago City sets, over a thousand pieces and typically ¥1000–¥2000, with a full street scene and a large minifigure roster. They look spectacular on display and suit experienced collectors with room to show them.

## Buying Advice by Budget and Need

New builders on a tight budget should prioritise minifigure sets or single-ninja mechs: low cost, quick sense of achievement. If mechanical vehicles are your thing, go straight for the mid-to-large mechs and dragon sets — they offer the most play value. Collectors who care about scene accuracy should focus on the Ninjago City building sets. On channels, prices differ noticeably between physical flagship stores, official online stores and secondhand platforms; the same set can swing by 20–30% depending on timing, so compare before you order rather than buying every new release at launch.

## Extending Ninjago Into MOC Building

Ninjago pairs naturally with MOC work: the theme's mechs, dragons and locations make excellent modification bases, and plenty of builders break multiple Ninjago sets down and rebuild them into their own mech squads. On the brickplan site you can search for player write-ups on Ninjago set picks, and go straight to MOC instructions for ninja mechs, East Asian style architecture and dragon themes, so the minifigures' adventures keep growing.

## Final Thoughts

Whether you were drawn in by the minifigures or by the heavy mechs, Ninjago delivers building fun that lasts. It starts with one ninja, and the collection grows with every build — the next classic set is probably closer than you think.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Ninjago worth getting into?**
A: Yes. Its strength is the minifigure, vehicle and location combination: all six element ninja have their own masks and weapons, and the mechs and dragons beat standard City sets on finish and articulation, with prices spanning roughly ¥80 to ¥1500.

**Q: How should I choose sets by budget?**
A: On a tight budget, start with minifigure sets or single-ninja mechs (¥80–¥200); if you like vehicles, pick mid-to-large mechs and dragons (¥300–¥800); for scene accuracy, look at Ninjago City sets (¥1000–¥2000).

**Q: Are Ninjago City sets good for shelf display?**
A: Yes. They run over a thousand pieces with a full street scene and a large minifigure roster, so they display with real impact and suit experienced collectors with space. They do take up a lot of room and usually cost ¥1000–¥2000, so plan the shelf first.

**Q: What should I watch out for when buying Ninjago?**
A: Channel pricing varies widely — physical flagship stores, official online stores and secondhand platforms differ a lot, and the same set can move 20–30% depending on timing. Compare before ordering, and don't feel obliged to chase every new release at launch.
