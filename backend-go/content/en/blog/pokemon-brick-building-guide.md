# Pokemon Brick Building Guide: Start With Pikachu

Pokemon have round, soft shapes that seem made for brick building. Yet plenty of builders finish their first attempt and feel the model is somehow off — stiff silhouette, wrong colors, or a blank, lifeless face. This guide starts with popular characters like Pikachu and walks through the techniques, the part choices and the build order that get you closer to the character you actually want on your shelf.

## Why Pikachu Is the Best Starter Character

Pikachu is an ideal entry point into Pokemon brick building. Its body breaks down into three simple sections — head, torso and tail — so the structure stays manageable while the silhouette is instantly recognizable. The rounded outline, the signature yellow-and-black color scheme and the lightning-bolt tail all give you good material to practice shape work on.

Finish one Pikachu and you will have worked through three core skills: curved surfaces, color transitions and characteristic symbols. After that, other characters become far less intimidating.

## Rounded Shapes and Color Matching

The whole craft comes down to two words: round and accurate.

For rounded shapes, do not stack large flat plates and hope for the best. Instead, use small 1x2 and 1x1 parts and slope bricks to close the edges gradually, stepping the right angles into a curve the way you would build an arch. For the head, a round baseplate combined with layers that shrink as they rise gives a full, spherical look.

For color, work out the ratio of main color, secondary color and accent color before you start — on paper or against a color chart. On Pikachu, bright yellow carries the body, while brown and black handle the ears and tail. Mixing parts of different sizes from inconsistent sources creates gaps and visible color shifts, so favor parts from the same brand and the same color code.

## Nailing the Face

The face is the character's soul, and it is where Pokemon brick building shows real skill. Eyes can be printed pieces, or you can build the pupil and highlight from a small round plate and a thin bar — leave a sliver of white to suggest the catchlight and the model immediately looks alive. For the mouth, one or two inverted slope bricks or curved plates create the curve of a smile, and a pair of red round tiles on the cheeks finishes Pikachu's trademark expression.

One rule worth remembering: keep the face concentrated on a single layer. Shifting a feature by one or two studs is often enough to change the entire mood.

## Choosing Parts and Building in Stages

Do two things before you place a single brick. First, group identical parts together according to the parts list. Second, split the instructions into three phases: frame, fill, detail.

- **Frame:** build the internal structural support of the body so the whole model stays rigid.
- **Fill:** use small parts to fill out and shape the outer form step by step.
- **Detail:** add the tail, ears, eyes and decorations last.

When you hit a floating section — a raised arm, for example — bridge it with a support element rather than letting it hang and collapse halfway through. While building in stages, check color and orientation against the instructions layer by layer, and photograph the model after each completed layer so you can trace back quickly if something goes wrong.

## MOC Instructions for Other Popular Characters

Once you are comfortable, you can move on to harder characters such as Charizard, Eevee and Snorlax. Plans for these Pokemon brick builds are not hard to find in the community, but quality varies widely. On BrickPlans (brickplan.cn) you can find plenty of Pokemon-themed MOC instructions shared by builders, most of them with high-resolution step photos and a complete parts list — download and build straight from the file. You can also study someone else's approach to the shape first, then adjust the colors and proportions yourself to produce a version that is uniquely yours.

Start with a single Pikachu, work through the three core skills of rounded shaping, color and expression one at a time, and you can bring the Pokemon you love into the real world one by one. May your brick pile eventually house a partner of your own.
