# SNOT Building Technique Guide: Studs Not On Top for MOC

SNOT stands for "Studs Not On Top." Regular building keeps studs facing up and stacks brick on brick. SNOT breaks that habit: bricks sit on their side, with studs pointing outward or even down. Once you can do it, walls get thinner, corners get cleaner, surfaces get flatter — a lot of the "high-end" feel in a MOC comes from exactly these moves. This guide takes SNOT from the parts bin through to finished builds.

## 1. Learn the Parts: Your SNOT Arsenal

SNOT depends on a handful of specialty pieces. Know them before you start, or you will stall halfway through a build:

- **Side-stud bricks:** the 1x2 brick with studs on the side (part 87087), the 1x1 with a single side stud (11211), and the 1x1 with studs on all four sides (4733). These are the core pieces of any sideways build.
- **Headlight bricks:** the 1x1 headlight brick with one stud (4070). Cheap and plentiful, it is the best entry point.
- **The improved 1x2 with two side studs (30236):** stronger under load, so use it where a joint has to carry weight.
- **Turntables:** the 2x2 turntable (3679 + 3680) for rotating and steering structures.
- **Hinges:** the 1x2 hinge brick (3937/3938) or a 2x3 hinge plate, for parts that open and close or for angled surfaces.

## 2. The Most Basic Sideways Move: Bricks Laid Flat

Lay a 1x2 side-stud brick on its side with the studs facing the direction you want to extend, then clip a plate or brick onto it. That is the opening move of SNOT. Many builders stumble on their first attempt: sideways joints take force in a completely different direction than normal stacking, so whatever you clip on comes loose easily. The fix is more interlocking — stagger 1x2 side-stud bricks against 1x2 plates so the contact area is larger instead of relying on a single point. When you press pieces together, seat the side studs as deep into the receiving brick's underside as they will go; do not settle for half a stud.

## 3. Flattening the Surface with Tiles and Studs-Down Thinking

The annoying part of sideways building is an uneven surface: a row of exposed studs and holes reads as an unfinished build. To make a side-built wall look like one solid slab, tile the outer face (1x2 tile 3069, 2x4 tile 3034) so every stud is hidden. For floors, flip the idea upside down: build a base frame first, then turn the tiles over so they form the underside, and the floor surface comes out perfectly flat. Roof slopes work the same way — side-build the angled skeleton first, then tile over it. It is far more stable than forcing the angle with stacked plates.

## 4. Turntables and Hinges: Making Structures Move

Turntable parts (3679 + 3680) suit rotating platforms, radar dishes and spinning turrets. Hinges suit roof slopes, hatches and folding structures. The common mistake is using too few hinges, so the angle cannot hold itself and collapses at the first touch. Use at least two hinges at a corner, or add a side-stud brick as a diagonal brace, and the structure stays put. With rotating assemblies, put a plate above and below the turntable to leave clearance for the turn — otherwise it jams.

## 5. In Practice: One SNOT Wall

Here is a build order for a SNOT wall that rarely goes wrong. Layer one: a normal horizontal row of 1x2 side-stud bricks (87087) with the studs facing inward. Layer two: 1x1 single side-stud bricks (11211) in a staggered pattern so the seams interlock. Next, tile the outer face with 1x2 tiles to smooth it. At the corners, use 4070 headlight bricks to join two wall faces into a clean right angle. Follow that sequence and the wall comes out thin and flat, with room left for a window opening — far more flexible than a solid wall of bricks.

## 6. From Instructions to Builds

SNOT looks complicated, but the core is only three things: know your parts, adjust the orientation, tile the surface. Do not be afraid to take a build apart and redo it. Start with a small wall, then try a roof slope; after two or three teardowns the feel comes to you. If you want MOC instructions to practise on, take a look at [BrickPlans](https://brickplan.cn) — plenty of the buildings and scenes there use SNOT techniques. Search for "architecture" or "street scene" and you will find them, with the sideways steps clearly marked in the instructions. Build one through once and you will have the technique.
