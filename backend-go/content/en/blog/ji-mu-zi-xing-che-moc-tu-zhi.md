# Brick Bicycle MOC Plans: Finding Them and Building Them Right

After more than ten years of building with bricks, bicycles remain my favourite MOC subject. The frame lines are clean, the mechanical detail is satisfying, and a finished bike looks great sitting on a desk. But brick bicycle plans come with their own traps: wrong proportions, steering that locks solid, chains that skip. I have hit all of them. Here is what I have worked out over the years so you can skip the dead ends and get a result you are happy with on the first try.

## Where to Find Brick Bicycle MOC Plans: Sources and Filtering

Start with the sources. Brick bicycle plans currently live in three main places: overseas plan sites, domestic communities, and plan marketplaces. Communities like BrickPlans have the advantage of written notes, clear difficulty grading, and player comments in the review section, so you can tell in advance whether a set of plans builds smoothly. When filtering, I follow a fixed order. Completeness comes first: does the plan include a parts list? Plenty of plans do not, and counting out parts by hand will drain your enthusiasm fast. Then check the part count. A bicycle MOC usually lands in the low hundreds of parts; anything above two thousand is a cautious choice for a beginner. Finally, check whether the author has published a revised version. Many authors release corrections for missing parts or parts that collide, so note the version number when you download.

## Frame Structure: Proportions and Reinforcement Are Everything

The frame is where proportion matters most. I work backwards from wheel diameter: fix the wheelbase first, then decide the height and angle of the frame triangle. For the main frame body, use Technic beams with holes rather than regular bricks. They are lighter and far more rigid. At the joints, pin it twice over with cross pins plus axle pins so the whole thing does not come apart when you pose it. The junction between frame and fork is the stress point of the model, so add one extra reinforcement beam there. That single step makes a large difference to how long the model holds together, and you can flex it repeatedly without anything loosening.

## Drivetrain and Steering: Making the Wheels Actually Turn

The drivetrain is where bicycle MOCs fail most often. Gears must share the same module, and the tooth ratio sets how the pedalling feels. If the plan includes a chain, match the chain link count to the tooth count exactly. One link short or one link loose and the chain will drop, so run it through a few free revolutions before you call it done. For steering I use a steering arm plus universal joint, leaving play in the steering column and clearance for the handlebar to rotate. That way neither photography posing nor a minifigure riding position gets stuck, and the photos come out looking natural.

## Minifigure Scale and Common Bike Types

Minifigure scale is the most important thing in a bicycle MOC and the easiest to overlook. A standard minifigure measures roughly seven to eight bricks tall when seated, so work out the seat-to-ground distance from that figure. Skip this and you end up with a minifigure hovering above the saddle. As for which bike to build first, I recommend starting from these: a road bike has clean, simple lines and suits a first attempt best; a mountain bike is more complex with more suspension play; a vintage messenger bike uses common parts, so there is plenty of room to modify it; and a tricycle or BMX gives you a minifigure pose that photographs beautifully in scene shots.

## Beginner Pitfall Checklist and Plan Resources

A few closing rules. First, gather the parts before you start; do not force a build with an incomplete inventory, because missing pieces break your rhythm. Second, bag the parts according to the plan's steps instead of tipping everything out together. Third, transparent and dark parts blur together quickly, so sorting them by colour into boxes ahead of time saves a lot of minutes. I have used this routine for ten years and have almost never had to rebuild.
