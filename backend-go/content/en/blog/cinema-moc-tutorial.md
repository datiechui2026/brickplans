# Brick Cinema MOC Tutorial: Marquee, Lobby and Stepped Seating

A cinema is the classic "inside and out" subject for street-scene builders: a showy period facade on the street side, and a real auditorium with seats inside. Plenty of builders finish the shell, lift the roof, and find nothing but empty baseplate. This tutorial breaks the build down piece by piece — facade, ticket hall, auditorium, projection booth — so you end up with a mini cinema that is complete from the pavement to the back row.

## 1. Overall layout: plan the shell and the interior together

Decide the scale first. The standard approach is a 16x32 stud footprint split front-to-back: the front third is entrance hall plus ticket counter, the back two thirds is the auditorium, with a walkway down each side. Build the roof so the whole level lifts off — either on hinge pieces or simply as a frame with a roof panel that drops into place — so the interior can be shown off at any time.

Roughly 60:40 of your bricks should go inside versus outside. Don't spend everything on the exterior walls; the interior is what makes a cinema MOC impressive.

## 2. Street facade: the sign is the cinema's face

The facade is the first thing passers-by (and viewers) see. The sign matters most: use SNOT bricks to stand the sign up vertically, spell the film title across the face in bright plates, then wrap the edge in light-tube pieces (trans-clear bars or light-guide elements) and the sign reads as lit straight away.

For the entrance, build a glass lobby: trans-clear panels on both sides of the door frame, a lightbox awning running across above, and a second, smaller sign stacked on top of the lobby roof. Go retro with the colour scheme — burgundy, gold and dark brown — and add geometric lines built from 1x1 round plates in stripes; it catches the eye far better than yet another plain white wall.

Leave space for a queue outside. Don't fill the pavement in front of the doors with planters.

## 3. Ticket hall and lounge: make the transition natural

Once inside the lobby, build the ticket area first: a half-round or square counter made from curved slopes, with a "Now Showing" board above it (a black plate with white round plates for lettering). On the wall, hang a few "posters" — small picture frames built from coloured plates.

For the lounge, add a row or two of sofas (brown curved pieces with backrest plates), a popcorn machine (red cylindrical piece with a trans-clear cup for the popcorn) and a floor lamp. These props are where a brick cinema MOC gets its lived-in feel, so don't skip them.

Leave a corridor between the counter and the lounge leading to the auditorium, and keep that corridor darker — dark bricks with ceiling lights — as a lead-in to the brightness of the auditorium.

## 4. Auditorium: stepped seating is the soul

The auditorium is the highlight of the whole build. Make the floor stepped: raise it level by level with plates, dropping from back to front so the front rows sit low and the back rows high. Each step carries a row of seats (dark red or dark blue curved pieces with backrests), with an aisle down the centre.

Put the screen on the front wall: a large white plate framed in dark bricks, with a row of curtains (light-coloured curved pieces) in front of it. Hang round plates from the ceiling as downlights. To make the picture read as lit, run an LED strip behind the screen or along the ceiling and light it from the screen side; against dark walls, the auditorium immediately gains that immersive feel.

## 5. Projection booth and lighting details: don't forget backstage

Every real cinema has a backstage, and that's the projection booth. Leave a small mezzanine high on the rear wall of the auditorium: a small platform holding a projector (technic pieces plus a round dish element, lens pointing at the screen), a console beside it, and two small windows cut into the ceiling.

Lighting sets the cinema mood. Use warm yellow light in the lobby and corridor and cooler light in the auditorium (or light only the screen area) — the contrast between the two colour temperatures does the work for you. Plan the wiring in advance: leave channels in the roof panel and the back wall so cables don't dangle out of the doorway.

## 6. Build order and pitfalls to avoid

Recommended order: baseplate and zoning, then auditorium stepped seating and screen, then ticket hall and lounge, then the projection mezzanine, then facade and marquee, then roof and lighting runs.

Three things go wrong most often:

- Building only the shell and no interior, so lifting the roof reveals an empty box.
- Leaving the sign flat against the wall instead of SNOT-mounted, which kills all sense of depth.
- Skipping the stepped floor, so the minifigures in the back rows are completely blocked by the front rows.

Remember those three rules — interior before exterior, sign standing proud, seating stepped — and your brick cinema MOC will stand out from the rest of the street.
