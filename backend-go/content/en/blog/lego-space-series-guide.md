# LEGO Space Sets in 2026: Which One Should You Buy First?

Short answer up front: the two LEGO Space sets most worth picking up right now are the City rocket launch pad and the Creator space station. The first runs about CNY 500 for 800 pieces and works brilliantly with kids; the second sits near CNY 900 for 1,900 pieces and suits builders who want a longer, more detailed project. When you shop, check the age label and the piece count first, then judge play value — that is how you avoid a set that ends up gathering dust on a shelf.

## Step 1: Choose by age and difficulty

LEGO Space sets are split across age bands such as 4+, 6+, 8+ and 10+. The age label is essentially a difficulty rating, so use it as your first filter.

- **Ages 4–6:** go for the large City rocket. The pieces are bigger, the steps are short, and a child can finish most of it with light help.
- **Ages 7–10:** the Technic Mars rover is the interesting pick here. It uses gears and mechanical linkages, so the build teaches something while staying playable.
- **Ages 10 and up:** the Creator space station is the one to aim for. It has far more detail per step and rewards patience.

If your budget is tight, prioritise mid-range sets in the 500–1,500 piece range. That band gives you enough building time to feel worthwhile without tipping into a set that takes a whole weekend.

## Step 2: Pick by theme and scene

Once the age band is settled, choose the scene you actually want to look at on the shelf.

- **Launch-day scenes:** the rocket launch pad set, which includes a launch tower, a fuel truck and astronaut minifigures.
- **Deep-space exploration:** a planet base or the space station, both built around interior detail rather than a single big silhouette.
- **Mechanical structures:** the Mars rover, where the appeal is the drivetrain and suspension rather than the exterior.

2026 brings a new angle as well: a connectable lunar research station. Its modular design means multiple sets can be joined into one larger layout, which makes it a strong starting point if you plan to expand later. Before you commit, look at real builder photos on brickplan.cn to confirm the finished dimensions and the colour scheme — box art is generous, and photos are not.

## Step 3: Watch value and plan for repeat purchases

Minifigures and printed parts are the standouts of the Space line, and rare parts tend to hold their value, so they matter when you compare two sets at the same price. As a rule, favour the set with more pieces and an exclusive minifigure over one with a flashier box.

Build time is a useful planning number, especially if you are buying for family sessions:

- 800 pieces: roughly 2–3 hours
- 1,900 pieces: roughly 5–6 hours

For collectors, timing the purchase matters more than the set choice. Space sets usually drop 15%–20% below the standard price during promotions, so waiting for a sale is rarely a bad decision.

## FAQ

**Which LEGO Space set is best as a gift for a child?**
For ages 6–8, the City rocket launch pad is the safest pick: about CNY 500, 800 pieces, simple to build, and it comes with minifigures and a launch tower, so there is plenty to play with afterwards. The value is hard to beat.

**How is LEGO Space different from ordinary brick sets?**
The Space line carries exclusive minifigures, printed parts and specialised curved elements, and the theme accuracy is high. It is also fully compatible with other LEGO sets, which widens the ways you can expand it.

**What price is reasonable for a Space set?**
CNY 600–1,000 is a sensible range for a mid-size set, while smaller sets of around 800 pieces are worth picking up at CNY 300–500. A promotion near 30% off is a genuinely good deal.

**How does a beginner judge whether a set is too hard?**
Read the age label and the piece count together: the age label reflects handling difficulty, the piece count reflects time. For a first purchase, choosing one band below the child's age is the safer move.

One last suggestion: space-themed sets combine well, so once you own two of them, try mixing the modules into a single station layout of your own. If you build something worth showing, post it on brickplan.cn and share it with other builders.
