# Countryside Brick Building Tutorial: Farmhouse to Fields

## Plan Your Countryside Scene

Before you pick up a single brick, spend some time on the overall layout. The heart of a countryside scene is the harmony between nature and daily life, so you are not aiming for towering skyscrapers — you want an open, quiet, rural feeling inside a small footprint. Divide your baseplate into functional zones first: put the farmhouse on one side, spread the fields across the middle, tuck a small pond on the other side, and then use fences and trees to link the zones together naturally. Once you have chosen a baseplate, sketch the road route and the position of the main buildings so you know where everything goes — the actual building then flows in one go.

Colour choice is the other key to a countryside look. Use greens for fields and lawns, cream and warm brown for the farmhouse walls and roof, and a touch of blue for the pond. That keeps the palette warm and unified, with a calm, comforting feel. Don't forget to leave open space for minifigures to move around — it makes the scene feel alive.

## Build the Farmhouse and Trees

The farmhouse is the visual centre of the scene. Lay the foundation first, then build the walls from cream bricks, remembering to leave openings for the door and windows. For the roof, use sloped bricks in red-brown or grey to create the classic cottage roofline. Don't skip the chimney — one small angled stack on top instantly suggests a lived-in home. Add a short flight of steps and a stone-slab path at the door, running out towards the fields.

Trees bring the scene to life immediately. Stack brown bricks for the trunk, then cover it with light and dark green pieces for a full, fluffy canopy. Plant one or two large trees in front of the farmhouse and scatter low shrubs along both sides of the road to create layers of varying height. If you want a rounder canopy, mix green parts in different sizes so the foliage looks natural and thick.

The fields are the base colour of the countryside. Build tidy rows from light and dark green bricks, and work in gold or yellow pieces to suggest ripe wheat ready for harvest. A few small flowers and rocks along the field edge lift the level of detail straight away. If you have room, add a small vegetable plot enclosed by a low fence, planted with red tomato pieces — it is lively and fun.

## Fields, Water Features and Fences

Water breaks up the flatness of the scene. Build an irregular pond from trans-blue bricks and shape the shoreline with light grey or brown slope bricks for a natural edge. Float a green lily pad or a small wooden boat on the surface so it catches the light — the whole scene suddenly feels animated. Fences define the zones: build countryside-style wooden fencing from white or natural wood-coloured thin bars and plates, running along the fields and paths. It looks good and ties the separate areas into one whole.

## Finishing Touches

With the main build complete, move on to the most enjoyable stage. Check that every section is secure and press down gently so the parts sit tight together. Then add the details of daily life: a small flower pot by the door, a scarecrow standing in the field, a little fishing stool by the pond, a warm yellow lamp under the eaves. These tiny additions are usually what communicate the warmth and everyday life of the countryside.

Finally, look at your build from different angles, adjust the position of the trees and tidy the line of the road so the countryside scene feels more natural and balanced. Photograph this quiet corner of rural life and share it with your friends. With careful planning and patient building, anyone can recreate their own ideal countryside in brick.
