# LEGO Radar Vehicle MOC: Rotating Antenna & Chassis Build

The short version: a radar vehicle lives or dies by its continuously rotating parabolic antenna. Mount a 4x4 or 6x6 turntable as the rotation base, lay curved panels half a brick offset to form the dish, and give the chassis shock-absorber suspension.

Radar trucks are among the most recognizable vehicles in military brick building — the huge parabolic dish, the boxy radome and the squat off-road chassis give it real presence on a desk. In this MOC guide I'll break the design down into four parts: the rotating antenna mechanism, the radome structure, the chassis and suspension, and the cockpit details, with part-picking advice for Technic and turntable pieces along the way.

## Rotating Antenna Mechanism

The rotating antenna is the soul of the whole vehicle, and the core part is the turntable. I recommend a 4x4 or 6x6 turntable: fix the lower half to a body bracket, connect the upper half to the antenna base, and the antenna will spin continuously with very little resistance. If you want motorized rotation, hide a Technic gearbox inside the chassis — an XL Motor driving an 8-tooth gear into a 24-tooth gear for two-stage reduction, then feeding power to the gear teeth on the side of the turntable. Leave a clutch gear between the gearbox and the turntable so a jam doesn't burn out the motor. The manual version is simpler: run a crank handle out the side of the turntable and turning it swings the antenna — just as convincing.

## Radome Structure

The radome carries the antenna array, and the most reliable approach is building the parabolic profile from curved panels. Start with a 3x3 or 4x4 cross-shaped frame, then lay the curved panels on layer by layer, offsetting each layer by half a brick — this noticeably increases overall rigidity. On the inside of the curve, use hinges to attach the support arms so the whole dish can pitch between 0 and 90 degrees: flat when stowed, upright when operating. For extra detail, ring the edge of the dish with 1x1 slopes to suggest array elements, and add a thin rod on top as a probe — the military look jumps immediately. Watch out: curved panels work loose easily, so pin every layer with Technic pins or connector pegs as a secondary fixing.

## Chassis and Suspension

For the chassis, use a frame of 6x10 or larger with one suspension group at the front and one at the rear. Build each suspension from Technic shock absorbers paired with swing arms: one end of the arm attaches to the frame, the other to the wheel axle, and the spring sits diagonally between them — it soaks up bumps and gives the body a stance much closer to a real military truck. Steering is optional, but fit independent shocks on all four wheels, otherwise the weight of the antenna will crush the nose down. Leave space in the middle of the chassis for the battery box and receiver, route the wiring along the main rails, and secure it with 1x2 bricks with holes — tidy, and easy to disassemble for maintenance.

## Cockpit Details

The cockpit is the area that decides how finished the model looks. Start with a 6-wide front frame to set the outline, use a transparent curved windscreen element, and add 1x2 slopes on both sides as a transition. Inside, fit a seat element and a steering wheel, and show the radar screen with a printed element or sticker. On the roof, add a round 2x2 turntable with a warning light on top — rotate the base to simulate a search sweep. Don't forget a spare tire and tool box at the rear, and use hinges for a rear hatch that opens, storing spare antenna support parts inside. Play value doubles instantly.

## FAQ

**Q: The antenna judders when it rotates — what do I do?**
A: First check that the top and bottom halves of the turntable are pressed fully flat, then check whether the drive axle is bent. Keep the gear mesh clean and don't press it too tight, and the antenna will rotate continuously with very little resistance.

**Q: The curved panels on the dish keep falling off. How do I reinforce them?**
A: Insert Technic pins between each layer of curved panels, or swap the large curved panels for 2x2 curved bricks. Build the 3x3 or 4x4 cross frame first, then lay the curved panels layer by layer with a half-brick offset — overall rigidity improves noticeably.

**Q: How should the chassis suspension be built?**
A: Use a frame of 6x10 or larger with one suspension group front and rear. Pair shock absorbers with swing arms: one end of the arm to the frame, the other to the wheel axle, the spring diagonal between them. It absorbs impacts and keeps ground clearance.

**Q: Is a brick radar vehicle MOC beginner-friendly?**
A: It's a medium-to-advanced build. The hard parts are centering the turntable and tuning the suspension, so build one or two wheeled vehicles first. Bag by bag, it takes a few hours to complete.

**Antenna juddering?** Check that the two halves of the turntable are fully pressed flat, add a little grease at the gear mesh, and confirm the drive axle isn't bent. **Curved panels falling off?** Insert Technic pins between layers, or replace the large curved panels with 2x2 curved bricks. **Nose dipping?** Move the battery box rearward, or fit stiffer springs in the front suspension. **Short on parts?** Prioritize the three key categories — turntables, shock absorbers and curved panels — and substitute freely elsewhere.
