# Street Scene Building Tutorial: Plan a Modular Brick City

Street scenes are probably the most rewarding line in MOC building: you can recreate the old street you remember, or invent a city that only exists in your head. They are also the most planning-hungry — get the baseplate footprint wrong and every building you add later has to be redone. This **street scene building tutorial** moves through four stages — planning, main structure, street, modular assembly — and each one gives you numbers you can build straight from.

## Why are street scenes such a classic brick MOC theme?

The appeal is practical: a single building stands on its own, but line up a row and you get a neighbourhood with real life in it. One building tells one story; the whole street becomes a community.

The core strengths of a **street scene building tutorial** approach are:

- **Modular design**: each building is built independently and can be combined freely
- **Endless expansion**: the street can keep growing and the city can keep extending
- **Rich detail**: from signage to street lamps, every detail is a chance for creativity
- **Scene interaction**: minifigures, vehicles and plants make the street feel alive

## Step 1: Plan your street scene

### Choose a style

Before you start building, decide on the overall style of the street. Common options:

**European classical**
- Features: pointed roofs, arched doors and windows, stone-textured walls
- Typical buildings: clock tower, cafe, bookshop
- Suitable colours: beige, brown, dark red

**Modern urban**
- Features: glass curtain walls, geometric shapes, clean lines
- Typical buildings: office block, apartment, convenience store
- Suitable colours: grey, blue, white

**Chinese traditional**
- Features: upturned eaves, red walls and green tiles, traditional ornament
- Typical buildings: teahouse, herbal medicine shop, townhouse
- Suitable colours: red, gold, dark brown

**Themed and creative**
- Features: breaks the rules and mixes in imagined elements
- Typical buildings: fantasy shop, steampunk workshop
- Suitable colours: anything goes, no fixed palette

### Street layout

In any **street scene building tutorial**, layout is what decides the final effect:

- **Standard street**: buildings on both sides facing each other, road in the middle
- **Corner street**: needs a corner building to make the transition
- **T-junction**: good as the end point of a street run
- **Plaza layout**: buildings arranged around a central square

### Planning module sizes

Recommended dimensions for a standard street module:
- **Single module width**: 16-stud or 32-stud baseplate
- **Module depth**: 16 or 32 studs
- **Building height**: ground floor plus 2-3 upper floors is a good target
- **Road width**: 6-8 studs including the sidewalk

## Step 2: Build the main structure

### Foundation and ground floor

The foundation of a building determines the stability of the whole thing.

Key points for ground floor design in this **street scene building tutorial**:
- Leave space for the entrance and use an arched or square door frame
- Install display windows: use transparent glass parts to show the interior
- Design steps: steps 1-2 studs high make the building feel more real
- Ground floor height: it is usually taller than the upper floors, which gives a sense of space

### Facade design

The facade is the most direct expression of a building's style:

**Brick wall effect**
- Stack 1x1 and 1x2 bricks alternately
- Use colour variation to suggest weathered brickwork
- Special parts such as brick-textured plates add to the surface quality

**Modern facade**
- Use smooth tiles for a flat wall surface
- Use glass parts for large windows
- Keep lines simple and emphasise the geometry

### Roof design

The roof is the building's hat, and different roof styles match different building types:

- **Flat roof**: modern buildings; handy for details like air-conditioning units
- **Pitched roof**: European buildings, built from slope bricks
- **Chinese roof**: curved parts to recreate upturned eaves
- **Terrace**: flat roof with railing, room for tables, chairs and plants

## Step 3: Build the street details

### Road and sidewalk

In this **street scene building tutorial**, road detail is what sells the realism:

- **Carriageway**: dark grey baseplates, optionally with white lane markings
- **Sidewalk**: light grey baseplates, one plate higher than the road
- **Crosswalk**: white 1x1 plates laid out as stripes
- **Kerb**: 1x1 bricks or slope bricks along the edge

### Street decoration

The details that bring a street to life:

- **Street lamps**: transparent parts combined with lamp-post parts
- **Benches**: brown and green parts joined together
- **Trash bins**: a simple small build
- **Mailbox**: the classic red shape
- **Planters and trees**: plenty of greenery along the street
- **Signage**: shop signs made from tiles, with stickers or printed parts

### Add minifigures and vehicles

Minifigures are the soul of a street scene:
- **Pedestrians**: put in different characters to add everyday life
- **Vehicles**: brick cars, bicycles, even a bus
- **Scene interaction**: someone waiting for the bus, someone walking a dog, someone drinking coffee at the kerb

## Step 4: Modular assembly techniques

### Connection standards

Make sure different building modules can be combined freely:

- **Consistent baseplate height**: all modules use baseplates of the same thickness
- **Aligned interfaces**: connect modules with technic pins or clips
- **Reserved passage**: leave a walkway between modules

### Handling corners

Corner buildings are the hard part of a street scene. This **street scene building tutorial** recommends:

- Use a 45-degree or curved corner
- Give the corner building a distinctive facade treatment
- Make sure the sidewalk runs continuously around the corner

## Advanced tips: making the street more realistic

### Weathering

Use darker parts to suggest the age of a building:
- Dark bricks at wall corners to simulate grime
- Different coloured parts on the roof to simulate aged tiles
- Faded treatment on signage

### Interior detail

Interiors are visible through the windows:
- Cafe: tables and chairs, coffee machine, counter
- Bookshop: bookshelves, till, reading lamps
- Residence: sofa, TV, table lamp

### Night scene

Install LED lights inside the buildings:
- Interior light through the windows creates a warm atmosphere
- Street lamps light the road and add depth
- Neon signs build a busy commercial street

## Wrapping up

This **street scene building tutorial** has covered the main line: plan first, then the main structure, then the street, then modular assembly. When you actually start, don't aim too big — build one 16-stud-wide unit properly first, then think about attaching a second one.
