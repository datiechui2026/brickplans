# Chinese Bricks vs LEGO: Which Brands Are Compatible?

## Introduction

![Build steps mixing Chinese bricks with LEGO](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/chinese-bricks-lego-compatibility/chinese-bricks-lego-compatibility-2.jpg)

"Can I mix Chinese bricks with LEGO?" is really a question about money. If you can top up your parts bins with cheaper elements without wrecking the structure of a LEGO-designed model, your MOC budget can drop by half. That makes Chinese-brick-to-LEGO compatibility a topic you cannot avoid. Below you'll find brand-by-brand verdicts, the key test data, mixing strategies and the myths worth clearing up.

## 1. Why compatibility matters so much

### What compatibility really means

For MOC builders, part compatibility directly determines how free you are to create. If you find a great MOC plan and then discover it can only be built with one specific brand, your options collapse. The stronger the cross-brand mixing ability, the bigger your creative space.

### How compatibility is judged

Judging whether bricks work together comes down to a few points:

1. **Stud and tube tolerance fit** — is the tightness right?
2. **Colour consistency** — does the same colour differ between brands?
3. **Clutch power** — how firmly do parts hold once connected?
4. **Moulding precision** — are edges rough, is there flash?

## 2. Compatibility reviews of major Chinese brands

### Gobricks (Gao Zhuan)

**Compatibility score: ★★★★★**

Gobricks is widely regarded as the most precise Chinese brick brand right now. Its mould accuracy is very close to LEGO's, and clutch power sits in the middle — neither too loose nor too tight. Gobricks trans-clear parts have high light transmission and its colours are very clean.

- Mixing with LEGO: near-perfect compatibility
- Standout strength: more than 3,000 part types, wide colour range
- Best for: MOC building, restocking your parts library

Many MOC plans recommend Gobricks parts precisely because that precision guarantees a plan can be reproduced.

### Double Eagle (Cada)

**Compatibility score: ★★★★☆**

Double Eagle is known for technic. Its beams, pins and gears are highly compatible with LEGO Technic. Standard bricks, however, occasionally show a slight colour deviation.

- Mixing with LEGO: technic parts are perfect, standard bricks differ slightly
- Standout strength: broad technic range, strong value
- Best for: mechanical MOCs, technic builds

### SEMBO

**Compatibility score: ★★★★☆**

SEMBO's part quality has improved noticeably in recent years. Basic bricks are very compatible, though special parts can occasionally run tight.

- Mixing with LEGO: basic bricks are fine, watch the special parts
- Standout strength: rich military-themed parts, high-quality printed elements
- Best for: military MOCs, dioramas

### Mould King

**Compatibility score: ★★★★☆**

Mould King focuses on technic sets, especially motorised versions. Its technic parts are compatible with LEGO and precision control is solid.

- Mixing with LEGO: technic parts compatible, power component connectors are interchangeable
- Standout strength: good value motors and power components
- Best for: motorised MOCs, remote-control models

### Qiji

**Compatibility score: ★★★☆☆**

Qiji is acceptable on basic bricks, but special-part precision and colour consistency lag behind the top tier.

- Mixing with LEGO: basic bricks usable, test a small batch first
- Standout strength: low price, good for bulk filling
- Best for: internal fill in non-critical structures

## 3. Hands-on testing: the key numbers

### Clutch power test

We ran a clutch power test using standard 2x4 bricks, with LEGO as the baseline and a maximum score of 10:

| Brand | Clutch score | Feel when connecting |
|------|-----------|---------|
| LEGO | 10 | Perfect |
| Gobricks | 9.5 | Slightly tight but smooth |
| Double Eagle | 9.0 | Balanced |
| SEMBO | 8.5 | A little damped |
| Mould King | 8.5 | Balanced |
| Qiji | 7.5 | Occasionally loose |

### Colour consistency

Across the three basic colours — white, black and red — Gobricks and Double Eagle showed the smallest deviation. SEMBO's white occasionally runs warm, while Qiji and some smaller brands deviate more visibly.

### Moulding precision

Under a microscope, Gobricks comes closest to LEGO, with clean, sharp edges and no burrs. Double Eagle and SEMBO follow, while some budget brands show slight flash along part edges.

## 4. Mixing strategies: getting the most out of compatibility

![Close-up detail of brick studs and tubes](https://brickplan2-1317217681.cos.ap-hongkong.myqcloud.com/blog/chinese-bricks-lego-compatibility/chinese-bricks-lego-compatibility-3.jpg)

### Strategy 1: high-precision brands for structure

On load-bearing structures and connection points in an MOC, use Gobricks or LEGO parts to keep the build stable.

### Strategy 2: value brands for filler

Internal fill and non-visible faces can use cheaper brands. It doesn't affect the look and it saves money.

### Strategy 3: keep colour unified first

If a model depends on colour consistency — a pure white building, for example — use one brand throughout. If colour matters less, such as camouflage on a military model, mixing is fine.

### Strategy 4: match special parts to the specialist

For special parts such as hinges, gears and tyres, pick the brand strongest in that area. Technic goes to Double Eagle; printed elements go to SEMBO.

## 5. Clearing up compatibility myths

### Myth 1: all Chinese bricks hurt your fingers

Early Chinese bricks really were too tight and uncomfortable. Today's Gobricks and Double Eagle have clutch power that feels perfectly comfortable.

### Myth 2: mixing damages parts

As long as precision is within a reasonable range, mixing brands does not damage parts. Brands that are far too loose will hurt the building experience, though.

### Myth 3: compatible means identical

Even the most compatible Chinese brand differs subtly from LEGO. Those differences rarely affect a build, but they are worth knowing about if you chase a flawless feel.
