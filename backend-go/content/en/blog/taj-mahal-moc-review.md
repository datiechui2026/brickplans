# Brick Taj Mahal MOC Review: 4,500 Pieces of White Symmetry

The verdict up front: this Taj Mahal MOC renders the main mausoleum at mini scale, roughly 40×40 studs at the base and about 30 cm tall. All four facades are strictly symmetrical, the central dome transitions smoothly, and the result is a genuine display-piece build.

The Taj Mahal has been called "a teardrop on the cheek of time," and it sits near the top of most architecture MOC builders' wish lists. This particular design compresses the main mausoleum into a mini-scale model: about 40×40 studs across, around 30 cm high, and squarely in large-display-piece territory.

## Shape and Accuracy: Symmetry Taken Seriously

The Taj Mahal's defining feature is perfect axial symmetry. Here the four elevations are almost identical, and the central dome is stacked layer by layer from curved bricks and dome elements, giving a notably smooth curve. The four corner minarets are slender and tall, each capped with a small dome that closes the silhouette. The designer also reproduced the main entrance: inverted arch bricks form the classic iwan portal, and it reads instantly as the Taj Mahal. The base platform is paved with white smooth tiles, with a small pavilion on each of the four sides. The overall outline is highly faithful.

## Color Plan: Layers Built on Pure White

White bricks dominate the model, which is both the appeal and the challenge — a large field of pure white easily flattens out. The designer's solution is to run three whites of different color temperature in separate zones: cream-white for the dome, pure white for the walls, and light grey-white for shadowed faces. The subtle differences naturally carve out three-dimensional form. Accent colors are minimal: only a small number of trans-light-blue elements at the reflecting pool, which preserves the building's austere character.

## Structure and Movable Details

As a monument, this is a static model, but there are several structural points worth noting. The central dome is hollow, and the top lifts off to reveal a small prayer chamber underneath. The four minarets attach to the main body through hinge elements, so their angle can be adjusted slightly and they can be laid flat for transport. The arcade around the base uses SNOT (studs-not-on-top) construction to turn smooth tiles outward, which looks considerably more refined.

## Build Experience: Patience Balanced Against Payoff

The build is a real test of patience — those four identical facades have to be assembled four times over. The designer mitigates this with symmetric sub-packaging: the left and right halves are separated, so you finish one side before starting the other, which cuts search time substantially. Expect roughly 10 to 12 hours in total, ideal for spreading across a few weekends. Structurally the model leans heavily on interlocking and cross-axle reinforcement, so the finished piece is very solid and safe to move around.

## Part List

The part count is about 4,500, of which white-system bricks make up roughly 80% — lots of 1×4 and 1×6 bricks plus curved bricks. The dome section needs several dozen dome elements and curved plates in various sizes. The special parts are mainly arch bricks, hinge elements, and a small number of printed window-lattice tiles. The inventory is dominated by common brick types, so sourcing the parts is relatively affordable.

## Who It Suits

This Taj Mahal MOC is best suited to builders with some experience, especially those drawn to architectural aesthetics and long, focused sessions. Displayed on a bookshelf or in a cabinet, it will be the visual anchor of the room. **Q: Is the Taj Mahal MOC hard to build?**
A: On the harder side. The central dome has to be closed off by stacking curved bricks layer by layer, and the slender corner minarets must stay perfectly vertical. Following the instructions takes about 8 to 12 hours; build in stages and sort parts by color.

**Q: How do you get depth out of an all-white Taj Mahal?**
A: Through color-temperature zoning. The designer uses pure white, cream-white and light grey-white for the walls, dome and shadowed faces respectively, so the tonal differences carve out form naturally instead of reading as a flat white mass.
