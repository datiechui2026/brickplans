# Candy Shop MOC Review: Windows, Awnings, Candy Displays

The short version: this candy shop plan is built on a standard 32x32 baseplate module, so it sits flush beside any other street scene you own. What makes or breaks it is the facade — large clear window panels for the storefront, a mint green cornice against a warm yellow baseplate, and a striped awning that photographs beautifully.

Sweet shops are always the mood-setters in a street layout: bright colors, dense detail, and a built-in cozy feel. This particular MOC follows the standard 32x32 baseplate module and lines up with any modular building. Below I go through three build details — the display window, the awning, and the candy shelving — then cover how to convert it to a lit version.

## Facade: The Display Window Is the Shop's Eyes

A candy shop facade lives or dies on its display window. The plan uses **large clear glass panels** as the main window body, backed by a layer of 1x1 trans round plates standing in for candy jars, plus a warm yellow baseplate behind them to fake the look of interior lights being switched on. Above the window, a ring of 1x2 curved bricks forms a mint green cornice that plays cleanly against the cream white walls.

When you build the window, leave at least one stud of frame thickness on all four sides and finish the edge with 1x1 bricks. That way the glass sits flush in the opening instead of bulging past the wall line. The shop sign spells "SWEETS" in letter bricks, mounted on a layer of dark pink plates as a backing — enough contrast that you can identify the shop from across the street.

## Awning: The Most Photogenic Element in the Scene

The candy-striped awning is the standout design in this plan. It builds the wavy canopy from **red and white 1x2 slope bricks**, hangs 1x1 round plates along the front edge as a fringe, and hides the frame under the second-floor plate. Two 1x2 technic bricks plus technic pins anchor it, which lets you adjust the tilt angle by a small amount.

One easy mistake: the wave in the canopy comes from offsetting each slope brick by half a stud. Follow the plan piece by piece or the surface will show an obvious staircase. Remember to leave room under the awning for two bar stools and a lollipop-shaped lamp post — that corner alone looks like a postcard of the street.

## Interior Displays and Modular Conversion

The ground floor holds a checkout counter and three candy display racks. The candy itself is 1x1 round plates sorted in rainbow order — cheap to source and very effective. Upstairs is a small baking room with an oven and a mixing bowl. The whole building locks to its neighbors through baseplate studs and can be lifted out to display on its own whenever you want.

To make the shop stand out at night, run a warm white LED strip inside the display window edge and route the wiring through a hidden channel in the second-floor back panel down to the base. Nothing shows on the outside. Overall the plan is compact and detail-dense at a medium difficulty — a solid pickup for anyone growing a street layout.

## FAQ

**Is the candy shop MOC worth building?**
Yes. It uses the standard 32x32 baseplate module and sits beside any modular building; the window and awning are detail-rich and give the scene its cozy feel, which makes it a good fit for street-scene collectors.

**How do I make the display window feel atmospheric?**
Use large clear glass panels as the main window body, back them with 1x1 trans round plates standing in for candy jars, and add a warm yellow baseplate to fake the look of interior lights. Cap the top with a ring of 1x2 curved bricks for the cornice.

**Can it be connected to other street models?**
Yes. The plan is based on a standard 32x32 baseplate with the same connections as other street buildings, so it sits directly alongside them. Placing the candy shop mid-block works well — the bright color scheme draws the eye.

**Which parts build the awning?**
Alternating slope bricks or curved plates create the striped effect, supported by two thin posts. The awning is one of the most photogenic elements in a street scene, so pick colors that contrast with the shop's main palette.
