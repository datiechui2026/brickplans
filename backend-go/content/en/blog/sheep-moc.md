# Brick Sheep MOC Instructions Review: 680 Parts, Curly Fleece

Verdict up front: this sheep MOC is the small-and-precise end of animal building. It runs about 680 parts, the finished model stands roughly 10 cm tall, the instructions cost around ¥25 and the loose parts about ¥90, with a build time of roughly two hours. The fleece, made from curved slopes plus round plates, gets you about 90% of the way to the real thing, the four legs hold the model up solidly, and the color scheme is clean. The instructions are downloadable on brickplan.cn — search "sheep" — and include a full parts list.

## 1. Shape and Resemblance: Curly Fleece and Dopy Charm

The fleece is the standout feature. The back is covered in layer upon layer of curved slope pieces, with the gaps between them filled by off-white 1x1 round plates. From a short distance it reads as one fluffy mass rather than a stack of individual pieces, which is where the roughly 90% resemblance figure comes from. The head does its part too: black round plates for the eyes and pinkish-brown pieces for the drooping ears. A ball joint at the neck lets you tilt the head down into a grazing pose, and that slouched, slightly dopey stance is what sells the model as a sheep rather than a generic white blob.

The color scheme stays disciplined — off-white as the main body color with light brown as an accent — so it looks clean rather than busy. That restraint is also why it sits well on a desk: it doesn't fight with anything else on the shelf.

## 2. Build Experience: Structural Stability

The legs are what make or break a standing animal, and here the solution is straightforward. Each leg is built up from two to three layers of plates, then connected to the torso through a ball joint. Once all four feet are on the table the center of gravity is settled — a light push won't tip it over.

The instructions are split into six major steps, each one keeping the parts count under ten pieces. That's a deliberate choice and it pays off: you're never digging through a heap of hundreds of pieces looking for a single 1x1. The manual is clear throughout, and the whole thing comes together in about two hours.

The one thing to watch for is the fleece layer. Every one of those round plates has to be pressed down fully, one at a time, and fingers get sore long before the back is finished. Use a brick separator or pressing tool for this section rather than your thumb.

## 3. Value: Instructions and Parts Cost

Instructions are priced at about ¥25, within a ¥20–40 range. Sourcing the loose parts yourself from the parts list costs roughly ¥90, putting a full set at about ¥115 — under ¥0.17 per piece, which is very reasonable for an animal MOC. If you already keep off-white and light brown in your stock, that cost can drop to around ¥60. Given the resemblance of the finished model and the build experience along the way, the price is fair.
