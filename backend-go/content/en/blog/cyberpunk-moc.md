# Cyberpunk LEGO MOC Guide: Neon Colors, SNOT and Lighting

The short version first: neon is the visual core of cyberpunk. Hold the buildings in dark gray, black and navy so the scene stays dim and oppressive, then accent with Trans-Neon Orange and Trans-Neon Green — backed up by lighting and SNOT structure.

When you picture cyberpunk, what comes to mind? Rain-slick streets flickering with neon, towering stacked billboards, future architecture piled in layers. Dropped into the brick world, those elements are exactly what makes a cyberpunk MOC so compelling. This guide looks at three angles — palette, structure and lighting — to help you build a street corner nobody forgets.

## Neon Color Scheme: The Visual Core of a Cyberpunk MOC

The loudest label on any cyberpunk MOC is its neon palette. Use dark gray, black and navy as the main building colors so the mass stays under a low-light, oppressive feel; then accent with Trans-Neon Orange, Trans-Neon Green and magenta, keeping those three colors to roughly 10%–15% of the total area. Under natural light, trans-neon parts take on a candy-like translucent quality. Line up 1x1 trans bricks and 1x1 trans round plates to simulate light strips and the "electronic city" mood lands instantly. For the ground, lay dark gray plates with a few trans-neon yellow pieces scattered in to fake the reflection of standing water — that is the small trick behind a lot of great cyberpunk builds.

## Future Architecture: SNOT and Technic Structure

Cyberpunk buildings carry masses of vertical signage, external piping and cantilevered floor slabs, and that is where SNOT (Studs Not On Top, i.e. side building) becomes the core technique. Use 1x1 bricks with studs on the side, or headlight bricks, as your direction changers, and you can fix tiles, trans pieces and stickers anywhere on a facade. External piping reads well as flexible hose combined with Technic bushes — route the pipes so they weave between floors and the visual layering fills out immediately.

For overhangs, reach for Technic: lock Technic beams into the base skeleton with Technic pins, then tile over the beams. That carries load far better than stacking pure bricks, and it is the most stable way to support a projecting viewing platform. At floor junctions, use 1x2 hinge bricks to introduce slight offsets — it makes the silhouette of a future building more organic and less regular.

## Lighting and Trans Pieces: Making the City Glow

Lighting is the finishing touch on a cyberpunk MOC, and the part that photographs best. If your budget allows, add light bricks — no question. The trick is to bury the light brick inside the building and let the glow escape through trans bricks and trans window panels; it reads softer and more natural than an exposed light. For a giant rooftop billboard, spell characters with tiles, back them with a layer of trans bricks, and pair with a light brick to get a backlit sign effect. Want a stronger rainy-night feel? Run a ring of trans blue bricks along the top eaves to mimic neon reflecting off falling rain — it looks outstanding in night shots. No light bricks on hand? Build static neon strips from trans-neon parts instead; they photograph just as well in daylight, which is part of why cyberpunk MOCs are so beginner-friendly.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I settle on a palette for a cyberpunk MOC?**
A: Use dark gray, black and navy as the main building colors to keep that low-light, oppressive feel, then accent with Trans-Neon Orange and Trans-Neon Green. Keep the main colors above 70% of the area and let neon only guide the eye.

**Q: What if I've used too much neon?**
A: This is the most common beginner mistake. Keep main colors above 70% and leave neon in just a few spots. If it still looks busy, cut half the trans pieces — the image cleans up immediately and the oppressive mood returns.

**Q: How do I keep future-building walls from looking flat?**
A: Use SNOT to turn brick sides outward, mixing smooth wall planes with three-dimensional blocks, then layer plates of different sizes for staggered relief. Avoid a single flat wall — cyberpunk gets its future feel from stacked volumes.

**Q: How do I firm up a loose SNOT wall?**
A: Check whether you used Technic pins with friction, or add a 2x4 plate on the back for reinforcement. On large SNOT walls, line the inside with a layer of bricks as a backing board to spread the load.

The beginner trap is overloading the neon so the whole thing looks busy. Keep the main colors at 70% or more, and let neon do nothing but lead the eye. If a SNOT wall wobbles, check that your Technic pins have friction, or add a 2x4 plate behind it. Once the main build is done, drop in a hovering motorcycle, a few street lamps and a minifigure in a raincoat, and the street scene comes alive.
