# Best MOC Communities for Brick Builders: China and Global

Every MOC builder eventually hits the same wall: you can only reverse-engineer so much on your own. Finding builders who share your taste, and a place to trade ideas with them, is one of the fastest ways to improve your building and keep the inspiration flowing. Whether you have just finished your first alternate model or you have been designing for years, an active community makes the hobby considerably more rewarding. Here are the platforms worth your time, starting with the Chinese-language scene and moving outward.

## MOC Communities in China

### 1. Baidu Tieba: the Brick MOC board

Tieba is one of the largest Chinese-language forum platforms, and its brick MOC board has a steady stream of regulars. Members post their own builds, share instruction files and write up build notes on a daily basis. The tone is friendly, and newcomers asking basic questions usually get helpful answers rather than silence. Two tag categories are worth following first: instruction sharing and newcomer help, which together surface most of the high-quality content quickly.

### 2. Xiaohongshu: the MOC creator circle

Xiaohongshu has become a major showcase for MOC builders in recent years. Search for brick MOC or MOC instructions and you will find a large volume of polished build photos and process videos. The platform's recommendation system gives good builds a real chance of being seen, which makes it useful for creators trying to build an audience. Many posts also include instruction links and parts lists, so the resource density is comparable to a dedicated forum.

### 3. Bilibili: the MOC video community

Bilibili is where most Chinese-language brick video creators publish. Content ranges from unboxing and reviews to build tutorials, finished-model showcases and technique breakdowns. The MOC tutorial and brick modification sections are good places to pick up practical building methods. If you learn better from video, our own [BrickPlans blog](/en/blog) has plenty of material on MOC basics and advanced techniques that works well alongside video tutorials.

### 4. WeChat and QQ groups

Group chats are the most direct way to talk with other MOC builders. There are hundreds of active groups, split by theme: military, mecha, architecture, modular street scenes and more. Joining one gives you real-time feedback and access to group-organised MOC contests and bulk parts buys. The practical route in is to follow a few creators on Xiaohongshu or Bilibili first; their profile pages usually list a group entry point.

## MOC Communities Outside China

### 1. Rebrickable

Rebrickable is the largest MOC instruction platform in the world, with a huge library of builds and a parts database behind it. You can search for and download instructions, and use the site's parts inventory feature to check whether you already own everything a given model requires before you commit. The community discussion areas are busy too, making Rebrickable the first place to look when you want to know what the international scene is building right now.

### 2. Eurobricks Forum

Eurobricks is one of the longest-running brick forums, with a dedicated MOC section. Discussion there is deep and technically serious, and many well-known MOC designers post their work and tutorials on the board. English is the main language, but the threads are image-heavy, so the language barrier is lower than you might expect.

### 3. Flickr brick MOC groups

Flickr hosts a large number of brick MOC groups across every theme and style. Many top-tier designers publish high-resolution photos of their builds there, which makes it an excellent place to study construction detail and hunt for inspiration. Groups such as LEGO MOC and Brick Central are good starting points.

## Getting Real Value Out of a Community

Signing up is the easy part; the value comes from how you use the platform. A few suggestions:

- **Share your own work.** Do not stay a permanent lurker. Post your builds, even small ones, and you will get constructive feedback you would not get building alone.
- **Enter the contests.** Many communities run themed MOC competitions on a regular schedule. They are a good way to push your skills and get your work in front of more people.
- **Organise your resources.** On [BrickPlans](/en/blog) you will find instruction files and tutorials that pair well with community discussion, which makes the learning process considerably faster.

MOC communities are the soil that brick creativity grows in, and every build posted and every thread argued over makes that soil a little richer. Whichever platform suits your language and your style, the important part is to keep building and keep sharing. Hopefully this list helps you find the circle you belong in and gives you a place to let your ideas run.
