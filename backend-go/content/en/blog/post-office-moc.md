# Brick Post Office MOC: Build a Cozy Street Scene Landmark

In a brick-built town, if the flower shop is the romantic one and the fire station is the tense one, the **brick post office MOC** is the warmest landmark on the block. It doesn't rely on a flashy silhouette. Instead, a classic red-and-green color scheme, a round-arched entry and an old wall clock tell the story of the letter-writing era better than any dramatic shape could. This tutorial walks you from palette to facade to interior details so you can build the post office everyone wants on their street.

## 1. The Classic Palette: Red and Green Are the Soul of the Post Office

Half of a post office's identity comes from its colors. The traditional postal pairing is deep green with warm red, plus gold as an accent — a contrast that reads as "post office" at a glance. Start with off-white or light grey as the main wall color, use deep green for door frames, window frames and the awning, put warm red on the roof and the sign, and reserve gold strictly for the emblem and lettering details.

Watch the ratio of red to green. Too much red looks garish, too much green looks gloomy. Roughly 70% white wall, 20% green and 10% red produces the best result.

## 2. Arched Entry: Three Ways to Build an Elegant Porch

The round arch is the signature facade element. There are three approaches, in rising order of difficulty. The simplest is to span the top of the doorway with a 1x2 arch brick. The intermediate version uses SNOT (studs not on top) to lay a run of plates sideways above the opening, arranged into a curve for a fuller arch. The advanced version builds a double arch — an outer arch plus an inner arch to create porch depth — with a cornice line pressed on top.

Above the arch you'll usually set a sign brick printed with "POST OFFICE" (or a sticker tile), then add two wall lamps. The cozy feeling arrives instantly.

## 3. Mailbox and Wall Clock: The Small Details That Sell It

Small details are what give a street scene its life. A red cylindrical mailbox by the door is mandatory: stack two or three layers of 2x2 round bricks, cap it with a dome piece, and apply a printed tile on the front as the mail slot. On the exterior wall, hang a **classic wall clock** made from a 1x1 round plate for the face and a small black bar for the hands, mounted above the porch. It is both standard post office kit and a time marker for the whole street.

You can also set a few potted plants on the windowsill and park a postal cart by the door — two small wheels plus a hopper. These cheap little additions make the whole building feel alive.

## 4. Interior: Counter and Sorting Station

Don't let your post office be an empty shell. At minimum, furnish two functional zones. The first is the counter area: build a wooden counter from 1x2 dark brown bricks, then set a scale on top (a 1x1 round plate with a small round tile), a stamp, and a few letters made from two stacked 1x1 tiles.

The second is the sorting station. Line one wall with a row of angled letter cubbies built from 1x2 plates and divider bricks, and tuck a few white printed letters inside. Hang a pendant lamp above the counter, and lay the floor in a diagonal pattern using 1x1 bricks in a contrasting color. Put a minifigure in front of the counter and the scene's story is complete.

## 5. Modular Street Compatibility: Fitting In With the Neighbors

For your **brick post office MOC** to join your town, it has to follow the shared rules of modular street buildings. Build the main structure as a standard sandwich: a 32x32 baseplate at the bottom, the main floor volume in the middle, and a roof that lifts off in one piece so you can show the interior. Lock the walls together with staggered 2x4 bricks, and leave exposed studs between floors so the levels can be separated.

Keep the height level with the buildings next door — roughly 30 to 34 brick courses — and keep the porch footprint to no more than one minifigure's width. That way the post office lines up cleanly with the flower shop and the bakery beside it and forms a continuous street.

## Final Thoughts

From the red-and-green palette and the round-arched porch to the mailbox, the wall clock and the counter inside, every detail is there to serve one goal: warmth. Set this **brick post office MOC** into your street scene and it becomes more than a building — it becomes the most story-rich landmark on the block.
