# Angry Birds MOC Blueprint Review: Is This IP Build Worth It?

A decade on, the red bird on the slingshot still lives in a lot of builders' memories. So what happens when a classic mobile-game IP meets brick MOC design? Below we review this Angry Birds MOC blueprint across four areas — accuracy, parts, build experience and finished result — to help you decide whether it deserves a few days of your building time.

## Accuracy: The Character Reads Instantly

The whole point of an IP model is whether it looks like the thing. The best surprise here is that the blueprint nails the details that make the character recognizable: the round, chunky body of the red bird, the angry white eyebrows, the sharp orange-yellow beak and the signature puffed-out chest are all reproduced faithfully in brick.

The designer did not take the lazy route of a ball plus a pair of eyes. Instead, layered curved pieces and sloped wedge bricks carve out the bird's outline, and the resulting curves look genuinely smooth.

The green pig is just as well handled. Its green snout and the two little moustache strokes are convincingly done, and the lopsided expression gives it exactly the villainous attitude you remember. Paired with scene elements such as the slingshot and the pig fortress, the overall IP atmosphere comes through strongly. If there is a weakness, it is that a few small attachments — the pig's ears, for example — are simplified. Judged as a MOC, though, this is well above average.

## Parts: Mostly Basic Bricks, Very Approachable

This Angry Birds MOC blueprint is restrained about parts. Nearly everything is a common basic brick, plate, wedge or corner piece; there are no rare limited-edition or printed elements. That means no expensive overseas sourcing — you can fill the gaps from your own loose-parts bins or from ordinary sets, which is friendly to beginners and to builders on a budget.

The trade-off is that the design uses a lot of same-colored pieces, so sorting matters more than usual. Pre-sorting by color will noticeably speed up your build. The parts list itself is clear, and the blueprint states the quantity of each element used at every step, so you are unlikely to hit the classic mid-build "I'm missing a piece" moment.

## Build Experience: Solid Structure, Moderate Difficulty

The step breakdown is thoughtfully laid out. Head, body, wings and facial features are assembled as separate modules, which makes mistakes easy to spot and reduces the risk of having to tear work down and redo it. The internal frame uses standard SNOT (studs-not-on-top) techniques to fix the expression pieces firmly onto curved surfaces, and the finished structure is sturdy enough that it holds together when handled.

For experienced builders this sits at the low-to-mid difficulty range — the main body takes roughly three to four hours. Beginners need not worry either: the blueprint uses clear color separation and viewing-angle cues, so as long as you follow it patiently you will not get lost. The one small complaint is that the slingshot connection feels a little loose, so it is worth reinforcing at the key joints.

## Finished Result: Good for Display and for Play

The finished model looks great. Sitting on a desk or in a display cabinet, the glaring red bird is instantly recognizable and immediately brings back memories of the game for anyone who played it. The posable design also keeps it from being a static ornament — the wings and eyebrows can be angled to produce different expressions, which makes it genuinely fun to fiddle with.

Taken as a whole, this Angry Birds MOC blueprint strikes a good balance between accuracy, approachability and finished appearance. It works both as a physical keepsake of a childhood favorite and as a solid example for learning brick-shaping techniques. If you are looking for a playful, moderate-difficulty MOC blueprint based on a well-known IP, this one is well worth a try — and when you finish, the bird that kept you company through your teens will be standing quietly in front of you, glaring and grinning at the same time.
