# How to Install a Brick Light Kit: MOC Wiring Guide

Your MOC looks great in daylight, but after dark it loses something. If you want lit windows and glowing headlights, installing a brick light kit is the step you cannot skip. This guide walks through the whole process, from picking the right kit to routing wires, hiding them and troubleshooting when a lamp refuses to light.

## 1. Know Your Light Kit Types First

Light kits for brick models fall into three broad groups, each with its own use case:

| Type | Features | Best suited to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Static light kit | Always on, low cost | Street scene shop windows, city night scenes |
| Flicker / breathing light kit | Onboard control chip, programmable | Traffic lights, neon signs |
| Motor-linked dynamic kit | Lights plus motors | Headlights that track movement, ferris wheel night scenes |

Domestic light kits have improved a lot over the past two years: affordable and broadly compatible. Imported brands such as Brickstuff pay closer attention to lamp head dimensions and wire quality. When buying, check three things: whether the lamp heads fit your baseplates, whether the wires are soft and bend-tolerant, and whether the control box supports multiple parallel branches.

## 2. Prep Work: Rehearse Before You Build

The worst approach to light kit installation is fitting and adjusting as you go. Do three things before you start:

1. Light up the entire kit on the table and test every lamp, ruling out dead units.
2. Plan the wire route for each lamp and mark where wires will pass through.
3. Have tweezers and a thin pry tool ready; you will be pulling bricks and re-routing wires constantly.

## 3. Core Steps: Route, Connect, Conceal

### Routing principle: hide wherever possible

- Run wires through the empty space inside the building, favouring paths that are already obscured, such as under a second-floor plate or along the inside of a wall corner.
- Where a wire must pass through a wall, use a brick with a hole or a natural gap rather than forcing the cable.
- Gather the main trunk line along the edge of the baseplate to reduce crossings and tangles.

### Connection order: battery box, then splitter, then lamp heads

1. Connect the battery box or control box first and confirm power is working.
2. Use a splitter to divide the main line into several branches; running too many lamps down one branch causes brightness loss.
3. Connect the individual lamp heads last, lighting each one as you go to verify it.

### Three tricks for hiding wires

- Pair dark-coloured wire with dark bricks for the least visual noise.
- Mount lamp heads on transparent or holed pieces so the light spreads evenly.
- Tuck the power box into the base or inside the building, leaving only a switch hole on one side.

### Colour temperature: warm versus cool

Lighting is not just about brightness; colour temperature sets the mood. Warm (yellowish) light suits shop windows and residential buildings, while cool white is more realistic for headlights and street lamps. If you mix them, keep the dominant colour temperature consistent within a single area, otherwise the scene looks messy. It is also worth adding a master switch to the control box's power line so you can cut power to everything at once, saving energy and extending LED life. For models on long-term display, a USB power cable is more stable than batteries.

## 4. Troubleshooting Common Problems

- **One lamp will not light:** swap the lamp head first, then check the splitter; it is usually a poor contact.
- **Everything looks dim:** too many lamps are wired in parallel on one branch. Reduce the number on each run or use a higher-spec battery box.
- **Lights flicker:** check whether the batteries are drained and whether connectors have worked loose.
- **Wires are visible and ugly:** press them down with same-colour tiles or hold them with small clips.

## Final Thoughts

Light kit installation is not complicated. The whole thing comes down to four words: plan before you build. Do enough planning up front and the wiring ends up clean and tidy.
