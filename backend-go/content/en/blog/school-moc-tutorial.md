# School MOC Tutorial: Build a Brick Campus From Scratch

Want to build a school out of bricks? This **school MOC tutorial** walks you through a full campus scene from zero, one stage at a time. Whether you are new to MOC building or already have a few models behind you, the planning logic and construction techniques below will give you a workable path.

## Why Build a School-Themed MOC?

Everyone has some memory tied to a school. That is exactly why school MOCs are so popular: a campus is full of everyday life and story potential. The teaching building, the sports field, the library, the cafeteria — every corner can be rebuilt in brick.

The advantages of a school MOC:

- **Modular construction**: classrooms, corridors and the sports field can be built separately, which lowers the difficulty
- **Rich scene potential**: minifigures, desks, blackboards and plants make the model feel alive
- **Educational value**: a good parent-and-child project that builds spatial thinking and hands-on skills

## Preparation Before You Start

### Parts List

Before you begin this **school MOC tutorial**, gather these basic parts:

- **Basic bricks**: a supply of 2x4, 2x2 and 1x2 bricks for the main structure
- **Windows and doors**: transparent window elements are the key ingredient for a teaching building
- **Baseplates**: a 32x32 or 48x48 baseplate as the campus foundation
- **Minifigures**: teachers, students and other characters
- **Decorations**: plants, flags, desks and chairs, and other small accessories

### Tools and Instructions

Before building, browse other users' campus MOCs on BrickPlan.cn for inspiration. You can also plan your layout in advance with brick design software such as Studio or LDD.

## Step 1: Plan the Campus Layout

### Decide on Scale

Base the size of the campus on how many parts you actually have. Beginners should start with a **small campus scene** containing just 2-3 main buildings.

### Design the Functional Zones

A complete campus usually contains these areas:

- **Teaching zone**: the main teaching building with classrooms and offices
- **Activity zone**: a sports field, basketball court or gymnasium
- **Living zone**: cafeteria and dormitories (optional)
- **Landscape zone**: flower beds, trees and a flagpole

### Draw a Sketch

Draw a top-down view of the campus on paper and mark the position and size of each building. It takes a minute, but it prevents the classic problem of running out of space halfway through the build.

## Step 2: Build the Main Teaching Building

### Foundation and Walls

 The teaching building is the core of the campus. Start with a baseplate as the foundation, then raise the walls course by course.

**Wall-building tips:**

- Use a staggered stacking method, like a real brick wall, to make the structure more stable
- Leave window openings in the walls and fit transparent bricks as glazing
- Separate each floor with plates to create a visible floor line

### Classroom Interiors

The heart of any **school MOC tutorial** is detail. Inside a classroom you can add:

- A teacher's podium and blackboard (built from dark tiles)
- Desks and chairs (1x2 bricks combined with small plates)
- Bookcases or storage cupboards
- Blackboard displays at the front and back of the room

### Roof Design

For the roof, you can use slope bricks to build a traditional pitched roof, or plates for a flat roof. A flat roof is more convenient for details such as antennas and air-conditioning units.

## Step 3: Build the Campus Environment

### Sports Field and Facilities

The sports field is where the campus energy shows. Use a green baseplate for the grass and white lines to outline the running track. A basketball court can be laid out with 2x2 tiles for the court markings.

### Greenery and Landscaping

Dress the campus with plant elements:

- Trees: brown bricks for the trunk, green leaf elements for the canopy
- Flower beds: 1x1 round tiles to suggest flowers
- Lawn: green baseplates laid out as grass

### Campus Entrance

The school gate is the landmark of the campus. Special elements can form an arched gate, topped with a school name plaque, which makes the whole scene read as a real school.

## Step 4: Add Minifigures and Details

### Cast Your Characters

Populate the scene with suitable minifigures:

- Teacher minifigures (with accessories such as books and a pointer)
- Student minifigures (mix hairstyles and outfits for variety)
- Campus staff (security guards, cleaners and so on)

### Build Scene Stories

The thing that makes a campus feel alive is story. Try these setups:

- **In class**: students listening to a lesson in the classroom
- **Break time**: students playing on the sports field
- **Flag-raising ceremony**: the flag going up on the flagpole in the middle of the field
- **Home time**: parents picking up children at the gate

## Advanced Techniques: Make Your School MOC Stand Out

### Lighting Effects

Add LED light sets to the teaching building for interior lighting. At night, the effect is striking.

### Modular Design

Split the campus into modules (a classroom module, a sports field module) so it can be taken apart and rearranged. This is the advanced approach recommended in this **school MOC tutorial**.

### Seasonal Variation

Try building the same campus in different seasons — a spring campus with cherry blossom, an autumn campus with fallen leaves. It adds another layer to the model.

## FAQ

**Q: What if I don't have enough parts?**
A: Start with the smallest possible scene and expand gradually. You can also look at other creators' parts lists on BrickPlan.cn for reference.

**Q: What scale should the campus be?**
A: Use minifigure scale (roughly 1:48) as your baseline, and make sure building sizes stay in proportion to the minifigures.

**Q: Where can I find instructions?**
A: BrickPlan.cn has a wide range of school MOC instruction resources — search for "school MOC" for inspiration.

## Closing Notes

You should now have the basic method for building a brick campus scene: plan the zones first, raise the teaching building, then fill in the environment and the characters. Remember that the point of MOC (My Own Creation) is creativity and personality, so don't feel bound to a fixed formula. Experiment freely, enjoy the build, and rebuild the ideal school in your head out of bricks.
