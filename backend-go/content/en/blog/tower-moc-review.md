# Brick Tower MOC Review: An 80 cm Skyline Centerpiece

Towers are the most visually arresting corner of the brick architecture category, and this MOC pushes the idea of "rising into the clouds" about as far as a limited baseplate allows. Here is a full breakdown of how it is designed, how hard it is to build, and whether the parts count justifies the price.

## Design Highlights

The first thing that strikes you is the structural proportion. The designer steps the shaft inward tier by tier, so a modest footprint turns into a visual height of more than 80 cm. A rotating observation deck and a spire topped with a lightning rod complete the silhouette, giving the model the elegance of a classical clock tower without losing the aggressive verticality of a modern skyscraper.

The exterior mixes dark grey and light grey in a deliberate two-tone patchwork. In direct sunlight the contrast reads as strong shadow lines; under indoor lighting the same surfaces pick up softer gradations, so the tower looks layered rather than flat from any angle.

The detail that deserves the most credit, though, is hidden inside: a spiral staircase. The designer uses 1x1 corner pieces to create a continuous rotating ascent, and every step seats tightly against the next. Turn the assembly and you can watch the floors progress one after another. It is the clearest demonstration of the technical ambition behind this tower MOC, and it is the part experienced builders will photograph first.

## Build Difficulty

The model sits in the mid-to-advanced range, officially rated 4 out of 5 stars. The main shaft combines SNOT technique with conventional brick stacking, which is where newer builders will feel the learning curve. The tower crown is the real test — the spherical dome section demands solid angle calculation, and a mistake of one plate early on will not show up until several rows later.

The instructions handle this well. Key steps carry color-coded callouts and multi-angle views, and BrickPlans supplies a step-by-step parts breakdown list alongside them. Based on an actual build, expect roughly 6 to 8 hours from sorting to the final spire.

## Parts and Value

The model uses about 3,200 pieces. The share of specialized elements is low — the overwhelming majority are ordinary bricks, which makes replacing anything you are missing straightforward.

The one weak point is the volume of dark grey 1x4 bricks. The design leans on them heavily, and they are the least forgiving part of the parts list if you are building from your own stock. If your budget is tight, substituting a similar color in the interior rows is a reasonable compromise, since the two-tone exterior effect survives the swap.

Given the finished size and the amount of interactive detail — the rotating deck and the working staircase in particular — the price lands firmly on the good-value end of comparable tower builds.

## Conclusion

This is a polished, highly playable tower MOC with a well-resolved structure and very few compromises. If you want to push your technique into SNOT-heavy vertical building, or you simply want a landmark that dominates a shelf, it earns its place. The obvious next step would be a matching night-lighting kit from BrickPlans — a tower this tall deserves to actually light up after dark.
