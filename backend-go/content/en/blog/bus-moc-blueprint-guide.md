# Brick Bus MOC Guide: Proportions, Interior and Steering

A bus is one of the most recognizable vehicles you can put in a city layout. Get the proportions right and a single bus can pull an entire street scene together. But compared with sports cars or construction vehicles, a bus is long, bulky and far less forgiving about structure and shape. This guide walks through the key decisions in a brick bus MOC plan: scale, interior, chassis and how the finished model sits in a street scene.

## Proportions: Single-Decker or Double-Decker First

The first step with any bus MOC plan is deciding what kind of bus you are building. A single-decker sits low with clean, flowing lines and suits a modern urban commuter look. A double-decker carries retro and tourist associations, hits harder visually, and naturally becomes the focal point of a street scene.

For scale, match the standard modular street footprint of a 32x32 baseplate and aim for roughly 1:48. Body length works best between 16 and 24 studs. Shorter than that feels cramped, longer makes the model hard to turn and awkward to park. Build in this order: lay down the chassis frame first, then use long beams to lock in body width and roof height, and add the windows last. On a double-decker, the floor between the upper and lower deck must be built from plates or beams to carry load, and you need to reserve space for the staircase. Skip that and the model ends up top-heavy and unstable.

## Interior: Seats, Grab Rails and the Cab

A bus interior is where information density really shows. A typical plan gives you three or more rows of seats, usually built from 1x2 or 1x3 bricks with backrests, spaced apart to leave a central aisle. Close the underside of the seats with plates and you get storage space for free. Run thin rods along the ceiling as grab rails and the sense of realism jumps immediately.

Leave a boarding area on the door side, and add small details like a coin box or a card reader. The cab is the heart of the whole vehicle: steering wheel, dashboard and driver's seat are all non-negotiable. For the steering wheel, mount a technic part with a cross hole to the dashboard using a short pin. Add pedals and a gear lever and the driving position suddenly feels inhabited. If you want more fidelity, build SNOT structures on the inside of the windows for curtains or advertising panels, so the interior reads faintly from the street.

## Wheels and Steering

The wheel assembly decides whether the bus actually rolls. Use tires of moderate diameter and run cross axles through the chassis so all four wheels sit on the same line and the model moves smoothly. A single-decker is fine with two standard wheel sets. A double-decker has a longer body, so it is better served by a twin-axle setup with extra chassis rigidity in the middle to stop the floor sagging.

If your plan does not include steering, add a simple steering linkage to the front wheels yourself using hinge parts, so the front end swings. Playability goes up a level straight away. During installation, leave enough axle sleeves and washers at the front and rear wheel sets to keep tires from rubbing the fenders, and hold ground clearance at two to three plates. That reads as realistic and still clears the road surface of your street modules. A solid wheel assembly is the foundation for both display and play.

## Pairing With Street Scenes

Even a beautifully built bus needs the right stage. Park it beside a bus stop on a street module, add a stop sign, a shelter and a queue of minifigures, and you have an instant morning-commute story. City layouts usually put a coffee shop or convenience store next door, so position the bus stop where it meets the sidewalk module and lay gray plates as the road surface to tie everything together.

For color, use a main body color that echoes the surrounding buildings, then one or two accent colors to separate the body from the window band, so the bus does not visually fight the street. Seat minifigures inside the cabin and place others at the door, and the whole street starts to feel alive.
