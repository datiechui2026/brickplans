# BBQ Shop MOC Review: A Street-Scene Build Full of Detail

Short version first: this barbecue shop MOC is a solid set of instructions, but it suits intermediate builders more than beginners. The grill and the skewers capture that smoky street-food feel beautifully, though the high share of specialty parts means new builders will need to plan their spares carefully. If you build for detail, it is worth the effort.

As a whole, the design nails the bustle of a roadside barbecue stall: a smoking grill at the door, skewers hanging on display, and a lit dining area on the second floor. Every angle is worth photographing, and once it is parked in a street scene it becomes the liveliest building on the block.

## 1. Part Accuracy (Score 8/10)

The standout is the grill at the shopfront, which uses curved pieces and transparent orange elements to suggest glowing charcoal. The shop sign uses a printed tile for the barbecue lettering rather than stickers, and the accuracy is high. The full design runs to roughly 920 parts, with specialty elements making up about 20 percent — a little above a typical small street-scene shop, but the extra variety buys noticeably better detail.

## 2. Build Experience (Score 7.5/10)

Expect about 3.5 hours of building. The grill chimney and the roof tiles involve a lot of repeated stacking and take some patience. The instructions themselves are clear step by step, but it is worth sorting parts into boxes by color beforehand, otherwise hunting for pieces will eat a lot of time. The finished structure is stable, and at roughly 24 cm for the two floors it has real presence in a street layout.

## 3. Interior Details and Value (Score 8.5/10)

The ground floor recreates a prep counter, a condiment shelf and a freezer cabinet. Upstairs is a dining area with tables, plus a small pull-out charcoal drawer mechanism that adds a bit of play value. The instructions cost about 18 yuan and the parts come to roughly 120 yuan in total, which makes value moderately good. One drawback: transparent orange pieces are pricey when bought individually, so builders on a tight budget can substitute standard orange elements.
