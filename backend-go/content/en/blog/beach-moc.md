# How to Build a Beach MOC: Sand, Waves, Umbrella & Palms

Here is the short version: a 40x40 beach MOC using roughly 2,600 parts costs about 550 CNY in materials and takes around 10 hours — an afternoon plus an evening. Four things carry the whole build: beige pieces for the sand, six stacked layers of blue for the surf, a red-and-white umbrella as the focal point, and palm trees to finish it off. Follow the order below and the result is ready to sit in a display cabinet as summer decor. Instructions are available at brickplan.cn.

## Step 1: Lay the Sand and Set the Shoreline

Start with a 40x40 beige baseplate. Sand covers about 60% of the baseplate area, positioned toward the front and left. Do not run the shoreline in a straight line — use 1x1 round plates and 1x2 curved bricks to build a naturally bending curve. Scatter a small number of dark brown pieces across the sand as grain texture, then mix in a few 1x1 transparent pieces to imitate the glint of sand catching the light. The sunny feel appears immediately.

## Step 2: Stack the Waves in Six Graded Layers

Divide the water into six layers running from the shore out to deep water: the first layer is light blue right against the shoreline, then sky blue, lake blue, royal blue and deep blue in sequence, with navy as the outermost layer. Offset each layer by half a brick from the one before it so the surf reads as pushing forward in steps. Between the second and third layers, sandwich a row of white 1x1 pieces to imitate foam. The sea takes up about 40% of the baseplate; once all six graded layers are stacked, the water gains real depth.

## Step 3: Umbrella and Palm Trees

Build the umbrella canopy from alternating red and white 1x1 round plates, about 8 cm in diameter. The pole is a 1x1 cylinder brick pushed into the middle-to-back area of the sand, with two 2x4 plates set beside it as loungers. For the palm trees, stack brown 2x2 bricks into a trunk 20 to 25 cm tall, then build the crown from four green curved plates and hang two brown 1x1 round plates as coconuts. Plant two trees, one tall and one short, and place the umbrella and trees so they do not line up — the scene comes alive at once. Finish by scattering a few coloured 1x1 pieces on the sand as shells and starfish, and you are done.

## FAQ

**Q: How many parts does a beach MOC need?**
A: This plan uses about 2,600 parts, of which blues make up roughly 40% and beige sand about 30%. If you build only the waves without the palms and umbrella, you can cut that to around 1,800 parts. If you run short on blue, alternating light and dark blue still gives a good effect.

**Q: What does a beach MOC cost to build?**
A: With loose parts already on hand, material cost is about 550 CNY, and blues and beiges are the biggest share. If you buy everything new, budget 700 to 900 CNY. To save money, prioritise ocean-themed sets to part out — blue bricks are common in them.

**Q: How do I make the waves look more realistic?**
A: The core is six graded blue layers stacked with an offset, each shifted half a brick, with white pieces tucked in at the foam line. Also make sure the shoreline is built from curved bricks — a straight coastline is the most common beginner mistake, and fixing that one point improves the result immediately.

**Q: Can a beach MOC and a snow mountain MOC share parts?**
A: Some parts can be shared. Baseplates, basic bricks, slopes and 1x1 pieces are all universal; the main difference is the colour palette. A beach uses a lot of blue, beige and white, while a snow mountain uses a lot of white, light grey and dark grey. If you build both, white bricks can be shared, saving roughly 15% of the parts cost.

**Q: Is a beach MOC a good gift?**
A: Yes. The finished model measures about 32x32 cm, looks good on display and carries a summer mood, which makes it a good choice for friends who like ocean themes. Add a clear acrylic display case to keep off dust for another 30 to 50 CNY, keeping the overall budget under 600 CNY.
