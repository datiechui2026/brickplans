# Brick Moon Base MOC Guide: Modules, Power and Terrain

Of all the themes builders return to, sci-fi space holds a special pull. A complete brick moon base MOC turns the idea of landing on the Moon into a small three-dimensional world you can hold in your hands. Whether this is your first space build or you are looking to break out of a flat, predictable layout, this guide walks through the whole process: modular design, power systems, lunar terrain and the minifig details that sell the scene.

## Modular Design: Start With a Single Pod

The first step is not to build the entire base at once. Break it into separate functional units that connect to each other — that is the most practical approach for any space-themed MOC.

The **habitat pod** is the core module. It usually uses a cylindrical or curved structure to suggest a pressurised crew compartment. To push the sci-fi feel, add transparent parts along the outer wall as portholes and leave usable space inside. The **dome pod** is the visual centrepiece: a hemispherical transparent cover that can read as either a greenhouse or a central control room. The sense of depth that translucent parts create is one of the strongest elements you can put in a space scene.

Between the pods you need **airlocks and connecting corridors**. An airlock is the transition space between a spacecraft and the outside environment; in a MOC it can be shown with a small hatch plus a ring-shaped interface. Run lengths of grey-white tubing between the habitat pod, the dome and the lab module, and the base immediately gains a clear circulation route — and a much stronger sense of layering.

## Power Systems: Make the Base Glow

The life of a moon base MOC often hides in the energy details. Deployable **solar panels** are the most recognisable component of any space base. Use dark blue or purple plates for the photovoltaic surface and mount them on dark grey frames angled outward — it looks authentic and reads well at a glance.

Beyond solar arrays, you can add a small nuclear reactor module or a backup battery pod, using orange and yellow parts as warning colour blocks. If you want more atmosphere, hide an LED light inside the dome or the control room: by day you look at structure, by night you look at light and shadow — two different experiences from one build. Spreading the power system sensibly also makes the overall logic of your moon base MOC hold up to closer inspection.

## Lunar Terrain: Building a Bleak but Beautiful Surface

The ground under the base deserves just as much care. Stack grey and off-white plates into uneven ground, then deliberately leave hollows to suggest meteor craters and piled regolith. A few techniques: use curved slope pieces for gentle inclines, rough hinge parts to mimic fractured rock, and finish with a scatter of small transparent sand-dust pieces standing in for stardust.

Adding one or two **lunar modules** or landers to the background is the finishing touch. Keep the lander simple — a support column plus a crew cabin, with four legs planted firmly on a ring-shaped stand — so it sits at a different height from the main base and creates a stepped composition. Place a signal tower or a moon rover further out and the story of your little lunar town is complete.

## Astronauts and Detail Minifigs: Bringing the Story to Life

Minifigs are the soul of a space scene. Give your astronauts white spacesuits, transparent helmets and jetpacks, and pose them mid-stride across the surface. Station a technician or two at the airlock checking equipment and the base instantly feels operational. Details decide the result: a maintenance tool beside a hatch, a lunar map sticker on a wall, an operator standing at a console — these small touches are what keep a brick moon base MOC interesting on a second and third look.

When the build is done, give it a name — something like "Alpha Lunar Outpost" or "Dawn Base." On brickplan.cn you can find more space-themed MOC instructions and reference builds, swap layout ideas with other creators, and refine your own moon base step by step.

From a single baseplate to a full installation, the appeal of a moon base MOC is that it turns a distant patch of sky into something you can touch and keep improving. Hopefully you find your own version of that landing-day feeling while you build.
