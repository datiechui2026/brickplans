# Brick Skyscraper MOC Review: A 1.2m Skyline Build

Today we are taking a full look at a brick skyscraper MOC. Based on a modern city landmark, the design compresses a supertall silhouette into a manageable footprint while keeping the imposing presence intact — exactly the kind of project city-theme builders keep coming back to.

## Product Overview

The instructions are designed for a model roughly 1.2 meters tall, built as a modular stacked structure. From bottom to top it splits into three main sections: the crown, the standard floors, and the podium. The detailing in the plans is dense — the curtain wall glazing is handled so light passes through, and both the rooftop antenna and the revolving restaurant are reproduced. Finished and standing on a desk, the tower nearly reaches the ceiling, and the visual impact is strong. The plans can be downloaded at https://brickplan.cn, the documentation is clearly laid out, and the parts list is precisely annotated.

## Build Difficulty

As a large-scale architectural MOC, this sits at medium-to-high difficulty. The core challenge is the vertical load-bearing structure of the internal support skeleton — if the standard floors are not aligned, the whole tower ends up leaning. The cantilevered structure in the crown section is also demanding and requires repeated adjustment of the connector angles. It suits builders who already have medium-sized MOC experience and can read plans fluently. Beginners should start with a smaller architectural model first.

## Parts Review

The parts mix is dominated by a large quantity of transparent pieces plus light gray wall bricks. The transparent elements have even light transmission and produce an excellent curtain wall effect, while the structural pieces clutch firmly and bear weight well. Worth calling out: the corner pieces are highly accurate — after hundreds of repeated stacking cycles they still hold tight, with almost no looseness. The one drawback is the high number of specially shaped elements, so gathering the complete parts inventory may require extra purchases. Fortunately the plans already provide substitution solutions.

## Building Experience

The build flows well overall. Assembling sub-modules first and then combining them keeps a steady sense of progress. The repeating standard floors do get a little tedious, but finishing each level — capping it off — delivers a strong sense of achievement, and the moment the crown finally goes on really does feel like topping out above the clouds. The instruction steps are divided sensibly, and with the discussion threads in the BrickPlan community, there is always a solution when you hit a bottleneck.

## Finished Model

The completed tower has serious height and a crisp outline. Under room lighting the transparent curtain wall refracts the city-at-night look and photographs beautifully. Placed in a study or in the corner of an office desk, it is both a centerpiece and a ready-made conversation starter. Paired with smaller architecture models and street scene sets from the same theme, it forms an entire miniature city skyline and the sense of scene jumps immediately.

## Verdict

★★★★☆ — four stars. In both parts quality and structural design, this brick skyscraper MOC is top-tier, and the finished model has genuine collectible value. The missing star is for the relatively high cost of sourcing the parts and the fact that it is not especially beginner-friendly. If you love architecture and city themes, these plans are worth your time — and more standout MOCs are waiting to be explored at https://brickplan.cn.
