# Where to Buy Loose Brick Parts: A Budget MOC Buyer's Guide

## Why MOC builders keep watching domestic parts channels

The biggest cost in MOC building is not the instructions — it is the parts. Filling a single design with official parts can easily run into the thousands, which most builders simply cannot absorb. Domestic Chinese brick parts have improved noticeably in recent years, hold stable compatibility, and cost only a fraction of the price. That is exactly why loose-part builders have shifted toward domestic parts channels. But the more channels there are, the more traps there are: some stores sell by weight at rock-bottom prices, while others charge several dimes for a single piece. Here is a walk-through of the main channels.

## Taobao and Pinduoduo: the everyday replacement battleground

**Taobao** is the channel loose-part builders use most, and its strength is sheer variety — countless stores and a very complete range of part types. Search for "loose brick parts" or "brick replacement parts" and you will surface plenty of stores that specialise in loose parts, many of them organised by brand. **Pinduoduo** wins on price, especially for bricks sold by weight and when stacking promo discounts, but store quality varies a lot and mixed lots and colour deviation are more common there. The advice is the same on both: favour older stores with plenty of reviews, and when the parcel arrives, spot-check colour and clutch first. Taobao suits precise replacement parts; Pinduoduo suits stocking up on basic bricks in bulk.

## Official brand stores and flagship stores: the safe choice

Every major domestic brick brand runs its own official flagship store, and some offer an **official replacement service** — if a set is missing or has a broken part, you can request a free or low-cost replacement. That is the most valuable thing an official store offers. On top of that, many brands sell standalone "parts packs" and "supplement packs" with consistent colour and guaranteed quality. They cost more than loose-part channels, but they save you a lot of hassle. If your MOC needs large quantities of one brand's special parts or printed parts, going straight to the official store is the safest route — particularly for technic parts and gears where clutch quality matters most.

## Secondhand platforms: paradise for bargain hunters

Secondhand marketplaces such as Xianyu are a hidden treasure chest for loose-part builders. Plenty of people leaving the hobby dump entire boxes of bricks at absurdly low prices, and those boxes often hide piles of general-purpose bricks, minifigures and even out-of-production parts. Search keywords like "bricks by weight" or "bricks, leaving the hobby", check the seller's own photos to confirm condition, and once the box arrives a single sorting session gives you a free parts library. The downside is that you take what you get and parts may be missing, so it suits builders willing to invest sorting time — not works that demand exact colour matching.

## Parts-list tools and mini-programs: precision at speed

If your design comes with a complete parts list, the more efficient route is **parts-list matching websites and mini-programs**. These tools import your list directly, automatically price every line by brand and part number, and let you order in one click — no searching item by item. They usually connect to multiple warehouses, so you can choose "one brand throughout" or "mix brands to push the price down", which makes them ideal for builders doing accurate replicas. They cost more than by-weight bricks, but what you buy is certainty: whatever you need, you can actually get. For high-precision builds, that is worth it.

## By-weight bricks versus exact replacement: making the call

This is the decision every loose-part builder has to think through. **Bricks sold by weight** (commonly 20–50 RMB per jin, roughly 500 g) are cheap, high-volume and great for building up a basic brick library; the catch is random part types and uncontrollable colours, so they only work for stocking generic bricks. **Exact replacement** is priced per piece, with the part type and colour matching your list precisely — the right choice for building from instructions. My suggestion is to walk on two legs: keep the base library fed with by-weight bricks, then top up the gaps in a specific parts list with exact replacements when you actually build. You keep stock without wasting money.

## Practical ways to save when you order

A few techniques that genuinely work. First, **hit the spend thresholds**: during big sale periods, loose-part stores on Taobao and Pinduoduo almost all take part in spend-and-save promotions, so pooling the parts for two or three builds into one order can knock another ten percent off the unit price. Second, **compare across stores**: the same part can differ several times over between shops, so it pays to request bulk quotes instead of assuming. Third, **check your inventory first**: tick off the parts you already own in your inventory sheet and buy only the gaps, avoiding duplicate purchases. Fourth, **mix brands to cut cost**: use cheaper brands for structural parts and better-quality ones for visible exterior parts, and you get both cost control and good looks.

## The short version

The channels for domestic brick parts are genuinely rich: Taobao and Pinduoduo handle everyday replacement, official stores give you certainty, secondhand platforms let you grab bargains, and parts-list tools deliver precision. Feed your stock with by-weight bricks, build specific projects with exact replacement parts, and stack promo discounts when you order — run that combination and your costs stay in a very comfortable range.
