# Potala Palace MOC Review: 4,000-Part Red and White Palace Build

Quick verdict first: this Potala Palace MOC instruction set is a remarkably complete piece of design work. It runs about 4,000 parts and finishes at roughly 32 cm tall with a 45 cm × 28 cm footprint. At an average of CNY 0.15 per brick, the total cost lands around CNY 600, and the build takes roughly 14–16 hours. It suits intermediate to advanced builders, and among Tibetan-style architecture MOCs currently available it is the most convincing red-and-white colour reproduction you can get.

## 1. Shape and Detail Accuracy

The soul of the Potala Palace is the colour contrast between the Red Palace and the White Palace, and the instructions treat them as two distinct build zones rather than one mass. Dark red and off-white bricks are kept apart throughout, with the Red Palace sitting high in the centre while the White Palace spreads out to both sides along the slope of the mountain. The proportions sit close to the 3:1 visual relationship of the real building, which is what gives the model its silhouette when you stand back from it.

Surface texture is handled with 1×2 tiles, used across the walls to suggest the feel of Tibetan rammed earth rather than leaving rows of exposed studs. Windows are recessed with small dark pieces, and that inset treatment is what stops the facades from reading as flat panels. Overall accuracy comes in at about 88% — high for a fan-designed model of this scale, without pretending to be an exact architectural replica.

The stair section is the other standout. Continuous slope bricks carry the zigzag walkway in front of the White Palace, climbing in layers rather than as a single ramp. Seen head-on from the front, that stepped rise is what makes the model feel like it is built into a hillside.

## 2. Build Experience

The instructions are broken into 10 major steps of roughly 400 bricks each, and the diagrams are clear throughout. Because the red and white walls are assembled as separate zones, parts sorting stays efficient — you are working from a fairly narrow colour palette at any one time instead of hunting through the whole 4,000-piece pile.

The one place that demands attention is the flat roof structure. Its internal support has to be assembled strictly in the order the instructions give; skip ahead or substitute a connection and the later layers that cap the roof will sit loose. That is the difference between a model you can pick up by the walls and one that needs two hands under it.

There are also a small number of unusual parts at the joints between the stairs and the palace walls. Check the parts list against the BrickPlan inventory before you order and you should end up with nothing missing — those joint pieces are exactly the ones most likely to be left out of a self-sourced order.

At about 14–16 hours total, the build splits naturally into a focused two-day weekend: roughly one day for the walls and one for the stairs, roof and finishing detail.

## 3. Value and Who It's For

At 4,000 parts and CNY 0.15 average per brick, the total comes to roughly CNY 600.
