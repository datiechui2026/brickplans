# How to Build a Brick Police Station: Step-by-Step MOC Guide

A police station is one of the essential anchor buildings in any city street scene. Whether you are new to brick building or a seasoned builder, a solid police station tutorial helps you finish this landmark with fewer dead ends and a better result. This guide walks you from parts prep through the final decoration so you can build a convincing police station of your own.

## 1. Before You Start Building

### 1.1 Parts List and Sorting

Before you begin this police station tutorial, gather the parts you will need. A standard police station street-scene model typically calls for:

- **Baseplate**: a 32x32 stud baseplate
- **Wall bricks**: standard 2x4 and 2x2 bricks, mainly in gray and blue
- **Doors and windows**: glass window frames, transparent glass bricks
- **Roof parts**: slope bricks and flat tiles
- **Interior furnishings**: desks, chairs, computers, police equipment
- **Minifigure accessories**: police officers, police caps, handcuffs
- **Exterior decoration**: police station signage, street lamps, fencing

Sort your parts by color and type before you build. It speeds up assembly considerably.

### 1.2 Tools and Workspace

Set out sorting trays or compartment boxes, a brick separator, and a flat, level work surface. A tidy workspace makes the whole build smoother and saves you from hunting for a single 2x2 in the middle of the walls.

## 2. Building the Main Structure

### 2.1 Baseplate and Ground Floor

**Step 1: Lay the baseplate.** Place the 32x32 baseplate flat on your work surface.

**Step 2: Build the ground-floor frame.** Run gray bricks along the edge of the baseplate to form the exterior walls, leaving an opening for the main entrance that is 4 to 6 studs wide.

**Step 3: Lay the floor.** Use dark gray tiles to suggest floor tiles.

**Step 4: Install the ground-floor interior.** The ground floor holds the reception hall, the duty room, and a temporary holding cell. Position the counter, desks and chairs, and use transparent bricks as the partition for the holding cell.

### 2.2 Second Floor and Office Area

Office space occupies the second floor. Cap the ground-floor walls with tiles to serve as the floor slab, then build the second-floor exterior in a different color so it contrasts with the level below. Keep the windows relatively small to read as an office building. Inside, lay out the chief's office, the officers' work area, and a meeting room.

### 2.3 Roof and Rooftop Design

A modern-style police station suits a flat roof. Build the main roof with slope bricks, then add rooftop railings using fence pieces. Place a police antenna and a simple helicopter landing pad on the roof.

## 3. Exterior Scene Building

### 3.1 Facade and Signage

Spell out "POLICE" with printed bricks and mount a police badge above the main entrance. Install a police light made from a yellow transparent round brick at the door, and dress the door columns with thick, sturdy brick pillars to convey authority.

### 3.2 Police Car and Outdoor Fixtures

Build a black-and-white police car with transparent red and blue bricks on the roof to imitate light bars. Mark out parking spaces in front of the station and add fencing and road barriers to make the scene feel active.

## 4. Interior Details and Scene Layout

### 4.1 Interrogation Room and Holding Area

Build the interrogation table from dark-colored bricks and use dark transparent bricks to imitate a one-way mirror wall. Enclose the holding cell with fence pieces and place a simple bed inside.

### 4.2 Technical Equipment Area

Use printed pieces as monitor screens, and build a weapon cabinet from transparent door pieces to hold batons, handcuffs and similar accessories. Stack drawer pieces into filing cabinets to add realism to the scene.

## 5. Building Tips

### 5.1 Structural Stability

Pay attention to how bricks interlock as you go. Press each completed layer down gently with your hand to make sure the bricks are fully seated. On a large building, add support columns inside so floors do not collapse under the weight above.

### 5.2 Color Scheme

Gray, blue and beige work well as the main palette, with yellow and red as accents (the police lights). Avoid too many bright colors so the station keeps its serious, professional character.

### 5.3 Scene Expansion Ideas

Once the basic police station is done, you can connect it to a fire station and a hospital to form a complete city emergency services block. You can also add a chase scene, or design your own badge and unit number to make the station uniquely yours.

## 6. Final Thoughts

With the parts list, floor-by-floor structure, and detail work covered above, you have the full sequence from prep to decoration. Brick building rewards creativity combined with patience, and each build is a small world made by hand. Start laying bricks, and adjust as you go. There is no single correct answer in brick building, only the version you decide to build.
