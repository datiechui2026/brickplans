# How to Make LEGO Instructions: Sketch to Digital PDF

Plenty of builders finish a model they are happy with and immediately want to turn it into instructions that other people can share and rebuild. It looks intimidating, but the workflow is simpler than it appears — the trick is finishing each stage in order instead of jumping straight into software. This guide breaks the process into five stages and recommends a tool for each one.

## Step 1: Sketch by hand and lock the idea down

Everything starts on paper. Draw the front, side and top views of the model, and mark the key structures and the load-bearing areas. The sketch does not need to be neat; what matters is recording the build order and the positional relationships between parts, so you do not forget the details once you move to a screen. Grid paper is the best choice here, since it makes it easy to line brick positions up.

## Step 2: Photograph and scan the physical build

Once the model is actually built, photograph it from several angles and keep the files. Shoot close-ups of the connection points and of how special parts are attached, and wide shots of the overall shape. If you have access to a scanner, scan the three views as well — a scan holds proportions more accurately than a photo, so it can be used directly as an underlay during digitization. Save the original image files rather than compressed pictures pulled from a chat app.

## Step 3: Digitize in design software

This is the core step. Bricklink Studio is the mainstream choice right now: it is free, fully featured, supports importing the parts library, drag-and-drop assembly and automatic step generation. The job here is to "replicate" the physical model inside the software — build up layer by layer following your sketch, checking parts against the real model as you go, and then manually split the assembly into steps so that every step has a clear before-and-after relationship. Beginners can work through the built-in tutorials first to get comfortable with the shortcuts.

## Step 4: Generate the parts list

When digitization is done, the software automatically counts the quantity and colour of each part — that is your parts list. Always check it by hand, especially for rare parts and very small pieces, where part numbers are easy to mix up. Note the part number and the part name in the list so that other people can actually buy what they need.

## Step 5: Export a PDF and a 3D preview

Studio exports both a PDF building instruction booklet and a 3D preview file in one click. The PDF is for printing and sharing; the 3D preview lets other people rotate the model on their phone. Before exporting, go through every step to confirm it reads clearly and delete any meaningless transition steps.

## Publishing and sharing

Once the instructions are finished, they are ready to publish. Many builders organize their files on dedicated platforms so that other fans can browse by theme. brickplan.cn, for example, supports uploading PDF instructions together with a 3D preview: after publishing, fellow builders can download the files directly and leave feedback, which is genuinely useful for improving the instructions.

Making your own instructions is an enjoyable process in itself. Going from a single sheet of sketches to a complete, reproducible digital file is just as satisfying as the build that started it — so pick up the pencil and get going.
