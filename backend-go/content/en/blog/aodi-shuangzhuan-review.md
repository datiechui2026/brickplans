# Auldey Bricks Review: China's Household Toy Brand

For plenty of builders who grew up in China in the 1980s and 1990s, the name Auldey — the company also known as Audi Double Diamond — still brings back a familiar advertising jingle. The brand has accompanied several generations of domestic toy fans, and its brick range is worth a closer look. This review walks through the product lines, part quality, building experience, signature series and value for money so you know exactly what you are buying.

## Brand and Product Lines

Auldey sits under Guangdong Alpha Animation and Culture, a company with more than two decades of toy manufacturing behind it. Compared with many newer brick brands that grew out of contract manufacturing, it has built up real depth in quality control systems, IP licensing resources and retail channel coverage.

Its brick catalogue currently covers three broad areas: preschool bricks aimed at ages 3 and up, technic-style parts and sets aimed at older kids and teenagers, and themed sets tightly tied to the parent company's own animation IPs. The overall positioning is mass market and family — sets designed to be bought in a toy aisle or a supermarket rather than a specialist hobby shop, and to be built together by parents and children.

## Part Quality and Building Experience

In hand, Auldey's clutch power lands in the upper-middle range for domestic bricks. Tolerance on most bricks is controlled fairly evenly, and the press feel is moderate — you don't get the excessively loose connections or the knuckle-bruising tight fits that low-end domestic parts are known for. That consistency matters most on larger builds, where a few loose bricks early on can turn into a wobbly model later.

Mould lines are handled cleanly and there is little sprue residue left on the parts, which is friendly to younger builders who are less careful about trimming and cleanup. The weak spot is technic elements: gears and links are not as smooth-running as those from top-tier brands, and repeated assembly and disassembly leaves slight wear over time. For most players this is completely acceptable — the parts work, they just don't have the silky rotation of premium components.

## Signature Series

The most distinctive thing about Auldey is its IP tie-in range. Drawing on national-level properties from the parent company such as Armor Hero, Super Wings and Balala the Fairies, it has released a large number of character brick figures and scene sets with reasonable likeness, and these are popular with both kids and parents.

For MOC builders, this is the quietly useful part: licensed character sets are a steady source of special elements and minifigure parts. If you build character-focused models, Auldey sets work well as a parts pool rather than as display pieces. Beyond the IP lines, its city street scene, fire and police themed series cover the mainstream everyday building subjects that most families look for.

## Value and How It Compares

On price, Auldey sticks to the approachable route expected of a household name. Cost per brick usually sits in the middle-to-low band among comparable domestic bricks, and combined with regular promotions it often delivers strong value for money.

Against SEMBO, Qiji and Keeppley, Auldey has the edge in IP depth and brand recognition, but falls a little behind some of the premium-leaning domestic newcomers on ultimate part feel and on the breadth of original set design. Against LEGO, the price advantage is obvious, which makes Auldey a sensible entry point for everyday building and parent-child play rather than a collector's centrepiece.

## Verdict

Taken as a whole, Auldey bricks are balanced across quality consistency, IP tie-ins and affordability. They suit younger builders and family settings especially well, where clean moulding, forgiving clutch and recognisable characters matter more than hairline precision. Advanced builders chasing the ultimate building feel or original MOC material can still treat the range as a source of special parts and everyday stock bricks.
