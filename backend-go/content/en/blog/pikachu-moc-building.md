# How to Build a Pikachu MOC: Round Shape, Ears, Tail

The short version: with Pikachu, roundness comes first. Build the base from large curved pieces and dome pieces so the head is round, the body is round and the outline has no sharp corners, then add the ears, the red cheek spots and the jagged tail.

## Roundness Is the First Rule of a Pikachu Build

Pikachu is recognizable because of its chubby body and its signature color scheme. The first rule of building one in bricks is roundness: a round head, a round body, and an overall outline with no angular corners. You get that roundness mainly from curved pieces and spherical pieces. Use large curved bricks or a dome piece as the base of the head, ring the body with curved pieces to form a barrel shape, then smooth the transitions with panel pieces. On proportion, Pikachu's head-to-body ratio is close to 1:1, with a big head and a small body, which is what makes it look cute. Beginners should start with a chibi seated pose: the structure is simple and the rounded look comes out most easily.

## Ears and Cheeks: The Signature Features You Cannot Skip

The black ear tips and the red cheeks are essential. Build the ears from long yellow bar pieces and cap the top with black pieces to make the signature black tips. The ears should stand upright, attached at the base with hinge pieces or ball pieces so they can be tilted slightly. For the red cheeks, attach red round plate pieces to both sides of the face, positioned diagonally below the eyes. Size and placement directly affect how cute the model looks: too high and Pikachu looks fierce, too low and it just looks odd. Make the eyes from black round pieces with a highlight dot. The gaze should be round and innocent, and slightly oversized eyes boost the cute factor.

## Tail and Body Pose

Pikachu's lightning-bolt tail is highly recognizable. Build the tail from yellow pieces into a jagged lightning outline, with brown pieces at the base as the connection. You can make the tail a fixed shape, or split it into two sections joined by hinge pieces so you can set different angles. On pose: a seated Pikachu needs visible legs and feet with short front paws, using small yellow pieces plus brown soles. A standing Pikachu needs the legs slightly apart and the center of gravity kept low so it stands steadily. To make the model livelier, add a ball joint at the head so you can tilt it or turn it to look back over its shoulder.

## Expression and Details: Bringing Pikachu to Life

The expression is where a build comes together. Pikachu's classic looks are the squinting smile and the wide innocent eyes; stickers or printed pieces are the easiest way to do eyes and mouth, and you can build them from parts if you want a 3D look. Make the mouth from small red or dark pieces to form a smiling curve, and place the cheek blush as described above. For details, build the two brown stripes on the back with brown bar pieces. This is an important feature of Pikachu's back, so don't leave it out. Add a brown transition piece where the body meets the tail so the joint reads naturally. When you finish, look over the whole silhouette: the round areas should be round, and the pointy ears should be pointy.

## Color Scheme and Recommended Parts

The color scheme is fixed: bright yellow for the main body, black for the ear tips, eyes and mouth, red for the cheeks, and brown for the tail base and back stripes. Recommended parts to have ready: yellow curved pieces in various sizes, panel pieces, black round pieces for the eyes, and red round plates for the cheeks. If you want an easier route, look for Pokémon-themed brick sets, which usually include printed pieces and dedicated parts. If you want to build from scratch, work through the points above with loose parts. Beginners should not mess with the palette: the classic colors are exactly why Pikachu looks so cute.

## Scene Pairing and Display Tips

A finished Pikachu can be displayed on its own or placed in a small scene: a green grass baseplate, a few Poké Balls and a couple of small flowers with Pikachu standing in the middle, and the atmosphere is instantly there. For photos, pick a light-colored background and a side angle, which shows the ear tips and the tail at the same time, and gives the best shot. A brick-built Pikachu makes a great desk ornament and gift; put it in a display case afterward to keep the dust off and it will stay cute for a long time. Start with a small seated Pikachu and work your way up to more complex poses, and enjoy the build.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I get a round look on a brick-built Pikachu?**
A: It mostly comes down to curved and spherical pieces: use large curved bricks or a dome piece as the base of the head, and close the body into a round shape with curved panels. Avoid right-angle bricks on the outline: once sharp corners appear, the model's recognizability drops noticeably.

**Q: How do I make Pikachu's ears and cheeks?**
A: Build the ears from long cone pieces or slope pieces mounted at an angle, with black pieces capping the tips. The cheeks are two red 1x1 round plates placed symmetrically below the eyes. Get these three points right and the character's expression appears immediately.

**Q: How do I recreate the tail?**
A: Use slope pieces and wedge pieces to build the jagged, zigzag line: starting from the rear of the body, extend and change angle step by step, keeping the lightning outline wide on the outside and narrow on the inside. Connect the tail base with hinge pieces so you can fine-tune how far it tilts up.

**Q: Is Pikachu suitable for beginners?**
A: Yes. The structure is mostly curved pieces and basic bricks with no complex mechanisms, so the difficulty is low to medium. As long as you keep the left and right sides symmetrical and the shapes round, even a beginner can produce a highly recognizable model.
