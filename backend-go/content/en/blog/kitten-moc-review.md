# Cat MOC Review: 480-Piece Orange Tabby vs 2,500-Piece Ragdoll

The short version: the cat MOCs on brickplan are a great pick when you want a relaxing, comfort-building session. The most popular model, the orange tabby, needs only 480 parts and about 2 hours, while the 1:1 scale ragdoll sculpture runs to 2,500 parts and roughly 10 hours. Both work well as a desk display or as a gift.

## 1. Cuteness and Expression Accuracy

The whole point of a cat MOC is the face. The orange tabby uses curved bricks and slope pieces to shape a rounded, chunky body, and the angles of the ears and tail were clearly adjusted many times during design — the sitting pose looks completely natural rather than stiff. The ragdoll takes the opposite approach: a large number of smooth tiles builds up the impression of long fur, and the eyes are printed pieces, giving that bright blue stare a lot of life.

## 2. Build Difficulty

At 480 parts, the orange tabby sits at beginner level. The instructions only ask for 2-4 parts per step, so even a child can finish it without help. The 2,500-part ragdoll is an advanced build. It leans heavily on SNOT (studs not on top) side-building techniques, so it is better suited to builders with at least 3 months of experience.

## 3. Cost and Value for Money

Part cost comes to roughly CNY 80 for the orange tabby and roughly CNY 400 for the ragdoll. Compare that with finished cat ornaments, which often run CNY 200-300, and building the MOC yourself not only saves money but lets you recolour freely — follow the brickplan instructions to build a cow cat or a calico instead, and you end up with something one of a kind.

## 4. Display and Placement

The finished models measure 12 cm and 35 cm respectively. The smaller one suits a bookshelf or office desk; the larger one is a good fit for an entryway display. The brickplan instructions also include a base design, and the anti-tip layout is a thoughtful touch — you do not have to worry about kids knocking it over.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I get the cat MOC instructions?**
A: Search for cat designs on brickplan and you will find several. After registering you can preview them for free; once you have paid, you download the full PDF and a parts list, buy the parts from that list, and start building. The whole process happens on the website, which is convenient.

**Q: Can I build the cat MOC with LEGO parts?**
A: Yes. The brickplan cat instructions rely mostly on standard bricks and are compatible with mainstream brick brands. Special-colour parts may need to be bought separately, though, so check the parts list before you order. If something is missing, it is easy enough to buy the extra piece later.

**Q: Is a cat MOC a good gift for a girlfriend?**
A: It is a very good one — the finished model is cute and shows real thought. The 480-piece orange tabby is low in difficulty and quick to finish, and handing over something you built yourself makes it feel more special. It pairs well with brickplan's gift instruction box set, and the result generally goes down well.

**Q: How is a cat MOC different from an official cat set?**
A: MOC versions have livelier expressions, come in more size options, and let you change the colour scheme freely. Official sets win on complete part counts and polished instructions, but the posing is more limited. If you want something distinctive, go with the MOC; if you want an easy, fuss-free build, go official.
