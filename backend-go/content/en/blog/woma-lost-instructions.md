# WOMA Instructions Lost? How to Find Replacement Manuals

WOMA has built a solid following among brick fans thanks to friendly prices and a wide range of original series. But plenty of builders know the scenario: you finish a set, the parts end up scattered across the floor, and when you finally want to rebuild it or order a missing piece, you realise the instruction booklet is gone. Don't panic — a missing manual does not turn a set into scrap. Here is how to get the drawings back.

## Don't Give Up Just Because the Manual Is Gone

A manual looks like a single folded sheet, but it is the soul of the whole set. It records the building order for every step, the part numbers you need at each stage, and what the finished model should look like. Lose it and the obvious problem is that you can no longer follow the official sequence to recreate the model.

The good news is that most WOMA instructions exist in electronic form somewhere, and there are more recovery routes than most builders expect. Before you start hunting, confirm exactly which set you have lost the manual for. The product number printed on the side of the box (in formats like WOMA-XXXX), the series name, and the part count are all key details for tracking down the drawings later. Write them down first — a two-minute note now saves a lot of guessing when you are searching, and it also stops you downloading the wrong PDF for a similar-looking set.

## How to Find a Digital WOMA Manual

This is the first route to try, and for most sets it is enough. WOMA distributes electronic building instructions through several channels. The common methods:

- **Official website and social accounts**: Log in to WOMA's official channels and look for an "instruction download" or "drawing lookup" entry point. Enter the product number to retrieve the matching PDF.
- **Online store customer service**: Contact the customer service team at the official flagship store where you bought the set, quote the product number, and most agents can send a digital manual straight back to you.
- **Brick instruction communities**: Third-party sharing platforms host large numbers of official and re-photographed instructions uploaded by builders. Search the product number and you can usually locate yours quickly.

Of these, communities dedicated to sharing brick instructions are usually the least hassle. There is no official account to register and no waiting for a customer service reply — you simply search the set number and download. If your set is older or was bought secondhand, these archives are often the only place the drawings still exist, so it is worth checking there even if you plan to try the official route as well.

## Backup Options Outside Official Channels

If the official channels don't have the right drawing for now, there are a few other lines of attack:

- **Reverse-engineer from photos of the finished model**: Use official promo images or builder photos, sort the parts by colour and shape against the parts list, and work back through the structure step by step. This works especially well for sets with simple structures, where the frame and any large sub-assemblies are easy to read from a clear photo.
- **Use the parts list to sort and replace pieces**: Even if you never rebuild the model, you can sort the parts into storage according to the list. That keeps everything accounted for and makes future MOC conversions far easier, because you already know what you own.
- **Ask the brick community**: Post a request on a brick forum or in a builder group chat. Helpful builders are often happy to share the same instructions, and someone may even photograph the relevant pages for you.

## Build the Habit of Archiving Instructions

Rather than scrambling after the manual is lost, it is better to build a good habit from day one: when you open the box, scan or photograph the paper manual into your archive, or download the digital version immediately and save it to cloud storage. A quick photo set taken on a phone is usually enough, as long as each step page is legible. That way, even if the paper copy disappears or gets torn, the drawings stay within reach.
