# Space Station Brick MOC: Build a Modular Orbital Station

Space is one of the most popular themes among brick builders, and a space station MOC sits right at the sweet spot between engineering realism and pure imagination. Compared with a single spacecraft, a full station is big and structurally complicated, which is exactly why many beginners back away from it. The trick is modular design: once you break the build into dockable units, a large station becomes very manageable. This guide walks you through planning and building your own modular station from zero.

## Designing in Modules

Modular thinking is the core philosophy of a space station MOC. Real stations are assembled in orbit from individual modules docked one at a time, and copying that logic helps you in two ways: it makes the parts list easier to plan, and it lets the build progress in stages. Start with a rough sketch on paper and divide the station into major modules — core module, laboratory module, docking nodes and solar arrays. Build and check each module on its own, then join everything at the end. The payoff is simple: if one section disappoints you, you only rebuild that section instead of tearing down the whole model.

## Core Module and Habitation Module

The core module is the hub of the station. It links every other segment together and is usually the thickest cylinder in the build. Start it on a wider baseplate, wrap curved panels around to form the hull, and leave a corridor running straight through the middle to suggest the docking tunnel. The habitation module is where detail work pays off: use transparent pieces for portholes, then build bunks, control consoles and display screens from bricks in contrasting colors — a few small stools go a long way toward making the interior feel lived in. Remember that the more enclosed a hull is, the more effort the inside deserves. A good build holds up when someone looks through the windows.

## Docking Hardware and the Robotic Arm

Docking structure decides whether your modules actually hold together, so standardize it. Pins and hinge pieces used consistently let every segment plug in and pull out quickly. Add a short adapter ring between two segments and the joint immediately reads as a real docking mechanism rather than a plain seam. The robotic arm is the detail that sells the whole model: build a multi-segment arm on articulated joints, anchor one end to the hull and finish the other with a gripper claw. It can be posed in a working position and gives the station a sense of movement. If you have the parts, park a small shuttle hovering alongside — the scene comes alive instantly.

## Solar Arrays and Radiators

Solar panels are the station's power source and its most recognizable visual feature. Use flat plates as the substrate, tile the surface with blue or gold cell arrays, and extend them outward on support booms to both sides. To keep the overall footprint under control, build double-fold arrays that sit stowed and swing open when deployed. Do not skip the radiators. Real stations dump waste heat through large radiator panels, so angle silver-grey or white smooth tiles out from the sides of the hull. This explains the design logic of your station and adds welcome layering to an otherwise flat surface.

## Astronaut Details and Display Lighting

Finally, add the soul of the build. Place a few astronaut minifigures outside the hull holding tools, or show them through the portholes, and the station suddenly has a story. Small touches inside — a miniature plant, a flag — make it feel human. When the model is finished, mount it on a clear display stand and hide an LED strip at the base or along one side so light leaks out between the portholes and the solar panels. The sci-fi mood jumps immediately.

The real pleasure of building a space station MOC is taking an idea apart into individual bricks and then putting it back together as something solid. Build the basic version from this guide first, then add a laboratory module, a satellite or even a space telescope step by step.
