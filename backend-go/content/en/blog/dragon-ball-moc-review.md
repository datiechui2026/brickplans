# Dragon Ball MOC Instructions Review: Goku vs Vegeta Brick Builds

Short verdict first: Dragon Ball MOC instructions are mature on both character likeness and scene design. Character builds sit in the 100–300 yuan range, and anime brick fans will get their money's worth.

Dragon Ball carries a lot of childhood adrenaline for a lot of builders, and recreating a Super Saiyan fight scene in brick is a bucket-list project for many anime fans. In this Dragon Ball MOC instructions review we look at character likeness, scene design and the building experience to work out which of the popular Dragon Ball designs are actually worth buying.

## Character Likeness: The Detail Battle Between Goku and Vegeta

Characters are the soul of a Dragon Ball MOC. A strong instruction set reproduces Goku's spiky hair and Vegeta's cold expression precisely, using nothing but the arrangement of individual pieces. In this review we compared the head build of base-form Goku against his Super Saiyan form: the first sketches the silhouette with curved slopes, while the second needs a large number of gold parts to convey the energy-burst state — a step that noticeably raises the difficulty. Vegeta's armor lives or dies on clean lines, and if the shoulder armor angle is off even slightly, the whole presence of the figure drops.

That contrast is worth understanding before you buy. Base-form Goku is largely a shape problem: rounded slopes, careful layering, and a hair mass built up piece by piece until the spikes read from every angle. Super Saiyan Goku adds a colour problem on top of it, because the gold parts have to stay consistent across the head and torso or the finished figure looks patchy. Vegeta is the opposite kind of challenge — fewer dramatic parts, but far less margin for error, since the armor is all straight edges and sharp transitions.

## Battle Scenes: Kamehameha Effects and Shenron Displays

If characters test modelling skill, scenes test imagination. How the transparent effect parts of a Kamehameha connect to the figure, and how ground-cracking motion is simulated with individual bricks, both say a lot about the designer. The standout in this review was the coiled Shenron scene: the dragon's body is segmented with hinge pieces so it can be posed in several coiled positions, and paired with transparent support columns it delivers serious visual impact.

Effect parts are the hardest thing to get right in a display model, because transparent pieces cannot be locked into a shape the way standard bricks can. Good designs use them sparingly and anchor them to the base so the pose survives being picked up. The same logic applies to the debris under a Kamehameha — small bricks scattered around a broken plate read as movement, while a tidy pattern reads as nothing at all.

## Building Experience and Instruction Quality

From the experience of this Dragon Ball MOC instructions review, instruction quality directly decides how much fun a build is. We checked parts lists, step-by-step diagrams and callouts for special pieces. Most Dragon Ball designs divide their steps sensibly; for the more complex scenes, sorting parts by colour before you start is the sensible move. On price, character instructions usually sit in the 100–300 yuan range, while scene designs cost somewhat more because of the piece count — overall value for money is still reasonable.

A complete parts list matters more than a pretty cover. If a special piece is missing from the inventory, you usually discover it at the worst possible moment, deep into the build, and on a display model the missing piece is often one that carries the pose. Step diagrams and special-piece callouts are the other two things to check before you commit.
