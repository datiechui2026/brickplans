# One Piece Brick Sets Reviewed: Thousand Sunny, Merry and Crew

Any One Piece brick recommendation comes down to two families: Straw Hat crew character sets on one side, and the big ship sets that define the Grand Line era on the other. I have built Thousand Sunny, Merry and the Luffy, Zoro and Chopper character sets, so this hands-on review sorts out accuracy, playability and buying order to give you a checklist you can actually use.

## Thousand Sunny: the strongest ship set

Thousand Sunny is the most instantly recognizable ship in the One Piece brick lineup. The instructions handle the lion-head bow, the red hull and the masts very well, and the lion's mane is built up in layers of orange-red parts so it reads with real presence even from a distance. The hull uses a compartment layout: the deck, the cabin and the gun ports all get interior structure, which means the finished model is as displayable as it is playable. The weak point is that a few of the mast connectors are brittle and pop loose when you move the model, so it is worth locking them down with glue or reinforcement parts once the build is done. Overall the build volume is medium to large, which makes it a good fit for builders with some experience behind them.

## Merry: nostalgia and value

Merry takes the nostalgia route. The sheep-head bow is accurately done, the hull structure is simpler than Sunny's and the part count is lower, with prices usually running from a few dozen yuan to just over 100 yuan, which makes the value hard to beat. Most of the play value sits on the deck and in the cabin, where you can seat minifigures and stage the classic "setting sail" scene. The downside is that the design feels its age: the details lean old-school and the masts and sails are simpler than Sunny's. It suits anyone on a limited budget who still wants an iconic ship on the shelf.

## Straw Hat crew sets: the minifigures are the soul

Minifigure quality decides how collectible a One Piece brick set really is. In the Straw Hat sets, Luffy, Zoro and Sanji each get a dedicated minifigure, and the signature details are all reproduced: the straw hat, the three-sword style, the curly eyebrow. Luffy's rubber arm even has a stretchable structure, which makes him more fun to handle than the average figure. The shortfall is that the female characters' outfit details are relatively simplified, and minor characters such as Helmeppo simply get a generic body, which may leave serious fans unsatisfied. The part count on these sets is small, so a beginner can finish one in a single afternoon.

## Playability versus shelf presence

On playability, the ship sets clearly beat the character sets: cabin doors open, minifigures can be placed inside, and you can pose a whole sailing scene. The character sets win on collecting and display, because lined up together they create that "Straw Hat crew assembles" atmosphere that carries a shelf. One practical point in their favour is that most One Piece brick sets use compatible parts, so they clutch cleanly with LEGO pieces and mixing in bricks you already own is easy.

| Set type | Strength | Weakness | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Thousand Sunny | Accurate lion-head bow, interior structure, strong presence | Brittle mast connectors | Experienced builders |
| Merry | Sheep-head bow, low price, compact part count | Dated details, simple masts and sails | Budget buyers and newcomers |
| Crew character sets | Signature minifigure details, quick build | Simplified female outfits, generic minor characters | Collectors and display |

## Buying advice

A sensible One Piece brick recommendation splits by buyer. New builders should start with Merry or one of the character sets, since both are cheap and quick to finish. Experienced builders can go straight to Thousand Sunny as the flagship of the collection.
