# Doraemon MOC Blueprint Review: Can Round Shapes Be Built?

Doraemon may be one of the hardest anime characters to recreate in brick form. That round blue body, the round head, the round belly — all of it fights against the basic cube. So every time a **Doraemon MOC blueprint** shows up, the same question comes back: can this rounded shape actually be built? After testing several recently, the answer is yes — but it depends heavily on how good the instructions are.

## Roundness: The Soul Is in the Face

The most critical part of any **Doraemon MOC blueprint** is the face. Blueprints that get it right use slopes and rounded plates to shave the silhouette down layer by layer, then place black, white and red pieces precisely to form the eyes, the nose and the signature whiskers. The blueprint I built was careful about the face: curved pieces close off the jawline, so the overall read is no longer boxy — it has that soft bun-shaped cheek look. The blue-and-white color blocking is clean as well, with no odd colors thrown in just to save parts, and the finished model is instantly recognizable on a shelf.

Weaker blueprints give themselves away immediately. The face comes out as a polygonal block head that looks like a robot from a distance and loses the whole goofy charm of the character.

## Build Experience: Detailed Steps, High Repetition

Building this type of blueprint has a particular character: **a lot of repetitive work**. A rounded shape means constantly adding small pieces to fill out curves and lining up each layer, and the same motion repeats dozens of times. The upside is that the step logic is clear and it is hard to make mistakes. The downside is that it takes time, and it asks a certain amount of hand strength — if small pieces are not seated firmly, the curved surface will not come out round.

The instructions break the steps down well. My advice is to press each completed layer flat with the palm of your hand as you go, rather than waiting until the end to reshape everything. Part quality overall is fine: the tolerances on the rounded pieces and slopes are well controlled, and nothing came out noticeably loose.

## Who It Suits

A **Doraemon MOC blueprint** is better suited to builders who care about the display result: you finish it, you put it on a shelf, and you do not necessarily handle it over and over. If this is your first time building a character subject that is not square and blocky, start with a smaller blueprint — just the head or a half-body version — to get familiar with curved construction before taking on the full-body model. It also works well as a parent-and-child build: the steps are simple and easy to follow, kids can hunt down the pieces while adults handle alignment, and the finished model on the shelf feels like a real accomplishment.

## Verdict

Rounded shapes are not impossible to build. What matters is whether the blueprint is willing to put the effort into the face curves and the color blocking. This **Doraemon MOC blueprint** lands evenly across both fidelity and build experience, and it is a worthwhile pick for anyone who likes anime display pieces.
