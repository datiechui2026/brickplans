# Mario Brick Build Guide: Pipes, Blocks, Characters & Levels

Say Mario and the images arrive all at once: the green warp pipe, the gold blocks, the red mushroom, and the small guy in the red cap and overalls. Rebuilding those pixel memories in brick form is what makes Mario building so addictive. This guide walks you from a single pipe to a character, then to a full playable-looking level scene, one step at a time.

## Pipes: Build the Barrel First, Then Close the Mouth

The warp pipe is the signature of the Mario world, and it is also the part of a Mario build where details matter most. Start by ringing curved or half-round pieces into a circular cross-section for the barrel, then wrap narrow plates around the rim to close it off. Pull the opening slightly inward so it reads as a mouth you could actually drop into — that inward lip is what creates the sense of depth.

For a tall pipe, stack several sections and leave a visible seam between each one; the layering shows up immediately. For a pipe lying on its side, decide which way the opening faces before you do anything else, then lock the body into the baseplate. Sort your dark green and light green parts into separate piles before you start — otherwise you will hit the halfway point unable to tell which shade belongs to which layer.

## Blocks: Mushrooms, Question Blocks and Coin Piles

Block-shaped elements are the accents that make a Mario build feel like Mario. For a red-and-white mushroom, use a red hemisphere for the cap, a white round plate plus a short cylinder for the stem, and small light-colored dots for the spots on top. A handful of those and you have a mushroom patch.

The question block lives or dies on being square. Use smooth tiles for the main body, set a printed question-mark piece into the face, and wrap a dark border around all four sides. Placed next to a pipe or at the edge of a platform, it carries exactly the right "hit it and something pops out" feeling. Coins work best as small stacks of yellow slopes or round plates, tucked into corners as easter eggs — the scene comes alive right away.

## Characters: Small Blocks Can Still Look Right

You do not need exotic parts to capture a character; recognizability is enough. For Mario, use a red hemisphere for the brim, red bricks for the crown of the cap, a white round piece for the face, then two small black elements for the eyes and mustache. Blue plates make the overalls. Put that signature color scheme together and he is instantly readable.

Goombas and Koopas follow the same logic. The Goomba gets a red top with white spots on a short body; the Koopa gets a green shell and a round head. Stand them at the edge of a platform and the whole scene picks up its game-like feel.

## Level Scenes: Layer the Baseplate, Stagger the Heights

The heart of a level scene is uneven platforms. Lay out a large baseplate to fix the direction of travel, then stagger platforms at different heights and connect them with slope pieces to suggest the jump path. Stepped brick piles, a small tunnel with a liftable lid, and a corner hiding a star are all crowd-pleasing details.

Ring the edges of the scene with green parts for grass, and use transparent blue pieces to suggest sky or water — the build instantly looks like a frame captured mid-play. Once the level is done, place the pipes, blocks and characters one by one according to the plan, and you have a Mario level you can set on a desk.
