# Where to Find LEGO Building Instructions: 5 Reliable Sources

Every brick builder runs into the same problem sooner or later: the manual is gone, or the second-hand lot you bought arrived with a pile of loose parts and no paperwork. So how do you find LEGO building instructions? There are far more places to look than most people expect. Below are five proven methods, listed in the order we recommend trying them, that will get you a clean, high-resolution PDF fast.

## 1. The Official LEGO Building Instructions Center (Start Here)

LEGO offers free instruction downloads at **lego.com/service/buildinginstructions**. Type the **set number** into the search box — 10265, 21303, anything you like — and you can download the matching PDF directly. The official scans are the sharpest available and cost nothing. This is the most reliable answer to the instruction question: genuine sets are almost always here, you can filter by release year and theme, and you can read the file online on a phone without downloading it first.

## 2. Third-Party Databases: BrickLink and BrickSet

If the official site comes up empty — usually the case with retired or early sets — **BrickLink** (bricklink.com) and **BrickSet** (brickset.com) are the two heavyweight databases. BrickLink links instructions from the "Instructions" tab on each set's page. BrickSet goes further, maintaining a dedicated **instructions index** that covers thousands of historical sets along with their parts lists. When searching, double-check both the set number and the year, because sets that share a name are easy to mix up.

## 3. MOC Communities: BrickPlan and Rebrickable

For **original MOC instructions**, you need a dedicated MOC community. **Rebrickable** (rebrickable.com) is the largest MOC platform in the world and lets you filter builds by the parts you already own, which saves a fortune in orders. **BrickPlan** (brickplan.cn) is the friendlier option for Chinese-language builders, offering high-resolution MOC instruction downloads and step-by-step building tutorials. The community is welcoming and the instructions are quality-checked, so newcomers can jump in without much experience.

## 4. Search Engine Tricks: site: and filetype:pdf

Search engines can pinpoint instructions too, provided you **use the advanced operators properly**. Searching **"set number filetype:pdf"** on Google or Bing filters results down to PDF files only. Combining something like **"set number site:rebrickable.com"** locks the search to a specific site. Add keywords such as "instruction" or "manual" and your hit rate climbs sharply. These tricks are especially useful when hunting instructions for retired sets.

## 5. Video Instructions on Bilibili and YouTube

Video platforms are a valuable backup. On Bilibili, searching a set number together with the local term for "building tutorial" surfaces plenty of creators who film **a complete page-by-page flip-through of the manual** — effectively a moving set of instructions. On YouTube, searching "set number building instructions" turns up a large number of high-definition flip-throughs and 3D build demonstrations. This is the right approach when the official PDF is missing but you still want to see the model actually going together. **Check the comments first** to confirm the video is clear and complete before building along with it.

## Finding Instructions by Set Number After the Manual Is Lost

When the manual disappears, the first job is finding the set number. It is printed on the **side of the box**, on the **small round badge on the instruction booklet cover**, and inside the studs of individual parts. Once you have the number, work through the sources in order: LEGO's official site, then BrickSet, then BrickLink, then search engines. More than 90% of sets can be tracked down this way.

It also pays to **judge scan quality and authenticity**. Instructions from legitimate sources are normally full-page scans with crisp, sharp text. Files that are blurry, missing pages, or covered in multiple watermarks are usually second-hand photos of a manual.

As for **free versus paid MOC instructions**: free files are ideal for practising and warming up, while paid instructions — usually only a few dozen RMB — deliver higher-precision PDFs and far more detailed step-by-step directions, and the purchase supports the creator in making more.

Finding the high-resolution instructions is only step one; the satisfaction comes from the build itself.
