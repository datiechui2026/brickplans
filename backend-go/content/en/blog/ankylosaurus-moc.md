# Ankylosaurus MOC Tutorial: 420-Piece Build With Movable Tail Club

Here is the short version: this ankylosaurus MOC uses about 420 parts, stands 12 cm tall and measures 28 cm from nose to club, and takes roughly 1.5 hours to build. The frame stays rigid and the tail club actually moves, which makes it one of the most impressive pieces a dinosaur fan can put in a display case. brickplan.cn hosts the full instructions, and even a first-time builder can finish it in one sitting.

## A Quick Ankylosaurus Primer: Why It Looks Like That

The ankylosaurus was the Cretaceous period's living tank. It was covered in bony plates and spikes, and its signature weapon was the heavy club at the end of its tail, which in life weighed tens of kilograms. Its back armor was made of many interlocking polygonal scales stacked in overlapping layers like plate armor. When you build an MOC, nail these three features, a low wide body, the tail club, and the back scales, and you are halfway to a convincing model.

## Step 1: Build the Chassis and Legs

Start by laying out a base that is 8 studs wide and 22 studs long using long bricks, then reinforce it with plates so it will not twist. The four legs use 2x2 cylinder bricks as joints; set the front and back legs at different heights to suggest a walking pose. Remember to leave a reinforcing beam inside the belly area, so the model will not sag later when the heavy back armor goes on.

## Step 2: Create the Back Armor Plates

The back is the heart of this build. Alternate 1x2 slope bricks with wedge plates to lay out two rows of scale ridges, then fill the gaps between them with 1x1 curved bricks to form a turtle-shell-like dome. This step uses about 120 parts, making it the most parts-hungry section of the whole ankylosaurus, but the payoff is immediate.

## Step 3: Install the Tail Club

The club is made from two 1x2 bricks with holes clamping a 2x2 round plate, connected to the end of the tail with a technic pin so it can swing up and down. The tail shaft is strung together with 3 ball joints, which keeps it flexible while still holding a pose. Once it is on, give the club a light push and it will rebound like a pendulum, which makes the model genuinely fun to handle.

## Step 4: Head and Finishing Details

Build the head from slope bricks into a flat triangular shape, add two 1x1 round plates for eyes, and angle the mouth slightly upward. Finish by dotting a few silver studs along the edges of the back to suggest bony spikes, then use black plates for the toes. When everything is in place, check that each joint is snug and adjust the tail angle. Your ankylosaurus is done.

The full MOC takes about 1.5 hours of actual building time and around 420 parts, of which roughly 60 are specialized pieces (curved bricks, wedge plates, and technic pins).
