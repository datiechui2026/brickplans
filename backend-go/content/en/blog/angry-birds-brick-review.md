# Angry Birds Brick Set Review: Is Qiji's Licensed Line Worth It?

## Introduction

Angry Birds has been part of global pop culture since Rovio launched the first game in 2009. The red bird with the permanent scowl, the triangular yellow one, and those endlessly punchable green pigs have all become instantly recognisable symbols.

So what happens when the IP meets brick building? In this review, BrickPlan takes a close look at Qiji's Angry Birds sets, judging them on part quality, the building experience, how faithfully the game is reproduced, and whether the price makes sense.

## Set Overview

The main Angry Birds brick sets on the market come from a few sources: Qiji, LEGO, and a number of domestic compatible brands. Qiji, one of the better-known Chinese brick makers, secured an official licence from Rovio around 2020 and released several Angry Birds ranges.

The line we are reviewing here is Qiji's **Classic Levels series**, which covers several signature scenes: the pig castle, the slingshot launch platform, and the bird nest. Individual sets run from 200 to 800 pieces and are aimed at builders aged 6 and up, as well as adult collectors.

## Part Quality: A Deep Dive

### 1. Brick Precision and Clutch Power

Qiji's Angry Birds parts are a genuine surprise on the precision front. Clutch power sits in the sweet spot — connections are not so loose that a piece falls off at a touch, and not so tight that you need your teeth to separate them. Against other domestic bricks at the same price, Qiji's moulding sits in the top tier, roughly 90% of the way to LEGO.

One small detail worth knowing: some darker parts (dark grey, black) feel tight when you assemble and disassemble them, while lighter colours (white, light yellow) sit a little looser. This varies slightly between production batches, but it does not affect the build overall.

### 2. Plastic Material and Feel

Parts are made from ABS, with smooth surfaces, no flash or burrs, and no noticeable smell. That matters for a children's toy — plenty of parents worry most about formaldehyde in cheap plastic. Qiji handles this well, and the product carries 3C certification, so it is safe to hand to kids.

### 3. Colour Accuracy

The colours are bright and consistent. The red bird's true red and the pigs' signature green both land where they should. There is no visible colour variation or scratching across bricks, and printed elements such as the eye and mouth expression pieces are sharp and clean, with no ghosting or misregistration.

## Build Experience: From Unboxing to Completion

### Packaging and Instructions

The box is well designed, with a render of the finished set on the front and the play features shown on the back. If you keep packaging, the box itself displays nicely.

The instructions are printed in full colour with clear steps, and part callouts and step diagrams are labelled properly. A beginner can work through them without extra help. One caveat: some steps combine several sub-steps into a single diagram, so first-time builders need to look carefully.

### The Build Process

A full build takes roughly 1 to 3 hours, depending on the size of the set and the builder's experience.

Take the **Pig Castle** set as an example. The build breaks into several stages:

**Stage one: the base.** A large baseplate forms the foundation, then the castle footing and perimeter walls go on. This part is straightforward and good to do together with a child.

**Stage two: the main structure.** The castle's core goes up here — towers, walls and the pig rooms inside. Alignment needs care at this stage, or the roof will end up crooked later.

**Stage three: details and decoration.** This covers the pig flags, window bars and, most importantly, the slingshot launcher.

**Stage four: characters and staging.** The birds and pigs are placed into the scene to finish the display.

The highlight of the whole build is the slingshot mechanism — once assembled, it really does fire a bird across the room and into the pig castle. That build-and-play approach is far more engaging than a purely static display model.

## IP Accuracy: How Much Nostalgia Is It Worth?

### Character Models

Let's get to the point: **the character likenesses are very strong.**

Red's signature unibrow and angry expression, Chuck's triangular silhouette, and Bomb's rounded black body all come across well in brick form. Most of these character parts use dedicated moulds rather than standard brick assembly, which is why the shapes land so accurately.

The green pigs are just as good — round bodies plus the unmistakable snout make them instantly readable.

### Scene Accuracy

The game's classic level elements are all reproduced: the slingshot launch, the pig fortress, wooden crates and stone walls. Crucially, the slingshot — the core mechanic — is preserved, which is the detail longtime players will appreciate most.

### Trade-offs vs. the Game

Naturally, a brick medium cannot replicate the game's physics, such as parabolic trajectories or explosion effects. Qiji works around this with clever mechanical design — a spring-loaded launcher, knock-down walls — to simulate the interactive feel as far as possible. For a brick product, that is a solid result.

## Value for Money

In the Classic Levels series, sets generally cost between 50 and 200 RMB, which works out at roughly 0.2 to 0.3 RMB per piece. That is mid-range for domestic bricks, and the value is reasonable.

Bear in mind this is an officially licensed product, and licensing fees add cost, so the pricing is fair. LEGO's own Angry Birds sets are long discontinued, and secondhand prices can run several times higher.

## Who Is It For?

**★★★★★ Angry Birds fans**: if you played the game seriously, the nostalgia value here is hard to beat.

**★★★★☆ Parents**: kids aged 6 to 12 are a great fit. The difficulty is moderate, the characters are appealing, and the launching play gives it more pull than a static model.

**★★★☆☆ Brick collectors**: it is not the hard currency that LEGO is, but a well-made, officially licensed domestic set still looks good on a shelf.

**★★☆☆☆ Builders chasing complex techniques**: MOC specialists and fans of intricate construction will find these sets on the simple side. They are better suited to relaxed building.

## Verdict and Buying Advice

### Pros

- ✅ Officially licensed, with highly accurate characters
- ✅ Good part quality and a comfortable building feel
- ✅ The slingshot mechanism adds real play value
- ✅ Sensible pricing and solid value
- ✅ Great for parent-and-child building

### Cons

- ❌ Inconsistent tightness across some colours
- ❌ Instruction steps are occasionally unclear
- ❌ Larger sets have limited working features
- ❌ Special character moulds are hard to reuse with other bricks

### Buying Advice

If this is your first Angry Birds brick set, start with the basic **slingshot set** (around 80 RMB) to get a feel for the build quality and play value. If you like it, move up to the larger **Pig Castle** (around 180 RMB) for a richer scene and more to do.

Collectors should keep an eye on Qiji's **limited edition sets**, which usually include exclusive characters or special scenes and hold some collectible value.

## Final Thoughts

Qiji's Angry Birds sets are a genuinely heartfelt piece of IP merchandise. They do the licence justice, and they give longtime players a new way to revisit an old memory through bricks. For kids it is simply a fun toy; for adults it is a small piece of nostalgia.

If the Angry Birds sets appeal to you, they are worth trying firsthand. It is never too late to enjoy a build.
