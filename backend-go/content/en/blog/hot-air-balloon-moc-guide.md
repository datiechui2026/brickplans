# How to Build a LEGO Hot Air Balloon MOC: Sphere, Basket, Ropes

Here is the short version: a hot air balloon MOC succeeds or fails on the curve of the envelope. Build it with a meridian-and-parallel method — 1x2 bricks running vertically as meridians, curved bricks laid horizontally as parallels to close each ring — and the surface comes out more than 30% smoother than simply stacking round plates. The basket and the ropes, by comparison, are the easy part.

## Step 1: Build the sphere frame

Take 8 1x2 bricks and use them as meridians, radiating out from the center of a 1x1 round plate at the top, spaced roughly 45 degrees apart. Start each meridian with 3 sections of 1x2 brick, then extend outward to 4 sections so the frame forms a narrow-top, wide-bottom funnel. Keep the maximum diameter of the envelope at 16 studs.

## Step 2: Fill in the surface

Working between adjacent meridians, insert 2x4 curved bricks horizontally. Begin with the first ring near the top and add 2 pieces per ring, for 7 rings in total. The widest ring takes 8 2x4 curved bricks to close the circle; above that, reduce by 1 brick per ring, then cap the top with 1x1 slope bricks to form a smooth dome.

## Step 3: Assemble the basket

Build the basket on a 4x4 stud baseplate. Stack 1x2 brown bricks four layers high for the walls, roughly 5 studs tall. At each of the four corners, fit a 1x1 turntable piece so the navigation lamps can rotate. Add 2 1x2 plates underneath as reinforcing crossbeams — this raises load capacity by about 40%.

## Step 4: Connect the ropes

Use 4 hose pieces, each 16 sections long, as the main ropes. Run them down from four diagonal points at the base of the envelope and clip them to the four corners of the basket with 1x1 round plates. Add 4 short 8-section ropes in between as cross bracing, creating a stable triangular suspension that keeps the swing within 10 degrees.

## Step 5: Final check and decoration

Set the envelope directly above the basket and confirm that all 4 main ropes are the same length — no more than 1 section of difference. Finish by applying 6 2x2 printed tiles to the envelope surface as colored stripes, and place 1 2x2 minifigure base inside the basket. You now have a mini hot air balloon you can carry around by hand.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q: The sphere keeps bulging halfway through the build and the curve looks wrong. What should I do?**

A: Bulging means you have used too many curved bricks. Remove the ring that bulges most and rebuild it with 1x2 plates underneath as a spacer. Sight along the side of the model after every completed ring and correct as you go — this saves about half the time compared with finishing the whole envelope and then tearing it back down.

**Q: The ropes won't hang properly because the hose pieces are too stiff. Any fix?**

A: Swap the hoses for a chain of 1x1 round bars, or butt-join two 8-section hoses with a 1x1 turntable piece in the middle to create a joint. brickplan hose pieces become more pliable when warmed, but they set again as they cool, so watch the heating time.

**Q: The balloon won't stand up and falls over at the slightest touch.**

A: A high center of gravity is the usual cause. Add 4 2x2 flat plates inside the bottom of the basket as counterweight, or raise the basket walls by 1 layer. To test, push the envelope gently with your hand: if it rights itself, the center of gravity is acceptable; if it keeps rocking for more than 3 seconds, you need more weight low down.

**Q: I want to build a bigger balloon. How do I scale it?**

A: Going from 16 studs in diameter to 24 studs means increasing the meridians from 8 to 12 and the curved-brick rings from 7 to 9. Remember that the surface area of a sphere is roughly 3.14 times the diameter squared; estimating your parts needs from that formula will keep you close.
