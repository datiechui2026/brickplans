# Train Station MOC Tutorial: Platforms, Tracks and Station Clock

After more than ten years of building, I've done plenty of modular street scenes, but train stations remain the subject I keep coming back to. They carry the weight of a real building and the motion of a railway at the same time, and that contrast photographs beautifully. This **train station MOC tutorial** collects the design points I've tested over and over, from the platform and waiting hall through the tracks and footbridge to the signature station clock, so you can build something that is both stable and good-looking.

## Space Planning: Lay Out the Station First

Before you touch a single part, the layout matters more than the inventory. I split the whole scene into three layers running front to back: the forecourt and waiting hall entrance up front, the platform and rails in the middle, and the secondary platform plus footbridge closing things out at the rear. Lay at least two tracks side by side and leave the platform width between them, with the platform surface sitting slightly above rail height so trains and minifigure passengers can be placed later. Fix your baseplate size first, mark every functional zone on a 1:1 top-down plan, and only then start laying bricks — the whole build stays organised that way.

## The Waiting Hall and Platform: The Visual Core

The waiting hall is the visual centre of the station, and I'd build it as a double-height structure. Use large glass windows on the front facade with either a pitched or a flat roof to get that airy feel of an old terminus. The ground floor holds the ticket hall and the entrance gates; the upper level becomes a waiting gallery with a few rows of benches and a wall clock inside. The platform runs tight against the front of the hall, surfaced with light grey tiles, edged with dark bricks along the platform line, and roofed over by columns topped with capitals. Watch the seam where the platform meets the waiting hall — if it isn't tight, the canopy will lift when you press it down.

## Tracks and Train: Bringing the Scene to Life

The trackwork is the centrepiece of any train station MOC tutorial. My approach: lay two runs of grey sleeper bricks across, evenly spaced, then mount silver rail pieces on top, leaving coupling points at both ends so you can extend the layout later or run a train in and out. When you lay track, make absolutely sure the two runs stay parallel and identically spaced, or the train's wheelsets won't line up with the rails. For the waiting train I build a modular locomotive plus coaches, with a working wheel set and a cowcatcher on the loco; parked at the platform edge with a few minifigure passengers, the scene instantly gains a story.

## Footbridge and Secondary Platform: Building Depth

If you want the station to stop looking flat, the footbridge is the detail that does it. I span a bridge across the tracks at the middle of the platform: beam pieces form the skeleton, plates surface the deck, railings run down both sides, and a staircase at each end drops down to platform level. Beyond the looks, the bridge ties the upper and lower levels of the space together, which enriches the visual layering immediately. At the far end of the secondary platform, add a few signal light posts and station signs picked out in light green or dark green — the cool-warm contrast against the grey architecture makes the whole scene read in three dimensions.

## The Station Clock and Finishing Details

A station with character always has a signature clock. On the top of the waiting hall facade I build a four-faced clock: white round plates for the faces, black hands, and a clock tower rising half a storey above the main building so it becomes the highest point of the entire scene. Then fill in the life — benches outside the waiting hall, trash bins, a small fountain on the forecourt — layer by layer.
