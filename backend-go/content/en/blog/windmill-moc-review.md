# Brick Windmill MOC Review: Blades, Gears and Build Difficulty

Among the many subjects builders choose for a MOC, the **brick windmill** has stayed consistently popular. It offers a romantic, pastoral look on the outside and a precisely engineered transmission inside, which makes it a textbook case of looks and mechanics working together. This review looks at the model from three angles: design, mechanism, and difficulty.

## Design Highlights: More Than Just Good Looks

What lands first with this windmill MOC is how faithfully it captures Dutch countryside character. The build uses red brick walls paired with a dark wood-toned tower, and the pointed roof sits in well-judged proportion to the four oversized blades. Inside the tower there are openable viewing windows and staircase details, so even with the blades standing still it works as a display piece in its own right.

The builder was clearly deliberate about colour and part selection, leaning heavily on uncommon corner pieces and slope pieces. The result is a tower with smooth, rounded lines, avoiding the heavy blocky feel that many MOCs fall into. The model runs to roughly two thousand pieces and stands around forty centimetres tall, which gives it real presence on a desk or in a display cabinet.

## The Mechanism: Where the Blades Come Alive

The heart of any windmill MOC is the set of blades that actually turns. This one uses a gear reduction arrangement: turn the handle and it drives the main shaft, which brings the four blades around in a slow, smooth rotation with almost no catching or stutter. The cleverer touch is a linked assembly hidden inside the tower — as the blades turn, the millstone inside rotates in step, recreating the motion of grinding grain.

The mechanism is not complicated, but it earns its keep through clear logic and high reliability. It holds up to repeated handling without working loose, and spinning it a couple of turns while it sits on the shelf delivers a quiet mechanical satisfaction. That is exactly what separates a windmill MOC from a purely static model.

## Build Difficulty: A Sweet Challenge for Advanced Builders

Judged on part count and structural complexity, this windmill MOC sits at the **intermediate to advanced** level. The gears and transmission call for some building experience, and several steps require you to line up axle holes correctly — misalign one and the whole thing may refuse to turn. That said, the instructions break the build into fine-grained steps, each with a clear parts list and viewing angle, so following them is not hard in itself.

It suits builders with a foundation who have already completed a few mid-sized sets. If you are a complete beginner, start with a smaller windmill of the same type first, then come back to this one — the experience will be much smoother.
