# Brick Storage Boxes: How to Sort and Store Your Parts

The most frustrating part of brick building usually isn't the build itself — it's the parts scattered across the floor. Whether you're working on a modular street, a mech or a vehicle MOC, a thousand-plus pieces end up mixed together and finding a single 1x1 brick can mean half an hour of digging through bins. This brick storage box guide walks through the main product types, what to check before buying, and how to plan a setup at each budget level, so sorting stops being a chore and building starts on a good note.

## Common types of brick storage boxes

Storage products on the market fall roughly into four categories by use case:

- **Compartment boxes (sorting cases)**: plastic boxes with multiple adjustable dividers, ideal for a first pass by color or part type. The default choice for beginners.
- **Drawer-style part cabinets**: stacks of transparent drawers that can be layered and labeled, suited to larger collections stored long-term by broad part category.
- **Sealed carry cases / tool boxes**: fitted with a handle and latches, easy to move around — good for offline meetups or building away from home.
- **Individual small trays (grid trays)**: each compartment lifts out on its own, handy for staging parts by build step and returning them afterwards.

## Choosing by part count and part size

The biggest mistake when buying storage is grabbing the largest size available without thinking. A better approach is to count roughly what you own, then decide how to combine containers:

- **Under 2,000 parts**: two or three compartment boxes with around 20 cells each is plenty. Favor models with removable dividers so you can resize cells later.
- **2,000 to 10,000 parts**: combine compartment boxes with a drawer cabinet. Use drawers for broad categories — plates, bricks, minifigure parts, technic axles — and keep compartment boxes for the small, high-frequency pieces.
- **Over 10,000 parts, or MOC-focused**: go straight to a large multi-tier part cabinet, and add small grid trays for temporary sorting while building. That combination is the most efficient.

Also check cell depth. Small pieces like 1x1 and 1x2 bricks need shallow, densely packed cells, while baseplates, wheels and technic axles need deep cells or large drawers — otherwise they either don't fit or are impossible to dig through.

## Planning your spend by budget tier

If you don't know where to start, use these tiers to plan the investment:

- **Entry tier (under CNY 50)**: a few transparent compartment boxes plus a pair of sorting tweezers covers early-stage organization.
- **Intermediate tier (CNY 100–300)**: a drawer-style part cabinet with a dust cover, plus label paper or a label maker. The difference in tidiness is immediately visible.
- **Advanced tier (CNY 300 and up)**: consider storage racks with LED lighting and adjustable tilt, combined with shelving to form a complete work station. Aimed at heavy users.

Pay attention to materials when buying: thicker PP plastic is more flexible and resists cracking, and a sealing gasket keeps out dust and moisture — especially important for builders in humid southern regions. Transparent bodies let you identify contents at a glance and save a lot of searching time.

## Sorting and organizing tips

Even the best boxes only pay off with the right habits. A few practical techniques:

1. **Broad categories first, then color**: split into bricks, plates, axles, minifigure parts and so on, then subdivide each category by color. Don't start with color or you'll drown in it.
2. **Keep high-frequency parts separate**: put the most-used pieces — 2x4 bricks, 1x1 bricks — in a small grid tray within reach, and store the rest properly.
3. **Labels on the outside, not the inside**: stick labels on the front of drawers, using numbers or pictures instead of text so both sorting and finding are more intuitive.
4. **Do a regular part audit**: after disassembling a set or finishing a MOC, put the leftover parts back in place so clutter never accumulates.
