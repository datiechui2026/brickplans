# MOC Bridge Review: Stone Arch vs Cable-Stayed Builds

Short answer first: among the brick MOC bridge plans on brickplan, the stone arch bridge and the cable-stayed bridge are the two worth your time. The arch build runs 1,800 pieces, takes about 8 hours, and measures 40 cm long when finished. The cable-stayed version runs 2,600 pieces, takes about 12 hours, and its tower stands 35 cm tall. Both handled load far better than expected.

## 1. Exterior Design

The stone arch bridge uses dark grey bricks to suggest a blue-grey cut-stone finish. The arch opening curves smoothly, and the details hold up close in — bridge-deck railings and small stone lion figures included. The cable-stayed bridge goes the other way: a white tower paired with silver cables for a clean, modern silhouette.

Both plans include a water base, and with blue transparent elements laid in, the scene photographs extremely well. This is the kind of build that looks good in a photo the moment you finish it.

## 2. Structure and Load Capacity

The thing that ruins a bridge MOC is collapse. The stone arch handles it with wedge-shaped bricks keyed into the arch ring; in testing it carried a 3 kg weight. The cable-stayed bridge reinforces the inside of its tower with cross axles, and the cables use fishing line instead of standard elements, so tension is adjustable. A small car model sitting on the deck is no problem at all — this is a model you can display and handle without worrying.

## 3. Build Difficulty

The stone arch sits at the entry-to-intermediate level. The arch ring itself has to be built in strict instruction order, while the rest is mostly repetitive work. The cable-stayed bridge demands patience during cable tensioning — work through the brickplan instructions step by step rather than tightening everything at once. Beginners should budget roughly 2 extra hours to get comfortable with the process, and the finished result feels like a real achievement.

## 4. Scene Pairing

brickplan also carries matching small-scene plans — trees, a rowboat, pedestrian figures — that combine with the bridges into a diorama or a shelf display. A finished bridge is 40 to 50 cm long, so measure your display space before you start; a lit base makes it look better still.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I find brick MOC bridge plans?**

A: Search "bridge" on brickplan and you will see arch bridges, cable-stayed bridges, covered bridges and more. Each listing states its part count and difficulty, so check the preview images before deciding which one to download. The filter tools make it quick.

**Q: Can a finished brick MOC bridge actually bear weight?**

A: Yes. The stone arch bridge carried 3 kg in testing, and the cable-stayed bridge, with its cables tensioned, holds small car models steadily. As long as you reinforce the key joints as the plan specifies, both display and handling are fine — the load performance is stable.

**Q: What does it cost to build a brick MOC bridge?**

A: The 1,800-piece stone arch bridge comes to about 300 yuan in parts; the 2,600-piece cable-stayed bridge about 450 yuan. Add the plan cost and you are still under 500 yuan — close to half the price of a finished scenery set. Good value, and affordable on a student budget.

**Q: How is a MOC bridge different from an ordinary scenery set?**

A: The MOC structure is more realistic. The arch ring, the cables and the load paths are engineered to hold up to scrutiny, and you can freely change the dimensions and colour scheme. Ordinary scenery sets lean decorative — most have decks that cannot carry weight, so there is far less to play with.
