# Brick Supermarket MOC Guide: Storefront, Shelves & Checkout

If any building in a street-scene layout feels most lived-in, it is the supermarket. More than a grand shopping mall or a quiet coffee shop, a brick supermarket MOC — crammed with shelves, queuing minifigures and glowing checkout screens — captures everyday street life better than anything else. This guide walks you through the storefront, the shelving and the checkout details in order, so you can build a miniature shop that feels genuinely open for business.

## 1. Start From the Street: Fix the Supermarket's Overall Size

Before you pick up a single brick, decide what role this store plays in your street scene. Is it a freestanding big-box store, or the common corner-shop format — a sales floor at street level with housing above? That decision drives the building's width, floor height and number of bays. At street-scene scale, a standard supermarket works best at roughly four to six studs wide and one to two storeys tall: enough room for rich interior detail without stealing the show from the buildings next door.

Once the massing is settled, map the facade onto the interior. Full-height glass display windows should line up with the checkout or deli area, the side door with the staff corridor, and the window display positions with promotional dump bins. When function and appearance correspond one-to-one, the MOC reads as coherent inside and out.

## 2. Storefront Design: Win the First Glance

A supermarket facade lives on two things: transparency and buzz. Large clear window elements are essential, paired with a shop sign and awning above them, and the commercial street feel appears instantly. Build the sign as a printed piece or lettered bricks hung in the centre of the fascia; make the awning from striped or sloped pieces, with the classic red-and-white pairing being the most recognisable.

The finishing touch is the window display. Promotional posters, shopping carts and fruit baskets piled into a small mountain can all be built quickly from common parts. Add a few carts and minifigures at the entrance — one customer pushing a cart through the door — and the picture immediately comes alive. The richer the storefront, the more energy the whole street gains.

## 3. Shelf Displays: Build the "Fully Stocked" Look

Shelving is the heart of a brick supermarket MOC and the part that most rewards patience. Build several rows of shelves from matching shelf plates, three to four tiers per run, and use small coloured plates, transparent pieces or printed elements to stand in for goods. Cracker boxes, drinks bottles, bread bags, cans — mixing small parts of different colours and shapes creates the visual illusion of a well-stocked store.

There is a technique to the arrangement, too. Wall-hugging shelves can run taller and act as a backdrop, while the centre of the floor takes low shelving and promotional dump bins, giving the space a stepped, layered feel. Don't neglect the produce section, which carries the colour: pile red, green and orange small pieces into melons and fruit, add a small weighing scale, and place a minifigure customer choosing beside it to complete the picture.

## 4. The Checkout Counter and Detail Touches

The checkout is the busiest corner of any supermarket and deserves careful work. A complete checkout area usually includes the counter top, a cash register, a scanning station, a bag holder and storage cabinets behind. Build the register from a small tile piece combined with a display element to suggest screen and keypad, and top it with a small "scanned" screen piece — the more specific the detail, the more convincing it is.

A queue is the best tool for adding story. Place two or three minifigures in front of the counter: one paying, one looking down at a phone, another waiting with a full cart. That everyday flow of people makes the brick supermarket MOC feel instantly alive. Finish with environment details — refrigerated cabinets, freezer doors, fire exit signage and ceiling light fixtures — and the atmosphere is complete.

## 5. Beginner Tips and a Parts List

If you are new to supermarket MOCs, start small: a single shelf run plus a checkout vignette. Once you are comfortable stacking small parts and balancing colours, expand into a whole store. Put extra thought into the windows and the sign, because they form the first impression of the facade; fill the interior in priority order — shelving, checkout, cold storage, then props.

On the parts side: stock plenty of transparent window elements and glass door pieces for the facade; assorted small plates, round bricks and curved pieces as merchandise; standard bricks and plates for shelving and walls; printed elements (price tags, promo stickers, logos) for accents; plus a handful of cash registers, shopping carts and minifigures. Sorting the merchandise parts into boxes by colour will speed up the build considerably.

Get the storefront, shelving and checkout right, then invest in the small lived-in details, and your brick supermarket MOC becomes the busiest corner of the whole street scene — whether it sits on a display table or on your desk, it carries that everyday bustle with it.
