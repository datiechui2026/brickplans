# Brick Pet Shop MOC: Cages, Pets and Storefront Guide

A pet shop is the most charming building on any modular street: cages, animals, a signboard — point the camera anywhere and there is a story. This guide walks through cage structure, pet elements and storefront signage so you can put a corner pet shop on your street that is both cute and convincing.

## 1. Choosing a Plan and Planning the Layout

### Plan and Footprint

When you choose a brick pet shop MOC plan, aim for 1600-2200 pieces, a 16x16 stud footprint and a two-storey structure. The ground floor is the display area (cages, pets, checkout); the upper floor is the grooming room. That is a lot of function on a simple frame, which makes it a forgiving first project.

### Parts List

For the main body you need roughly 600 white and off-white bricks and plates, about 80 trans plates and trans window frames (cages eat a lot of glass), around 100 orange and brown pieces for accents, assorted pet elements (cats, dogs, hamsters and so on), and a handful of printed elements for the store sign, price tags and display boards.

## 2. Pet Cages: Glass Doors and Structure

### Cage Body

The cages are the heart of the shop. Line one wall with four cages in a row, each one a 4x4 space framed with white elements, and fit a transparent door to the front (a trans plate plus a 1x2 door frame element). Build the hinge from a 1x2 plate with hole and a pin, so the door really swings open and shut. Drop a light grey plate into each cage floor to suggest a tray — it makes the cage look easy to clean.

### Stacked Cages

For extra realism, go two levels: a large dog cage (5x4 space) on the bottom and a smaller cat cage (3x4 space) above, divided by a plate. Leave ventilation gaps along the top of each cage by spacing out 1x1 plates; visually it reads as airflow. Add a printed price tag beside every cage door with the breed and the price, and the pet shop feeling arrives immediately.

## 3. Pet Elements: Simple to Advanced

### Quick Pet Builds

Pets can be built from existing small elements. A cat is a 2x4 car-body element plus a white head element and triangular ear elements; a dog is a brown 2x4 brick with a dark brown head element and a round nose element. The hamster is the easiest of all — a 1x1 round element with two half-round ear elements. Each one takes a minute or two, so build a crowd of them and fill the cages; the shop is instantly busy.

### Advanced Pet Scenes

At the advanced end, add pet supplies inside the cages. A cat tree is 1x1 post elements with three stacked plates; a food bowl is a 1x2 shallow dish element with a small brown round element; a toy ball is a coloured 1x1 round element. These are tiny details, but they push the minifigure-and-pet interaction as far as it will go.

## 4. Storefront Design and Signage

The storefront is where the looks come from. Make the sign three-dimensional: spell out "PET" in white plates, or build a cartoon dog head (white head element, round nose element, floppy ear elements) and mount it above the door — far more recognisable than a flat printed tile. Put a large display window on each side of the door and fill them with pet toys (coloured round elements and ball elements) and pet food bags (printed elements); passers-by will want to walk in. Keep the palette white plus orange for a warm, lively feel: orange on the door frame, the awning and the sign, a clean contrast against a white-and-green pharmacy next door.

## 5. Interior Details and Minifigure Interaction

The interaction points are what make this brick pet shop MOC work. Put a cash register on the counter with the shop owner minifigure standing beside it (apron and walkie-talkie). On the counter, add a pet weighing station — a white tile plus a printed scale tile — where a cat can sit and be weighed. Stand an adoption information board by the door, or park a pet stroller (a 2x4 plate plus four small wheel elements) outside. Add warm yellow lights and they glow through the cage glass onto the animals, giving a late-night street scene that is pure comfort. ## 6. Common Problems and Reinforcement

With so many glass cage doors opening all day, hinges work loose: double up at the hinge with a 1x1 element with hole plus a pin. Push the divider plates in the stacked cages all the way down so the "pets" upstairs cannot drop through. White exterior walls are a large blank surface, so run an orange belt line across them to break up the monotony. When you are finished, check the front of the build at three points — display windows, sign and cages — and your pet shop is done.
