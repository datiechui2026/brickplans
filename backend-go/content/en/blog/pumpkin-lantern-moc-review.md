# Halloween Pumpkin Lantern MOC Review: 680 Parts, LED Glow

The short version: this Halloween pumpkin lantern MOC lands at roughly 680 pieces, about 14 cm across and 12 cm tall, for around 120 yuan. Build time is roughly 3 hours, and there are 2 LED light units inside. Overall score: 8.5/10. The shape reads clearly as a pumpkin, the lighting works well, and the holiday mood is strong, which makes it good value as a Halloween desk piece. The instructions come from brickplan.cn, and the package also includes bonus builds for a bat, a spider and a small graveyard scene.

## Shape: The Pumpkin Gets Its Roundness Right

The shell is built from orange curved pieces and slope pieces arranged into the classic ribbed pumpkin silhouette, 12 ribs in total. A green stem sits on top, and 4 small support feet are added underneath. Two sets of printed face tiles are included and can be swapped: a smiling face and a spooky face. The eyes and mouth are cut-outs rather than solid tiles, so light can pass through. Shape score: 9/10. The only small letdown is a slight visible seam where the ribs meet.

## Light Units: The Soul of the Build in Low Light

There are 2 LED light units inside. One sits within the pumpkin body; the second is built into a small hand lantern hanging alongside. The light is warm yellow leaning orange, and once it filters through the orange bricks the colour reads exactly right. In testing under low light, the glow through the cut-out eyes and mouth is very obvious and the face outline stays readable. The downside: the battery compartment is in the base, so changing batteries means lifting the whole base off. Lighting score: 9/10.

## Festive Atmosphere: One Set Covers a Whole Tabletop Scene

In the box you also get a bat, a spider web, a tombstone, two little ghost minifigures and a fence baseplate about 20 cm long. Set them out together and you have a complete little Halloween scene. The lantern can be displayed on its own or combined with the extras, so the festive score comes in at 8.5/10. If you want a bigger layout, build two or three of the same model and stagger them at different heights.

All told, this pumpkin lantern MOC suits builders who want a Halloween tabletop display without spending much, and the light units are the biggest plus. If holiday themes are your thing, you can also search brickplan.cn for the Christmas tree and Easter egg instructions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is the light on this pumpkin lantern MOC actually bright?**

A: Yes, quite bright. Both LED units are warm yellow leaning orange, and in low light the glow through the cut-out face is clearly visible — you can make out the smiling outline. It is hard to see in daylight, so it looks best at night with the room lights off, when the mood really lands.

**Q: How long does the build take, and is it good to do with a child?**

A: About 680 pieces and roughly 3 hours of building, with a low difficulty level that suits kids aged 8 and up working alongside a parent. The parts are mostly large pieces with nothing tiny and easy to lose, and the step-by-step brickplan instructions flow smoothly.

**Q: Is 120 yuan worth it?**

A: Yes. For 680 pieces including 2 LED light units and 6 scene accessories, that works out at about 0.17 yuan per piece. Halloween-themed parts usually carry a premium, and this set sits in the budget range. A finished display piece in the same shape would cost 60 to 70 yuan on its own, and building it yourself also lets you swap the faces.

**Q: Can the face be changed, and can the light colour change?**

A: The face can be changed — the smiling and spooky printed tiles both use a clip design, so you can swap them whenever you like. The light colour is fixed at warm yellow leaning orange and cannot be changed, but you could add a cool white LED yourself for a two-tone effect.

**Q: Can the lantern go outdoors?**

A: Long-term outdoor display is not recommended. The bricks themselves are not bothered by water, but the LED battery compartment is not waterproof and rain can short it out. A balcony or a sheltered spot under the eaves at the front door is fine — just bring it in when it rains. Indoors on a desk is the safest option day to day.
