# Qiji MOC Instructions Review: Budget Chinese Brick Builds

The domestic Chinese brick market has exploded in recent years, and **Qiji** has built a solid reputation among MOC builders on the strength of friendly pricing and steadily improving quality. This review takes a professional look at what Qiji MOC instructions actually deliver.

## Product Overview

Qiji's MOC instructions focus on two main categories: **technic mechanical structures** and **military vehicles**, with part counts typically falling between **800 and 3,000 pcs**. Compared with official LEGO instructions, Qiji's MOC designs lean pragmatic — compact structures, high part efficiency, and very little of the padding you see when a design is stretched to hit a part-count target. You can find plenty of Qiji-style MOC instruction resources on [BrickPlans](https://brickplan.cn), covering difficulty levels from beginner through advanced.

## Build Difficulty

**Difficulty rating: ★★★☆☆ (moderate)**

Qiji's assembly logic is clear and the steps are divided sensibly. For anyone with six months or more of building experience, following the instructions should be smooth with few sticking points. That said, some technic models demand patience when installing the gear train — especially when several gears have to mesh and their angles need adjusting. Take your time and align each step with the callouts in the diagram.

## Part Quality

Qiji's part quality control has improved noticeably over the past two years:

- **Tolerance control**: Good overall, with moderate clutch power. Compared with first-tier brands such as Gobricks, roughly 5% of parts sit slightly loose or slightly tight, but this does not affect overall structural stability.
- **Sprue marks**: On the vast majority of parts the sprue gate is flat and clean. Occasional small burrs show up and can be scraped off with a fingernail.
- **Color consistency**: Color matching within the same batch is excellent. Dark parts — particularly dark grey and black — show slight variation between batches, which is normal at this price point.
- **Special elements**: Qiji's technic cross axles and gears are solidly made, with durability that holds up well.

## Building Experience

A full build takes roughly **4-8 hours**, depending on the part count of the model. Print quality on the instructions is sharp and the arrow callouts in the step diagrams are clear, though some of the perspective views for complex angles could be refined further. One real highlight is Qiji's **sensible part bagging**: bags are packed in build order, which cuts down part-hunting time considerably. For a product positioned on value, that is a strong experience.

## Finished Model

Structural rigidity on the completed build is excellent — normal handling won't produce loose or falling parts. On technic models the moving components run smoothly, and once the gear train has been carefully adjusted it performs as intended. On appearance, Qiji's MOC designs hold up well on detail accuracy; some curved transitions are less smooth than LEGO's, but given the price gap the value is outstanding.
