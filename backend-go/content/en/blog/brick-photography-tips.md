# Brick Photography Tips: Shoot Pro-Looking MOCs With a Phone

Here is the short version: brick photography does not require expensive gear. Master these five steps — soft diffused lighting, a solid-color background, a 45-degree low angle, macro close-ups and light post-processing — and a phone will get you about 90% of the way to a professional look. Beginners should lock ISO at 400 or lower and keep the shutter at 1/60s or faster. A cheap reflector board costing a few dozen yuan will do more for your photos than swapping camera bodies.

## Step 1: Lighting — Soft Beats Strong

Brick surfaces are covered in studs, and hard direct light produces harsh glare and ugly shadows. The safest approach: put your build next to a window and diffuse the sunlight through a sheet of white A4 paper or tracing paper. Place a white foam board on the opposite side as a fill light and the shadows soften immediately. For night shooting, use two desk lamps angled in from left and right at roughly 45 degrees, about 30cm away, with brightness turned down to the lowest setting to avoid overexposure. Keep one principle in mind: the softer the light, the more convincing the brick texture looks.

## Step 2: Dial In Your Phone Camera

Open Pro mode and set three things: fix ISO at 100–400 to prevent noise; set white balance to 5600K so white bricks do not turn yellow; use a shutter speed between 1/60s and 1/125s so handheld shots stay sharp. When shooting, tap and hold on your subject to lock focus and exposure, then drag down slightly to reduce exposure compensation by 0.3 to 0.7 stops — that preserves highlight detail. If your phone has no Pro mode, tap the sun icon on screen to lower exposure manually. For macro shots, hold the phone 5–8cm from the build and use 2x zoom instead of moving in closer; the image quality is better.

## Step 3: Choose a Background, Find Your Angle, Then Edit

For backgrounds, use solid-color card stock or a matte tabletop. Keep one sheet each in black, white and gray — the whole set costs under 20 yuan. Avoid busy patterns that pull attention away from the build. On angles: a 45-degree low angle gives the most immersive feel, a top-down shot suits flat layouts, and a side-on eye-level view works best for architectural detail. After shooting, edit in Snapseed or Xingtu: lift shadows by 10–15%, pull highlights down by 10%, add a touch of clarity, and finally unify the color temperature. Shoot 20–30 frames per build and publish only the best 3–5. For more shooting examples and original image parameters, visit brickplan.cn to see what other builders share.

## FAQ

**Q: Can a phone really produce professional-looking brick photos?**

A: Yes. A phone's main camera is more than capable — lighting and settings are what matter. Set ISO and white balance as described here, lock focus, and pair that with soft light and a solid background. Your keeper rate will climb from under 30% to over 80%, which is more than enough for social media.

**Q: What if my brick photos keep catching reflections?**

A: Reflections come mainly from hard, direct light. Switch to a diffused source — light through white paper, or add a layer of tracing paper in front of the lamp — and drop exposure compensation by 0.3 stops. If a small spot still reflects, paint over it with the healing brush in post; it usually takes two minutes to clean up.

**Q: Is a macro lens worth buying?**

A: Not if you only shoot brick detail occasionally. Your phone's 2x zoom at a working distance of about 8cm already covers most needs. If you shoot minifigures or printed parts close up every week, then consider a clip-on macro lens in the few-dozen-yuan range — the value is much better at that frequency.

**Q: Do dark and light bricks need different settings?**

A: Yes. Dark bricks absorb light, so add 0.3 to 0.7 stops of exposure compensation and strengthen your fill light. Light bricks blow out easily, so lower exposure instead. Meter each scene separately, aiming for white bricks that are not overexposed and black bricks that still show texture — getting both right is the ideal.
