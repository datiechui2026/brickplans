# Airplane MOC Blueprint Review: Fighters and Airliners

## Product Overview

Airplane MOCs are one of the most visually striking categories in brick building. Whether it is a sleek fighter jet, an elegant airliner, or a vintage biplane with a propeller, bricks can capture the aerodynamics of a flying machine in a way no other medium quite matches. In the BrickPlans community, airplane blueprints stay consistently active, with designers releasing new original airframes all the time.

Airplane MOCs mostly fall into two camps: **military aviation** (fighters, bombers, transports) and **civil aviation** (airliners, business jets, seaplanes). Military designs emphasize weapon loadouts and maneuverability, while civil designs focus more on smooth fuselage curves and interior detail.

## Build Difficulty

Difficulty varies a lot by airframe. The general ratings look like this:

- **Beginner (★★☆☆☆)**: small propeller aircraft, 200-500 pieces, simple structure
- **Intermediate (★★★☆☆)**: mid-size fighters and business jets, 500-1200 pieces, careful attention needed on wing angles
- **Advanced (★★★★☆)**: large warbirds and airliners, 1200-2500 pieces, includes landing gear and internal structure
- **Expert (★★★★★)**: flagship airliners and heavy fighters, 2500+ pieces, full interior plus movable control surfaces

The hardest part of any airplane MOC is handling the **wing curvature and the fuselage transition**. Getting a smooth shape demands a lot of slope bricks and curved bricks.

## Parts Review

Airplane MOCs favor a specific spread of parts:

- **Color profile**: white, light gray and dark blue dominate; military builds also bring in green and camouflage schemes
- **Signature parts**: transparent cockpit canopies, engine fan blades and landing gear wheel sets are the parts that say "aircraft" at a glance
- **Structural strength**: the wing-to-fuselage joint is the weak point under load, and a good blueprint will design internal reinforcement there

On brands, **Gobricks** performs especially well for airplane MOCs thanks to part precision, and its color consistency on white parts is well controlled. **Double Eagle** transparent elements have good light transmission, which makes them a solid choice for cockpits.

## Building Experience

The build itself is where the fun lives. You start with the fuselage skeleton, then lay on the skin, mount the wings, add the tail and power system, and finish with the landing gear and detail work. A well-made blueprint walks you through the whole aircraft from nose to tail.

Worth calling out: many airplane blueprints include **movable parts** — retractable landing gear, spinning engine fans, working ailerons and rudders. These features add a lot to how playable the finished model is. On [brickplan.cn](https://brickplan.cn) you can filter by the movable tag to jump straight to airplane designs with working components.

## Finished Model Display

A completed airplane MOC is ideal for hanging display or for sitting on a dedicated stand. At 1:48 scale a fighter measures roughly 20-25cm long, while a 1:72 airliner can reach 40-50cm. Pair it with a clear display stand and the aircraft looks like it is cruising in open sky — the visual payoff is excellent.

Many builders also design ground support scenes around their aircraft, adding fuel trucks, tow tractors and ground crew to bring the whole airport diorama to life.
