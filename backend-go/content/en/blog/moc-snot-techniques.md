# SNOT Techniques for MOC Builds: Hide Every Stud

Most builders' first MOC is a squared-off pile of bricks with studs bristling from every surface. It holds together fine, but something always feels missing. Then you try SNOT for the first time and realize bricks can be attached sideways, flipped over, and locked together with every stud hidden — leaving walls that are flat, clean and smooth. That is what this guide to SNOT techniques for MOC building is about.

SNOT stands for "Studs Not On Top." It is the umbrella name for a whole family of connections that break the standard stack-it-upward rule. Once you have these techniques, you unlock the most important upgrade in MOC work: going from simply stacking bricks to actually designing a model.

## What Is SNOT and Why Does It Matter for MOCs?

The conventional approach puts studs facing up and builds layer on layer. SNOT breaks that rule, letting parts connect at all kinds of angles and orientations so the finished model sits closer to the real thing.

**The core value of SNOT:**

- **Removes the studded look**: walls, roofs and floors become flat and smooth
- **Enables detail**: carving, window frames and surface texture all become far more precise
- **Breaks the shape**: sloped walls, curved surfaces and recessed structures become possible
- **Lifts the finish**: the model immediately reads as a scale model rather than a toy

Put simply, SNOT technique is the dividing line between a beginner build and a master-level one.

## Sideways Building: The Most Basic and Most-Used SNOT Move

Sideways building is the foundation of every SNOT skill. The idea is straightforward: use parts that carry a side stud or an anti-stud to turn studs that normally face upward onto a vertical face, giving you a smooth side wall.

Common "direction-changing" parts include:

| Part type | Use |
| --- | --- |
| 1x1 brick with side stud | Adds a sideways connection point to a standard brick |
| Pin with a stud on the head | The key connector for sideways building |
| 1x2 brick with side studs | Moves studs from the top face to the side |
| Turntable | Delivers rotation and angle changes |

**The basic sideways building sequence:**

1. Place the side-stud connector on your base
2. Clip an anti-stud part onto the side stud
3. Keep laying bricks horizontally on top of that anti-stud part
4. You now have a smooth vertical wall

Once you can run this sequence, you can produce smooth building facades, the flanks of a vehicle, and even faces and expressions on figures.

## Reverse Attachment: The Art of Hiding Studs

If sideways building is about turning, reverse attachment is about concealing. The principle: behind any surface that needs to be smooth, build a clever part arrangement that traps the studs where nobody can see them.

**Common reverse attachment methods:**

- **Anti-stud plates**: mount connectors on the underside of a plate so the front face is completely flat
- **Inner lining structure**: build a studded skeleton first, then clip smooth panels over the outside
- **Inverted mounting**: flip parts around so the studs face inward and the clean face points outward

Reverse attachment is especially good for furniture tops, wall decoration and statue bases — anywhere the finish needs to be fine. It is also one of the clearest signals of an experienced builder.

## Advanced Combinations: SNOT in Real Builds

Individual tricks are easy to learn. Combining them is where real SNOT technique lives. Here are some combinations you can put to work straight away:

### 1. Smooth exterior walls

Build an entire flat wall with sideways techniques, handle the corners with reverse attachment, then add a decorative line of 1x1 studded bricks. You get detail without a flat, lifeless surface.

### 2. Curves and slopes

Pair turntables with hinge parts so your SNOT structure can turn corners. Round towers, curved roofs and angled eaves all become simple.

### 3. Detailed furniture

Use reverse-attached panels for the surfaces of tables, chairs and cabinets, then build drawer fronts and handles from small SNOT assemblies. A convincing interior scene follows quickly.

### 4. Figures and mechs

Apply SNOT to facial expressions, shoulder curves and mechanical joints. Characters become more expressive and mechs gain a much better finish.

**Practical tips:**

- Watch part orientation when disassembling — many SNOT structures fall apart the moment you pull one piece
- Test on a small area first, confirm it is solid, then expand across the whole surface
- Temporarily mark connection points with parts in a contrasting colour so you can adjust easily
- Lean on plates and perforated parts — they are usually the key to a SNOT assembly

## Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

New builders hit the same traps when learning SNOT. Here is how to sidestep them:

1. **Looks over strength**: a smooth structure without an internal frame will fall apart. Always reinforce it
2. **Parts facing the wrong way**: anti-studs and regular studs are easy to confuse, so check the stud direction before pressing parts together
3. **Chasing total smoothness**: making every wall flat actually destroys the depth. Keep some studs as texture and the model reads better
4. **Ignoring load paths**: large MOCs need to carry weight, and side-mounted structures cannot take much pressure

## Conclusion: Make SNOT Part of Your Core Skill Set

SNOT technique is not some unreachable expert-only ability. It is a fundamental skill every builder who is serious about MOC work should own. Start with sideways building, work your way into reverse attachment, then combine the two — and you will watch your models get sharper and closer to the picture in your head.
