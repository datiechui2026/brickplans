# Brick Villa MOC Review: A Dream Garden House in Bricks

Brick villas have long been one of the most popular branches of architecture MOCs, and builders keep coming back to them for a simple reason: a house gives you a facade to detail, an interior to design and a structure to engineer all at once. We recently got our hands on a villa design billed as a "garden house," and it promises precision both outside and in. Here is our full review.

## Exterior Design: A Facade Full of Detail

This villa MOC is instantly recognisable. Three staggered pitched roof levels, large floor-to-ceiling windows and a front yard enclosed by a garden fence combine into a classic European-style house silhouette. Rather than relying on one big gesture, the designer builds character through contrast: brown and white brick-textured elements sit against dark window frames, so the facade reads in clear layers instead of flattening into a single wall.

The roof does much of the work here. It is assembled from stacked angled and slope pieces, and the transitions between roof levels look natural rather than abrupt. Proportions hold together across all three storeys, which is harder to pull off than it sounds — many house MOCs lose their balance once a third level goes on top. The result has that "dream home" quality that makes it look right at home in a display cabinet.

## Interior Structure: A Sensible Floor Plan

Open the instructions and the interior planning turns out to be just as considered as the shell. The ground floor holds a living room and an open-plan kitchen. The second floor carries two bedrooms and a study. The third floor is given over to a terrace and a storage room. The staircase sits centrally, which keeps circulation smooth and means no room is stranded at the end of a dead-end corridor. Each functional space is scaled appropriately for the footprint — nothing feels crammed, and nothing feels like leftover space.

The standout feature is the removable floor design. Floor plates can be lifted out, letting you look straight down into the rooms below or even rework the interior layout yourself. This is the detail that lifts the model from a static display piece to something you can actually play with, and it is the kind of thing that separates a well-thought-out MOC from a pretty facade with nothing behind it.

## Parts List and Build Experience

The villa uses roughly 3,200 pieces. The variety is broad, but the bulk of the inventory is made up of common standard elements, which means most builders can source the whole thing from a parts list without hunting for rare pieces or paying collector prices for a handful of odd moulds.

The build itself runs about 6 to 8 hours. The instructions are clearly labelled and the step sequence is logical, so you rarely hit that familiar problem of hunting for a piece you swear should be in the bag. The one place to slow down is the roof: it calls for a relatively large number of special angled pieces, and beginners will want to check each one against the diagram carefully rather than eyeballing the shape. Sort those pieces out before you start the roof section and the rest of the build flows smoothly.

## Verdict

Overall, this villa MOC performs well on all three fronts that matter for a building like this: the shape is faithfully realised, the interior is genuinely complete rather than an afterthought, and the build experience holds up over the full 3,200 pieces. It is a strong fit for architecture MOC fans looking for a display piece with substance — and because of the removable floors and open interior, it also works well as a shared build with kids, who tend to enjoy seeing the rooms appear as the walls go up.

Whether you plan to keep it in a cabinet or leave it open for play, it delivers a satisfying sense of accomplishment at the end. If you have been looking for a brick villa MOC worth committing a weekend to, this design is well worth a try.
