# Brick Bird's Nest MOC: Weaving the Steel Lattice Exterior

Bottom line first: in a brick-built Bird's Nest, almost all of the difficulty sits in the woven steel lattice on the outside. Once you see that crossed, offset 1x2 bricks reproduce that weave, the rest of the build falls into place. The finished model runs to roughly 1,800 parts, takes about 6 hours, and measures 40 cm long, 34 cm wide and 15 cm tall. The full instructions are downloadable on brickplan.cn.

**Step 1: Base and seating bowl.** Tile the base with 2x4 plates in two flat layers; the double skin keeps the footprint from flexing later when you press columns down onto it. Inside that footprint, stack ordinary bricks into a bowl-shaped seating tier. Each row sets back two studs from the row below, which produces the rake of a real grandstand without needing a single sloped piece. Scatter 1x1 round plates in mixed colors across the seating surface to suggest a crowd. Random placement matters more than any pattern here, so resist the urge to make it tidy.

**Step 2: The outer steel ring.** Stand columns around the edge of the base, then bridge them with 1x2 and 1x4 bricks laid in a crosshatch so the courses overlap in a # grid. Offset each successive layer by half a brick. That deliberate mismatch is what reads as weaving rather than as stacking, and it is the single most important trick for getting the Bird's Nest silhouette. Work around the ring in one direction and check as you go that the diagonals stay visually continuous; a lattice that drifts out of step is very hard to correct later without tearing sections out.

**Step 3: The top ring beam.** Long bar pieces run right around the top to close an elliptical ring beam, fixed back to the lattice with connector pieces. Leave openings on the east and west sides. The real stadium's roofline is asymmetric, and those two gaps are what stop the model from looking like a generic oval bowl with a rim on it.

**Step 4: Entrance and details.** On the south side, build a sunken entrance and glaze it with transparent pieces for the curtain wall. A few red pieces dotted across the facade echo the stadium's night lighting. Finish with a low 1x2 wall around the perimeter of the base. It costs almost no parts and makes the model read as a finished object rather than a fragment of one.

Seen from a high angle, the two structures stay clearly separate: the concave bowl of the seating inside, the woven steel skin outside. brickplan.cn carries the complete parts list, with about 1,800 parts in the main structure and 1x2 bricks making up roughly 40 percent of them. Sort those by color before you start and you will save a good chunk of the build time that would otherwise go on hunting for parts in a pile.

## Common questions

**Q: How do I get a brick Bird's Nest MOC to actually look like the Bird's Nest?**

A: The outer lattice has to be crosshatched and offset, stacking the 1x2 bricks like a weave instead of lining them up flush. The seating has to be concave, bowl-style, and the lattice steps over half a brick on every layer. Get those two things right and the shape reads immediately, even before the details go on.

**Q: What do 1,800 parts cost?**

A: At an average of ¥0.3 per loose part, roughly ¥540. If you mix in parts you already own, the cost can drop to about ¥200. The brickplan.cn instructions list the quantity needed for each part type, so you can compare against your own stock before deciding what to order.

**Q: Can the model be lit?**

A: Yes. Leave a cavity about 3 cm high inside the base and run two LED strips through it; the light looks best coming out through the gaps in the lattice. Warm white is the safe choice. For the red night-time look, add a red translucent plate in front of the strip.

**Q: How is this different from an ordinary stadium brick set?**

A: Standard stadium sets are square stands plus a roof. The Bird's Nest MOC is an elliptical bowl with a woven steel facade. The offset lattice is far more demanding to build, so the model is noticeably harder, but it is also far more recognizable when it is done.
