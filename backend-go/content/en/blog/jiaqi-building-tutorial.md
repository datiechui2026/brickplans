# Jiaqi Brick Building Tutorial: Beginner to Confident Builder

Jiaqi has built a following among brick fans for its original themes, friendly prices and strong design work. Whether you have just opened your first set or you are looking to tighten up your building habits, this guide walks through the whole process: sorting the box, reading the instructions, the techniques that keep builds smooth, and what to do when something goes wrong.

## 1. Opening the Box and Sorting Parts

A brand-new Jiaqi set is tempting to start immediately, but a few minutes of sorting will save you far more time later.

Start with a flat, well-lit table and lay down a light-coloured mat or a sorting tray so small parts cannot roll away and vanish. Open the packaging and separate the contents by colour and by type into small tubs or compartmented trays. Jiaqi normally splits parts across several numbered bags, so open one bag at a time and sort it before moving to the next. Tipping everything out at once is the fastest route to a pile of mixed parts you cannot search through.

Sort into broad everyday groups: basic bricks, plates, technic parts (axles, gears, pins), minifigure parts, and transparent or otherwise special pieces. Keep parts that share a colour but differ in shape in separate compartments. When you build you locate a part by two things at once — colour and shape — so the finer your sorting, the quicker you find it.

## 2. Reading the Instructions

Understanding the manual is the single most important skill in this whole process. Jiaqi instruction booklets use step-by-step diagrams: every step has a clear 3D render showing the part types and quantities required.

A few things to watch for:

- **Check the step number.** The current step is marked at the top or bottom right of the page (Step 1, Step 2 and so on). Build in order and do not skip ahead.
- **Follow the arrows and highlights.** Newly added parts are picked out in colour or with arrows. That means only the highlighted area goes on in this step; everything else is structure you already finished.
- **Learn the connection types.** Arrows and projection lines show how parts engage. Note the difference between pushing a part in horizontally and pressing it down vertically.
- **Watch for mirrored parts.** Plenty of models are symmetrical left to right, and the instructions will call out a mirrored sub-build. Distinguish left from right — a flipped assembly is easy to miss and annoying to undo.

Once these reading habits are in place, even a large model breaks down into a run of small, clear steps. That is the core idea here: every complex build is just a stack of simple ones.

## 3. Building Techniques That Keep Things Smooth

A handful of practical habits make a noticeable difference to both speed and how solid the finished model feels:

1. **Build in rows, press in layers.** Assemble a row or a full layer loosely, then press the whole group down together rather than forcing each stud individually. The load spreads evenly and you avoid ugly gaps between parts.
2. **Press correctly.** On larger plates, push with both thumbs working from the outer edges toward the centre, or brace the assembly against the edge of the table. Never hammer a single point with force — you will stress the part and your fingers.
3. **Use the separator.** Some Jiaqi sets include a brick separator. To remove a wrongly placed part, slot the tool into the seam and lever gently; it takes less effort and leaves the part undamaged. Without one, a fingernail or a piece of stiff plastic works, but watch for scratches.
4. **Check symmetry and orientation immediately.** On mirrored structures, compare the finished side against the instructions right away. Finding an error at the end means a full rebuild.
5. **Leave some play in technic assemblies.** With axles, gears and pins, line the holes up first and twist the part in rather than shoving it. It goes in more smoothly and does not chew up the end of the axle.

With these in place, your builds will go faster and stand up better.

## 4. Common Problems and Fixes

Even experienced builders hit snags. These are the ones that come up most often:

- **Parts that are too tight or too loose.** For a tight fit, lightly sand the mating surface or use the separator to help press it home. For a loose fit, check whether you have used the wrong variant, or swap in a spare of the same colour.
- **Missing or damaged parts.** Work through the parts list in the manual item by item first. If something genuinely is missing, contact Jiaqi customer service for a replacement — the set number is usually all you need to look it up.
- **Discovering a mistake halfway through.** Stay calm, then use the separator to undo back to the step where things went wrong and re-check the diagram. Work from the outside in, easiest connections first.
- **Foggy or scratched transparent parts.** Handle clear pieces as little as possible and wipe them with a slightly damp soft cloth. Dry paper towels are what leave the fine scratches.

None of these are reasons to panic. Patience and a careful read of the instructions solve almost everything.

## 5. Storing and Displaying Your Build

Finishing the build is only half the job — looking after it keeps the model in good shape. Dust the surface with a soft brush or a small air blower, and keep the model out of direct sunlight so the parts do not fade. Display it on a stable, level surface, and consider an acrylic display case if dust and humidity are a problem. For moving technic models, work the joints occasionally so the connections do not stiffen up from sitting still for months.

## 6. Where to Go Next

You now have the full workflow: opening and sorting, reading the manual, building technique and troubleshooting. Whether you are working on a street scene, a mech or a technic model, careful step-by-step work gets you a result you will be happy with. Build more sets, try different themes, and your skill level will climb without you really noticing.
