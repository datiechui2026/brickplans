# Brick Blueprint Website Review: Best Sites for MOC Instructions

Anyone who builds MOCs knows the hardest part isn't buying the parts — it's finding a blueprint you can actually trust. Brick instruction sites have multiplied over the past couple of years, and quality varies wildly. I've used more than a dozen of them, and this review pulls together hands-on experience to explain how to pick the right source for your builds.

## The Main Categories of Instruction Sites

Today's brick blueprint sites fall roughly into three groups. The first is the classic overseas sites: huge libraries, long histories, but English-first interfaces that mean you spend a while working out the registration flow before you can download anything. The second is the aggregator sites, which pull popular instructions into one place. They update fast, but the sources are mixed, and it's common to hit missing parts lists or compressed, low-res images. The third is the Chinese-language community platforms, such as brickplan.cn, which focus on the experience of Chinese-speaking builders — instructions and parts lists can be previewed directly in the browser, which is the friendliest option for newcomers.

## Instruction Quality and Clarity

Quality is the metric I care about most. Take the same modular building instruction: one site offers a high-resolution PDF original where part numbers and step images stay sharp when you zoom in; another only has a low-resolution screenshot where you can't even tell the colors apart halfway through the build. In testing, the established sites had the most consistent high-res files, but many popular instructions sit behind a subscription before you can get the original. Community platforms usually have files uploaded by the original designer, so completeness is better guaranteed — and if a part is missing from the list, you can report it straight in the comments.

## Update Frequency and Popular Themes

How often a site updates decides whether you get to build new designs early. Technic, modular buildings and mecha have been the hottest themes of the past two years, and the leading sites post something new almost every week. Aggregators move fastest, but popular instructions are often claimed first by whoever uploads them. Original-design platforms update on a steadier rhythm with more controllable quality. If you follow classic re-creations or niche subjects, look to individual designers in the Chinese-language community — their instructions tend to match the parts libraries local builders actually have on hand.

## Free vs Paid Models

Pricing differs a lot between sites. Some make basic instructions free and charge for high-resolution versions; others run a subscription where one payment unlocks everything. Check each platform's page for current prices — I won't list them here. My rule of thumb: build a few free instructions first, confirm the clarity and parts lists meet your expectations, then decide whether to pay. For builders on a tight budget, community platforms like brickplan.cn often have free instructions shared by players themselves, which is good value.

## Recommendations

Taken together, there is no single best brick blueprint site — only the one that suits you. If your English is solid, spend time on the established overseas sites. If you want speed and trending themes, watch the aggregator platforms. For most Chinese-speaking builders, I'd start with a community platform: preview the instructions, download, and talk to the designer whenever you need to. Hopefully this review of brick blueprint sites saves you some wrong turns and gets you to a finished MOC you're happy with sooner.
